The 45.8% net income growth is stale: H1 2026 GAAP net income fell 29.5% to $78.9M, adjusted net income 24.5%. ## 1. What the business actually does
Jefferson Capital buys portfolios of charged-off and insolvency consumer receivables at deep discounts and collects them across the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and Latin America. In 2025 it invested $832.0M for $13.03B of face value, 6.4 cents on the dollar, and collected $998.7M (10-K, Item 7). Sellers are Fortune 500 creditors, banks, fintechs, telecoms and auto lenders; the top five were 45.2% of 2025 purchases and the top one 23.6% (10-K, Item 1). Collections run through a legal channel and a voluntary channel, with domestic call centres deliberately outsourced.

## 2. Why it is mispriced, the edge case
A fifteen-month-old IPO with a controlling sponsor selling into an optical earnings decline. J.C. Flowers holds 32,721,807 shares, about 53%, every one registered for resale on the July 2026 S-3 shelf (10-Q; DEF 14A 2026). Flowers sold 11.0M shares at $19.63 in January and the CEO 385,000 at $20.50 alongside (Form 4, 2026-01-13 and 2026-01-14); no insider bought in twelve months. Meanwhile reported EPS looks like it is falling, because H1 2025 carried a partnership tax rate and an $8.3M compensation accrual reversal while H1 2026 carries a full rate and $16.9M of IPO restricted-stock amortisation, and because Conn's contributed $32.4M less of segment operating income (10-Q, Item 2). A screener sees earnings down 30%; the cash business grew collections 18%. The gap is real but not large: at 9.2x normalised earnings and 3.1x tangible book nothing is priced for distress, and a 53% holder with registered stock is a lid on the multiple until it clears.

## 3. Credit quality and unit economics
The credit question for a purchaser is whether ERC is honest. ERC is $3,364.2M, 46% collectable through 2027, with $1.1B expected in twelve months (Q2 call, CEO). A 1% error moves pre-tax income by $28.3M, about 15% of normalised pre-tax profit (10-K, Item 7). Changes in recoveries have been positive and rising: $6.0M in FY2025, $16.1M in H1 2026, $9.0M in Q2 alone. The CFO said the model's objective "is accuracy and not necessarily conservatism" and guided to single-digit millions a quarter (Q2 call). Cash efficiency was 72.2% in Q2, 67.8% excluding Conn's and Bluestem, against a high-60s guide. Legal collections rose 54% to $64M, front-loading court costs, up $9.3M to $21.7M; H1 operating cash flow fell to $82.9M from $210.0M partly for that reason (10-Q, Item 2).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Face debt $1,425.9M: $400M of 9.50% notes due 2029, $600M of 8.25% notes due 2030 after a $100M add-on on 20 August 2026, and $225.9M drawn on a $1.15B revolver maturing 2028 (10-Q; 8-K 2026-08-21). The $300M of 6.00% notes due August 2026 were defeased on 13 August with revolver draws, so the blended coupon is stepping up. Tangible equity of $413M supports a $1.95B receivables book, and additional paid-in capital is negative $32.7M, the residue of pre-IPO distributions. The company repurchased 3.0M shares for $58.9M in January to support the sponsor's offering and pays $0.24 a quarter (8-K 2026-08-13). Founder-CEO David Burton owns 3.39M shares, 5.5%, plus 3.06M unvested restricted, and took $3.8M in 2025 including $2.5M of dividends on his own equity. Flowers retains consent rights over acquisitions above $100M and debt above $150M (DEF 14A 2026).

## 5. Management: what they said versus what they did
No guidance is given, so the test is limited. Leverage at 1.71x, forward flows at $480.7M and July deployments of $185M are consistent with the strategy described. Asked whether ERC growth was at risk, Burton answered "the clear answer is no" (Q2 call). Against that, management guided cash efficiency to the high 60s and court costs to hold at Q2 levels, both checkable in Q3, and entering Mexico, adding auto as a third performing asset class and buying the $196.3M Bluestem portfolio inside eighteen months is a lot of new direction for a business whose stated edge is underwriting discipline.

## 6. Valuation
$141.4M normalised on 61.7M fully diluted shares is $2.29 a share. Base: collections compound about 10% as the 2025 and 2026 vintages season, court costs normalise, normalised EPS reaches $2.50 in 2027 at 9x, so $22.50 plus about $1.00 of dividends, $23.50. Bear: unemployment passes the 6% to 7% level Burton names as where liquidation rates break (Q2 call), ERC is cut 10%, a $283M pre-tax charge on the company's own sensitivity that removes two years of profit and half of tangible equity; EPS $1.60 at 6x, $10. Bull: the auto inflection holds, deployments run $650M a year, ERC compounds mid-teens, Flowers finishes selling and the stock rerates to 12x $2.80 in 2028, $34. Weighted 50/25/25 gives about $22.75 against $20.96, roughly 9% upside. Cross-check: ERC of $3,364.2M at 72.2% cash efficiency is about $2,429M of net cash, less $1,405.5M of net debt leaves $1,024M undiscounted for equity from the existing book against $1,293M of market value, so redeployment does the rest of the work. Reverse DCF: at 9.2x normalised earnings with a 4.6% dividend, the price implies essentially no real long-term growth in normalised after-tax profit.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 results in November show whether July's $185M and the auto push lift ERC back above $3.38B. Further sales under the S-3, or their completion, move the overhang either way. FY2026 results in March 2027 give the first clean full year at a normal tax rate. Float growth improves index eligibility.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
Risks: a consumer recession cuts liquidation rates; CFPB or state action restricts legal-channel collection; client concentration at 23.6%; and as a controlled, emerging-growth company there is no auditor attestation on internal controls yet (10-K, Item 1A).
1. Total ERC below $3,364.2M at both 30 September and 31 December 2026, that is two more sequential declines. Redeployment would not be replacing runoff.
2. Changes in recoveries prints negative in any quarter, or goes negative on a twelve-month basis. Each 1% of ERC is $28.3M pre-tax.
3. Cash efficiency excluding Conn's and Bluestem below 65% for two consecutive quarters, against the high-60s guide.
4. Net debt to adjusted cash EBITDA above 2.5x, the top of management's range, or Flowers selling more than half its remaining 32.7M shares below $20.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Jefferson Capital is a good debt purchaser wearing a misleading screen row: the 7.1x P/E comes from a year taxed at 13.9% because the company was still largely a partnership, and the apparent 30% earnings decline in H1 2026 is mostly a full tax rate, IPO stock compensation and the roll-off of an exceptional Conn's portfolio, while the cash business grew collections 18% and ERC 18% at a sector-leading 72.2% cash efficiency ratio. Normalised at 25% tax it earns about $141M, so you pay 9.2x earnings and 3.1x tangible book for a 37% return on tangible equity and a 4.6% dividend, a fair price rather than a bargain. Two things keep it off the buy list: J.C. Flowers still owns 53% with every share registered for resale and has sold 11 million in eight months, and $413M of tangible equity supports a $1.95B receivables book where a 1% error in estimated remaining collections costs $28M pre-tax. ERC resuming sequential growth, or the sponsor stake clearing, would make this an idea.
