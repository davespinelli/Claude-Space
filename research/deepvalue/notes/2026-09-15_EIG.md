## 1. What the business actually does
A Reno-based specialty writer of guaranteed-cost workers' compensation for small and mid-sized businesses in the lowest four of seven hazard groups, sold through independent agencies and a direct channel (Cerity), operating everywhere except the four exclusive state-fund states, with 46% of gross premiums written in California (10-K, Item 1). All five insurance subsidiaries are rated "A" (Excellent) by AM Best. Underwriting is subsidised by float: net investment income was $116.7M in 2025 on a $2.45B portfolio. A 1999 loss portfolio transfer covering the old Nevada state fund runs off through an $85.3M Deferred Gain amortising about $6M a year. An excess workers' compensation product launched in February 2026 bound its first policy in June (10-Q, Item 2).

## 2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case
There is none, and that caps the verdict. The GAAP artefact is real, since CY2025 net income of $10.8M is not run-rate, but the market fixed it long ago: the stock is 3.0% below its 52-week high after a 29.4% six-month gain on $6.2M of daily volume (universe_v2.csv, 2026-09-14 screen). No spin, no index event, no forced seller. The largest holder, BlackRock at 19.8%, is also a paid investment manager to the company (DEF 14A). The one buyer of size was the company, which took 2,464,081 shares for $104.5M in H1 2026 at about $42.41, roughly 15% below today's price (10-Q, Stock Repurchases); across the 12 most recent of 44 Form 4 filings, all 14 rows were grants or tax withholding, a capped sample that signals nothing either way.

## 3. Combined ratio and reserve development
This decides it. The combined ratio excluding the LPT went 96.0% in 2023, 98.6% in 2024 and 111.7% in 2025 (10-K, Item 7). Two things broke together: the current accident year loss and LAE ratio rose about eight points to 72.0%, which management attributes entirely to "increased cumulative trauma (CT) claim frequency in California," and prior-year development swung from $18.4M favourable to $39.6M adverse, concentrated in accident years 2023 and 2024, the two most recent closed years.

The accident year pick has not improved since. It was 72.0% on voluntary business in both Q1 and Q2 2026, "consistent with the same ratio recorded for accident year 2025" (8-Ks 2026-04-29, 2026-07-29). Reserves have held, with no voluntary development in either quarter and a Q2 full actuarial review confirming it, which is genuinely reassuring. But the combined ratio excluding LPT was 107.7% in Q1 2026 against 102.8% a year earlier, and 106.7% in Q2 against 106.4%, with Q2 flattered about 150 basis points by a $2.5M premium restitution, so the underlying figure was nearer 108%. The expense ratio is rising too, 22.8% versus 21.7%, because underwriting expenses fell $3.4M while the premium base fell $24.2M. Eighteen months of re-underwriting have produced a 20% smaller book at the same loss pick and a worse expense ratio.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Investments, cash and equivalents were $2,449.4M at 30 June 2026 against $1,775.2M of unpaid losses and LAE. Of the $125.0M of debt, $105.0M is FHLB advances at 3.74-3.87% maturing mostly May 2029 and $20.0M a revolver draw at 5.15%; covenants require consolidated net worth above $800.0M and debt to capitalisation under 35%, and the company reports compliance. The constraint is at the holding company: EICN and ECIC each paid their maximum ordinary dividend in Q1 2026 and "cannot pay dividends for the remainder of 2026 without prior regulatory approval," leaving $34.2M at EHI to fund a $1.36 annual dividend and $113.0M of remaining repurchase authorisation (10-Q, Liquidity). Pay is aligned in form: the short-term metric, Adjusted GAAP Calendar Year Combined Ratio, printed 112.0% against a 98.5% target and bonuses paid at 25-27% of target, while the long-term PSU metric is change in adjusted book value per share, which a sub-book buyback mechanically improves (DEF 14A).

## 5. Management: what they said versus what they did
CEO Katherine Antonello called Q2 "another quarter defined by discipline" and led with "diluted earnings per share grew 29% year-over-year and adjusted earnings per share grew 46%, even as net income was essentially flat." Both are true and both are share count. Her Q1 claim that "the steps we took in 2025 to tighten pricing and underwriting are working as intended" is not visible in the loss pick she herself reports as unchanged at 72.0%. In 2025 the company returned $217.2M to holders and grew adjusted book value per share by $0.24, or 0.5% (10-K, Item 7): the year's entire earnings power went to the underwriting loss, and buyback accretion barely covered it.

## 6. Valuation
Base, 50%: the combined ratio excluding LPT stays near 106%, net premiums earned stabilise near $680M, net investment income holds near $108M, adjusted net income runs $45-50M and buybacks slow; adjusted book value per share reaches about $56 and the market keeps paying 0.95x, so $53 plus $1.36 of dividends, or $54. Bear, 25%: CT frequency keeps running, the accident year pick moves to 75%, accident years 2023-2025 take another $30-40M of strengthening, the combined ratio reaches 112%, adjusted earnings go to zero and the multiple falls to 0.75x, so about $39. Bull, 25%: re-underwriting earns through, the accident year ratio falls to 68% by late 2027 with the expense ratio back to 21.5%, the combined ratio reaches 100%, adjusted net income is about $80M or $4.85 per share on 16.5M shares, and the market pays 1.15x adjusted book, so about $67. Probability-weighted value is $53.50, roughly 7% above price.

Reverse DCF: at 0.94x adjusted book, a 9% cost of equity and 2% growth, the price implies a sustainable return on adjusted equity of about 8.6%, roughly $82M a year. With $110M of net investment income less $5M of interest, that requires underwriting at break-even, a combined ratio near 100%, against the 106.7% just reported and 111.7% last year.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
Risks: reserve inadequacy on a long-tail book already strengthened once; single-state concentration where Item 1A concedes cumulative trauma frequency "accelerated beyond historical levels"; holdco dividend capacity exhausted for 2026; and debt raised to buy stock rather than to write business.

Kill criteria, any one of which makes this PASS wrong:
1. Current accident year loss and LAE ratio excluding LPT on voluntary business below 70.0% for two consecutive quarters, as disclosed in the quarterly supplement.
2. Gross premiums written declining less than 5% year over year while the combined ratio excluding LPT prints below 102%.
3. Adverse prior accident year development on voluntary business above $10M in any quarter, or above $25M for a full year, which confirms the bear case instead.
4. Quarterly repurchases below $10M for two consecutive quarters, or holding company cash and investments below $25M against $34.2M at 30 June 2026, meaning the levered buyback has hit its capital constraint.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
PASS, conviction 3. Employers Holdings is a well-rated, conservatively reserved workers' compensation writer whose screen row genuinely misleads, since CY2025 net income of $10.8M carried $39.6M of adverse reserve development and $20.4M of realised investment losses, and the screen's 1.11x tangible book ignores the $85.3M Deferred Gain that makes the real multiple 0.94x adjusted book. Correcting the artefact does not produce an idea, because there is no edge case and no seller: the stock sits 3.0% below its 52-week high after a 29.4% six-month run, and the only identifiable buyer of size was the company itself at about $42.41 in the first half. What you buy at $50.01 is 18-19x normalised after-tax profit, or 0.94x book against a trailing adjusted return on equity of 1.1%, for a business whose combined ratio excluding the LPT has gone 96.0%, 98.6%, 111.7% and 106.7%, whose current accident year loss pick is unchanged at 72.0% after eighteen months of re-underwriting, whose gross premiums are down 19.6% and whose expense ratio is rising on the shrinking base. The per-share growth management leads with is a buyback funded with $125.0M of new debt at a holding company whose two largest subsidiaries cannot pay it another ordinary dividend this year, and the reverse DCF says the price already assumes a combined ratio near 100%. Wait for two consecutive quarters of an accident year loss ratio below 70% and this becomes a real idea; until the loss pick moves, the equity is a bond portfolio with an underwriting loss attached.
