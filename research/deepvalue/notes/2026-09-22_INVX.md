## 1. What the business actually does
Innovex makes and rents consumable downhole products used across the life of an oil or gas well: liner hangers, frac plugs, centralisers, float equipment, fishing tools, artificial-lift accessories, subsea and surface wellheads. The pitch is "Big Impact, Small Ticket": cheap parts whose failure is expensive (10-K 2026, Item 1). About 70% of 2025 revenue was product sales, 17% rental and 13% services; 52% came from US and Canadian land. It holds roughly 769 patents and serves 1,719 active customers, the top ten at 26% of revenue, with Saudi Aramco the largest end user. The company was assembled in September 2024, when Legacy Innovex reverse-merged into Dril-Quip and took the Innovex name; DWS, SCF, Citadel, DIS and TCO followed (10-K 2026, Item 1; 10-Q Q2 2026).

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is none that the price reflects. The one genuine technical seller is Amberjack Capital, which held 33.2% in March 2026 (DEF 14A 2026), is out of lock-up under the Stockholders' Agreement, holds registration rights, and sold 5,000,000 shares at $28.71 in an August secondary, leaving roughly 17.8M shares, about 25% of the company (Form 4, 2026-08-10; 8-K 2026-08-10). That is a real multi-year overhang. But the market is not discounting it: the stock is 13.6% off its 52-week high, up 69% on twelve-month momentum, trades $27M a day and is covered by Barclays, Citigroup, Jefferies and Johnson Rice on the call. The mispricing the screen thought it found runs the other way, because GAAP operating income flattered by serial asset-sale gains made a fairly priced business look like a 16.7x one.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
Adjusted EBITDA margin has sat at 20-21% for five straight quarters (8-K 2026-08-03), capex is 2-3% of revenue, and free cash flow was 63% of Adjusted EBITDA in Q2. Returns are mid-teens at best: management's own ROCE was 10% in FY2025 and 12% for the twelve months to June 2026, against a "long-term target of high-teens ROCE" (Q2 call, CFO). Pricing power is asserted rather than shown; the 10-K concedes "a majority of the revenue we earn is based upon product sales at market pricing" and names Baker Hughes, Halliburton, Schlumberger, Weatherford and NOV among competitors (Item 1A, Item 1). The end market is flat, with Rystad expecting roughly 33,000 wells a year through 2028 (10-Q Q2 2026). The offshore order book is the real positive: three Asia awards worth $60-80M of revenue converting from 2027, a $20M Malaysian tension riser package and a first XPak trial (Q2 call, CEO). Middle East air-freight costs took about $1.5M out of Q2 margins and persist in Q3.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Clean: $222.1M cash, $25.1M of debt and finance leases, nothing drawn on the revolver, and no maintenance covenant unless excess availability falls below 20% (10-Q Q2 2026). Capital allocation is M&A first: TCO at $95M against $16.4M of 2025 operating income is 5.8x pre-tax EBIT for a business with 25% Adjusted EBITDA margins and sub-1% capex (8-K 2026-06-15). Buybacks are token, 625,000 shares for $9.3M in FY2025 against a $100M authorisation, and share count drifts up, from 68.6M in March 2026 to 69.9M after the TCO issuance. The $51.6M legal accrual is a cash claim not yet paid. Alignment is thin: the CEO owns 514,182 shares on a $550,000 salary with a 100% target bonus set on Adjusted EBITDA, and long-term incentives are half TSR-ranked against the VanEck OIH index, half ROCE-linked with a 10% floor (DEF 14A 2026). Nobody bought stock in the open market in twelve months; the President of North America sold twice.

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
Credible operators so far. The FY2025 bonus plan targeted $195M of Adjusted EBITDA with a $156M threshold; they delivered $188M and took 90% payouts (DEF 14A 2026). Q2 revenue and Adjusted EBITDA both landed at the high end of guidance, and the Dril-Quip integration is nearly done. Two things to watch: the ROCE definition has been revised twice in eighteen months, to exclude acquisition costs and now litigation, and gains on asset sales have landed inside operating income in five consecutive quarters, convenient given the EBITDA-linked bonus excludes them but the GAAP line does not.

## 6. Valuation

Reverse DCF: at a 10% cost of capital the current $1.71B enterprise value implies roughly 5% perpetual growth in normalised after-tax operating profit, from a business in an end market Rystad expects to stay flat at about 33,000 wells a year through 2028.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 results in early November test whether legacy-Innovex EBITDA beats the roughly $48-54M implied ex-TCO. The subsea awards management expects to announce "over the next six months" are the 2027 swing factor. A resolution or appeal ruling on the Impulse Litigation removes a $51.6M cash claim. Amberjack's next block sale is the recurring overhang.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
Risks: a cyclical end market with no backlog visibility in the 52% of revenue that is NAM land; supplier concentration in frac plugs and intervention tools, with magnesium sourced largely from China (10-K 2026, Item 1A); Section 232 steel tariffs, which the CFO confirmed are not covered by the refund programme (Q2 call); integration risk on six deals in three years; and Amberjack's contractual freedom to compete with the company. The PASS would be wrong if any of the following are observed:
1. Adjusted EBITDA margin above 23% for two consecutive quarters.
2. Management-disclosed pro forma organic revenue growth above 8% year over year for two consecutive quarters.
3. Gains on sale of assets below $2M per quarter for three consecutive quarters while income from operations stays above $35M per quarter.
4. Amberjack's holding falling below 10% of shares outstanding while the price still implies under 15x normalised after-tax operating profit.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary a smart friend could repeat
PASS, conviction 4. Innovex is a good capital-light business at a full price, not a cheap one: the screen's 16.7x comes from leaving $51.4M of asset-sale gains inside operating income and taxing at 25% when the company actually pays 37.7%. Fix both and the $1.71B enterprise value is 22x normalised after-tax operating profit, 26.5x at the real tax rate, and about 20x pro forma for TCO. What you buy is a net-cash, 20%-EBITDA-margin consumables maker earning 12% on capital employed against its own high-teens target, whose 48% FY2025 revenue growth was the Dril-Quip merger and three bolt-ons rather than activity, and whose management, asked to strip the acquisitions out, says the base business is "up slightly" in a market Rystad expects to hold flat at 33,000 wells a year through 2028. The one real technical story, a sponsor still holding a quarter of the shares with no lock-up and a fresh 5.0M-share secondary behind it, argues for waiting rather than buying, because at 13.6% off its high and up 69% over twelve months nothing has been marked down for it. The $60-80M offshore order book converting from 2027 is worth watching; a probability-weighted value of about $22 against $27.33 is the reason not to own it today.
