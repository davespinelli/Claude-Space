## 1. What the business actually does
A Denver-based ultra low-cost carrier flying 165 Airbus single-aisle aircraft on US leisure and near-international routes, all financed under operating leases expiring between 2027 and 2038 (Q2 release, Fleet). It sells low base fares plus optional bags, seats, bundles and memberships, and earns loyalty economics from a Barclays co-branded card; about 70% of 2025 tickets were sold direct (10-K 2025, Item 1).

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is no forced seller, and the screen row is wrong in both directions. It reads CY2025 (revenue $3,724M, operating loss $149M, free cash flow -$600M) against a September 2026 price, missing a genuine commercial inflection. It is far more wrong the other way: it sets EV at $717M by taking $300M of non-current debt against $955M of cash, ignoring both the $206M of current maturities and the entire leased fleet. The company's own disclosure that debt to capital including operating leases is 97% against $136M of equity implies roughly $3.9 billion of capitalised lease liabilities, and the material-cash-requirements table shows $6,455M of undiscounted lease obligations (10-Q Q2 2026, Liquidity). Real EV is about $4.8 billion.

Who is selling is the opposite of an edge. Over twelve months insiders bought nothing and sold $87.1M (Form 4 summary). Group Holdings - Frontier LLC, a 10% owner, sold 11,700,000 shares at $7.20 on 2026-07-09, twenty days before the record Q2 print (Form 4, 2026-07-13). CFO Mark Mitchell sold 125,000 shares at $8.09 on 2026-08-05, cutting his holding from 178,785 to 53,785 (Form 4, 2026-08-06; DEF 14A 2026, note 18), and SVP Operations Trevor Stedke sold to zero the same day. Two directors resigned within five weeks (8-Ks 2026-06-12, 2026-07-17). The shares sit 26% below the 52-week high but up 65% over six months: a stock that ran and gave some back.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
The Q2 inflection is real and large: RASM rose 28% to 11.52 cents on 8% more capacity, revenue was a record $1,279M (+38%), and adjusted pre-tax margin improved from -7.5% to -1.9% (Q2 release). Almost all of it is fare, not the ancillary machine. Fare revenue per passenger went from $40.94 to $63.04 (+54%) while total ancillary per passenger was flat at $68.42, having moved only from $60.55 (2021) to $67.57 (2025) (10-K 2025, Item 1). That is industry pricing, which is cyclical and reversible. Costs move the wrong way underneath: H1 CASM excluding fuel rose 26% to 9.31 cents, with maintenance up 110%, D&A up 188% and rent up 50%. Fuel was $4.17 a gallon in Q2, up 77%, and the guide assumes $3.70 in Q3 and $3.50 in Q4.

The decisive number is what supports reported profit. Sale-leaseback gains on newly delivered aircraft run through the other-operating line: $302M in 2025 and $94M in H1 2026 (10-K 2025, Item 7; 10-Q, Cash Flows). LTM adjusted EBITDAR is about $830M, of which roughly $306M, some 37%, is those gains; excluding them EV/EBITDAR is 9.2x, not 5.8x. The record says the same slowly: a five-year cumulative adjusted pre-tax loss of $476M on $16.5B of revenue, a -2.9% margin, best year +1% (DEF 14A 2026, Appendix A).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Liquidity was $1,156M at June 30, 2026, but $220M is an undrawn revolver, and equity fell from $491M to $136M in six months (10-Q, Liquidity). H1 operating cash flow of $191M looks healthy only until you read its composition: a $362M net loss plus $88M of non-cash items plus $465M of working-capital inflows, of which $503M was an other-liabilities build driven by the Barclays prepaid loyalty consideration, lease-return accruals and the TSA reserve. Excluding working capital, operations consumed about $274M. Flight equipment purchase obligations are $10.4B through 2033 against a firm commitment for 155 aircraft, only 16 of which have committed leases. There is no buyback and no dividend. Indigo Partners, whose principal William Franke beneficially owns about 99.5M shares, collects $1.5M a year in consulting fees (DEF 14A 2026, Related Party Transactions).

## 5. Management: said versus did
CEO Jimmy Dempsey, promoted from CFO on 2026-01-07 after Barry Biffle's departure, said on July 29 that "our transformation plan is delivering meaningful results" and guided Q3 RASM up over 20%. Four weeks later the company signed a second Early Return Agreement for 13 more A320neos on top of the 24 already returned, taking $60-80M of non-cash and $90-120M of cash charges mostly settling in 2028-2029, and direct-leased 10 A321neos from AerCap (8-K 2026-08-31). Fleet churn of that scale a month after a record print is not a finished transformation, and the July guide to exit 2026 with 171 aircraft (8-K 2026-07-07) predates it.

## 6. Valuation
Net debt including leases is about $3.44B against a $1.37B cap, so the equity is a roughly 3.5x-levered stub and each half-turn of EBITDAR multiple is worth about $2.19 a share, 37% of the price. Base: 2027 revenue $5.4B at a 19% adjusted EBITDAR margin ($1,026M), 5.5x, gives $9.55. Bear: $5.0B at 15% (fuel stays high, competitors restore capacity, fare gains fade), 5.0x, gives $1.47. Bull: $5.6B at 22%, 6.0x, gives $17.00. At 35/40/25 the weighted value is $8.58, 44% above price, with a bear case 75% below it. Reverse DCF: at $5.96 the price implies Frontier permanently earns an adjusted pre-tax margin near 3%, three times its best year as a public company against a five-year average of -2.9%.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results (late October) test the guided +20% RASM and the $3.70 fuel assumption and carry the first ERA-2 charges. Q4 is guided to breakeven to $0.20, the first positive adjusted quarter in over a year. First Class seats land by end-2026, the 10 AerCap A321neos by Q1 2027, the 8 remaining Avolon sales in H1 2027.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
Risks: all 155 remaining orders carry Pratt GTF engines subject to an ongoing inspection programme (10-K 2025, Item 1A); dependence on sale-leaseback appetite, which funded $441M of 2025 financing cash flow; 97% debt-to-capital leaves no cushion. Kill criteria:
1. RASM growth below 5% year over year in any quarter.
2. Adjusted CASM excluding fuel, stage-adjusted to 1,000 miles, above 8.00 cents for two consecutive quarters (Q2 2026: 7.42 cents).
3. Total liquidity below $900M at any quarter end (June 30, 2026: $1,156M).
4. Sale-leaseback gains above 50% of adjusted EBITDAR in any half-year.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Frontier's commercial turn is real, but nothing here is a mispricing you can underwrite. The screen's $717M EV omits roughly $3.9 billion of capitalised aircraft leases, so true enterprise value is about $4.8 billion, or 5.8x LTM adjusted EBITDAR and 9.2x once you strip out the $306M of sale-leaseback gains that are 37% of that EBITDAR. The RASM recovery is almost entirely fare, with ancillary revenue per passenger flat, so it is industry capacity rather than a company asset. Equity fell from $491M to $136M in six months, H1 operating cash flow was positive only on a $465M working-capital swing led by the Barclays prepayment, 2026 is still guided to a full-year adjusted loss, a second early-return agreement on August 25 added $90-120M of cash charges four weeks after the record print, and no insider bought a share in twelve months while $87.1M was sold, including a 10% holder at $7.20 and the CFO cutting 70% of his stake at $8.09. Probability-weighted value of $8.58 is 44% above $5.96, but that is a levered option on assumptions the filings cannot settle, with a bear case 75% below price.
