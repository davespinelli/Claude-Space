## 1. What the business actually does
Douglas Dynamics makes the snowplows and salt spreaders professional snowplow contractors bolt to pickup trucks (FISHER, WESTERN, SNOWEX), plus truck-mounted service cranes and dump hoists (VENCO, VENTURO, acquired November 2025). That is Work Truck Attachments, $295.7M of FY2025 sales, sold through roughly 3,000 distributor points of sale into an installed base the company believes exceeds 500,000 units, replaced every 9 to 12 years (10-K, Item 1). Work Truck Solutions ($360.3M) makes municipal snow and ice equipment under HENDERSON and upfits Class 3-8 work trucks under DEJANA; about half its revenue is dealers, 40-50% fleet and government.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is no edge case, and I will say so plainly. The register is already full of professional value money: Allspring 10.27%, Conestoga 10.14%, Pzena 10.07%, BlackRock 7.09%, Vanguard 5.86% (DEF 14A). Directors and officers as a group own 1.545%, and there was not one open-market insider purchase in the trailing twelve months; the sole open-market transaction was a 977-share sale by the Controller (Form 4, 2026-03-11). The screen's apparent profit decline is a GAAP artefact of the prior-year sale-leaseback gain, but correcting it makes the business look better rather than cheaper, the opposite of the pattern this desk hunts.

One unexplained fact earns the watch. A Form 4 tax-withholding transaction prices the stock at $53.95 on 2026-07-01; the screen prices it at $41.03 on 2026-09-10. That is a 24% derating in ten weeks, after a beat-and-raise quarter, with no filing later than 2026-08-03 in this bundle to explain it.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
Gross margin has climbed steadily: 23.6% (2023), 25.8% (2024), 26.6% (2025) (10-K, Item 7), and 29.7% in H1 2026, with adjusted EBITDA of $68.1M, $79.3M and $97.9M across the same years. Normalised ROIC on trailing figures is 12.3% against $493.8M of equity plus net debt; strip the $116.8M of goodwill and $113.2M of intangibles and the operating business earns well over 20%, the real evidence this is a good franchise. FCF conversion was 1.12x normalised NOPAT in 2025, though capex of $11.1M was only 1.7% of sales and management now guides 2026 capex "towards the higher end of the traditional range of 2% to 3%" (8-K 2026-08-03), roughly doubling it.

Two cautions sit inside those numbers. Backlog fell to $328.0M at 2025 year end from $348.0M (10-K, Item 1) even as revenue rose 15.4%. And Q2 2026 GAAP income from operations fell to $35.4M from $37.0M despite 10% higher sales: Attachments adjusted EBITDA margin dropped to 27.7% from 29.2% and Solutions adjusted EBITDA fell 21% to $8.8M on flat sales, management citing "softer demand in select commercial business lines" (8-K 2026-08-03, CEO). The non-weather leg is the one weakening.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
The March 2025 refinancing left a $150M term loan and a $125M revolver, both maturing 2030-03-26 at SOFR plus 1.375-2.000% (10-K, Item 7), with undemanding maturities of $7.4M inside a year, $17.6M in years one to three and $119.0M in years three to five. Dividends were $27.9M in 2025 against $63.6M of FCF. Buybacks were $6.0M in 2025 and $6.0M in H1 2026 with $32.0M left on the authorisation (10-Q, Item 2), enough to offset dilution but not to shrink the count. Pay is tied to adjusted operating income (50%), free cash flow (30%) and adjusted EBITDA margin (20%); 2025 delivered $88.9M against a $74.2M target and $64.7M FCF against $40.0M (DEF 14A). Those targets assumed average snowfall, so the beat is again the winter.

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
Guidance moved one way all year: 2026 adjusted EBITDA guided originally at $100-120M, raised to $110-125M in May and to $120-135M in August, with adjusted diluted EPS now $2.90-3.40 (8-Ks 2026-05-05, 2026-08-03). The CFO credited "the strength of pre-season orders for the Attachments segment" while flagging "anticipated softness in some of our commercial markets", candid on both sides. The outlook assumes "average snowfall in the fourth quarter of 2026", so it does not need another freak winter, but it banks the one just had.

## 6. Valuation
Base: 2026 lands at the guidance midpoint, sales $785M and adjusted EBITDA $127.5M. Less $15.5M D&A and about $12M of stock compensation (H1 2026 alone was $7.0M) gives roughly $100M normalised operating profit and $75M after tax, or 15.2x the current EV and about 14.5x once seasonal working capital unwinds by December. Bear: winter 2026-27 reverts to average or below and Solutions commercial stays soft, adjusted EBITDA falls toward $100M, after-tax profit to about $54M, and 13x gives a $700M EV, roughly $22 per share. Bull: Venco and municipal Solutions keep compounding, 2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches $145M, after-tax profit $88M, 17x gives $1.50B EV, about $57 per share. Weighted 50/30/20 that is roughly $40 against $41.03: fair, not cheap. Reverse DCF: the current price implies about 2.4% perpetual growth off the guided 2026 normalised after-tax profit at a 9% unlevered discount rate, which is undemanding only if 2026 is a normal year; measured against the 2021-2025 average adjusted EBITDA of $82.3M, the same price is about 22x mid-cycle earnings, which is not.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
Steel was 6.1% of revenue in 2025 and only partly hedged; the Supreme Court struck down certain IEEPA tariffs on 2026-02-20 and the refund and replacement position is unresolved; an ERP implementation is in flight (10-K, Items 1A and 7). Kill criteria:
1. Snowfall in core markets for the season ending March 31, 2027 comes in below the 10-year average (disclosed in the 10-K and the Q1 release).
2. Work Truck Solutions adjusted EBITDA declines year over year for two consecutive quarters; it already fell 21% in Q2 2026.
3. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands below the $120M low end of the raised guide, or initial 2027 guidance is below $110M.
4. Net debt exceeds 2.5x company-defined adjusted EBITDA at a December balance-sheet date.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Douglas Dynamics is a genuinely good franchise: a 500,000-unit installed base on a 9-to-12-year replacement cycle, gross margins rising through 26.6%, north of 20% returns on tangible capital, and free cash flow covering the dividend twice over last year. The screen's stated 18% profit decline is false, since it counted a $42.3M 2024 sale-leaseback gain as operating profit and normalised operating profit actually grew 34% in 2025, but correcting that makes the company look better without making the stock cheaper, and the screen separately understated net debt by $52M by missing $57M of revolver borrowings. At $41.03 you pay about 19x trailing and 15x guided normalised after-tax profit for a year whose growth came from the heaviest snowfall in a decade following seven below-average winters, with backlog down 5.7%, Solutions commercial demand softening, capex about to double, and not one insider buying a share. The interesting fact is the 24% derating since July 1 that none of these filings explains; establish what caused it, and if the answer is a sector derate rather than a company problem while the 2026-27 winter starts normally, this becomes an idea nearer $30.
