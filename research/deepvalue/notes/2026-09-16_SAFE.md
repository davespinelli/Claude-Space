## 1. What the business actually does
Safehold buys the land under commercial buildings and leases it back for 30 to 99 years triple-net, so the tenant pays taxes, insurance, maintenance and capex; rent escalates at a fixed rate or CPI capped at 3.0% to 3.5%, and at lease end the land and building revert for nothing (10-K 2025, Item 1). The portfolio is 172 leases, $7.1B gross book value, 92-year weighted-average term, 44% multifamily and 39% office, 21% Manhattan, rent coverage 3.4x, ground-lease-to-value 52%, with estimated unrealised capital appreciation of $9.8B (8-K 2026-07-30, EX-99.2).

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is an overhang but no dated dislocation, so this cannot exceed WATCH. Star Holdings, the liquidating trust spun from iStar in 2023 and managed by Safehold itself, owns 18.8%; MSD Partners affiliates own 8.5%, bought at $37.00 in 2022 and re-registered for resale on 2026-03-25 (DEF 14A 2026-03-31). That is 27% of the register in indifferent or motivated hands, but it is an old slow drip, not a forced sale, and the stock is only 19.7% off its 52-week high. The proxy puts $100 invested 2020-12-31 at $22 by 2025-12-31 against $138 for the MSCI US REIT Index: a verdict on discount rates applied to a 92-year asset.

## 3. The screen's FFO number, and what the cash does
The screen used net income plus depreciation, $114.5M + $8.5M = $123.0M. That fails because 77% of assets ($3,674.6M of sales-type leases plus $2,131.0M of Ground Lease receivables against $7,520.0M) are financial receivables that are never depreciated, and accumulated depreciation on the $743.8M of owned real estate is only $55.3M. FFO therefore equals net income, P/FFO is a P/E, and Safehold publishes no FFO or AFFO itself. Under that P/E sits the accretion: $110M of rent not collectible for decades was booked in the year to 6/30/26. Against $114.5M of CY2025 net income the company produced $47.8M of operating cash flow and paid $50.9M of common dividends (10-K 2025, Item 7); H1'26 cash flow of $26.0M against roughly $25.5M of dividends fell "due primarily to an increase of cash payments for debt service and costs associated with two hotels" (10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Ratings are A3 / A- / A- (S&P upgraded from BBB+ in November 2025), 18-year weighted-average maturity, no corporate maturities until 2029, $1.4B undrawn on the revolver (10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A). The June financing shows how the cash gap is managed: $225M of notes due 2056 at a 6.615% all-in coupon but a 4.00% starting cash coupon, reaching 6.615% only in year 21, with the difference accruing to principal (8-K 2026-06-16). At the 2:1 ceiling new equity comes from partners, not stock: Brookfield bought 49% of a seven-asset venture at a $348M gross valuation on about $14M of annualised cash rent, roughly a 4.0% cap rate (8-K 2026-06-12), lifting NCI from $31.6M to $193.1M. Safehold repurchased 1.1M shares for $16.3M at $15.00 average in H1'26 with $33.7M left, but on 2026-05-14 shareholders added 3,000,000 shares to the 2009 LTIP, 4.2% of shares (8-K 2026-05-15). Across twelve Form 4s there were no open-market buys; the one open-market trade was director Robin Josephs selling 9,500 shares at $14.56 on 2026-09-09.

## 5. Management: what they said versus what they did
The CEO called Q2 "a strong second quarter, increasing origination volume, growing UCA and adding investment capacity" (8-K 2026-07-30, EX-99.1), accurate and silent on the cash flow that fell. There is no numeric guidance to score. The December 2025 president hire carries 700,000 PRSUs vesting on stock-price hurdles reaching 249% above the 2025-11-28 close (DEF 14A 2026-03-31), and a ground lease company now runs two hotels directly, $25.8M of H1 revenue against $26.1M of expense, while suing its Park Hotels tenant and guarantor after a 2025-10-22 termination notice (10-K 2025, Item 7).

## 6. Valuation
Portfolio cost basis at 6/30/26 was $6,605M; add $65.0M of loans, the $112.6M Star Holdings term loan, $26.1M of cash and restricted cash and $68.9M of other assets, subtract $4,653.7M of debt, $233.3M of other and intangible liabilities and $193.1M of NCI: NAV at cost is about $1.80B, **$25.39 per share**. At $13.78 the market pays roughly **0.87x cost basis**, a ~4.3% initial cash cap rate on rent escalating about 2% a year.

- **Base (55%), $15.50:** portfolio holds at 0.95x cost (NAV $20.73), long rates flat, market keeps a 25% discount for 2x leverage and a 90-year horizon, plus the 5.2% dividend.
- **Bear (25%), $7.50:** long rates up 100bp and the portfolio marks at 0.80x cost (NAV $6.74), plus a default at the forborne New York office asset. Equity is 17% of total capitalisation, so a 13% markdown halves it.
- **Bull (20%), $21.00:** rates fall, the portfolio marks at cost or better (NAV $25.39), Caret gets credited, the discount halves.
- Probability-weighted **$14.60**, about 6% above price.

Reverse DCF: the current price implies the portfolio is worth about 87 cents on its cost dollar, a ~4.3% cash cap rate against the 30-year Treasury of 4.99% at the June notes pricing (8-K 2026-06-16), with zero credit for the $9.8B of estimated UCA.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 results in late October 2026, where operating cash flow is the line that matters; a ruling in the Park Hotels litigation; resolution of the forborne New York office ground lease; deployment of the remaining $400M of sovereign-wealth venture capacity; long-end rates.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
1. Trailing-twelve-month operating cash flow below common dividends paid (about $51M) for two consecutive quarters. At $26.0M in H1'26 this is already at the line.
2. Portfolio Ground Rent Coverage below 3.0x (3.4x at 6/30/26), or a default by the tenant under the forborne significant New York office asset.
3. Total debt / total equity above 2.25x (2.01x at Q2'26), or a downgrade from any of A3 / A- / A-.
4. Shares outstanding up more than 3% year over year while the stock trades below book.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Safehold is a real investment-grade ground lease portfolio of 92-year duration at 0.40x book and about 0.87x its own cost basis, and Brookfield's June 2026 purchase at roughly a 4.0% cap rate is an arm's-length mark saying the assets are worth close to cost. But the screen's 7.9x P/FFO is an artefact of a company that reports no FFO and depreciates almost nothing, because 77% of its balance sheet is financial receivables; on cash the multiple is about 18.8x, and that cash ($47.8M in 2025, $26.0M in H1'26 and down 29%) barely covers the $51M dividend, with a 3.8% portfolio cash yield below the 3.9% cash cost of debt, leverage at the stated 2:1 ceiling, and a new 2056 note that accrues rather than pays 2.6 points of coupon. There is no dated edge case either: Star Holdings and MSD are an old overhang, the stock is 20% off its high, insiders bought nothing in twelve months and a director sold on 2026-09-09, and probability-weighted value of $14.60 is 6% above price. The equity is a thin sliver of a large levered bond, so this is a long-rates call dressed as a value stock.
