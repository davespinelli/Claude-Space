## 1. What the business does
First United Bank & Trust is a 23-branch community bank in Oakland, Maryland, lending across four Western Maryland and three Northeastern West Virginia counties (10-K 2026, Item 1): $2.08B assets, $1.57B gross loans, $1.74B deposits at 30 June 2026. The book is 35% one-to-four family residential, 22% non-owner-occupied CRE, 19% C&I, 12% owner-occupied CRE, 6% construction, 3% each consumer and multi-family (8-K 2026-05-07, slide 14). Attached is a real wealth franchise: $1.84B of trust and brokerage assets under supervision at end-2025 against $1.36B in 2022, producing $11.3M of the $20.6M of fee income.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is not one, and I will say so plainly. The candidates are thin float and no coverage: 6.45M shares, $1.0M average daily dollar volume, a register the company itself calls heavily retail (DEF 14A 2026, charter-amendment proposal). But the market has found it. Five-year total shareholder return is 138.4% against 25.1% for the S&P US Small Cap Banks index, one-year 26.9% against 18.1% (8-K 2026-05-07, slide 23), and the stock sits 4% below its 52-week high at 1.42x tangible book. A bank earning 13-14% on tangible equity is supposed to trade near 1.4x. The screen found a good bank, not a cheap one.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Credit is clean: non-accruals 0.29% of loans, non-performing assets 0.37% of assets against 0.51% a year ago, net charge-offs 0.04% annualised, allowance 1.31% of loans and 456% of non-accruals (8-K 2026-07-20). One caution: accruing loans 30+ days past due rose to 0.50% from 0.27% on one large commercial credit. Non-owner-occupied CRE $354.1M plus multi-family $48.9M plus construction $102.2M is roughly 177% of total risk-based capital, inside the 300% supervisory threshold, with no metropolitan office over four stories and no big-box retail (10-Q Q2 2026). Margin is the whole earnings story: loan yields rose 15bp year over year to 6.16% while interest expense fell, helped by repaying a $25.0M brokered CD at 4.23% in January and $65.0M of FHLB advances in March 2026. Non-interest-bearing deposits have drifted from 34% of the base in 2021 to 25%, and the bank states it is asset-sensitive, so Fed cuts work against the 3.98% margin. Uninsured and uncollateralised deposits are a low 17%.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Holding-company debt is $30.9M of 2004-vintage trust-preferred debentures at 6.80%, plus $69.2M of quarter-end overnight borrowings repaid in July. Buybacks are token: shares fell 0.7% in H1 2026 to 6,453,836. The dividend rose to $0.26 quarterly from $0.22, a 27% payout. Directors and officers own 8.1% (DEF 14A 2026). Form 4s are the soft signal: twelve-month net open-market activity is 11,250 shares sold for $500,750 against 280 bought for $12,801, the sales being the Chief Wealth Officer (5,000 at $43.68), the Chief Banking Officer (5,250 at $45.29) and a director, all in late July after the re-rating; the buys are dividend-reinvestment dribbles.

## 5. Management: said versus did
Carissa Rodeheaver handed the CEO role to Jason Rush on 1 January 2026 and retired as Executive Chairman on 7 May; Rush took the chair on 27 May (8-K 2026-05-28). Published targets are a 53-58% efficiency ratio, 1.25-1.60% core ROAA and 13-15% core ROATCE (8-K 2026-05-07, slides 19-20); 2025 delivered 58.19%, 1.28% and 14.25%, and H1 2026 adjusted ROAA is 1.36%. Against that, Rush spent $2.2M on consultants to renegotiate the core-processing contract and has quantified no savings, saying only it "will position us for improved efficiency in future years" (8-K 2026-07-20). A claim to check, not to credit.

## 6. Valuation
Base: margin holds near 3.9%, loans +4%, expenses +3%, normalised EPS about $4.40 in 2027 at 11x, or 1.4x a tangible book compounding to roughly $34.5, gives about $48. Bear: the Fed cuts, the asset-sensitive book reprices down while the 2.20% cost of deposits stays sticky, margin returns to 3.6%, provisions normalise to 25bp, EPS about $3.30 at 1.05x tangible book, about $34. Bull: core-contract savings are real, efficiency reaches 53-55%, loans +6%, EPS $5.00 at 12.5x, or a sale at 1.8-2.0x tangible book, $60-65. Weighted 50/25/25 gives about $48, some 8% above the price plus a 2.35% dividend. Reverse DCF: at 1.42x tangible book, a 10% cost of equity and 4% long-run growth, the price implies a 12.5% ROTCE sustained in perpetuity, slightly below what the bank earns today. The market is paying for continuation, not improvement, which is fair rather than wrong.

## 7. Catalysts
Q3 2026 results in late October are the first clean quarter without the consulting charge and should print adjusted EPS near $1.15; a quantified core-processor saving would be the real catalyst. Beyond that, further wholesale-funding paydowns, and the stated intent to "cultivate relationships for potential future bank and wealth expansion" (8-K 2026-05-07, slide 24), which makes First United a likelier acquirer than a target.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
Specific risks: asset sensitivity into a cutting cycle; eight legacy pooled trust-preferred CDOs carried at $15.1M with $2.1M of unrealised loss on Level 3 marks; the unmarked $24.0M HTM shortfall; thin liquidity on exit. Kill criteria, observable quarterly:
1. FTE net interest margin below 3.75% for two consecutive quarters.
2. Non-accrual loans above 0.60% of loans, or accruing 30+ day past dues above 1.00%, in two consecutive quarters.
3. Adjusted efficiency ratio above 60% for two consecutive quarters, meaning the core-contract spend bought nothing.
4. Net insider open-market selling above $1M in any rolling six months, or a dividend cut.
The thesis turns constructive below roughly 1.15x tangible book, about $36, with credit unchanged.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 3. First United is a well-run $2.1B community bank in Western Maryland whose earnings have been transformed by margin recapture, from a 3.26% net interest margin in 2023 to 3.98% in Q2 2026, lifting normalised after-tax profit to $27.8M and ROTCE to 13-14.6% while credit stayed pristine at 0.29% non-accruals and 0.04% charge-offs. None of this is a secret: the shares returned 138% over five years against 25% for small-cap banks, sit 4% off their high at 1.42x tangible book, and 1.56x once you mark the $24.0M hole in the held-to-maturity book that the screen ignores. At 10.3x normalised earnings the price implies a 12.5% perpetual ROTCE, roughly what the bank already earns, so you are paid 2.35% to wait for improvement management has promised but not quantified, while two EVPs sold into the rally. Good bank, fair price, no edge; revisit below about 1.15x tangible book, or if the core-processor savings are put on paper.
