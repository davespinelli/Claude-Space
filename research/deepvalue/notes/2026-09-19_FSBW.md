## 1. What the business does
1st Security Bank of Washington is a $3.18B-asset bank in Mountlake Terrace, running 33 branches across Puget Sound, the Tri-Cities, Vancouver and now greater Portland (8-K 2026-08-24). Commercial and Consumer Banking earned $6.8M in Q2; Home Lending, which originates one-to-four-family loans mostly for sale, earned $1.1M (8-K 2026-07-21). The distinguishing asset is indirect home improvement lending: $502.2M at 30 June 2026, about 19% of gross loans, roughly 29,000 loans averaging $18,000, sourced through 33 contractors and dealers in 13 states, of which **five produced 74.5% of 2025 originations** (10-K, Item 1). These are seven to twenty year fixed loans secured by fixtures the 10-K concedes are practically impossible to repossess (Item 1A).

## 2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case
Honestly, there is none today. At 1.05x tangible book for a bank earning 10.4% on tangible common equity, the price is what the arithmetic deserves. The stock is 2.4% below its 52-week high and up 18% in six months, so no forced sellers, no index deletion, no spin-off. The only structural inefficiency is size: $1.1M average daily volume and 7.3M shares keep institutions out, with only BlackRock (6.3%) and Dimensional (5.4%) above 5% (DEF 14A). The nearest future edge is that Q3 2026, the first quarter consolidating Pacific West, will carry merger and intangible charges that make GAAP earnings look worse than the run rate. That is a reason to watch, not to buy.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Margin is the strength: NIM 4.30% in Q2, unchanged y/y, on a 6.54% earning-asset yield and 2.41% cost of funds. Credit is the worry. Headline ratios improved (nonperforming assets 0.49% of assets versus 0.60%, reserve coverage of nonperformers 199%), but partly because the bad loan was written off rather than cured. Underneath, indirect home improvement net charge-offs ran 0.36% in 2023, 0.67% in 2024 and **1.10% in 2025** (10-K, Item 1 charge-off table); my calculation from the Q2 ACL table puts Q2 2026 near 1.2% annualised ($2.067M of charge-offs less $584,000 of recoveries). Management cites "elevated delinquency levels within portions of the portfolio" (8-K 2026-07-21). The reserve on that book was 2.74% at year end; total ACL slipped to 1.17% of loans from 1.20%. CRE is not the problem: 37.8% of loans, with non-owner-occupied office only $43.7M. Construction is $415.2M, 54.4% speculative residential vertical. Deposits are decent: 25.7% noninterest-bearing checking, uninsured 26.9% at year end, brokered cut to 5.2%, but a 107% loan-to-deposit ratio means $324.5M of borrowings do real work. November 2025 flooding in western Washington and Oregon is a disclosed live credit risk (Item 1A).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
The best part of the story. Common shares fell from 7,729,951 at year-end 2024 to 7,320,801, down 5.3%, via $15.4M of 2025 repurchases and $3.6M in Q2 2026 at an average $41.81, below the current price. A new $5.0M authorisation runs to August 2027 (8-K 2026-08-24), about 1.6% of market cap. The dividend is $0.29 quarterly, the 54th consecutive, plus a $0.22 special in August 2025. Holding company unrestricted cash is thin at $8.1M (Item 1A). Directors and officers own 12.9%. Against that: the 2026 Equity Incentive Plan approved in May authorises 315,000 shares, 4.3% of the count, and Form 4s show zero open-market insider buys in twelve months against 3,576 shares sold for $153,411.

## 5. Management: said versus did
Joe Adams retired as CEO on 31 May 2026 under a plan announced in August 2025; Matt Mullet, CFO from 2011 and already bank CEO, took over (8-K 2026-06-01). Continuity, not upheaval. The proxy's own 2025 scorecard is candid: net income down to $33.3M from $35.0M, and "return on average assets and return on equity trended closer to peer average" (DEF 14A). Incentives were still paid at 37.5% to 62.5% of salary against 50% targets in a down year, the one place pay and performance diverge. No numeric guidance exists to score them against beyond the 10.1% tangible book growth they delivered.

## 6. Valuation
Base: ROTCE holds near 10.3%, TBVPS reaches about $44.5 in twelve months after deal intangibles, multiple stays 1.05x, giving $46.7 plus $1.16 of dividends, near $47.9, up 9%. Bear: indirect charge-offs climb to 1.8%, about $9M a year versus $5.9M in 2025, costing roughly $0.38 per share after tax, the construction credit resolves worse than carried, ROTCE falls to 8% and the multiple to 0.85x on $43 of book, near $37.7, down 14%. Bull: merger saves land, consumer losses revert toward 0.7%, ROTCE reaches 12% and the multiple 1.3x on $45, near $59.7, up 36%. Weighted 55/25/20 gives about $47.7, roughly 9% above the price. Reverse DCF: at 1.05x tangible book with 10.4% ROTCE and 5% sustainable growth, **the current price implies a cost of equity near 10.1%**, so the market demands almost exactly the return the bank produces, granting neither franchise premium nor credit discount.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results in late October, the first consolidating Pacific West and the first credit read under the new CEO. Resolution of the commercial construction relationship, which management expects in the second half of 2026. Execution of the $5.0M buyback. Fed cuts would help a bank funding at 2.41% with 107% loans to deposits.

## 8. Kill criteria (pre-registered)
1. Indirect home improvement net charge-offs above 1.50% annualised for two consecutive quarters, from the quarterly ACL table (1.10% FY2025, about 1.2% in Q2 2026).
2. ACL on loans below 1.10% of gross loans while nonperforming loans rise, meaning earnings funded by reserve release (1.17% at 30 June 2026).
3. NIM below 4.00% for two consecutive quarters (4.30% now), or TBVPS falling in a quarter for reasons other than AOCI.
4. Active dealer network materially below 33, or disclosed loss of any of the five dealers supplying 74.5% of indirect originations.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 3. FS Bancorp is a well-run, cheap-looking Pacific Northwest bank whose cheapness is mostly an illusion of the ratio: at 1.05x tangible book and 9.7x normalised earnings the price already matches a 10.4% return on tangible equity, and the reverse DCF confirms the market is asking exactly the return the bank earns. The good is real: a 4.30% margin that has not moved in a year, tangible book compounding 10% to 12%, buybacks near book that shrank the count 5.3% in eighteen months, 54 consecutive dividends, insiders at 12.9%. What stops it being an idea is equally real: the consumer engine behind that margin is deteriorating, with indirect home improvement charge-offs tripling from 0.36% to 1.10% in two years and still climbing against a 2.74% reserve, in a book sourced by five dealers supplying three quarters of originations and secured by fixtures the bank admits it cannot repossess. Add a subordinated note that just repriced from 3.93% to 7.34%, a 315,000-share incentive plan, zero insider buying, and an acquisition whose accounting appears in no filed balance sheet yet, and the right posture is to wait for the first post-merger quarter and evidence the consumer losses have rolled over, or for a price nearer 0.85x tangible book.
