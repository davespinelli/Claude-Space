## 1. What the business actually does
Oportun lends small, fixed-rate, fully amortising personal loans to people who cannot get bank credit, capped at 36% APR. Average unsecured loan $3,098 at a 35.2% weighted average APR over 38 months; a car-secured product averages $6,474 at 33.0% (10-K, Item 1). It reaches 2.8 million members through an app, website, telesales, 125 stores and 459 partner locations. Loans are originated almost entirely by a partner bank, Pathward, about 98% of 2025 originations (10-K, Item 1A), and funded with securitisations and warehouse lines.

## 2. Why it is mispriced: there is no edge case
There is not one, and that caps this at WATCH. Nobody is forced to sell. The stock is up 63.7% in six months and sits 5.3% below its 52-week high on $2.5M of daily volume. Both activists have signed standstills, Findell in July 2025 and the Radoff parties on 22 June 2026, the latter in exchange for two directors retiring at the 2026 annual meeting (8-K 2026-06-24). Neuberger Berman owns 13.1%, Forager 7.6%, Findell 6.5%, Castlelake and BlackRock 5.3% each (DEF 14A). What looks like cheapness, 1.04x tangible book against a 20.5% adjusted ROE, is the market declining to capitalise a return produced by falling funding costs at a lender whose book is shrinking.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and funding
Credit is the whole story. Annualised net charge-offs were 12.0% in Q2 2026 against 11.9%, and 12.3% for the half against 12.0% (10-Q). The strategic target is 9-11% "over time" (10-K, Item 1). The 30+ day delinquency rate improved to 4.0% from 4.4%, lowest since 4Q21, and that is the real leading indicator behind the guide. Vintage losses are harsher: 2022 at 22.4% of original principal, 2023 at 15.7%, 2024 at 9.8% with 36.1% still outstanding, against 10.8% for the fully mature 2019 book. Net interest margin ratio rose 274bp to 29.0% but risk-adjusted NIM only 69bp to 17.0%, the gap being worse fair-value marks. The book is carried at fair value, 87% of assets, marked off a 6.30% discount rate and a 12.22% remaining cumulative charge-off assumption; a 100bp rise in that assumption is roughly $27M pre-tax. Funding has genuinely improved: 2026-A notes priced at a 5.32% yield against 8.60% for 2024-1, and the 12.5% cash plus 2.5% PIK corporate term loan fell from $143.7M to $119.9M on $30M of Q2 voluntary prepayments.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
$139.6M unrestricted cash, $880.5M undrawn on $1,189.1M of warehouse lines committed into 2028-2030, all covenants met. No buyback, no dividend; free cash goes to the term loan, the right call at 15% all-in. Basic weighted shares went 46.0M to 47.9M in a year. Insiders bought nothing in the open market over twelve months against $360,905 of sales, and the new CEO held zero shares at 24 June 2026, having been granted 463,822 RSUs and 463,822 PSUs on 10 June (Form 4). Neuberger Berman is simultaneously the largest holder and the term-loan lender, disclosed as a related party in the proxy. Bonuses are struck 65% on Adjusted EBITDA and 35% on charge-off rate; the 2023-2025 PSUs paid nothing on TSR of negative 10.1%.

## 5. Management: said versus did
Management met or exceeded every 2025 guidance metric (DEF 14A), beat the top of Q2 revenue and Adjusted EBITDA guidance, then raised full-year Adjusted EBITDA 6% at the midpoint and improved the charge-off guide 20bp. That is a good record. But the bench has emptied: Raul Vazquez left in April 2026 after leading the company since its founding era, Doug Bland became CEO on 20 April, the 18-year Chief Credit Officer left 15 June, a new Chief Risk Officer started 17 June, two directors retired and a new audit chair arrived 11 August. Personnel expense rose 21.1% in Q2 on transition costs. Every credit judgement in the guide is being made by people weeks into the seat.

## 6. Valuation
At $7.66 the market pays 6.0x the midpoint of guided 2026 GAAP net income. **Reverse DCF: the price implies those earnings shrink about 2% a year forever at a 15% cost of equity, or grow about 3% a year at the 20% a thinly capitalised subprime lender arguably deserves.** Base: the guide lands and 2027 holds near $55M as the funding tailwind exhausts (Q2 cost of debt is already 6.3% against new issuance at 5.3%); 7.5x forward earnings, or 1.2x a $370M tangible book, gives about $9.25. Bear: charge-offs do not fall, H2 stays at 12.3% rather than the ~11.1% the guide needs, costing roughly $32M annualised pre-tax, and a 100bp revision to remaining cumulative charge-offs takes $27M off book; 0.7x a $310M tangible book gives $4.72. Bull: the 9-11% target is reached over two years, originations compound mid single digits, the corporate loan is retired adding about $18M pre-tax, normalised earnings reach $90M at 9x, or $17.63. Weighting 50/30/20 gives $9.57, 25% above price, with a bear 38% below. The arithmetic that decides it: guidance implies $54.2-65.1M of H2 pre-tax income against $19.2M in H1, roughly a tripling, of which about $14M comes from the charge-off rate falling and the rest from ordinary fourth-quarter seasonality.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. Annualised net charge-off rate above 11.9% in either 3Q26 or 4Q26. The whole H2 profit step-up rests on it falling to about 11.1%.
2. 30+ day delinquency rate back above 4.4% at any quarter end, reversing the indicator that justifies the guide.
3. Remaining cumulative charge-offs in the fair-value model above 12.6% (12.22% at 30 June 2026), or tangible book per share falling quarter over quarter.
4. Corporate financing balance flat or higher in any quarter, or a valuation allowance taken against the $61.3M of net deferred tax assets.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Oportun is a subprime installment lender at 1.04x tangible book and about 6x guided 2026 earnings whose entire profit recovery is a funding-cost story: Q2 pre-tax income rose $5.5M while interest expense fell $17.6M, part of that from a non-cash change in how interest on asset-backed borrowings is recognised, while revenue fell 4.5% in 2025, is guided down again in 2026, and originations fell 4.8% in the first half. The balance sheet is levered 6.5x behind a loan book that is 87% of assets and carried at management's own fair-value model, and the guide needs second-half pre-tax income to roughly triple the first half on a charge-off rate dropping from 12.3% to about 11.1%, a judgement now made by a CEO of five months and a Chief Risk Officer of three. There is no edge case here: the stock is up 64% in six months and 5% off its high, both activists have signed standstills, and no insider has bought a share in twelve months. Probability-weighted value of about $9.57 is 25% above the $7.66 price with a bear 38% below, interesting but not enough to underwrite before the November quarter shows whether credit actually turned.
