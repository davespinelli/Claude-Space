## 1. What the business actually does
Hanmi Bank is a $8.0B California state-chartered community bank founded in 1981, serving Korean-American and other immigrant business communities across nine states with 610 employees (10-K 2025, Item 1). Loans are 61.2% commercial property, 16.0% residential mortgage, 16.4% commercial and industrial, 6.2% equipment finance. It also originates SBA loans, selling the guaranteed portions and servicing $553.4M sold, and runs a trade finance book.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is none, and that governs the verdict. Nothing is forcing anyone to sell: the stock sits 2.7% below its 52-week high after rising 28.8% in six months on $5.8M of daily volume, with no spin, index event or GAAP artefact. What the screen calls cheapness is a stale-data effect the market has already priced.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposits
Margin is the story. Net interest margin went 3.08% in 2023, 2.78% in 2024, 3.15% in 2025 and 3.36% in Q2 2026 (10-K 2025, Item 7; 10-Q Q2 2026), on deposit repricing: cost of deposits 2.25% against 2.56%. But the company's own simulation says further cuts now hurt, a 100bp drop taking 4.79% off net interest income in twelve months and 7.39% in the second year, 200bp taking 8.80% and 14.94%. The engine that produced the recovery is modelled to run in reverse.

Headline credit is excellent, the detail less so. Nonperforming assets are 0.12% of assets against 0.26% at year end, allowance covers nonaccruals 710%, net charge-offs 8bp annualised. But classified loans rose from $25.9M to $45.7M in six months, criticized plus classified from $97.0M to $113.9M, and 30-89 day past dues from $19.9M to $32.8M, all from one $21.2M retail commercial real estate credit that went pass to special mention in Q1, then classified and delinquent in Q2. Behind it: lessors of non-residential buildings 24.4% of loans, hospitality 13.1%, office $479M, and CRE above 300% of Bank capital, exempt from the concentration guidance only because the book has not grown 50% in 36 months (10-K 2025, Item 1A). Equipment finance charges off 1.76% annualised and is being run down from $408M to $363M.

Funding is adequate rather than a moat: deposits grew 4.2% to $6.96B, noninterest-bearing 30.7%, loan-to-deposit 94.0%, but uninsured deposits are $3.12B, 44.9% of the total.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
H1 2026 equity rose $16.3M on $46.1M of earnings less $16.9M of dividends and $9.6M of buybacks at an average $27.90, with 1,991,495 shares (6.7% of the count) still authorised. Share count fell 1.6% year over year. The July refinancing halves the subordinated layer. The Bank could upstream $44.4M without regulator approval as of 1 July 2026. Insider ownership could not be checked.

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
No guidance or call was in the bundle, so this cannot be written to the desk's usual standard. What is checkable is action: management repaid all $150.0M of FHLB advances with deposit growth, shrank the worst-performing loan book, and bought stock at $27.90. Against that, noninterest expense grew 8.5% in H1 on a loan book that shrank, including $1.6M more professional fees and other operating expenses from "the resolution of administrative matters" and delinquent property taxes on a nonaccrual loan.

## 6. Valuation
Base (55%): margin plateaus near 3.30%, loans grow 2%, provision normalises to about 35bp as classified loans migrate, EPS about $3.00; tangible book compounds to roughly $29 by late 2027, at 1.15x that is $33.4 plus $1.12 of dividends, about $34.5.
Bear (25%): the Fed cuts 100bp, net interest income falls 4.8% on the company's own model, the $21.2M credit and the wider classified pool cost 55bp of provision, EPS falls to $2.20 and the multiple compresses to 0.95x tangible book: about $27.7 including the dividend.
Bull (20%): margin holds above 3.35%, loan growth resumes at 5%, the sub-debt swap and the buyback lift EPS to $3.40 at 1.35x tangible book: about $40.5.
Probability-weighted $34.0, about 6% above $32.115.
Reverse DCF: at 1.19x tangible common book, with a 10% cost of equity and 3% perpetual growth, the price implies HAFC sustains about 11.3% return on tangible common equity forever, against 10.7%, 8.0% and 9.3% actually earned on average equity in 2023, 2024 and 2025 (10-K 2025, Item 7).

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results in late October show whether the $21.2M credit moves to nonaccrual and whether margin holds after the refinancing. Any Fed cut is a negative catalyst on the company's disclosed sensitivity. Completing the $110.0M redemption removes roughly $1.5M a year of pre-tax interest. The buyback is the only lever fully in management's hands.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. Criticized plus classified loans exceed $150M (2.3% of loans) at any quarter end, or the $21.2M retail CRE credit moves to nonaccrual.
2. Net interest margin below 3.15% for two consecutive quarters.
3. Net charge-offs above 40bp of average loans annualised for two consecutive quarters.
4. Noninterest expense grows faster than revenue for two consecutive quarters while loans are flat or shrinking.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 2. Hanmi is a well-capitalised $8.0B Korean-American community bank whose screen row understates it in every direction, the trailing P/E being about 10.7x rather than 12.6x and ROTCE 10.8% rather than 9.4% because the screen charges FY2025 earnings against today's price while H1 2026 net income rose 40.8% to $46.0M. But the recovery is rate and provision, not franchise: $11.9M of the $14.8M net interest income gain came from lower deposit rates, the loan book shrank over the six months, and the company's own simulation says a 100bp cut takes 4.79% off net interest income in year one and 7.39% in year two, so the engine is modelled to run backwards. Credit reads beautifully at the headline and worse underneath, with classified loans up from $25.9M to $45.7M and delinquencies from $19.9M to $32.8M on a single $21.2M retail CRE credit, against a book that is 24.4% lessors of non-residential buildings and 13.1% hospitality. At 1.19x tangible book the price already implies about 11.3% sustained return on tangible equity, better than anything earned in the last three years, and there is no edge case: the stock is 2.7% off its high after a 28.8% run with no forced seller. Probability-weighted $34.0 is 6% above price, which does not pay for CRE concentration plus an adverse rate sensitivity. Wait for the Q3 print.
