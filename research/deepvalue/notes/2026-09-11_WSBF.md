## 1. What the business does
WaterStone Bank is a 1921-vintage Wisconsin thrift, 14 Milwaukee-area branches, 1.85% deposit share, ninth of 44 institutions in its metro (10-K 2025, Item 1). Its $1.68B loan book is 46% multi-family ($781.9M), 27% one-to-four family and 19% commercial real estate (Q2 8-K). Bolted on is Waterstone Mortgage Corporation, a national originator with 55 offices in 24 states that originated $2.05B of loans held for sale in 2025 for sale into the secondary market.

## 2. Why it might be mispriced, and the honest answer
The candidate edge is a conglomerate discount: a 48%-efficiency-ratio bank stapled to a mortgage originator running at 93% to 100% (Q2 8-K segment tables), so the consolidated 77.4% ratio makes the whole look mediocre. On TTM segment figures the bank earned $27.1M and the mortgage arm $2.8M.

That edge is largely spent. The stock is up 22.6% in six months and sits 0.7% below its 52-week high (universe_v2.csv). At 1.09x tangible book this is not priced as a broken thrift, and there is no forced seller, spin-off or index event.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Credit is clean: nonperforming assets 0.30% of assets at 30 Jun 2026, nonaccrual loans 0.39% of loans, allowance 1.06% of loans and 283% of non-performing loans, net recoveries of $122K in FY2025 (Q2 8-K; 10-K 2025, Item 7). Concentration is the offset: multi-family, commercial real estate and construction total $1,174M against roughly $340M of bank Tier 1 capital, about 3.4x by my calculation from the disclosed balances and the 15.43% Tier 1 to average assets ratio, above the 300% supervisory guidance threshold, and the company intends to keep growing these books (10-K 2025, Item 1A).

Time deposits are $895M of $1,421M, and the 10-K carries the risk factor "We rely heavily on certificates of deposit, which has increased our cost of funds" (Item 1A). That is why margin fell to 2.17% in 2024 and why it has since added 43 basis points: retail CD cost fell 4.11% to 3.57% while loan yields rose 5.73% to 5.93% (Q2 8-K).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Borrowings of $412M are almost all FHLB advances, $190M long-term maturing 2027 to 2030 (10-K 2025, Item 7). Capital return is the clearest positive: the dividend rose 13.3% to $0.17 in March (8-K 2026-03-17), April's authorisation of 2,000,000 more shares took the remaining total to 2,148,285, about 11.9% of the company (8-K 2026-04-27), and share count fell from 18,360K to 17,975K in six months. Insiders point the other way: 213,115 shares sold for $3.68M over twelve months, zero open-market purchases, with director Michael Hansen selling his entire 81,413-share position at $15.16 in October 2025 and director Patrick Lawton exiting all 49,255 shares near $18.60 in February 2026 (Form 4s).

## 5. Management: said versus did
CEO William Bruss called a "robust dividend payout ratio" a priority (8-K 2026-03-17), and the dividend rose and stock was repurchased in every quarter reviewed. On Q2 he was candid about the other half: mortgage "achieved solid growth in loan origination volumes ... but this growth was offset by softer sales margins" (Q2 8-K). Accurate: originations rose 5.6% to $621.8M while gross margin fell 3.84% to 3.60% and segment pre-tax income fell 20.2% (Q2 8-K segment table).

## 6. Valuation
Reverse DCF: at $21.36 against $19.53 of tangible book, with a 10% cost of equity and 2% growth, **the current price implies a sustainable return on tangible equity of about 10.7%**, above the 8.6% delivered over twelve months and the 9.65% of its best quarter.

- **Base (50%).** Margin plateaus near 3.0%, bank earns $29M, mortgage $3M, buyback cuts the count 5%. EPS about $1.90 in 2027 on tangible book of $20.50; 1.15x gives $23.60 plus dividends, roughly 14%.
- **Bear (25%).** Rates stay high, mortgage margin slips below 3.5% and the segment turns negative (it earned $22K pre-tax in Q1 2026), and a multi-family credit surfaces. Earnings $25M, ROTE 7%, 0.9x book: $17.60, down 18%.
- **Bull (25%).** Cuts reprice the certificate book another 50 basis points (roughly $4M pre-tax), a refinance wave lifts mortgage volume and margin together, or the board sells the bank. At 1.5x book, $29.30.

Probability weighted, about $23.40, roughly 10% above the price. Fair value, not a bargain.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results in late October: the margin print and whether retail CD cost breaks below 3.50%. Execution of the 11.9% authorisation. Federal Reserve cuts through 2027. A sale or separation of Waterstone Mortgage, nowhere signalled in the filings but the cleanest way to close the discount.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. Net interest margin below 2.90% for two consecutive quarters.
2. Mortgage banking segment pre-tax income negative in two of any rolling four quarters.
3. Nonaccrual loans above 0.75% of total loans, or any single-quarter multi-family charge-off above $3M.
4. Fewer than 150,000 shares repurchased in a quarter while the stock trades below 1.2x tangible book.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 2. Waterstone is a genuinely improved bank: net interest margin went from 2.17% in 2024 to 3.03% in the June 2026 quarter as an $865M certificate book reprices down faster than its multi-family loans roll, credit is near pristine at 0.30% nonperforming assets with net recoveries, and capital return is aggressive, with the dividend up 13.3% and an authorisation covering 11.9% of the shares. The screen understates this by pairing FY2025 earnings with a June 2026 balance sheet, so the real multiple is 12.8x trailing, not 14.5x. But the market has noticed: the stock is 0.7% off its 52-week high at 1.09x tangible book, which implies a sustainable 10.7% return on tangible equity against the 8.6% delivered, and 59% of revenue still comes from a mortgage originator running at a 93% efficiency ratio that earned $22K pre-tax in March 2026. Add two directors who sold out entirely and no insider purchase in twelve months, and roughly 10% probability-weighted upside does not pay for a 46% multi-family concentration. Worth owning at 0.9x book, not 1.09x.
