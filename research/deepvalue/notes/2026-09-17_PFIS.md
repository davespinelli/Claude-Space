## 1. What the business actually does
Peoples Security Bank and Trust is a 1905-vintage Pennsylvania community bank, $5.44B of assets, 40 branches across thirteen PA counties plus Middlesex County NJ and Broome County NY (8-K 2026-07-30, EX-99.1). It takes commercial and retail deposits and lends mostly against commercial real estate: of $4.30B of gross loans at 2026-06-30, non-owner-occupied CRE is 21.5%, 1-4 family 20.5%, C&I 17.0%, owner-occupied CRE 13.5%, multifamily 10.1%, construction 7.6% (investor presentation, August 2026). A $1.1B trust and wealth arm supplies part of FY2025's $21.7M of fee income (10-K FY2025, Item 7). It is number two by deposit share in Scranton-Wilkes-Barre at 15.5% and the seventh largest PA community bank, expanding into the Lehigh Valley, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh and Piscataway NJ.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is no edge case, and that caps the verdict at WATCH. The screen thought it had one because the merger year made FY2025 look like a 596% recovery. The real improvement is visible and already paid for: FTE net interest margin went 2.54% (2023) to 2.84% (2024) to 3.58% (2025) to 3.82% (Q2 2026), efficiency 64.1% to 56.5% (investor presentation, August 2026). The market noticed. The stock is up 39% in six months and sits 1.6% below its 52-week high (universe_v2.csv). Nobody is forced to sell: BlackRock owns 6.5%, Vanguard 5.0%, directors and officers 8.2% (DEF 14A 2026).

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Credit is clean, which is the point of maximum danger. Nonperforming assets were 0.34% of loans at 2026-06-30, up from 0.29% on one commercial relationship, allowance 0.98% of loans (8-K 2026-07-30, EX-99.1). FY2025 net charge-offs were 0.07% and the full-year provision $98 thousand, released partly on "improvement in qualitative factors driven by a reduction in commercial real estate concentration levels" (10-K FY2025, Item 7). That has turned: $4.5M provisioned in H1 2026 against a $39 thousand benefit in H1 2025.

Two concentrations matter. Total investment CRE is $1.687B, 283% of total risk-based capital, inside but near the 300% supervisory guideline, construction at 55% (investor presentation, August 2026). The $169.0M equipment financing book, 4% of loans, carries 11.4% of the allowance, charged off 0.83% in 2025 against 0.57% in 2024, and is reserved near 2.9% versus a 0.96% book average (10-K FY2025, Item 7); management shrank it from $179.1M having "tightened underwriting standards."

Margin quality is the second issue. H1 2026 net interest income included $6.5M of accretion of purchase-accounting discounts on acquired FNCB loans, down from $8.5M (8-K 2026-07-30, EX-99.1) against H1 pre-tax income of $37.0M. Deposit mix is decent, 21% noninterest-bearing, 31% uninsured, cost of deposits down to 1.68%, but brokered deposits at 7.7% and a noncore funding dependence ratio up from 11.2% to 16.8% show funding being stretched to keep loan growth going (10-Q 2026-06-30).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Equity is $536.2M, tangible $435.6M, still carrying $23.9M of net-of-tax unrealised AFS losses, about $2.39 a share, so tangible book growth from $38.75 to $43.51 is flattered by mark recovery as well as retained earnings. Liquidity is ample: $1.2B of remaining FHLB capacity and $568.8M at the discount window (10-Q 2026-06-30). Capital allocation is conservative to a fault: the dividend rose 19.9% to $2.47 in 2025, 41.6% of earnings, and no stock has been repurchased since 131,686 shares for $5.9M in 2023 (10-K FY2025, Item 7). Insider activity over twelve months is grants and tax withholding only, zero open-market trades (Form 4s, 12m), so no signal either way.

## 5. Management: said versus did
Champi concedes the miss plainly rather than spinning it: "While net income declined compared to last year, our performance was driven by robust loan growth" (8-K 2026-07-30, EX-99.1). On equipment finance they said they would tighten and improve asset quality, and the book shrank. On the merger they said a full year of combined operations would lift earnings, and FY2025 delivered $5.88 against $0.99. There is no numeric guidance to score. Succession is in motion: former President Thomas Tulaney's SERP was annuitised in August 2026 (8-K 2026-09-01).

## 6. Valuation
Base: accretion runs off, margin holds near 3.75%, provisions normalise to 15-20bp, ROTCE settles near 12%, tangible book compounds 8% to about $50.70 by end-2028 at 1.5x, roughly $76 plus $5 of dividends, about 7% a year. Bear: CRE and the equipment book bite, provisions run 25-30bp, earnings fall to about $4.50 at 1.2x tangible book, roughly $55. Bull: the curve steepens, deposit costs keep falling faster than asset yields, efficiency returns to 53%, ROTCE reaches 15%, and PFIS is bid for or does another accretive deal at 1.9x tangible book, roughly $90. Weighted 50/25/25 that is about $77 against $70.91. Reverse DCF: at 1.63x tangible book, a 13.7% ROTCE and a 10% cost of equity, the current price implies tangible book compounding at about 4.1% forever with that return sustained; strip the fading accretion and ROTCE is nearer 11.2%, at which the same price implies 7.6% perpetual growth, which this franchise has not shown organically.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results in late October, the first clean read on accretion and provisions. Any FOMC cut, which helps deposit costs faster than it hurts the 5.80% loan yield. Resumption of the dormant buyback. Further Pennsylvania bank consolidation, where a 15.5% Scranton share is a scarce asset.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
1. Nonperforming assets above 0.60% of loans, or quarterly net charge-offs above 0.20% annualised, for two consecutive quarters.
2. Brokered deposits above 10% of total deposits for two consecutive quarters (7.7% at 2026-06-30, from 2.5% a quarter earlier).
3. FTE net interest margin below 3.60% for two consecutive quarters, meaning accretion runoff is not being offset by deposit repricing.
4. Equipment financing net charge-offs above 1.5% of that portfolio in any fiscal year (0.83% in 2025).

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 3. PFIS is a genuinely better bank that the screen found for the wrong reason: the 596% earnings jump the model rewarded is the 2024 FNCB merger year unwinding, and on the clean comparison earnings are down, $2.95 a share in H1 2026 against $3.18. Real: margin repair from a 2.54% net interest margin in 2023 to 3.82% in Q2 2026, efficiency down to 55%, 7.6% organic loan growth, a clean 0.34% nonperforming ratio. Not yet in the numbers: accretion fading from $8.5M to $6.5M a half, provisions already turned from a benefit to $4.5M in six months, and a CRE book at 283% of total risk-based capital funded increasingly with brokered deposits. At 1.63x tangible book and 12.5x trailing earnings with the stock 1.6% off its high, you pay a fair price for a 13% return that probably settles nearer 11% once merger accounting stops helping. It becomes an IDEA near 1.2x tangible book, or on two quarters of margin above 3.75% without accretion. Not here.
