## 1. What the business actually does
Flanigan's runs 32 units in South Florida: 11 company-owned "Flanigan's Seafood Bar and Grill" restaurants plus a sports bar, 11 "Big Daddy's" package liquor stores, and 10 restaurants owned by affiliated limited partnerships it controls as sole general partner and consolidates (10-K, Item 1). It franchises five more, four to the CEO's family. Food is 61% of sales, bar 15%, package liquor 23% (10-Q). The LP structure is the point: outside investors fund each new restaurant, Flanigan's takes a 3% service-mark fee plus half of distributable cash once capital is returned, and consolidates the whole restaurant while owning 7% to 54% of it.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is none, and that caps this at WATCH. The stock is up 36.3% in six months and 51.2% on twelve-month-less-one-month momentum, only 14.6% below its 52-week high (universe_v2.csv). Nothing forces a sale: officers and directors hold 62.3% and the CEO 52.8%, and the Flanigan Family Stock Holdings operating agreement requires family shares to be offered first to other members before any open-market sale (DEF 14A). What exists is a permanent structural discount, not a temporary one: an NYSE American "controlled company" with three independent directors (DEF 14A), 1.86M shares, $1.5M daily volume, no equity plan, no calls and no 8-K in six months. That is a fact about the security, not a mistake anyone will correct.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
Operating leverage is real. Costs and expenses fell to 92.88% of revenue in Q3 FY2026 from 94.50%, taking operating income to $4,000k from $2,852k, up 40.3% (10-Q). Food and bar gross margin rose to 67.92% from 67.48%; package margin is 24.44% versus 25.21% over 39 weeks and management says it will fall further on "a reduction in pricing of certain package store merchandise to remain competitive." Payroll fell to 29.89% of revenue from 31.04% despite the Florida minimum wage increase. Comparable weekly food sales at company-owned restaurants rose 7.81% in Q3, but comparable bar sales rose only 0.72% over 39 weeks, which is the soft spot. Inputs are contracted: $9.2M of baby back ribs for calendar 2026 against $7.8M for 2025, "due to an increase in market price and quantity ordered" (10-K, Item 1). LTM capex excluding the $8.45M Stuart property purchase was $3.54M against $4.79M of D&A, so maintenance spending is light.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Debt rose to $31,434k from $20,618k in nine months, all deliberate mortgage financing: Calusa refinanced up to $11,100,000 from $5,676,856 to withdraw $5,495,000, plus new mortgages of $3,375,000 on Store #19 and $3,150,000 on Store #75 at 5.975-5.995% out to 2033-2041 (10-Q). A lender advancing roughly $17.6M against three properties is the best evidence in the bundle that owned real estate is worth well above carrying value, though these documents do not let me size it. Cash is $28,843k, working capital $20,509k, all covenants met. The dividend rose to $0.60 from $0.55. No buyback, no option plan. The CEO bought 3,866 shares at $29.50 to $32.00 across two Form 4s (2026-01-02, 2026-05-22) with zero sales, a good sign but trivial against his 983,047 shares.

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
Management gives direction rather than guidance and has been right: it said package and restaurant sales would rise in FY2026 and they did. The alignment problem is pay. The CEO earns 14.75%, and the CFO and COO 2.625% each, of annual income before taxes and D&A above $650,000, plus 5% each to the CFO and COO of pre-tax pre-D&A income from company-owned and the company's share of LP restaurants (DEF 14A, note 3 to the Summary Compensation Table). FY2025 named-officer pay totalled $4,027,000 against $5,033,000 of net income attributable to stockholders; FY2024 was $3,416,000 against $3,356,000. Those bonuses rose $597,000 while consolidated pre-tax income before D&A rose $3,457,000, so roughly 17% of incremental profit was paid out first. Officers and directors also collected $713,825 of LP distributions in FY2025 (DEF 14A, Related Party Transactions).

## 6. Valuation
Base (55%): FY2026 revenue about $221M, operating income about $13.0M, attributable net income about $7.4M or $3.98 a share at the actual tax rate; 12.5x gives about $50. Bear (20%): a hurricane closure quarter or a Florida consumer rollover, comps flat, package margin to 23%, operating income back to $9M, EPS about $2.69 at 10x, about $27. Bull (25%): Cutler Bay opens, package e-commerce compounds, FY2027 operating income $15M, EPS about $4.60 at 14x, about $64. Probability-weighted value is about $48.90, 11% above $44.23. Reverse DCF: at a 10% required return, $82.2M of market value against $5.7M of normalised attributable earnings taxed at 25% implies about 3% perpetual growth, roughly the annual menu-price increase with no new units. The price is neither demanding nor cheap.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
The FY2026 10-K, due around December 2026, covering the Q4 hurricane quarter and the full-year package margin. Whether the Cutler Bay site, bought for $2.2M in FY2025 with "site planning underway", opens as a company-owned unit or as another limited partnership, which decides how much of the next unit's profit shareholders keep. Further mortgage refinancings, which reveal appraised property values.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
All but one unit sits in South Florida through hurricane season, with windstorm cover carrying a $100,000 deductible plus 5% of insured losses and an explicit risk that adequate cover becomes unavailable (Item 1A). Kill criteria:
1. Package liquor store gross margin below 24.0% in two consecutive quarters.
2. Comparable weekly bar sales at company-owned restaurants negative year over year in two consecutive quarters.
3. FY2026 consolidated operating income below $12.0M in the December 10-K, meaning Q4 added less than $2.2M.
4. Net income attributable to noncontrolling interests above 38% of consolidated net income for a full fiscal year, against 37.2% in FY2025 and 34.7% for 39 weeks of FY2026.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 2. Flanigan's is a genuinely good little business, a family-controlled South Florida restaurant and liquor chain compounding on menu price and package-store traffic, with operating income up 40% in its latest quarter, 0.16x leverage, maintenance capex below depreciation and owned real estate a lender just valued at roughly $17.6M across three properties. But the screen's 12.6x is wrong in the company's disfavour, because about a third of consolidated profit belongs to the outside limited partners in the ten consolidated restaurant partnerships, which puts the honest multiple at 14.6x normalised attributable earnings and 12.4x reported, and named-officer pay of $4.0M against $5.0M of attributable net income took roughly 17% of every incremental dollar of pre-D&A profit before shareholders saw it. There is no edge case to underwrite: the stock is 15% off its high after a 36% six-month run, 62% is held by insiders who cannot easily sell, and what remains is a permanent controlled-company and liquidity discount rather than a mispricing. Worth waiting for is the December 10-K, which covers the hurricane quarter and says whether the package margin held and whether Cutler Bay opens as a company unit or as another partnership that hands a third of the next restaurant to somebody else.
