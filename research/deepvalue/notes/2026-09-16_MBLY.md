## 1. What the business actually does
Mobileye sells EyeQ system-on-chips and camera software to Tier 1 suppliers who integrate them into ADAS, plus higher-content bundles (Surround ADAS, SuperVision, Chauffeur, Drive) that add hardware and hands-free capability, Moovit mobility software, and since February 2026 humanoid robotics (10-K 2025, Item 1). It ships about 10 million systems a quarter at roughly $48 each. All EyeQ SoCs come from one supplier, STMicroelectronics, with no long-term pricing or quantity guarantee and termination at will by either side (10-K 2025, Item 1A). China was 23% of 2025 revenue and 26% of 2024.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is no positive edge case, and the screen's apparent one does not exist. What exists is an overhang: Intel holds 597,768,015 Class B plus 50,000,000 Class A shares, about 77% (DEF 14A 2026-04-24). Intel sold 50,000,000 shares into a secondary at **$16.50** in July 2025 while Mobileye bought $100M back from Intel at $16.046. The stock is now under half that. A controlling holder that has shown it will sell, at a parent under its own capital pressure, argues for a discount rather than against one. Two directors bought in the open market (Yeary 12,360 at $7.98 on 2026-07-30; Yeboah-Amankwah 11,841 at $8.46 on 2026-08-06, Form 4s), together under $200,000 — the only insider buying in twelve months.

## 3. Reconciling GAAP to normalised (artifact_flag is true, and the artifact is the screen's)
FY2025's $440M operating loss contains **no** impairment. It contains $443M of acquired-intangible amortisation and $277M of stock compensation (10-K 2025, Item 7). Add back the amortisation only and the business earned $3M. The screen instead added a charge taken fifteen months earlier. The charge pattern is also not non-recurring: $2,695M in Q3 2024, then **$3,788M in Q1 2026**, the latter triggered by "a 35% decline in the share price of the Company's Class A common stock" plus a higher discount rate for Middle East geopolitical risk and "increased uncertainty related to the evolving competitive landscape" (10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A). Two write-downs totalling $6.48B in six quarters is a business repeatedly marking down its own forward cash flows.

## 4. Unit economics, balance sheet and capital allocation
Q2 looks like a 46% profit improvement and is not. Of the $110M GAAP grant booked in Q2, the company says the Q1 and Q2 portions "were roughly equivalent", so about $55M belongs to Q1 (8-K 2026-07-23, EX-99.1). Like for like, Q2 operating loss was about $85M against $74M a year earlier, and adjusted operating income about $108M against $106M — flat. H1 operating cash flow **fell** to $210M from $322M on rising receivables (10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A). Stock compensation is guided to $396M for 2026 against $277M in 2025, up 43% on 4-7% revenue growth. The balance sheet is genuinely clean, no debt and $1.4B of cash, but $591M left in Q1 and the $250M buyback authorised in April has spent $24M.

## 5. Management: what they said versus what they did
The CEO calls the foundation "robust and highly profitable" (8-K 2026-07-23, EX-99.1) on a quarter with a GAAP operating loss. The decisive fact is the Mentee Robotics purchase: $900M for a private pre-revenue Israeli humanoid robotics company, of which CEO Prof. Shashua received 37.83% (about $341M) and CTO Prof. Shalev-Shwartz 13.07% (about $118M) as chairman/co-founder and co-founder; the CEO's son and son-in-law also received consideration (DEF 14A 2026-04-24). Over half the price went to the two executives setting the strategy. It closed 3 February 2026; five weeks later the company wrote off $3,788M of goodwill. The 10-K concedes Prof. Shashua "does not devote his full time and attention to our company" and lists five other ventures he chairs or runs (Item 1A). Intel's board designee resigned on 2026-08-08 (8-K 2026-08-12) and the EVP of Business Development moved part-time on 2026-09-03 (8-K 2026-09-10).

## 6. Valuation
**Reverse DCF:** at a 10% discount rate a $5.27B EV needs roughly $527M of perpetual owner earnings; owner earnings (adjusted operating income less stock compensation) are approximately zero in 2026, so the price implies revenue roughly doubles to about $4B at incremental margins near 26% — in a quarter when units grew 3% and price fell 2.4%.
**Base (50%):** revenue $2.5B by 2029, 22% adjusted margin ($550M), SBC normalises to $350M, owner earnings $200M at 20x plus $1.2B cash = **$6.20**.
**Bear (25%):** China keeps shifting to domestic ADAS suppliers, ASP erosion continues, revenue flat at $2.0B, adjusted operating income ex-grant near $200M, owner earnings negative; cash plus 10x $100M = **$2.60**.
**Bull (25%):** MOIA robotaxi scales and the Surround and Stellantis Cloud-Enhanced wins convert from 2027, revenue $3.2B at 25% adjusted margin, SBC $450M, owner earnings $350M at 25x plus cash = **$11.75**.
Probability-weighted **$6.69**, about 16% below $7.92.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 print in late October, the first clean read on whether ASP stabilises. MOIA Hamburg testing converting to paid service. Any Intel announcement on its remaining 77%. First 2027 guidance, carrying the Surround and Stellantis launches.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria (what would overturn the PASS)
1. Average system price flat or rising for two consecutive quarters (it ran $49.7, $51.7, $50.8, $49.3, $48.5).
2. Trailing adjusted operating income **excluding** the Israeli R&D grant above $300M (the FY2026 guide implies about $205M).
3. Intel's stake falls below 50% through a distribution or sale that clears the overhang.
A third goodwill impairment, or another related-party acquisition above $100M, would confirm the verdict.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
PASS, conviction 4. The screen's 0.4x is not an artifact worth reading into but two data errors stacked: it counted only the 252 million Class A shares and ignored Intel's 598 million Class B, understating market value by about $4.7 billion, and it added back a goodwill impairment taken in the third quarter of 2024 to 2025 operating income, inventing $2.3 billion of profit on $1.9 billion of revenue. Corrected, Mobileye is a $6.7 billion company with $5.3 billion of enterprise value that earned about $3 million of operating income in 2025 after amortisation add-backs and nothing after the $277 million of stock compensation it is guiding up 43 percent. The 2026 recovery is an Israeli R&D grant enacted in the second quarter: strip it and adjusted operating income was flat year over year, gross margin fell 354 basis points, units grew 3 percent while price fell 2.4 percent, and half-year operating cash flow fell from $322 million to $210 million. Around that sit a 77 percent holder who already sold at $16.50, a second goodwill write-off of $3.8 billion taken in March, and a $900 million purchase of a pre-revenue robotics company in which the CEO and CTO personally collected more than half the consideration five weeks before that write-off.
