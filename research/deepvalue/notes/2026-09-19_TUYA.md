## 1. What the business actually does
An appliance maker or OEM buys a Tuya module with embedded connectivity and firmware, pairs it with Tuya's cloud and app SDK, and ships a smart product without building the stack (20-F Item 4). Revenue comes in three lines: PaaS (68% of FY2025 revenue, modules plus platform), Smart Home and Robot Product (14%, finished devices) and AI Application and Others (13% in Q2 2026, cloud subscriptions such as video storage and energy management). It served 3,700 PaaS customers in 2025, of whom 291 were "premium" at over $100,000 of revenue, rising to 318 by 30 June 2026 and supplying 89.5% of PaaS revenue (6-K 2026-08-25). The holding company is Cayman, operations are mostly PRC, and the variable interest entity, Hangzhou Tuya Technology, was deregistered in November 2025, having contributed no revenue in 2023, 2024 or 2025 and held under 0.01% of assets (20-F Item 4).

## 2. Why it is mispriced, and the honest answer on the edge case
The arithmetic is stark: $1.59 a share of net liquid assets against a $1.64 price, so about $30M buys a business that earned $21M of normalised after-tax operating profit over twelve months. Two structural improvements are recent: the VIE is gone, and equity compensation, which ran at $65M to $68M a year through 2024, has effectively stopped.

But there is no informational edge. Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, Jefferies and CICC all asked questions on the Q2 2026 call, so this is a covered stock and the discount is a preference, not a discovery problem: investors decline to underwrite PRC-controlled cash inside a weighted-voting-rights structure where the founders alone decide whether it is distributed. No catalyst for that discount to close appears in the filings, which caps the verdict at WATCH.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
FY2025 gross margin was 48.2% (PaaS 48.3%, Smart Solution 23.7%, SaaS 72.5%; 20-F Item 5). Q2 2026 blended margin fell to 46.3% and PaaS to 46.8% on chip cost inflation the CFO says was passed through in dollars but not in percentage terms (Q2 call). TTM operating margin is 8.4% GAAP, 10.6% non-GAAP. The warning is the PaaS net expansion rate: 122%, 118%, 114%, 109%, 102% through the four quarters of 2025 (20-F Item 5). Q2 2026 growth of 16.0% suggests a rebound, but no 2026 expansion rate is disclosed, so the reacceleration is unconfirmed at the cohort level. AI Application growth fell from 17% in Q1 to 3.9% in Q2 on lumpy B2B project work (Q2 call, CFO).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
No borrowings. Of cash and equivalents at 31 December 2025, $148.3M sat in PRC subsidiaries, $739.2M in Hong Kong subsidiaries and $3.1M at the parent and other overseas entities (20-F Item 5.B). Three quarters of the cash is therefore already outside mainland capital controls, and neither Hong Kong nor the Cayman Islands withholds on dividends out (20-F Item 5, Taxation). Distributions were $33.0M in 2024, $69.8M in 2025 and $37.0M, or $0.0605 per ADS, declared 2 March 2026 and paid in April (20-F Item 5.B; 6-K 2026-08-25 note 5), so the 2026 payout is about half of 2025's, and the board declined an interim dividend for H1 2026. No listed securities have been repurchased and no treasury shares are held. Dilution is trivial today, 4.1M options and RSUs outstanding, but 56,980,259 shares, 9.3% of the issued count, remain available under the 2024 Share Scheme (6-K 2026-09-04). Insiders did nothing in twelve months: no open-market buys or sales, only share-class conversions and one tax withholding (Form 4s, 2026-04-28, 2026-07-08).

## 5. Management: said versus did
They delivered the efficiency promise: operating results went from a $47.6M loss in 2024 to an $11.5M profit in 2025, non-GAAP operating profit from $22.7M to $33.7M (20-F Item 5). They missed a specific forecast. On the Q1 2026 call management pointed to a seasonal rebound in device usage lifting the AI Application margin in Q2; the Jefferies analyst noted on the Q2 call that it did not happen, margin flat at 72.0% and growth down to 3.9%. The CFO answered candidly on mix rather than defensively.

## 6. Valuation
Base (50%): the operating business at 10x normalised after-tax profit is $212M, and the $976M of liquid assets takes a 10% governance and friction haircut to $878M, total $1,090M or $1.78 a share. Bull (25%): growth holds near the Q2 pace, non-GAAP operating profit reaches $45M in 2027, normalised after-tax profit of $34M at 15x is $510M, the cash discount narrows to 5%, total $1,437M or $2.34. Bear (25%): chip costs keep compressing PaaS margin, growth reverts to 2025's 8%, the Share Scheme is drawn at 4% of revenue, normalised after-tax profit falls to $11M at 8x, interest income drops with rates and the cash discount widens to 30%, total $773M or $1.26. Probability-weighted $1.79 against $1.64, about 9% upside with a hard asset floor. Reverse DCF: at $1.64 the market pays roughly $30M for an operation earning $21M after tax, implying either that the profit vanishes within about two years or that the $1.59 a share of liquid assets never reaches a minority holder.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results around November 2026: whether the $32.9M inventory build converts back to cash and PaaS gross margin stabilises as the CFO promised. FY2026 results and the dividend declaration around March 2027: a restoration toward the 2025 level, or a first buyback authorisation, would attack the discount directly. The next 20-F should disclose the 2026 PaaS net expansion rate.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. PaaS gross margin below 45% in two consecutive quarters (46.8% in Q2 2026, 48.3% in FY2025).
2. Cash, time deposits and treasury securities below $900M at any quarter end without a matching distribution to shareholders (from $976.1M at 30 June 2026).
3. Share-based compensation above $8M in any quarter, or issued shares up more than 3% in twelve months from 613,369,540 at 31 August 2026.
4. No dividend declared with the FY2026 results after $37.0M in 2026 and $69.8M in 2025.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Tuya trades at $1.64 against $1.59 a share of cash, time deposits and treasury securities, so the market pays about $30M for a debt-free IoT platform that grew revenue 16% last quarter and earned $21M of normalised after-tax operating profit over twelve months. Two things quietly got better: the VIE was deregistered in November 2025, and equity compensation fell from $68M a year to almost nothing, which is why GAAP profit looks like it inflected when underlying operating profit grew about 15%. Three quarters of the cash sits in Hong Kong rather than the mainland, and $140M has been paid out in dividends since 2024. But none of this is a secret: four global brokers cover the stock, so the discount is a structural refusal to value founder-controlled PRC cash, not an information gap, and the founders have given no sign of closing it, having halved the dividend, declined an interim payout and authorised no buyback. Meanwhile the PaaS net expansion rate fell to 102% by end-2025, gross margin is compressing on chip costs, and H1 2026 operating cash flow dropped by more than half on an inventory build. Downside is bounded by the balance sheet, but upside needs a capital-return decision only the founders can make.
