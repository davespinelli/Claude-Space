## 1. What the business does
Holding company for California Bank of Commerce, N.A., a $4.03B-asset national bank in San Diego with 14 branches and 11 commercial banking offices lending to small and mid-sized California businesses (10-K 2025, Item 1). The $3.03B year-end book was 60.0% commercial real estate and other, 20.0% C&I, 10.7% multifamily, 4.7% one-to-four family, 4.6% construction and land development. It is the product of two deals: Bank of Santa Clarita in 2021 and the all-stock merger with California BanCorp (CALB) on 2024-07-31, which added $1.43B of loans and $73.1M of goodwill.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is none I can defend. The stock sits 0.4% below its 52-week high after a 25.7% six-month move (universe_v2.csv), and the register is full of bank specialists who know what they own: Wellington 8.97%, AllianceBernstein 7.30%, FJ Capital 6.39%, Endeavour 5.46% (DEF 14A 2026). The one structural seller, Castle Creek, is already below 5% (13G filed 2026-01-29, DEF 14A footnote 6). The only real asymmetry runs against the buyer: merger accounting is flattering reported results, and it is the screen, not the market, that has been fooled. No edge case caps the verdict at WATCH; valuation takes it to PASS.

## 3. Credit, margin, deposits
Credit is genuinely improving. Non-performing assets fell to 0.44% of assets at 2026-06-30 from 0.97% at 2026-03-31, driven by full repayment of two nonaccrual loans on a 123-acre Los Angeles event venue after a cash sale closed 2026-05-04 (8-K 2026-05-05) plus a discounted note sale costing $127 thousand (8-K 2026-07-27). Substandard loans fell to $64.2M and special mention to $48.6M, though $14.9M was downgraded from pass to substandard in the quarter. Allowance is 1.13% of loans held for investment. The offset is concentration: CRE is **449% of total risk-based capital** (10-Q Q2 2026) against the 300% level at which the OCC applies heightened scrutiny, which the company flags itself (10-K 2025, Item 1A), and Item 1A warns that a few large real-estate relationships can move asset quality and earnings disproportionately.

The deposit franchise is the best part: noninterest-bearing deposits 37.4% of a $3.36B book, cost of deposits 1.31%, no brokered time deposits, no FHLB or discount-window borrowings (8-K 2026-07-27). That funding produces the 4.71% margin.

But 32bp of that 4.71% is purchase-accounting accretion (same source), and it is running out: $19.1M in FY2025 (46bp), $10.8M in H1 2025 (54bp), $6.6M in H1 2026 (31bp). Remaining net unearned discount on acquired loans was **$24.8M at 2026-06-30, down from $31.3M at 2025-12-31** (10-Q, loan table footnote). About two more years, decaying throughout. Ex-accretion the margin is roughly 4.39%, still excellent, but 2027 and 2028 will read as decline.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Clean. Borrowings average $34.4M, and the company intends to redeem all $35.0M of its 3.50% fixed-to-floating sub debt at par in Q3 2026 rather than let it reset to SOFR plus 2.86% (8-K 2026-07-27). Liquidity is $1.14B of borrowing capacity plus $211.0M of unpledged securities and $265.0M of cash; AOCI losses are 1.2% of tangible common equity. Capital return started late: only $3.25M of dividends in all of FY2025 (universe_v2.csv), rising to $0.12 a quarter from Q3 2026. Buybacks took 512,509 shares in H1 2026 at about $18.36 average, with 875,563 shares (2.7%) still authorised. Insiders and directors hold 8.14% (DEF 14A 2026). Twelve months of Form 4s show net selling of $149 thousand, which is noise: one director exercised and sold 7,500 shares at $21.19 (Form 4, 2026-08-04), director Frank Muller bought 450 shares at $22.13 (Form 4, 2026-09-09). Nobody is buying size.

## 5. Management: said versus did
Steven Shelton retired as CEO effective 2025-12-31; David Rainer, 69, became Chairman and CEO on 2026-01-01 under a contract to 2029-03-01 plus one year as Executive Director (DEF 14A 2026). He previously built and sold CU Bancorp. The 2025 derisking matched the talk: $8.4M of charge-offs taken deliberately to cut criticized exposure, brokered deposits eliminated, NPAs down (10-K 2025, Item 7). The gap is on earnings. The Q2 release is headlined "STRONG SECOND QUARTER EARNINGS" and Rainer calls it "a very solid second quarter" (8-K 2026-07-27), but H1 2026 net income of $28.1M was down 9.4% from $31.0M and diluted EPS fell from $0.95 to $0.86 (10-Q Q2 2026). Neither release volunteers that pre-tax pre-provision income is flat.

## 6. Valuation
Reverse DCF in the form that works for a bank: at 1.53x tangible book, a 10% cost of equity and 4% long-run growth, the price implies a sustainable ROTCE of **13.2%**. Reported Q2 was 12.62%; normalised for accretion and a 30bp provision it is 9.1%.

- **Bear (20%): $12.90.** California CRE deteriorates, provision goes to 60bp, ROTCE falls to 7%, the group de-rates to 0.9x tangible book.
- **Base (50%): $16.40.** Accretion runs off over two years, only partly replaced. Loan growth of 8% on a 4.39% core margin against 5% expense growth roughly offsets the $11.6M pre-tax accretion loss, EPS holds near $1.75 to $1.85, ROTCE settles at 10.5%, justifying about 1.08x tangible book rolled forward a year for retained earnings.
- **Bull (30%): $29.00.** Operating leverage and loan growth hold ROTCE at 12.5% through the roll-off, and a 69-year-old CEO with a 2029 end date sells the bank at the 1.9x tangible book California deals have fetched.

Probability-weighted **$19.50 against $21.81**, about 11% below the price.

## 7. Catalysts
Q3 2026 results in late October carry the sub-debt redemption and the raised dividend. The FY2026 10-K in March 2027 is the first filing where full-year accretion drops materially. The realistic re-rating event is a sale of the bank, which on Rainer's contract is a 2027 to 2029 question.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. **Kills the PASS:** pre-tax pre-provision income excluding accretion grows more than 5% year over year for two consecutive quarters, meaning core earnings are actually replacing the discount.
2. **Kills the PASS:** the stock trades below 1.15x tangible book, about $16.90 on the 2026-06-30 figure, without credit having deteriorated.
3. **Kills the PASS:** an announced whole-bank sale, or CRE concentration below 400% of total risk-based capital reached through capital growth rather than a shrinking book.
4. **Confirms the PASS:** substandard accruing loans rise above $90M from $64.2M, or the allowance falls below 1.05% of loans held for investment while substandard is rising.

## 9. Verdict and summary
**PASS, conviction 3.** California BanCorp is a good bank at a full price, and the screen thinks it is a cheap bank because merger accounting is doing the work. The 11.1x P/E, 13.7% ROTE and 1060% net income growth all rest on FY2025, a year containing $19.1M of purchase-accounting accretion and an $8.8M reversal of provision, measured against a FY2024 carrying $18.5M of day-one CECL provision on acquired loans and $16.3M of merger expense; management's own adjusted FY2024 net income was $32.4M, so real growth was 95%, not 1060%, and the run-rate now points the other way, with H1 2026 net income down 9.4% and pre-tax pre-provision income flat. Strip the accretion, which has $24.8M of discount and about two years left, and put a 30bp through-cycle provision on a book that is 449% CRE to total risk-based capital, and the bank earns roughly $1.30 a share and a 9.1% return on tangible equity: 16.8x, and a return below its cost of capital, against a price already implying 13.2%. The deposit franchise is genuinely good, credit is genuinely improving, and a 69-year-old CEO with a 2029 contract end is a real takeout option, which is why this is a 3 and not a 4. Revisit below 1.15x tangible book.

**What the screen got wrong.** (a) Net income growth of 1060.6% is measured against merger-charge-depressed 2024; adjusted FY2024 net income was $32.4M (10-K 2025, Item 7). (b) The 0.5% dividend yield is stale; the payout is $0.12 a quarter from Q3 2026, 2.2% at $21.81 (8-K 2026-07-27). (c) universe_v2.csv carries net debt of minus $230M and an EV of $471M, treating $265M of deposit-funded bank cash as corporate net cash; for a depository that has no meaning and was not used. (d) The 11.1x P/E and 13.7% ROTE both use FY2025, which included the $8.8M reversal of provision; normalised they are 16.8x and 9.1%.
