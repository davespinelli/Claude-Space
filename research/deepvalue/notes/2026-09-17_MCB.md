## 1. What the business does
Holding company for Metropolitan Commercial Bank, a New York state-chartered commercial bank founded 1999, nine banking centres (four Manhattan, plus Brooklyn, Great Neck, Lakewood NJ, Miami, West Palm Beach opened 9 June 2026). It writes $3M-$40M tickets to middle-market businesses and real estate entrepreneurs and funds itself through deposit verticals rather than branches: EB-5 escrow, title and escrow, 1031 exchange, charter schools, municipalities, property managers, bankruptcy trustees, borrowing clients (Item 1). A pure spread bank — fee income was $5.1M of $181.5M H1 2026 revenue after the 2023 crypto and 2024 Banking-as-a-Service exits.

## 2. Why it might be mispriced
No clean edge case, which caps the verdict at WATCH. The candidate story is optics: headline earnings have been dinged four years running by items management calls isolated — a $35.0M regulatory settlement in 2022, a $10.0M regulatory reserve in Q3 2024, a $37.6M provision in 2025 for one out-of-market CRE relationship, $13.3M plus $3.3M in Q2 2026 (slide 21, Item 7, EX-99.1) — while pre-provision revenue compounded from $64.1M in 2020 to a $173M annualised run rate. But nobody is forced to sell, the stock is 10.4% off its 52-week high and up 15% in six months, and the only identifiable sellers are insiders: net open-market selling of $2.66M over twelve months, almost all director Robert C. Patent (25,000 shares at $90-$96), against two 1,000-share CFO purchases.

## 3. Credit quality, margin, deposits
**Concentration.** Healthcare was $3.4B, 45.9% of loans at 30 June 2026, including $3.3B of skilled nursing facilities, up from 41.4% at year-end (10-Q) — roughly 3.5x tangible common equity in one reimbursement-driven industry. The defence is real: CRE SNF average LTV 73%, stabilised SNF at 2.0x debt service coverage, operators typically over 1,000 beds, mostly certificate-of-need states, no realised healthcare losses since 2002 (slides 16-17). The 10-K names the matching risk — budget reductions, funding restrictions or a change in payment methodology in states where healthcare customers operate could impair their ability to pay (Item 1A). Non-owner-occupied CRE was 304.1% of risk-based capital, still above the 300% supervisory threshold and up from 299.5% a quarter earlier despite the raise.

**Reserves.** The allowance fell from $97.1M (1.43% of loans) to $62.0M (0.85%), which the 10-Q attributes to charge-offs on two previously provisioned CRE relationships *and* "adjustments made to the Bank's allowance for credit loss estimation process in the first quarter of 2026". The ratio was cut 58bp in the same half-year that net charge-offs annualised at 1.35% (2.0% in Q2 alone) and concentration rose four points; coverage of non-performing loans fell to 92.6% from 111.7%. Non-performers did improve, $86.9M to $67.0M, and 97% of loans are pass-rated. **Funding:** non-interest-bearing deposits 20.6% of total, uninsured $2.1B (27%). The EB-5/title/charter-school block, the largest, shrank from $2,082M to $1,858M year over year while retail grew $553M to $953M (slide 18); the 10-K flags immigration policy and the proposed "Gold Card" programme as EB-5 risks.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
No FHLB advances or fed funds purchased at 30 June; only $20.6M of trust preferred debentures and $11.0M of secured borrowings. The Q1 2026 follow-on added 2,303,418 shares and about $205M of tangible common equity, implying net proceeds near $76 a share against $72.69 of year-end tangible book, and took CET1 from 10.7% to 12.9%. On 19 June the board authorised a $50.0M buyback; on 20 July it raised the dividend 40% to $0.35. Issuing near tangible book in March and authorising repurchases at 1.28x tangible book in June is poor sequencing, and no repurchases are yet reported. CEO Mark DeFazio held 150,583 shares in March 2026; 2025 pay was $4.78M on a $1.0M salary, and his agreement still carries a 280G excise-tax gross-up (DEF 14A).

## 5. Said versus did
The Q2 quote — "we made significant progress in the resolution of legacy asset quality matters" — is accurate, and the progress came through charge-offs. Unsaid: the same period's methodology change lowered coverage. The 2025 10-K blamed the rising CRE concentration ratio partly on funding buybacks and dividends at the holding company; three months later they added a bigger buyback and a bigger dividend.

## 6. Valuation
Base (55%): normalised EPS $8.13, credit settles near 40bp, tangible book near $84 by end-2027 at 1.2x, about $100 plus dividends. Bear (25%): reserves rebuild toward 1.43%, a $42.5M pre-tax catch-up (~$2.4 of book), plus 2% cumulative loss on the $3.3B healthcare book over two years (~$3.7 a share after tax), multiple resets to 0.9x — about $70. Bull (20%): NIM holds above 4%, loans compound 10%, credit normalises to 15bp (EPS ~$8.95), 1.5x tangible book — about $130. Weighted: roughly $99 against $90.50, about 9% upside. Reverse DCF: at 1.17x tangible book the price implies a sustainable ROTCE near 10.9% against a 10% cost of equity and 5% growth, almost exactly what the bank earns.

## 7. Catalysts
Q3 results (late October): whether the allowance rebuilds and whether Q2's C&I provision was contained. First repurchases under the $50M authorisation. Regulatory commentary on the 300%-plus CRE ratio. Medicaid reimbursement decisions affecting skilled nursing operators.

## 8. Kill criteria
1. Allowance below 1.00% of loans for two consecutive quarters while healthcare exceeds 45% of loans.
2. Net charge-offs above 0.50% annualised in any quarter after Q3 2026, or non-performing loans back above $86.9M.
3. Any skilled nursing facility loan disclosed as non-accrual — "no realised losses since 2002" is the load-bearing beam.
4. No shares repurchased under the June 2026 $50M authorisation by the 2026 10-K while the stock trades below 1.3x tangible book.

## 9. Verdict
WATCH, conviction 2. Metropolitan is a genuinely good deposit franchise: tangible book per share compounded 13.3% a year since the 2017 IPO, NIM is at a decade high of 4.08%, the deposit base is organic and unusually diversified across niche verticals, and capital is strong at 12.9% CET1. The screen's 15.8x P/E and 7.4% ROTE are artefacts of dividing pre-raise earnings by post-raise share count; the honest trailing multiple is 11.6x and normalised earnings power is about $8.13 a share. But at 1.17x tangible book the price already implies the ~11% ROTCE the bank actually earns, so there is no gap to close, and the fact that would change the answer cuts the wrong way: 46% of loans are healthcare, almost all skilled nursing, while the allowance was cut from 1.43% to 0.85% in the same six months that charge-offs annualised at 1.35% and a methodology change delivered a Q1 reserve release. Insiders are net sellers into the strength. Worth owning at a real discount to tangible book, or after two clean credit quarters with reserves rebuilt — not here.
