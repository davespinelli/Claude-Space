## 1. What the business actually does
PINE owns 128 freestanding single-tenant net leased retail properties, 4.5M sq ft across 31 states, 99.5% occupied, 9.2-year weighted average lease term, $50.0M of annualised base rent, 55% of it from investment-grade tenants (Lowe's 13%, Dick's 9%, Walmart 7%) (Q2 2026 press release). Average rent is $11.17 per sq ft and enterprise value per sq ft is $183 against a $289 peer average, so the basis is genuinely low. It has no employees and is externally managed by a subsidiary of CTO Realty Growth for 1.5% of total equity a year plus a 15% incentive fee above an 8% hurdle (10-K 2025, Item 1). Since July 2023 it has also originated first-mortgage construction, land and redevelopment loans: $167.0M face at a 13.2% weighted coupon including PIK, 1.6-year average remaining term, $85.4M unfunded.

## 2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case
There is a structural discount and a partial misread, not a clean mispricing. The structural part is probably permanent: an externally managed $320M REIT with $2.2M of daily volume, whose manager is paid on equity raised and whose termination fee is three times the average annual base fee (about $17M, roughly $0.90 a share), has no natural institutional buyer (10-K 2025, Item 1A). The misread is that the market appears to apply a private-credit multiple to the whole company, when the property portfolio is long-dated and sound and the loan book is 30% of assets. Nobody is being forced to sell. That combination caps this at WATCH.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
Two engines, very different economics. The property engine grew lease income 5.8% in FY2025 and 5.1% in Q2 2026, entirely from acquisitions at 7.4–7.6% initial cash cap rates, with real estate expenses flat at $2.1M a quarter (10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2). The loan engine supplied the rest. Its largest position is a $40.6M residential land loan in Austin, Texas at an 18.82% coupon including 4% PIK, maturing October 2028: 24% of the book and roughly $7.6M of annual interest (Q2 2026 presentation, schedule of commercial loans). An 18.8% coupon on raw land is a price of risk, not a yield opportunity. The CECL reserve across $186.1M of total commercial loans is $1.86M, or 1.0%. Property-side concentration is moderate (top tenant 13% of ABR); loan-side concentration is severe. And the property portfolio has been selling at losses: dispositions produced $4.8M of losses in 2025, $4.3M of it on four Walgreens, and impairments ran $3.2M, $1.7M, $7.4M and $0.9M across 2023, 2024, 2025 and 1H26 (10-K 2025, Item 7). A fourth straight year of write-downs is not a one-off.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
$369.5M face debt, all unsecured, nothing due before February 2029. Above the common sit $60.6M of 8% Series A preferred, non-callable until November 2030. Liquidity is the pressure point: $83.3M total ($80.5M revolver plus $2.8M cash) against $85.4M of unfunded construction commitments. Dilution is the year's story: shares went from 14,783,419 at Dec-2025 to 17,595,168 at Jun-2026, with 2,801,075 sold on the ATM at $19.31, and the ATM was upsized to $150M with forward sale agreements in April (8-K 2026-04-24). Per-share AFFO still rose, so issuance has been accretive so far, but the manager earns 1.5% on every dollar raised and the 10-K says so plainly: "our Manager may have an incentive to recommend that we issue additional equity securities at dilutive prices" (10-K 2025, Item 1A). Insiders are one-way and small: zero open-market buys in twelve months against 12,582 shares sold for $243,516, all by one director (Form 4, 2026-07-30 and prior). CTO holds 14.0% fully diluted. PINE's $20M Rivana loan is a carve-out of a 103-acre project on which CTO was already lender and raised its own commitment (DEF 14A 2026, related-party transactions).

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
July guidance moved up modestly and honestly: AFFO $2.11–$2.15 to $2.12–$2.15, net income $0.72–$0.76 to $0.81–$0.84, disposition volume cut from $30–60M to $20–40M (Q2 2026 press release). The dividend rose 6.7% to $0.320, the sixth increase since 2020, at a 52% AFFO payout. Albright called it "$77 million of investments at an attractive blended yield of nearly 9%" and said the company "opportunistically utilized our ATM program." Both are accurate. Unsaid: that 8.7% blend is a 7.4% property cap rate mixed with a 10.0% loan, and the year-to-date loan coupon is 13.1%.

## 6. Valuation
Base (50%): the loan book stops growing, repayments recycle into 7.4% cap properties, $0.10 of credit cost, 2027 AFFO about $2.05 at 9.5x = $19.50 plus the $1.28 dividend, roughly +14%. Bear (25%): the Austin land loan goes non-accrual and takes a 30% loss; AFFO falls to about $1.65, the multiple derates to 8x, $13.20 with the dividend at risk, about −25%. Bull (25%): the book repays at par over its 1.6-year life, lease income carries the story and a cleaner PINE re-rates toward 12x $2.10 = $25.20, +44%. Probability-weighted about $20.30 against $18.21, roughly +11%. Reverse DCF: at $18.21 against guided AFFO of $2.12–$2.15 the price implies an 11.7% AFFO yield, that is zero real per-share growth in perpetuity at an 11–12% cost of equity, or about $0.35 a share of permanent AFFO loss, equivalent to writing off 15% of the loan book and never replacing the income.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results in late October, the first full period for the Austin loan. 2027 guidance in February. The Management Agreement's current term expires 31 January 2027 and auto-renews unless two-thirds of independent directors act (10-K 2025, Item 1); any fee renegotiation or internalisation talk there is the largest single re-rating lever. The loan book's 1.6-year average term means most of it resolves by end-2027.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
1. Any commercial loan placed on non-accrual, or the CECL reserve above 2.5% of total loan face (1.0% at 30 June 2026), in either of the next two 10-Qs.
2. The Austin residential land loan ($40.6M face, 18.82% coupon, October 2028) extended, modified, upsized, or its PIK component raised.
3. Net debt / pro forma adjusted EBITDA above 7.0x, or total liquidity below unfunded construction commitments, for two consecutive quarters.
4. Common ATM issuance at a weighted average price below $18.00 in any quarter.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Alpine trades at 8.5 times its own guided 2026 AFFO with a 7.0% dividend covered at a 52% payout, against net-lease peers near 14.6 times, and it owns a 99.5%-occupied, 9.2-year, 55% investment-grade retail portfolio carried at $183 per square foot, about 60% of the peer basis. The reason it is cheap is visible in the filings rather than hidden: almost all the earnings growth is a construction and land loan book that went from $90M to $167M in eighteen months at a 13.2% coupon, 24% of it in one Austin land loan at 18.82% with 4% PIK, reserved at 1.0%, funded by issuing 19% more shares in six months through a manager paid 1.5% of every dollar raised who would cost about $17M to fire. Leverage is already 6.4 times EBITDA and $85.4M of unfunded construction commitments slightly exceed $83.3M of total liquidity. The net-lease half is worth more than the price; the credit half is worth an unknown amount less, and nothing public lets an outsider underwrite an 18.8% land loan. Wait for the book to season, through one clean repayment cycle or one non-accrual, before deciding which company this is.
