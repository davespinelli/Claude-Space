## 1. What the business actually does
Hope Bancorp is the holding company of Bank of Hope, an $18.99B-asset California state-chartered commercial bank headquartered in Los Angeles. It grew out of a Korean-American franchise and now describes itself as the largest regional bank serving multicultural customers across the mainland and Hawaii, with 45 Bank of Hope branches in eight states and 28 Territorial Savings branches in Hawaii (10-K Item 1; 10-Q Item 2). The engine is plain spread banking: commercial real estate is 57% of the $14.94B loan book, C&I 26% and residential mortgage 17%, funded by $15.88B of deposits, 22% of them noninterest-bearing. Fee income is small and steady, roughly $18-19M a quarter from deposit charges, trade finance and wire fees, and gains on sales of SBA loans.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is no edge case, and that decides the verdict. The screen flagged HOPE because 2025 GAAP earnings of $61.6M were cut roughly in half by one quarter of deliberate cleanup, a $38.9M loss on repositioning the securities portfolio and $21.5M of Territorial merger costs, both concentrated in Q2 2025 (10-K, non-GAAP table). That is a genuine artifact. It is also one the market has already priced: the stock is up 32.8% in six months, carries positive 36.1% twelve-month momentum and sits 3.1% below its 52-week high on $10.2M of average daily volume (universe_v2.csv). Nobody is being forced to sell an index-member bank near its high. The company agrees: it repurchased just 772,726 shares at an average of $11.28 in the first half of 2026, 0.6% of shares outstanding, and still has $26.6M left of a $50.0M authorisation the board approved in January 2022 (10-Q). Four years and less than half used is not a company that thinks its stock is cheap.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Credit deteriorated through 2024 and 2025 and is mending. Nonaccrual loans went 0.33% of loans in 2023 to 0.67% in 2024 to 0.90% in 2025, then back to 0.75% at June 30, 2026, helped by the payoff of one large CRE nonaccrual in Q1 (10-K; 10-Q). Coverage tells it less flatteringly: the allowance was 351% of nonaccruals in 2023 and 119% at the end of 2025, recovering to 137%. Net charge-offs were 0.24% annualised in Q2, and $18.6M of the $19.7M charged off in the half was C&I. The worry sits elsewhere: the CRE allowance rose from $85.1M to $94.0M in six months, which the 10-Q attributes to "increased refinance risk and downgrade of CRE loans." Non-owner-occupied CRE of $5.71B is about 261% of the holding company's $2.18B of total capital, under the 300% supervisory threshold but with an unusual mix, gas stations and car washes at 14% of CRE and hotels and motels at 10% against office at only 4%, at a 47% weighted average LTV. Margin is the good news, 2.96% in Q2 against 2.69%, on a 46bp fall in the cost of interest-bearing deposits versus a 15bp fall in loan yields. Funding is not pristine: uninsured deposits are $6.42B, 40% of the total and up from 38% at year end, with roughly $1.69B more in brokered, California State Treasurer and Hawaii public money. Efficiency ex-notable was 66.0% in the half, better than 68.6% in 2025 but far from 60.6% in 2023.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Equity rose $12.6M in the half as $62.6M of earnings met $35.8M of dividends, $8.7M of buybacks and a $7.3M increase in accumulated other comprehensive loss (10-Q). The AFS book carries a $200.5M unrealised loss, already inside tangible book. Liquidity is ample at $8.09B of borrowing capacity, cash and unpledged securities. The allocation record is the problem: share count went from 120.8M at the end of 2024 to 128.2M at the end of 2025 to fund Territorial while tangible book per share went from $13.81 to $13.71, so the deal was tangibly dilutive, and tangible book per share is $13.85 today against $13.51 at the end of 2021. The $0.56 dividend has not moved in five years and took 115% of 2025 earnings. No proxy or Form 4 data is in the bundle, so insider ownership and compensation alignment are unverified here.

## 5. Management: what they said versus what they did
The bundle has no call or release, so this rests on the filings. Management has done what it said on cost: merger costs fell 88% year over year and noninterest expense ex-notable was flat in the half despite a full second quarter of Territorial. It has also kept acquiring, announcing in Q1 2026 a purchase of certain assets and liabilities of SMBC's Commercial Banking Unit, subject to regulatory approval, on which the bundle discloses only $1.9M of Q2 expense and no size, price or accretion (10-Q Item 2). A bank whose last deal cost ten cents of tangible book per share buying again before returns have recovered is a fact to weigh, not a thesis.

## 6. Valuation
Base case: net interest margin settles near 3.05% as time deposits reprice against falling loan yields, provisions stay near $30M a year, ROTCE reaches about 8.5%, tangible book compounds to roughly $14.60 by the end of 2027 and the market pays 1.05x, giving about $15.30 plus $1.12 of dividends, so $16.4. Bear case: CRE refinance risk turns into losses, provisions double to $60M, ROTCE falls back to 5% and the multiple goes to 0.75x tangible book, about $10.50. Bull case: margin reaches 3.30%, efficiency falls to 62%, ROTCE reaches 10.5% and the market pays 1.3x a $15.00 book, about $19.50 plus dividends. At 25/50/25 the probability-weighted value is roughly $15.70, about 12% above price over two years, which is below a bank's cost of equity. Reverse DCF: at 1.014x tangible book, assuming a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth, the price implies a sustainable ROTCE of about 10.1%, against the 7.81% just delivered and an average of 6.4% across 2024 and 2025.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
The Q3 2026 report in late October, which shows whether margin held above 2.96% and whether the CRE allowance rose a third time. The SMBC closing and its first disclosed size and tangible book impact. The repricing of $3.9B of time deposits maturing within six months of June 30, 2026, visible in the Q3 and Q4 cost of deposits. Any use of the $26.6M of remaining buyback authorisation above $14.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
1. Net interest margin below 2.90% for two consecutive quarters (2.96% in Q2 2026). That kills the repricing thesis.
2. Nonaccrual loans back above 0.90% of loans receivable, or the CRE allowance rising for a third consecutive half-year from the $94.0M of June 30, 2026.
3. Annualised net charge-offs above 0.40% of average loans in any quarter (0.24% in Q2 2026).
4. Tangible book per share below $13.71 at any quarter end, meaning the SMBC deal or accumulated other comprehensive loss has eaten the book again.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 2. The screen's 28.9x P/E is a real artifact, since 2025 GAAP earnings carried $51.8M of after-tax notable items from the securities repositioning and the Territorial merger, and the honest trailing multiple is 13.8x on $130.0M of normalised earnings, with the operating turn genuine: Q2 2026 earnings ex-notable rose 40% year over year, margin widened 27 basis points to 2.96% as deposit costs fell three times faster than loan yields, and nonaccruals fell from 0.90% to 0.75%. But there is nothing to underwrite. The artifact has already been paid for, with the stock up 32.8% in six months and sitting 3.1% off its high on $10.2M of daily volume, and at 1.01x tangible book the price already implies a sustainable return on tangible equity near 10%, against the 7.81% just earned and the 6.4% averaged over the last two years. Underneath, tangible book per share is $13.85 against $13.51 at the end of 2021, the Territorial deal cost ten cents of it while adding 6.2% to the share count, the unchanged $0.56 dividend took 115% of 2025 earnings, the board has used less than half of a buyback authorised in January 2022, and the company is buying again, an unsized SMBC commercial banking unit, before returns have recovered. The CRE allowance rising on refinance risk and downgrades, alongside a loan book that is 14% gas stations and car washes, is the specific thing that could make the bear case real. Wait for Q3 to show margin holding above 2.96% and the CRE allowance flat, and for SMBC to be sized, and this becomes arguable at a lower price.
