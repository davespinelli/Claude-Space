## 1. What the business actually does
A Fortune 500 master limited partnership formed in 2005, run by the Slifka family. Wholesale buys, blends, stores and moves gasoline, blendstocks, distillates, residual oil, renewable fuels and crude through 54 liquid energy terminals from Maine to Florida and into the Gulf (8-K 2026-08-07). GDSO supplies and operates 1,505 gasoline stations, 286 of them company-operated convenience stores, mostly in New England and the Mid-Atlantic (10-Q, KPI table). Commercial sells to public-sector and industrial end users and bunkers ships, including the Port of Houston since October 2025 (10-K, "2025 Events"). FY2025 product margin: GDSO $846.0M, Wholesale $321.7M, Commercial $26.3M.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is no temporary mispricing, which under the desk's rule caps this at WATCH before anything else. There is a permanent structural discount: a K-1 rather than a 1099, 34 million units, $3.0M average daily volume, no index membership, and a general partner limited partners cannot vote out. The only proxy on file is from 2012, and the 10-K concedes the general partner "may cause us to borrow funds in order to permit the payment of cash distributions, even if the purpose or effect of the borrowing is to make incentive distributions" (Item 1A). That discount is two decades old and priced. What is new is a step-change in profit a screen reading FY2025 would miss, but the market has not missed it: the units sit 5.9% below their 52-week high and are up 10.8% over six months (universe_v2.csv). Nobody is being forced to sell.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
The jump is margin, not volume. H1 2026 combined product margin was $727.3M against $594.4M: Wholesale added $75.2M "primarily due to more favorable market conditions in gasoline and residual oil", GDSO gasoline $48.0M "primarily due to higher fuel margins (cents per gallon)", Commercial $8.9M on bunkering (10-Q, Item 2). Station operations, the only stable line, was flat at $132.8M versus $132.1M, and GDSO sold 683 million gallons against 740 million from 1,505 sites versus 1,553. GDSO cents per gallon ran 36.5 in FY2024, 38.3 in FY2025 and 36.1 in Q2 2025, then 49.8 in Q2 2026. Wholesale gasoline realised roughly $2.21 a gallon in Q2 against $1.44, a 54% price rally on flat volume; a rally that size across a $577.8M inventory book (10-Q) throws off realised gains that do not repeat, and it normally compresses retail margin rather than expanding it 38%. Both segments are above trend at once. TTM operating cash flow of $324.8M less $125.4M capex is $199.4M, an 11.5% yield, but FY2024 operating cash flow was $31.6M on a working-capital swing. 2026 capex is guided to $60-70M maintenance plus $75-85M expansion (10-Q) against FY2025's $91.5M total, so free cash compresses from here.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
$1,250M of senior notes at face (6.875% 2029 $350M, 8.250% 2032 $450M, 7.125% 2033 $450M), $278.1M drawn on a $1.5B secured facility maturing March 2028, $125.8M of sale-leaseback financing obligations and $369.9M of lease liabilities (10-K Item 7; 10-Q). Interest was $135.2M TTM, 28% of adjusted EBITDA; covenants were met at year-end 2025. Unit count has not moved: 33,995,563 at both June 2025 and June 2026. Series A preferred went in 2024 and Series B on 2026-07-30, swapping a 9.50% coupon for cheaper borrowings. Distributions ran $0.7600, $0.7650 and $0.7800 a quarter, $3.12 annualised, a 6.1% yield. The leak: the Q2 payment totals $32.1M while common units take about $26.5M, so incentive distribution rights absorb roughly 17 cents of every distributed dollar, plus $11.3M of H1 net income (8-K 2026-08-07). Global GP LLC bought 124,184 units for $6.0M over twelve months, including 10,000 at $51.19 on 2026-05-20, with essentially no selling (Form 4 summary).

## 5. Management: what they said versus what they did
Eric Slifka's Q2 language is generic: the platform "enables us to create and capture value across market conditions" (8-K 2026-08-07). That describes a trading business and is not a claim the margin is durable. There is no numeric guidance beyond capex and no filed transcript, so this section is thinner than the desk would like. What they did beats what they said: both preferred series retired, the 7.00% 2027 notes taken out early, the facility extended, no units issued.

## 6. Valuation
FY2025 delivered $383.0M of adjusted EBITDA with the acquired terminals in place at 38.3 cents per gallon; call mid-cycle $410M with credit for terminal ramp and Gulf bunkering. Base: $410M less $138M interest, $65M maintenance capex and $8M cash tax is $199M, less about $22M to the general partner, or $5.21 a unit; at 9.5x, or a 7.5% yield on $3.12, that is $48 to $52. Bear: margin reverts to 36 cents and wholesale normalises, EBITDA $360M, coverage about 1.2x, leverage 4.7x, the market demands 9% to 10%: $31 to $35. Bull: retail margin proves structural near 44 cents, EBITDA holds near $470M, the distribution reaches $3.60 at a 6% yield: $60 to $65. Weighting 30/45/25 gives about $48 against $50.87, before the coupon. Reverse DCF: at the 8x EV to adjusted EBITDA GLP has fetched on trailing numbers, today's $3.42B enterprise value implies sustainable adjusted EBITDA of about $427M, almost exactly halfway between FY2025's $383M and the TTM $482M, so the price already assumes half the windfall is permanent.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 results in early November are the first clean read on whether 49.8 cents survives a quarter without a price rally. A raise to $0.79 or $0.80 in late October would show the board's own view of durability. The 6.875% 2029 notes are callable at par from January 2027.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
GDSO gallons fell 8% year over year, and Item 1A flags electric and alternative-fuel vehicles and heat-pump conversion; the general partner holds a limited call right exercisable without a fairness opinion; environmental liabilities are $93.4M. Criteria that would overturn this PASS:
1. GDSO gasoline product margin per gallon (KPI table: product margin divided by volume) stays above 44 cents for two consecutive quarters.
2. Net funded debt over trailing adjusted EBITDA falls below 3.0x at a quarter end without an asset sale.
3. The general partner buys in or eliminates the incentive distribution rights.
Conversely, coverage (adjusted DCF after preferred over total declared distributions) below 1.3x for two consecutive quarters, or leverage above 4.5x, turns this from PASS to avoid.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
PASS, conviction 3. Global Partners is a real asset network, 54 terminals and 1,505 stations from Maine to the Gulf, run by an owner-operator family that bought $6.0M of units in the open market and has not printed a single new one. It only reached the qualifying list because the XBRL tagging caught $278M of revolver and missed $1,234M of senior notes, so the screen saw 1.1x leverage where the real figure is 3.5x and an 11.3x multiple built on a $1.98B enterprise value that is actually $3.42B. Correct the balance sheet and the partnership tax rate and you get 10.6x enterprise value to normalised after-tax profit, fair rather than cheap, and that profit is a peak: GDSO earned 49.8 cents per gallon in the June quarter against 38.3 for all of 2025 on 8% fewer gallons, while wholesale booked inventory gains from a 54% price rally. The units sit 6% off their high, the general partner takes roughly 17 cents of every distributed dollar, and the reverse DCF says the price already capitalises half the windfall as permanent. A defensible 6.1% yield for someone who wants a K-1 and a commodity cycle, but not a mispriced quality-growth small cap.
