## 1. What the business actually does
Fidelity Bank is a 1908-vintage Louisiana savings bank, converted from mutual to stock form on 22 October 2024 with 19,837,500 shares sold at $10 (10-K Item 1). It runs 18 to 19 branches across six southern Louisiana parishes, with a $744.9M loan book that is 33.4% commercial real estate and 31.0% one-to-four family residential, funded by $841.4M of deposits at a 1.62% average cost. The mortgage division, NOLA Lending, was sold on 1 March 2026 and is now discontinued operations.

## 2. Why it is mispriced, the edge case
The edge case is specific: a recent standard-form thrift conversion, over-capitalised at 29% CET1, with no analyst coverage, no index membership and no dividend, whose earnings were buried under a loss-making mortgage division now sold. Management completed two 10% buybacks and authorised a third on 12 June 2026 (8-Ks), retiring roughly 30% of the IPO share count inside twenty months, all below book. The sellers are IPO subscribers taking a 60% gain on their $10 cost, indifferent to book rather than wrong about it. But the edge is priced: the discount to tangible book is only 10%, so retiring 10% of shares adds about 0.8% to book per share, not the 3% to 4% a 0.65x discount would deliver.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Credit is drifting the wrong way. Non-performing loans rose from 1.72% to 2.27% of loans during 2025 and classified loans from $15.8M to $20.5M, while coverage fell: the allowance is 0.85% of loans and 37.3% of non-accruals, down from 48.1% (10-K Item 1). Non-performing assets were 1.37% of assets at 6/30/26 against 1.18% a year earlier, so it has not stabilised. The offset is low loss content, net charge-offs of 0.21% of average loans, because the problem book is first-lien residential. CRE runs at 63% average LTV, but hotels are 18.6% of it, roughly $46M, the concentration to watch. NIM fell to 4.20% in Q2 from 4.65% as loan yields dropped to 6.88% from 7.21% while certificates of deposit, 32% of deposits at 3.37%, repriced slowly, leaving a 92.62% efficiency ratio.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Equity fell from $314.5M to $283.8M in six months on $27.6M of buybacks, while FHLB advances rose from $78.3M to $111.2M, explicitly to fund securities purchases and the buyback (10-Q). Borrowing wholesale near 4% to repurchase stock earning a 1% ROE works only if the book is worth par. Against that, on 25 March 2026 the board granted the CEO 120,000 restricted shares and 300,000 options under a 2025 Equity Incentive Plan costing $2.6M a year pre-tax against $4.8M of continuing pre-tax income, while twelve-month insider open-market buying totalled $3,210 against $19,577 of sales (Form 4). Management is taking equity, not buying it.

## 5. Management, said versus did
Kept: the 19 May release promised to explore "the possibility of a third stock repurchase program," and it came three weeks later. Not kept: the 10-K strategy leads with growing core deposits and expanding Baton Rouge and Lafayette, yet both deposits and loans shrank. It guided to about $1.1M of 2026 salary savings; H1 non-interest expense rose 12.8%.

## 6. Valuation
Reverse DCF: at 0.905x book the price implies a sustainable return on equity near 9% on a 10% cost of equity and 2% growth, against the 0.4% the bank earns. The market is pricing the capital, not the earnings.

- **Bear (25%), $13.20.** Allowance moves to 1.5% of loans, a $4.8M pre-tax hit erasing a year of earnings, NIM below 4%, de-rating to 0.75x book.
- **Base (50%), $16.50.** The third buyback completes near $16.50, adding 0.8% to book per share and retained earnings 0.4%, so book reaches $18.00 at an unchanged 0.92x.
- **Bull (25%), $24.00.** The franchise is sold. A 19-branch bank with $841M of 1.62% deposits and 29% CET1 fits Hancock Whitney, Home Bancorp or Origin; 1.30x TBV on $18.50 of book in 2028 gives $24.

Probability-weighted value is about $17.55, roughly 10% above the price over two years. Not enough for the credit drift.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Completion of the third buyback; the Q3 2026 print, the first clean quarter with NOLA absorbed; a fourth programme or a first dividend; and eventually, willingness to sell. I could not verify a three-year post-conversion acquisition restriction in the documents read.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. Allowance to non-accrual loans falls below 35%, or non-performing assets exceed 1.75% of total assets, in any quarter.
2. Net interest margin below 4.00% for two consecutive quarters.
3. The third repurchase programme is not substantially complete within twelve months of the 12 June 2026 authorisation, or no fourth follows it.
4. Continuing-operations income excluding one-off gains stays negative for two consecutive quarters after Q3 2026.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 2. FB Bancorp is an over-capitalised Louisiana thrift that converted in October 2024 and has since retired about 30% of its shares below book while selling its loss-making mortgage division. Everything a conversion investor wants is here except the price. At 0.90x tangible book the discount is only 10%, so each 10% buyback adds under 1% to book per share, and you underwrite that thin accretion against a bank earning a 0.41% return on equity, with margin down 45 basis points, non-performing loans at 2.27% on 37% reserve coverage, and management that awarded itself a $2.6M-a-year equity plan against $4.8M of pre-tax income while buying $3,210 of stock. The screen's 64.6x P/E flatters it: that is FY2025 income against a run rate nearer $1.2M, and Q2 was profitable only because of a life insurance death benefit. Own it at 0.75x book, or once the post-NOLA cost base prints.
