## 1. What the business actually does
A $5.8B California bank holding company, 627 employees, serving small businesses from Mendocino to Kern County (10-K 2025, Item 1). Calling it a lender misleads: average loans were $683M in Q2 2026 against $4.57B of securities, a 14.2% loans-to-deposits ratio (8-K 2026-07-16, table 3). It is a portfolio of investment-grade bonds funded by an exceptionally cheap deposit base: 45.2% of deposits noninterest-bearing demand, 98.6% transaction and savings, an annualised 0.24% cost of funding all earning assets.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is none, and the verdict is capped at WATCH accordingly. WABC trades $7.7M a day, is 27% owned by BlackRock and Vanguard (DEF 14A 2026-03-13), and already carries a premium to the average community bank precisely because the market understands the funding. Nothing here is neglected, spun off, or forcibly sold; the screen flagged it on the share-count artefact above.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Loan credit risk is immaterial: nonperforming assets were $808 thousand at 30 June 2026 against an $11.6M allowance, 1.59% of loans (8-K 2026-07-16; 10-K 2025, Item 7). Commercial real estate is 68% of loans but only 8% of assets, so the usual concentration test does not bind. The real exposure is the bond book: $2.66B amortised cost of corporate securities carried at $2.54B, a $114M unrealised loss that pulls to par. All investment grade, but 38% sits in the BBB band and 57% is financial-sector paper, roughly 3.5x tangible equity in corporate credit with no allowance assigned.

Margin fell to 3.77% in Q2 from 3.85% a year earlier but rose from 3.74% in Q1, and earning-asset yield ticked up to 4.01% from 3.98%. Net interest income FTE was $52.674M in Q2 against $52.690M in Q1, essentially flat: first evidence that MBS redeployment offsets floating-rate runoff. Against that, management's own simulation shows net interest income down 2.2% over the next year with rates unchanged and 3.1% on a 100bp cut (10-K 2025, Item 7, Interest Rate Risk). Deposits do reprice: savings rates went 0.17% in 2023 to 0.77% in 2025 and noninterest-bearing demand slipped to 46.2% from 47.5%, though the 6% fall in average deposits in 2025 slowed to 1.0% in Q2 2026.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Westamerica retired about 2 million shares for $104M in 2025, after just 4 thousand in 2024 (10-K 2025, Capital Resources), then 1.621M more in H1 2026, 6.6% of the company in six months. On 24 April the board added 2 million shares to the authorisation, leaving 2,785,023 shares, 11.8% of the company, available through 31 December 2026 (8-K 2026-04-24). The dividend rose to $0.48 from $0.46 the same week.

Buying at 1.86x tangible book is accretive to EPS but dilutive to book value per share, which fell to $37.09 from $37.91 at year end, and the company names the risk itself, that it "could repurchase shares of its common stock at price levels considered excessive" (10-K 2025, Item 1A). The arithmetic still favours it while a 7.7% earnings yield beats roughly 3% after tax on more bonds, but this is capital consumption, not compounding: the H1 pace of about $115M returned against $54.7M earned exhausts the $220M of excess capital in roughly two years, after which buyback capacity falls to retained earnings near $65M, 4.8% of market value.

## 5. Management: what they said versus what they did
David Payne, 70, chairman since 1988 and CEO since 1989, holds 4.7% (DEF 14A 2026-03-13), though no successor is named and outside directors hold trivial stakes, several under 1,000 shares. He said the enlarged authorisation "recognizes Westamerica's financial strength" and that the company "continues to proactively manage shareholder's capital" (8-K 2026-04-24); it then retired 753 thousand shares the next quarter. There is no revenue or EPS guidance to hold them to, and the only forward number disclosed is the rate simulation, which points down.

## 6. Valuation
Base, 55%: net interest income holds near $52.5M a quarter, fees near $10M, expense creeps 2.5%, giving 2027 net income near $105M on 20.8M average shares, EPS $5.05. At 12x, $61, plus about $3.8 of dividends, so $65.
Bear, 25%: another 100bp of cuts lands the simulation's 3.1% hit, deposits resume a 4% decline, expense grows 3%. Net income $95M on 21.5M shares is $4.42; a shrinking bank derates to 10x, giving $44.
Bull, 20%: net interest income troughs in Q3 as 2.74% legacy corporates reprice at 4.82%, expense flat, full authorisation used. Net income $115M on 20.0M shares is $5.75; at 14x, $80.
Probability weighted that is about $62.55 against $59.09, roughly 6% over fifteen months. Reverse DCF: at 1.86x tangible book on a 12.8% return on average tangible common equity, a Gordon solve implies the price expects tangible book and earnings to compound at about 6.8% a year in perpetuity at a 10% cost of equity, or 4.6% at 9%, growth only the falling share count can supply.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 results in mid-October are the key print: a second consecutive flat-to-up sequential net interest income confirms the trough. The authorisation expires 31 December 2026, so renewal lands in Q4 or Q1. The Fed path is the dominant exogenous variable and cuts against the thesis.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
1. Net interest income FTE prints below $51.5M in any quarter, against $52.674M in Q2 2026 and $52.690M in Q1.
2. Average noninterest-bearing demand deposits fall more than 5% year over year in any quarter, against 3.4% in Q2 2026.
3. Quarterly buyback falls below 400 thousand shares while the stock trades under 2x tangible book, against 753 thousand in Q2 2026.
4. A credit loss allowance is established against the corporate securities book, or the BBB-band share rises above 45% from 38% at year-end 2025.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 2. Westamerica owns one of the cheapest deposit franchises in American banking, funding its earning assets at 24 basis points, and a 4.7% owner-operator is retiring close to 10% of the shares a year out of a capital position more than three times the regulatory minimum, which is why EPS grew in Q2 while profit fell. But the business underneath shrinks on every line, management's own rate model projects a further decline even with the Fed standing still, and at 1.86x tangible book and 13x earnings the price already implies mid-single-digit perpetual growth that the buyback alone must deliver, from an excess-capital pool with roughly two years left in it. The screen made it look cheap at 11.8x only by dividing last year's profit by this year's smaller share count. What to wait for is specific: two consecutive quarters of flat or rising net interest income alongside a continued buyback, or a price nearer 1.3x tangible book, about $43.
