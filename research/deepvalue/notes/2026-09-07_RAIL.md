## 1. What the business actually does
FreightCar America builds freight railcars, rebuilds and converts idled cars back into service, and sells parts through an Aftermarket segment (10-K 2025, Item 1). All production is at one AAR-certified plant in Castaños, Mexico, where 1,916 of its 1,986 employees work. Customers are financial institutions (78% of 2025 sales) and shippers (16%), and the top five were 75% of 2025 revenue, up from 48% in 2024. FY2025 revenue was $501.0M, gross profit $73.2M (14.6%), operating income $33.9M (Item 7).

## 2. Why it is mispriced, and the limit of the edge
The clean distortion is mechanical. On June 26, 2026 PIMCO's vehicle OC III LFE II LP net-exercised its 2020, 2021 and 2022 warrants and the company issued 13,619,377 shares, taking shares outstanding from 19,062,155 to 32,775,760 (8-K 2026-07-02; DEF 14A footnote 2). Screens read that as a 71.4% issuance, and `universe_under2b.csv` flags exactly that. It was not dilution: these were effectively penny warrants already inside the reported count, since weighted basic shares were 31,793,746 in Q2 2025, before any exercise (8-K 2026-08-03). Economics unchanged, optics changed. The exercise also flipped equity from negative $107.4M to positive $36.2M and cut the warrant liability from $168.5M to $14.0M, ending years of GAAP dominated by mark-to-market noise (a $32.2M loss in 2025 alone, Item 7).

The dirty distortion is that the stock is 52% off its high because the operating year fell apart. That is a miss, not a misunderstanding, and it caps this at WATCH.

## 3. Unit economics and what broke
Q2 2026 revenue was $113.1M on 927 deliveries versus $118.6M on 939, but gross margin fell from 15.0% to 5.5% and Manufacturing from 13.4% to 3.2% (10-Q). Adjusted EBITDA was $1.2M against $9.3M, and only $2.2M of the gross profit gap is one-time realignment cost. Management cites mix plus a ramp that "began later than planned due to customer delivery timing, reducing fixed-cost absorption," pushing deliveries into early 2027 (8-K 2026-08-03, CEO).

Backlog is the bull's exhibit: 3,972 units worth $344M at June 30 against 1,926 units worth $137M at December 31, on roughly 45% of industry new-railcar orders, with value per unit up from $71k to $87k as mix shifted from rebuilds to new cars. The filings never disclose that backlog's margin, and winning 45% of orders in a soft market at a 3.2% manufacturing margin is at least as consistent with buying share as winning it.

Aftermarket is the piece worth owning and is not yet compounding profit. Revenue went $18.2M (2024) to $27.1M (2025) to $20.2M in H1 2026 (+44.5%), yet segment operating income was $7.2M in both 2024 and 2025 and $4.49M in H1 2026 against $4.10M (Item 7; 8-K 2026-08-03 segment tables). Gross margin fell from 47% to 32% as the Carly Railcar Components distribution business was added. Growth so far is bought, not earned.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Cash $63.0M against debt carried at $100.7M, roughly $105M face on the $115M Blue Torch Term Loan due December 2028 at 9.7%, plus a $35M ABL with $24.5M available (10-Q). Net debt of $42M is 1.0x guided EBITDA, which is fine; the coupon costs about $12.5M a year against $40M of EBITDA, which is not. An excess cash flow sweep applies, and $7.3M of the $7.8M H1 financing outflow was term loan repayment: free cash flow belongs to the lender, not to buybacks. There is no asset floor either, since the plant is leased and $52.1M of the $276.1M asset base is a deferred tax asset.

OC III LFE affiliates held about 49.2% of outstanding stock at December 31, 2025 (DEF 14A related-party note); one warrant for 1,636,313 shares remains. The Castaños plant is leased from director Jesús Gil and his brothers, who also supply materials, trucking and a warehouse, with $3.36M of related-party payables at year end. The board renewed a 15% poison pill on August 5, 2026 running to July 5, 2027 and added Bradley Pickard, a rail and logistics investment banker, as a director on June 10, 2026 (8-Ks 2026-08-05, 2026-06-16).

## 5. Management: said versus did
On May 4, 2026 the company reaffirmed 4,000-4,500 deliveries, $500-550M revenue and $41-50M adjusted EBITDA, and the CEO called Q1's 16.8% gross margin "our highest quarterly gross margin in over a decade." Thirteen weeks later: 3,500-3,900 deliveries, $410-460M revenue, $36-44M EBITDA (8-Ks 2026-05-04, 2026-08-03). The reaffirmation did not survive a quarter.

The incentive design predicted the shape of the year. For 2025 the bonus plan paid adjusted EBITDA at 84% of target and backlog at 0%, and the Chief Commercial Officer carries backlog at 45% weighting against 25% each for EBITDA and cash flow (DEF 14A). In 2026 backlog rose 121% and manufacturing margin collapsed. All 12 Form 4s in twelve months are grants (code A); no insider has bought a share with the stock down 52%.

## 6. Valuation
Fully diluted shares about 34.4M (32.78M plus the 1.64M-share 2023 Warrant); net debt about $42M; EV about $286M.

Base (50%): FY2027 revenue $520M as backlog converts, $12M of Castaños savings run full year, aftermarket $50M, adjusted EBITDA $55M at 6.5x with net debt $35M, giving $9.30.
Bear (30%): backlog converts at Q2 margins and the order cycle rolls over, adjusted EBITDA $28M at 5.5x with net debt $45M, giving $3.20.
Bull (20%): deliveries above 4,500 with gross margin back to 15%, adjusted EBITDA $70M at 7.5x with net debt $25M, giving $14.50.
Probability-weighted value is about $8.50 against $7.10, roughly 20% upside for a business that earned a 3.2% manufacturing gross margin last quarter.

Reverse DCF: at $7.10 the $286M enterprise value requires about $28.6M of after-tax operating profit held flat forever at a 10% cost of capital, which is FY2025's $33.9M of EBIT taxed at 15% and never growing again.

The decisive number is the implied second half. Guidance of $36-44M against $4.3M booked in H1 requires $31.7-39.7M on H2 revenue of $233-283M, a 13.8% adjusted EBITDA margin at the midpoint against 8.2% for all of FY2025 and 7.8% in the best recent quarter. The guide asks for the best half-year margin in the company's modern history immediately after its worst quarter.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results, early November: the first period carrying the $12M of savings and the ramp, and the only real test of the guide. Exercise of the last 1,636,313-share warrant retires the $14.0M liability. A PIMCO monetization would clear a 46% overhang and is the largest potential re-rating event.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
Structural risks: one plant, in Mexico, leased from a director's family, exposed to tariff changes the company flags itself (10-Q); five customers at 75% of revenue; no tangible asset floor; 9.7% money against a cyclical order book. Kill criteria, all observable in filings:
1. Q3 2026 consolidated gross margin below 11%, or FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut again below $36M.
2. Backlog units decline sequentially at Q3 or Q4 2026, or backlog value per unit falls below $80,000.
3. Aftermarket segment operating income below $2.3M in each of two consecutive quarters despite revenue growth.
4. Net debt above $75M at any quarter end, or disclosure of a Term Loan covenant waiver or amendment.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 2. FreightCar America looks like a 71% share issuance on top of a broken cyclical, and only the second half is true: the 13,619,377 shares PIMCO received in June were penny warrants already inside the 31.8M weighted basic count a year earlier, so nothing was diluted, while the exercise cut the warrant liability from $168.5M to $14.0M and took equity from negative $107.4M to positive $36.2M. What is genuinely broken is the plant. Q2 gross margin fell from 15.0% to 5.5% and manufacturing to 3.2% on a late ramp, management reaffirmed guidance in May and cut it in August, and the remaining guide of $36-44M against $4.3M earned in H1 requires a 13.8% second-half EBITDA margin from a company that has never beaten 8.2% for a year. The backlog is real at 3,972 units and $344M, up 121%, but its margin is undisclosed and a 45% order share won at a 3.2% manufacturing margin reads as much like price as like product. Probability-weighted value is about $8.50 against $7.10, no insider has bought a share in twelve months, and free cash flow is swept to a 9.7% term loan. The setup is interesting for who owns it, a 46% PIMCO stake, a renewed 15% pill and a rail M&A banker just added to the board, but the price is not low enough to pay for a guide this back-end loaded. The November Q3 print settles it.
