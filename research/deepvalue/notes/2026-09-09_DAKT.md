## 1. What the business actually does
Daktronics designs, manufactures, installs and services large-format LED display systems: stadium video boards, scoreboards, digital billboards, highway message signs, airport and transit displays, plus the control systems and software that drive them (10-K, Item 1). It is vertically integrated, doing engineering, metal fabrication, electronic assembly, technical contracting and 24-hour field service from plants in South Dakota, Minnesota, Ireland and China, with a Saltillo, Mexico facility opening in fiscal 2027. Five segments by FY2026 sales: Live Events $321.1M, High School Park and Recreation $183.3M, Commercial $180.8M, International $76.9M, Transportation $76.7M. 2,693 employees; founded 1968, public since 1994.

## 2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case
**There is none I can identify, and that caps this at WATCH.** No spin, no index event, no impairment artifact (the screen's artifact flag is false, reported impairments are zero), no forced seller. The stock trades 29% below its 52-week high on $13.1M of daily volume with positive twelve-month momentum, and at least two brokers cover it. The nearest thing to a dislocation is informational: Daktronics gives no annual guidance, so a project business is priced off one quarterly order number, and that number just fell 19.6%. Management says Q1 orders "do not include a few substantial transactions negotiated in Q1, which are expected to book in Q2" (Q1 release). That is a testable claim, not a mispricing. Alta Fox owns 6.0% under a cooperation agreement that standstills it until the 2027 annual meeting (DEF 14A), so the one holder with an agenda is contractually quiet.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
Gross margin went 25.8% in FY2025, 27.3% in FY2026, 30.5% in Q1 FY2027, on "strategic pricing, continued operational efficiencies, and overall project mix" (10-K, Item 7) and then on tariff refunds (10-Q). Operating margin was 4.4%, 7.3%, then 10.6% in Q1, but Q1 FY2026 was also 10.6%, so quarterly margin is flat year over year. FY2026 segment contribution margins: HSPR 23.6%, Transportation 23.0%, Live Events 19.8%, Commercial 18.7%, International 7.4%. Live Events is the largest and among the least profitable, "heavily engineered with high dependence on indirect installation costs" (Q3 FY26 call, CFO), and is where the orders went: Live Events bookings fell 48.8% in Q1 FY2027. Returns are genuinely good, $48.1M of normalised after-tax profit on roughly $174M of ex-cash invested capital, about 28%, though management's own 17-20% ROIC target is struck on total capital including cash. Cash conversion is the weak link: FY2026 operating cash flow fell to $49.2M from $97.7M on receivables, contract assets and inventory (10-K, Item 7), and LTM operating cash flow of $54.6M less $14.7M of capex leaves $39.8M against a $950M market value. The 10-K names the real risk directly: results "are likely to continue to fluctuate and be substantially affected by the size and timing of large contract order awards" (Item 1A).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Net cash $144.1M, no borrowings, $36.7M of bonded work against a $190M surety line. Diluted share count fell 1.7% year over year. Since reinstating buybacks in late 2024 the company has bought 3.36M shares at a VWAP near $15.15 (Q3 FY26 call), including $25.6M in FY2026, but only $4.4M in Q1 FY2027 at $19.56; on 2026-09-01 the board replaced the old authorisation with a $34.5M program it calls "substantially equivalent" housekeeping. FY2027 capex was guided at $26.6M in the 10-K on 2026-06-24 and $13.2M in the 10-Q ten weeks later, unexplained, which is either discipline or a demand signal. Insider ownership is thin: directors and officers hold 4.6% in total and the CEO holds 7,089 shares against a base salary just raised to $600,000 (8-K, 2026-09-02); twelve months of Form 4s show one open-market purchase of 98 shares at $15.97 and two sales totalling about $150k. FY2027 incentives pay on operating income (60%) and revenue (40%) for both the annual bonus and the three-year PSUs (8-K, 2026-07-17), aligned with the plan, though nothing pays on cash conversion, which is the actual weakness.

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
The team is new. Reece Kurtenbach left as CEO in March 2025 and the board in February 2026; Ramesh Jayaraman became CEO on 2026-02-01 with under a year at the company; Howard Atkins has been *Acting* CFO since March 2025 while sitting on the board, an arrangement the proxy says runs "until the earlier of the date on which a new Chief Financial Officer is appointed." Eighteen months without a permanent CFO, at a company that already needed two activist cooperation agreements, is a real governance gap. On delivery, the April 9, 2026 investor day set FY2028 targets of 7-10% revenue CAGR, 10-12% operating margin and 17-20% ROIC, with an EPS scenario matrix of $1.30 to $1.90 against street estimates of $1.45 to $1.55. FY2026 delivered 10.9% growth (on 53 weeks) at 7.3% margin and Q1 FY2027 delivered 10.6% margin, so they are tracking so far.

## 6. Valuation
**Base (55%):** FY2028 revenue near $978M on an 8% CAGR, 11% operating margin, $108M EBIT at 11x EV/EBIT plus net cash grown to roughly $200M, about $29 in two years, or **$24** discounted at 10%. **Bear (30%):** the order decline is a cycle turn rather than timing; revenue flat near $850M, margin back to 8% ($68M EBIT) at 9x with $180M cash, about **$15**. **Bull (15%):** the plan lands at the top end, 10% CAGR and 12% margin for $122M EBIT at 14x, about $41, or **$34** discounted. Probability-weighted **$22.8, roughly 16% above the $19.67 price**. Reverse DCF: at a 9.5% discount rate, the $806M enterprise value against $48.1M of normalised after-tax operating profit implies about **3.3% perpetual growth** (about 4.3% on trailing free cash flow). That is well below management's own 7-10% revenue target, so the price does not require the plan to work. It does fully pay for a business at what may be a peak in Live Events conversion.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q2 FY2027 results in early December 2026 are the whole question: do the "few substantial transactions" book, does backlog rebuild toward the $360M of a year ago, and does gross margin hold 30% once the undisclosed tariff refund does not repeat. Secondary: a permanent CFO; the Mexico plant reaching full production, which management said would happen by Q2 FY2027 with minimal margin effect (Q3 FY26 call); Alta Fox's standstill expiring after the 2027 annual meeting; a tuck-in acquisition, which management says it is evaluating with no imminent target.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
1. **Q2 FY2027 orders below $200M**, or quarter-end backlog below $300M for the first time in seven quarters: the slipped transactions did not exist.
2. **Gross margin down year over year for two consecutive quarters**: the pricing and efficiency gains were tariff refunds and mix, not structure.
3. **Operating margin below 8% in two consecutive quarters**, against a 10-12% FY2028 target and 10.6% just delivered.
4. **No permanent CFO named by the end of FY2027 (May 2027)**, or insider net open-market selling above $2M.
Risks outside the kill list: fixed-price contract cost overruns, single-source semiconductor supply from Taiwan and China, and tariff rates the 10-K says the administration intends to raise to as much as 15%.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
**WATCH, conviction 3.** Daktronics is a better business than the screen's 17.6x suggests: roughly 28% returns on ex-cash invested capital, $144M of net cash, no debt, a leadership position in stadium and highway LED, and an operating margin that went from 4.4% to 10.6% in six quarters under a new CEO and an activist-refreshed board. But nothing here is mispriced. There is no GAAP artifact, no forced seller and no spin, the stock sits 29% off its high with positive twelve-month momentum on $13M of daily volume, and at 12.6x EV/EBIT and 16.8x normalised after-tax profit you pay a fair price for a project business whose orders just fell 19.6% and whose backlog fell 13.6% year over year. Management says a few large deals slipped into Q2, which December checks. Two further reasons to wait: FY2027 capex was halved from $26.6M to $13.2M within ten weeks with no explanation, and the company has run eighteen months without a permanent CFO while the acting CFO also sits on the board. Probability-weighted value of about $22.8 is 16% above price, not enough for a cyclical, guidance-free, project-driven manufacturer with no identifiable edge case. If Q2 orders recover above $250M and gross margin holds 30% without tariff refunds, this becomes a real idea.
