## 1. What the business actually does
Royal Business Bank is a $4.3B community bank serving Asian-centric communities from 24 branches in California, New York, Illinois, New Jersey, Nevada and Hawaii (8-K 2026-07-20, corporate overview). The book is mostly residential: single-family residential mortgages are 50.8% of loans, commercial real estate 38.6% (multi-family $748.4M), construction and land development 4.4%, commercial and industrial 4.6% and SBA 1.5% (10-Q Q2 2026). Underwriting is conservative on paper: of $1.28B of CRE only $20.4M, or 1.6%, was originated above 75% LTV, office exposure is $39.4M or 1.2% of loans, rent-controlled New York multi-family is $49.0M, and the construction book carries a 58% weighted average LTV (10-Q Q2 2026, loan tables).

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is none, and that caps the verdict. The screen row has the stock 4.3% below its 52-week high after a 29.0% six-month gain and 45.8% twelve-month-less-one-month momentum, so the market has already re-rated the recovery. No spin-off, no index event, no forced seller. FJ Capital holds 8.85% on a passive 13G/A filed 2025-11-12 and BlackRock 6.24%; the Kao family holds 8.11% and all directors and officers 6.78% (DEF 14A 2026). The only structural argument is neglect: $2.2M of average daily volume makes this untradeable for most institutions. That is a reason the name stays under-followed, not evidence that today's price is wrong.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposits
The credit recovery is genuine but less complete than the headline. Non-performing assets fell from $81.0M, or 2.03% of assets, at end-2024 to $53.5M at end-2025 to $43.6M, or 1.02%, at 2026-06-30 (10-K 2025, Item 7; 8-K 2026-07-20). Special mention loans collapsed from $91.3M a year ago to $20.3M and substandard from $91.0M to $61.5M; net charge-offs were 0.01% of average loans in Q2 against 0.42% a year earlier, and the allowance is 1.32% of loans and 184% of non-performing loans.

Two qualifications matter. First, the striking Q2 fall in non-performing loans, down $20.8M to $23.8M, is mostly a reclassification: $19.4M of it is one non-performing construction loan transferred into OREO, which jumped from $4.3M to $19.8M, so non-performing assets fell only $5.3M (10-Q Q2 2026). Second, new problem formation continues: $14.1M was added to substandard in H1 and $7.3M to special mention, and 30-89 day delinquencies ticked up to $9.0M from $7.9M. The clean part of the story is the residential book, where only $1.7M of $1.68B is criticised.

Margin and funding are improving on their own merits. NIM reached 3.06% in Q2 against 2.92% a year earlier as the cost of deposits fell to 2.81% from 3.05%; non-interest-bearing deposits are 17.5% of the total, wholesale funding is down to 3.5% from 5.8%, and Q2 brought $94.4M of retail deposit growth against a $43.6M wholesale runoff (8-K 2026-07-20). The 9bp sequential NIM dip is explained by the notes repricing from 4.00% to 6.98% on 2026-04-01 and a $430K Q1 FHLB special dividend; the July redemption reverses part of the first.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Capital is the most interesting asset here. CET1 of 18.00% and Tier 1 leverage of 11.86% imply, on my own arithmetic from those disclosed ratios, roughly $120M of excess capital against a 9% leverage target and about $160M against a 12% CET1 target, or 27% to 36% of market value. AFS marks are not a hidden problem: the $20.9M pre-tax unrealised loss is 4.5% of tangible equity and the held-to-maturity book is only $4.2M. Against that, announced returns are modest: 1M shares authorised, about 6% of the company and roughly $25M, through 2028-06-30 (8-K 2026-06-15), $4.5M repurchased in Q2, share count down 4.0% year over year, and a $0.16 quarterly dividend on a 26.7% payout, with $25.3M returned in 2025 (DEF 14A 2026). Alignment is weak: the CEO beneficially owns 16,232 shares and the CFO 7,797, and across 42 Form 4 rows in twelve months there were zero open-market purchases against $103,551 of sales.

## 5. Management: said versus did
Johnny Lee became President in January 2025 and CEO on David Morris's retirement in May 2025 (DEF 14A 2026). On the 2025 scorecard the board paid 115% on EPS, 150% on loan growth and 125% on retail deposit growth, and zero on credit: NPLs to loans finished at 1.35% against a 0.84% target, the one metric that mattered (DEF 14A 2026, incentive tables). Lee's Q2 language, "resolving problem assets to drive long-term shareholder value", matches what the filings show. The turnover is worth watching rather than fearing: a director retired at the annual meeting (8-K 2026-04-06) and the COO's contract was not renewed effective 2026-09-30 under a stated "leadership transition plan" (8-K 2026-09-08).

## 6. Valuation
Base, 55%: normalised EPS of $2.26 with 4% loan growth and an 8.4% ROTCE deserves about 1.0x tangible book; TBVPS compounding 6% plus the dividend reaches roughly $28.9 by end-2027, so $28.9, about 9% above price. Bear, 25%: substandard additions accelerate, the $19.8M of OREO sells at a 25% discount (about $0.29 a share), provisions normalise to 40bp or $13M a year, ROTCE falls back toward 6% and the market pays 0.75x a $27 book, or $20. Bull, 20%: provisions stay near zero, OREO clears at book, the remaining $80M of notes is redeemed for another $5.6M pre-tax, NIM reaches 3.25% and ROTCE 11% on a shrinking share count, at 1.25x a $29 book, or $36. Probability-weighted $28.10, 5.8% above price. Reverse DCF: using P/TBV equal to (ROTCE minus g) over (cost of equity minus g), at 0.975x with a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth the current price implies a sustainable ROTCE of about 9.8%, above the 8.4% normalised figure and just below the 9.44% H1 print. The market is paying for the recovery already having happened.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
The October 2026 Q3 print is the first to carry a full quarter of the $40M redemption and the first test of whether provisions stay near zero. Redemption of the remaining $80M of 7.02% notes, disposal of the $19.8M OREO property, and execution against the 1M-share authorisation are the three things that would convert excess capital into per-share value.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. Substandard loans back above $75M, the 2025 year-end level, for two consecutive quarters.
2. Provision for credit losses above $4M in any single quarter, roughly 48bp annualised.
3. OREO still above $10M at 2027-06-30, or disposed of at a loss greater than $3M.
4. Fewer than 250,000 of the authorised 1M shares repurchased by 2027-06-30 while the stock trades below 1.0x tangible book.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 3. RBB is a conservatively underwritten, heavily over-capitalised community bank whose credit problems are genuinely receding, and the screen's 14.1x P/E is a stale-data artefact: on LTM earnings it trades at 10.9x and 0.975x tangible book, with tangible book per share up 8.4% in a year, a 4.0% lower share count, CET1 of 18.00% and roughly 27% to 36% of its market value in excess capital. What stops it being an idea is that nothing is mispricing it: the stock sits 4.3% below its 52-week high after a 29% six-month run, no seller is forced, and the H1 earnings jump is almost entirely a provision that went from $9.1M a year ago to negative $0.2M. Charge a normal 20bp provision and the bank earns about $2.26 a share on an 8.4% return on tangible equity, which is roughly what 1.0x book is worth, and the Q2 credit headline flatters besides, since $19.4M of the $20.8M fall in non-performing loans was one construction loan moving into OREO rather than a resolution. This is a fairly priced bank with real optionality in its capital stack; the observable test is whether management redeems the remaining $80M of 7.02% notes and uses the buyback below book, and whether provisions stay near zero once the OREO is sold.
