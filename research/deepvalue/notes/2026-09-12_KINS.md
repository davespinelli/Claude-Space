## 1. What the business does
Kingstone writes homeowners, dwelling fire, condo, renters and umbrella policies through KICO, a New York carrier operating since 1886. Personal lines were 94.7% of FY2025 direct written premiums, livery physical damage 5.2%, and 98.0% of premium came from New York, concentrated downstate and coastal (10-K Item 1). Distribution is entirely through about 700 appointed producers, never direct; the top 25 brokers supply 39% of gross premiums (10-K Items 1, 1A). It was New York's 11th largest homeowners writer in 2025 and began writing California excess and surplus lines in Q2 2026.

## 2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case
Honestly there is not a strong one, and that caps this at WATCH. The operating shock is real: on 2 August 2024 two large competitors announced exits from New York personal lines, and in April 2025 a third writing about $70M of premium entered a DFS-approved Withdrawal Plan handing selected downstate policyholders to KICO (10-K Item 1). But it is not hidden. The stock is up 28% in six months, Gregory Fortunoff filed a 13D for 5.9%, BlackRock owns 5.4% (DEF 14A), and the company publishes formal guidance through an IR firm. Nobody is a forced seller. What the screen misreads is the direction of earnings, not the market's view of them.

## 3. Underwriting quality and reserves
FY2025 net combined ratio 75.0% from 80.0%; underlying loss ratio 44.4% from 48.2% on better Select frequency (10-K Item 7). Q2 2026 was the most profitable quarter ever at 70.2% combined. But read the half: 90.2% combined against 82.3%, net income down 36% to $9.7M, because Q1 winter storms produced $26.7M gross and $14.0M net catastrophe losses, a 12.0-point H1 cat ratio (10-Q; Q2 2026 PR). The program worked, recovering $12.7M. Two concerns. Q2's underlying loss ratio rose 4.4 points to 43.1%, so the underlying book deteriorated while the headline improved. And the reserve triangle shows net cumulative deficiency in all nine accident-year cohorts from 2015 to 2023, including $18.6M on a $40.5M reserve for 2018; only 2024 is redundant, by $1.1M (10-K Item 1). Kingstone is simultaneously raising net retention from $530k to $825k and cutting cession to 5%, amplifying any reserve error, and guidance assumes zero development.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Almost no leverage: $2.47M of equipment financing, the 13.75% notes repaid February 2025. KICO's RBC ratio was 5.30 at YE2025, though three IRIS ratios sat outside the usual range (10-K Item 1). The binding constraint is upstream cash: the maximum dividend KICO could pay the holding company without DFS approval was $635,645 at 30 June 2026, and the holdco put $5.1M into the California entity expecting nothing back within a year (10-Q). It signed a $5M Citizens Bank revolver on 1 July 2026. Against that, management announced a buyback and raised the dividend 20% to $0.06 while $15.9M of ATM capacity sits unused, after selling 613,000 shares at an average $16.00 during 2025 (10-K Item 7). Buying at $19 having issued at $16 with holdco liquidity this thin deserves scepticism. Directors and officers own 4.3%, the CEO 1.6%. Director Thomas Newgarden bought 44,502 shares for $653k in May at $14.36-$15.50; director Yankus sold 13,500 at $18.00 in April; the rest is grants and withholding.

## 5. Management: said versus did
Meryl Golden has run this since October 2023 and delivered: combined ratio from 80.0% to 75.0%, debt repaid, dividend reinstated then raised, the catastrophe tower taken from $280M to $500M with wildfire cover added and risk-adjusted cost down over 15% (Q2 2026 PR). Guidance issued 5 March has been affirmed twice. Her bonus is 3% of pre-tax operating income excluding investment results, a clean underwriting metric. The uncomfortable item is governance: CBIZ CPAs issued an adverse attestation on internal control at 31 December 2025, a material weakness from missing SOC 1 Type 2 reports for the quoting platform and general ledger, and on 18 August 2026 the company dismissed CBIZ and engaged Deloitte (8-K 2026-08-24). Hiring Deloitte is the right response and there were no disagreements, but this is the third audit firm in eighteen months and the weakness is unremediated.

## 6. Valuation
Reverse DCF: at $19.25 the shares trade at 2.17x tangible book, which on a Gordon frame with an 11% cost of equity and 5% growth **implies a sustainable ROE near 18% in perpetuity**, below the 24-30% guided for 2026 but well above what a 98%-New-York coastal writer earns across a full catastrophe cycle.
- **Base (55%).** Premiums compound 12%, combined ratio settles at the guided 74-76% underlying plus a 7.5-point cat load, ROE 20%, book value per share reaches about $11.60 by end-2027, 1.8x book. **$21.**
- **Bear (25%).** A named storm or California wildfire hits the $5.5M first-event retention plus a harder renewal, or the 2015-2023 deficiency pattern reasserts; combined above 95%, ROE 8%, 1.2x book on $10. **$12.**
- **Bull (20%).** The $500M premium plan tracks, California and Connecticut scale cleanly, ROE holds at 25%, 2.4x book on $12.30. **$29.50.**
Probability-weighted **$20.45**, about 6% above price. Not enough margin for a catastrophe-exposed monoline.

## 7. Catalysts
Q3 2026 results in late October, the first full test of the $500M tower through peak hurricane season. The FY2026 10-K in March 2027, showing whether Deloitte clears the material weakness and how the 2025 and 2026 accident years develop. The 1 July 2027 catastrophe renewal. Whether the buyback is executed or the ATM used instead.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. Net combined ratio above 90% in two consecutive quarters containing no PCS-designated named storm.
2. Adverse prior-year reserve development above 2 points of net earned premium in any quarter, against guidance of zero.
3. The FY2026 10-K again reports a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting.
4. California E&S direct written premium passing 10% of total direct premiums before two full wildfire seasons, or KICO's Demotech A rating placed under review or withdrawn (there is no A.M. Best rating; it was withdrawn at KICO's request on 6 July 2023).

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Kingstone is a genuinely good small insurer having a genuinely good run: two large competitors abandoned New York personal lines, Kingstone took the business, and direct premiums are growing 19% with policies in force up 10%. But the screen's 6.8x P/E stacks two artefacts, a 1.2-point catastrophe load against management's own 7.1% six-year average, and a quota share cession that fell from 27% to 5% and mechanically inflated revenue. Management itself guides 2026 EPS of $2.20 to $2.90 against roughly $2.85 earned in 2025, flat to down, and at 2.17x tangible book the market already pays for an 18% through-cycle ROE. Add nine straight deficient accident years from 2015 to 2023 just as the company keeps far more risk net, an unremediated adverse internal-control opinion with a third auditor in eighteen months, and a holding company that can draw only $636k from its subsidiary without regulatory approval while announcing a buyback. The business deserves following; the price does not yet pay you for the catastrophe tail.
