## 1. What the business actually does
A $7.53B Grand Rapids bank holding company with one branch, NYSE-listed in February 2025 and still an emerging growth company (10-K Item 1). Two segments. The Mortgage Purchase Program buys mortgages from independent mortgage bankers nationwide under short collateralised facilities, typically held under 30 days until the originator sells into the agencies; a warehouse lender in substance, it produced 30.4% of 2025 revenue and $44.4M of segment net income, up 71.3% (10-K Items 1A and 7). Retail Banking is a nationwide digital mortgage originator with 122 loan officers in 25 states, a CD-led deposit platform and $5.50B of servicing, and earned $39.0M, up 33.4%.

## 2. Why it is mispriced, the edge case
The edge is a classification and a calendar. By SIC code NPB is a Michigan state commercial bank, so every screen sorting on that code files it beside $400M community lenders; it is a national mortgage warehouse whose earnings power has little to do with Michigan. The calendar compounds it: the February 2025 IPO and the December 2025 Series A redemption break every mechanical year-over-year comparison of book value and share count, which is why this desk's own row reported 0.6% book growth for a bank that grew tangible book 15.5%. Nineteen months of reporting is short of the three years most screens require, and the float is tight: Williams holds 9.5%, directors and officers 18.3%, two legacy family trusts 14.9% (DEF 14A). Who is selling is unimpressive: director David Hooker trimmed 14,999 shares for $277K out of an 808,000 share position. Against that, Chairman and CEO Chuck Williams bought 163,312 shares for $2.75M on 25 and 26 August and 1 September at $16.74 to $16.95 (Form 4, 2026-09-02).

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
This is where the discount is earned. The allowance for credit losses is $9.436M, 0.15% of loans held for investment, down from 0.23% a year ago, and covers non-accrual loans only 11.80%, or 17.19% excluding loans guaranteed by the US government; non-performing loans are 1.23% of gross loans, 0.84% excluding guaranteed (8-K 2026-07-21). Management's defence is mix and it is real: growth is all MPP, short-dated agency-eligible paper, plus AIO first-lien home equity lines, while the higher-loss residential, construction and consumer book ran off $216.9M in a year and net charge-offs were $528K, 0.03% annualised. But a 0.15% reserve leaves no room: a 1% loss on the $6.48B held-for-investment book costs $65M pre-tax, about $1.40 a share of tangible book. The 28 August hire of a dedicated Chief Operating and Chief Credit Officer, taking those duties off the President, reads as management conceding credit oversight needed its own seat (8-K 2026-08-28). Margin has compressed three quarters running, 2.44% to 2.42% to 2.33%, on lower MPP yields, tighter warehouse spreads and falling SOFR (10-Q). Funding is the structural weakness: brokered deposits were $2.64B, 54.1% of deposits at year end (10-K Item 1A). There is no core deposit franchise here.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Capital is thin and deliberately worked: tangible common equity is 7.78% of tangible assets while assets grew 17.1%. The Series A preferred was refinanced in December 2025 with $70M of 7.50% subordinated notes due 2035, and $20M more followed in March 2026, taking subordinated debentures to $112.0M against $24.2M a year ago (10-K Item 7; 8-K 2026-03-13). That is holding-company leverage substituting for equity, a $10M annual drag. Share count is flat, no buyback, dividend 4% of earnings, so all capital funds MPP. Compensation is the flag: Williams' employment agreement pays him cash incentive equal to 10% of consolidated after-tax income every quarter, forfeited only if exam ratings slip or debt interest and preferred dividends go unpaid; 2025 total compensation was $7.95M against $71.6M of earnings to common (DEF 14A). That permanent toll is why to size this smaller than the multiple suggests.

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
Williams said on 21 July that performance "reflects the continued success in our Mortgage Purchase Program business, with 36% growth in portfolio balances and 42% growth in total loans funded over the prior year," and both reconcile to the supplement. What he did not lead with is that MPP balances grew only 8% annualised in the quarter once $489.0M of participations is counted, and that the 10-K warns third-party participants "are generally not obligated to maintain or renew their participation interests" (Item 1A). The 2022 to 2024 repositioning they describe is visible in the numbers: 54.76% efficiency while assets grew 17%, earnings to common up 51.9%. The company gives no numeric guidance, so there is nothing else to mark them against.

## 6. Valuation
Base, 50%: margin stabilises near 2.25%, MPP compounds low teens against the capital constraint, ROTCE settles at 13%, tangible book compounds 12.5% to $21.40 by mid-2028 at 1.25x, giving $26.80. Bear, 30%: volumes stay at the H1 2026 trough, spreads keep tightening, participations are withdrawn and a 1% loss emerges against the 0.15% reserve; ROTCE falls to 8% at 0.85x tangible book of $18.50, giving $15.70. Bull, 20%: rates fall, gain on sale recovers, ROTCE holds 17%, 1.6x on $22.50 gives $36.00. Probability-weighted $25.20 against $17.22, 46% upside. Reverse DCF: at an 11% cost of equity and 3% growth, 1.02x tangible book implies a sustainable ROTCE of 11.1% against the 15.19% just delivered, so the price assumes a quarter of current profitability is cyclical.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results in late October are the first test of whether margin has found a floor near 2.30%. A lower funds rate should relieve the brokered CD book faster than it hurts MPP yields, since deposits reprice on maturity while MPP is SOFR-linked and has already repriced. Beyond that: sell-side initiation, index inclusion, further buying by Williams.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
The thesis is wrong if this is a levered warehouse lender pretending to be a bank. Kill criteria, all in the quarterly release:
1. Allowance for credit losses to non-accrual loans excluding guaranteed falls below 15%, or annualised net charge-offs exceed 0.15% of average loans, in any two consecutive quarters.
2. Net interest margin below 2.20% for two consecutive quarters.
3. Wholesale funding ratio above 70%, or MPP balances participated to third parties fall below $250M in a quarter while MPP balances shrink.
4. Williams sells any shares in the open market, or his beneficial ownership falls below 8%.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
IDEA, conviction 3. Northpointe is a national mortgage warehouse lender filed under a Michigan state bank code, earning 15.2% on tangible common equity at 54.5% efficiency and trading at 7.1x annualised H1 2026 earnings and 1.02x tangible book, a price implying sustainable profitability of 11.1% rather than the 15.2% it is delivering. The screen missed it because the February 2025 IPO and the December 2025 preferred redemption made tangible book growth look like 0.6% when it was 15.5%. What you underwrite is funding that is 63% wholesale and 54% brokered deposits, a 0.15% allowance against 1.23% non-performing loans, and a chief executive contractually entitled to 10% of net income every quarter. What you are paid is a 46% probability-weighted gap to fair value, and the fact that the same chief executive, already holding 9.5%, bought $2.75M of stock at $16.74 to $16.95 in the three weeks before this price. Conviction is 3, not higher, because margin has compressed three straight quarters and the reserve leaves nothing for a credit surprise.
