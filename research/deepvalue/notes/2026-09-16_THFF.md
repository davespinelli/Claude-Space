## 1. What the business actually does
First Financial Bank, N.A. is the fifth oldest national bank in the United States, founded 1834 in Terre Haute, Indiana. It runs $6.18B of assets, 75 full-service banking centers, 6 limited-service centers, 9 loan production offices across Indiana, Illinois, Kentucky, Tennessee and Georgia, and $2.2B of trust assets (8-K 2026-09-04). Loans are 71.2% commercial, 20.7% consumer, 8.0% residential. Commercial exposure is 43.7% Indiana and 30.3% Tennessee, the latter entirely from the 2024 SimplyBank deal; Middle Tennessee is now the largest pipeline in the bank at $613M. The consumer book is 66% indirect auto ($610M, 72.5% above 700 FICO, 7.78% yield). Deposits are cheap and sticky: 20.7% non-interest-bearing, 1.46% cost, 19% uninsured (10-K, Item 7).

## 2. Why it might be mispriced: the edge case
Honestly, there is not much of one, which caps this at WATCH. The setup looks like a desk name: 11.9M shares, no earnings call, family management. But the market has already done the work. The stock is up 29% in six months, up 46% on twelve-minus-one momentum, and sits 5.7% off its 52-week high. At 1.66x tangible book this is not a forgotten bank. The nearest thing to an edge cuts against the buyer: screens still read a 67.5% earnings jump and a 14.6% book gain that are both partly optical. The one genuinely unpriced item is Hickory Point, announced three weeks ago into a small float with no call to explain it.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposits
Credit is the thing to watch. Non-performing loans went from $9.8M (0.25% of loans) at June 2025 to $27.1M (0.61%) at June 2026, up 176% (10-Q, Non-performing Loans). Coverage fell from 481% to 188%. Within that, $14.6M sits in one commercial "non-farm, non-residential" bucket and $14.2M of it carries no allowance, meaning management judges it collateral dependent and fully secured (10-Q, non-performing loans by class). Net charge-offs ran 24bp annualised in Q2 against a reserve that drifted from 1.21% to 1.14%; provision of $1.3M did not cover $2.7M of charge-offs. Still good credit in absolute terms, but the direction is wrong and reserve is being released into earnings.

Margin is strong and cyclical: 3.20% in 2021, 3.71% in 2024, 4.29% in 2025, 4.33% in Q2 2026. My own arithmetic on disclosed figures: investor CRE and multifamily of $1.886B against roughly $662M of Tier 1 capital is about 285% of Tier 1, or 264% including the allowance. Under the 300% supervisory screen, but not by much, and Hickory Point's book is 71.7% CRE.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Capital allocation is the best part of the story. CedarStone ($369M assets, $292M loans, $313M deposits) cost $25.0M cash and closed at a small bargain purchase gain (10-Q, note 12). SimplyBank cost $73.4M for $683M of assets. Hickory Point is $111.3M for $717M of assets at 135% of tangible book and 13.0x LTM earnings, a pay-to-trade ratio of 80%, with 34% cost saves, about 7% fully phased 2028 accretion, 3.2% TBV dilution and a 2.3-year earnback (8-K 2026-08-27, Ex-99.2). It also repairs the funding gap: Hickory Point runs a 70% loan-to-deposit ratio at a 1.27% deposit cost. No shares repurchased in twelve months despite 518,860 authorised; dividend $0.56 a quarter, 31% payout. Insiders and directors own 5.00%; BlackRock 8.60%, Vanguard 6.35%, Dimensional 5.91% (DEF 14A). Form 4 activity is trivial and net selling by $126k, almost all one lending officer's 2,209-share sale at $64.60.

## 6. Valuation
Base (50%): NIM eases to about 4.15%, charge-offs normalise to 30bp, Hickory Point closes in Q4 and adds about 6% in 2027; 2027 EPS near $8.35 on tangible book near $52. At 11x, $92. Bear (25%): the $14.6M CRE nonaccrual and its cohort migrate, charge-offs run 45bp, wholesale funding reprices as core deposits keep leaking, NIM back to 3.9%, EPS $6.25 at 9x, $56. Bull (25%): margin holds above 4.25%, cost saves land at 34%, deposits stabilise, 2028 EPS near $9.00 at 12x, $108. Probability-weighted $87.50 against $78.10, about 12% plus a 2.9% dividend. Reverse DCF: at 1.66x tangible book, a 10% cost of equity and 5% long-run growth, the price implies a sustainable ROTCE of roughly 13.3%, well below the 16.5% earned over the last twelve months. The market is already assuming margin and credit normalise, which is a reasonable assumption rather than an obviously wrong one. That is why this is not an IDEA.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Hickory Point shareholder vote and regulatory approval, targeted Q4 2026; the Form S-4 will show Hickory Point's standalone financials for the first time. Q3 results in late October will show whether NPLs keep falling and core deposits stabilise. Resuming the buyback (518,860 shares, 4.4% of the float) would signal surplus capital.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
1. Non-performing loans above 0.80% of total loans, or the $14.2M unreserved commercial real estate nonaccrual taking a specific reserve or charge-off above $4M, in any quarter.
2. Net interest margin below 4.00% for two consecutive quarters.
3. Period-end deposits excluding acquisitions down again year over year with loans/deposits above 95%.
4. Hickory Point terminated, repriced, or closing pushed past Q1 2027.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 3. First Financial is a genuinely good small bank: 4.33% margin, 1.48% return on assets, 16.5% return on tangible equity, 19% uninsured deposits, and a serial acquirer that has bought four banks since 2019 at prices like $25M for CedarStone's $369M of assets and 135% of book for Hickory Point. None of this is secret. The stock is up 29% in six months, sits 5.7% below its high, and at 1.66x tangible book and 10.7x normalised earnings the price already implies a fade to roughly 13.3% ROTCE. Two things the screen credits are partly optical: the 67.5% earnings growth is measured off a 2024 depressed by SimplyBank's day-2 provision and one large charge-off, and $45.6M of last year's book gain was the securities mark reversing rather than profit. Two things it missed run the other way: Hickory Point adds about 7% to 2028 earnings and repairs a funding gap that pushed loans/deposits from 84% to 92% while core deposits shrank $143M. Against that, non-performing loans nearly tripled to 0.61% with $14.2M of commercial real estate carrying no reserve at all, and my arithmetic puts investor CRE near 285% of Tier 1 capital just as they buy a bank that is 72% CRE. A probability-weighted $87.50 against $78.10 is a real 12% edge, but not enough compensation for a deteriorating credit book and a margin at a cycle high. Revisit after Q3 and the S-4.
