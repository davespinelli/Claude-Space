## 1. What the business actually does
Easterly buys, develops and manages Class A buildings leased to US government agencies, about 90% of revenue directly or through the GSA (10-K, Item 1). At June 30, 2026: 106 operating properties, 10.7M leased square feet, 98% leased, 9.2-year weighted average remaining lease term, 17.1-year average building age (8-K 2026-08-03). GSA rent is paid from the Federal Buildings Fund rather than direct appropriations, and delegated federal leases are guaranteed by that fund (10-K, Item 1). The VA, FBI and DEA are 42.0% of leased square feet and 47.3% of annualised lease income; California is 17.1% of lease income (10-K, Item 1A). On build-to-suits the government reimburses tenant improvements in lump sums, which sit in deferred revenue ($226.0M at Q2) and amortise into rental income, so reported maintenance capex is tiny: $7.6M in H1 on 10.7M square feet.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is no longer one, which caps this at WATCH. The federal-downsizing fear that forced a 1-for-2.5 reverse split in April 2025 (10-K, Item 7) and a dividend cut of roughly a third (dividends paid fell from $115.9M in 2024 to $94.6M in 2025 on a rising share count, per XBRL in universe_v2.csv, against the flat $0.45 rate since) has passed. The screen row shows the stock up 10.9% over six months and 5.8% below its 52-week high. What is left is a durable discount, not a dislocation: the market capitalises the portfolio near 9.4% against the 6-7% private-market history for credit-backed, long-lease federal buildings. BlackRock, Vanguard and State Street hold 30.9% (DEF 14A, ownership as of 2026-01-31), so a third of the register is indifferent, but indifference is not a catalyst.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
Property-level margin is sound: Q2 revenue $92.4M less property operating $20.0M and real estate taxes $9.1M leaves $63.3M, 68.5%. Below that line it is worse. Corporate G&A rose 31.5% in the quarter to $9.0M and 34% in H1 to $17.4M, "primarily due to an increase in employee costs and non-cash compensation" (10-Q, Item 2), against 12.9% H1 revenue growth; non-cash compensation alone went $2.8M to $4.2M. Interest expense rose $1.5M on "higher weighted average borrowings" (10-Q). That is why net income fell as revenue grew. The Core FFO versus CAD gap is the number to watch: maintenance capex nearly doubled in H1, and $7.5M of H1 Core FFO was amortisation of deferred revenue collected in earlier periods. Q2 CAD covered the roughly $21.7M of dividends and unit distributions 1.19x, against 1.38x a year earlier.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
$1.7B of debt: $1.0B unsecured notes, $500M term loans, $43.1M revolver, $149.3M mortgages (8-K 2026-08-03). The June 2026 $200M five-year term loan prices at SOFR plus 1.30% at current leverage (8-K 2026-06-30), about 4.9% on quarter-end SOFR of 3.62%, so the 2027-28 refinancing wall is a headwind, not a cliff; all covenants were met at June 30 (10-Q). Capital allocation is the weak point. The company issued 796,943 shares at $23.86 in Q2 for $18.8M net and refreshed its ATM on August 4 with $215.0M remaining and new contingent-forward mechanics (8-K 2026-08-05), while buying a three-building Glen Allen, Virginia campus for $44.6M leased to the Commonwealth with expiries from 2027 (10-Q, Item 2) and lending $7.0M of mezzanine at 12% (8-K 2026-04-27). Issuing equity at a 9%-plus implied cap rate to buy shorter-lease state-credit assets is at best neutral per share. Alignment is mixed: insiders hold 5.9% but 5.0% of that is founder Michael Ibe; CEO Crate holds 268,591 shares of which 200,000 are pledged against a credit line (DEF 14A); no open-market insider buys or sales in twelve months (Form 4 summary); and holders approved tripling the equity plan from 1.44M to 4.315M shares, about 9% of the company (8-K 2026-04-24). An entity Crate controls is paid about $537k in 2026 for services (DEF 14A, related parties).

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
Crate's Q1 line was "stable operating performance and the successful execution of our first mezzanine investment highlight our strategic approach to capital allocation" (8-K 2026-04-27); in Q2, "increased confidence in our earnings outlook" while raising guidance (8-K 2026-08-03). Both true, both narrow. Guidance went from $3.06-$3.12 in April to $3.07-$3.13 in August, a one-cent raise at each end. Neither release explains why CAD fell as Core FFO rose.

## 6. Valuation
Using NOI-equivalent of $269.4M (annualised Q2 EBITDA plus annualised G&A) and $1,712.7M of net debt across 48.16M fully diluted shares. Base: the cap rate compresses to 8.5% as the downsizing narrative fades and NOI holds, giving $1,456M of equity, $30 a share, plus $1.80 of dividends. Bear: two of the VA/FBI/DEA renewals fail and the 4.3% of lease income in exercisable soft term walks, NOI falls 8%, the cap rate stays 9.5%, equity is $896M or $18.60, and the dividend is cut again. Bull: federal leasing normalises to a 7.5% cap, equity $1,879M or $39. Weighted 55/25/20 gives $29, about 23% above the price, real but thin against 7.3x leverage. Reverse DCF: at $23.72 the price implies a 13.1% Core FFO yield and a 10.2% CAD yield, which at a 4.6% average cost of debt and 58% net-debt-to-enterprise-value means the market is capitalising a 98%-leased, 9.2-year-WALT federal portfolio at a 9.4% nominal cap rate, that is, zero real rent growth in perpetuity plus an eventual re-leasing loss.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 results in late October restate CAD coverage and guidance. The 2027 expiry cohort, 5.3% of lease income, is negotiated over the next twelve months and is the first clean read on post-downsizing renewal spreads. Fort Myers completes in Q4 2026 and Flagstaff in Q1 2027, converting $99.3M of construction cost into rent. A dividend increase, absent since the cut, would be the clearest signal CAD is durable. Eighteen to twenty-four months.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
1. CAD per fully diluted share below the $0.45 quarterly dividend in two consecutive quarters (it was $0.536 in Q2 2026).
2. Adjusted net debt to annualised quarterly EBITDA above 7.5x at any quarter end (7.1x at Q2 2026).
3. Portfolio leased percentage below 95%, or soft-term terminations removing more than 2% of annualised lease income across any two quarters (4.3% is currently exercisable; the portfolio is 98% leased).
4. More than $50M of ATM equity issued below $24 a share across any two quarters while acquisitions continue, confirming value-destructive funding.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Easterly owns genuinely good real estate, 10.7M square feet that is 98% leased to federal agencies on 9.2-year average terms backed by the Federal Buildings Fund, and at $23.72 the market capitalises it near a 9.4% cap rate, a wide discount to what credit-backed government buildings have historically fetched. It is not an IDEA because the discount is no longer a dislocation you can name: the 2025 panic has passed, the stock sits 5.8% off its 52-week high, and what remains is a REIT at 7.3x net debt to EBITDA whose cash available for distribution fell 4% in the first half while Core FFO rose, whose revenue growth is entirely acquired, whose G&A grew 34% against 13% revenue growth, and which funds growth by issuing stock at a cap rate above what it pays for assets. If two or three quarters show CAD coverage rebuilding and the 2027 renewal cohort clearing at flat or better rents, this becomes an IDEA at a similar price. Until then the leverage does the talking.
