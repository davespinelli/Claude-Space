## 1. What the business does
MVB Financial is the West Virginia holding company for MVB Bank: $3.55B of assets, seven branches (six in West Virginia, one in Virginia), 403 employees (10-K 2026, Item 1). Two businesses sit on one balance sheet. The community bank lends $2.48B, 72.9% of it commercial and non-residential real estate, with healthcare borrowers (physicians, nursing homes, pharmacies) at 25.0% of the book (10-Q Q2 2026). The Fintech bank supplies deposits and fees to payments, banking-as-a-service and online gaming clients nationwide: $1.25B of on-balance-sheet Fintech deposits at 30 June 2026, split $598.0M payments, $342.4M banking-as-a-service, $278.8M gaming and $28.8M digital asset, plus $627.9M held off balance sheet at program banks. MVB also holds equity method stakes in two mortgage originators (Intercoastal Mortgage, Warp Speed) and eleven private Fintech equity positions carried at $41.3M at end-2025, the top four of which are 91.1% of that (10-K 2026, Item 7).

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is none, and the honest reading is that the screen, not the market, is wrong. The stock is up 25.4% in six months and sits 3.3% below its 52-week high, with Wellington at 13.97% and BlackRock at 6.44% on the register (DEF 14A 2026). Nothing is being forced out. What looks like a cheap 10x bank is one whose last twelve months of earnings are roughly two-thirds one-time items and whose recurring profitability is mid-single-digit at best. If anything is mispriced it runs the other way: at 1.15x tangible book the market is already paying for a Fintech deposit franchise the income statement has not yet monetised.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Credit is adequate and improving at the margin but not clean. Non-performing loans were $29.2M, 1.2% of loans, at 30 June 2026 against $34.7M and 1.4% a quarter earlier, the fall driven by full repayment of the largest non-performing credit; criticized loans are 3.1% and classified 1.9% (8-K 2026-07-28). Net charge-offs ran 0.23% annualised in Q2 and 0.26% in FY2025 against 0.20% in 2024 (10-K 2026, Item 7). The allowance was rebuilt hard, from 0.93% of loans at December 2025 to 1.14% at June 2026, including a $3.2M rise in specific reserves on individually analysed loans and "light degradation in the adjusted allocation rates due to model updates" (10-Q Q2 2026); coverage of non-performers is 96.5%, up from 71%. Core tax-equivalent NIM excluding the loan payoff was 3.87% in Q2, 14bp of expansion, helped by repaying $40.0M of subordinated debt with a $20.0M revolver in Q1 for about $1.8M of annual savings (8-K 2026-04-29). The deposit mix is the franchise and the risk in one line: 34.4% non-interest-bearing, but $249.8M of the $278.8M of gaming deposits sits with three clients, and the 10-K names gaming deposit concentration as a specific risk factor (Item 1A).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Capital is adequate, not abundant: CBLR 10.3% at June against 11.4% a year earlier, diluted by 18% year-on-year loan growth, with the regulatory floor moving from 9% to 8% on 1 July 2026 (10-Q Q2 2026). Holding company debt is small: $34.1M of subordinated notes and $20.0M drawn on the revolver. Capital return is thin and self-cancelling: 48,432 shares bought back for $1.2M at $25.56 in Q2, while $2.8M of option exercises took shares outstanding from 12.716M at December 2025 to 12.947M at June, up 1.8% in six months. The $0.17 quarterly dividend, reaffirmed 19 August 2026, costs about $8.8M a year, 25% of GAAP earnings but nearly 60% of normalised. No open-market insider purchases or sales in twelve months (Form 4 summary). CEO Larry Mazza owns 6.79%, of which 339,566 shares are pledged as security for a loan funding his option purchases (DEF 14A 2026).

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
Management frames the gains as strategy: the CEO calls Victor and the Fintech mark proof of "our ability to both incubate and invest in innovative Fintech businesses" supporting "the long-term growth of the Company's earnings power" (8-K 2026-07-28). The pattern deserves the sceptical reading. Chartwell sold in February 2023 for an $11.8M gain; a sale-leaseback produced $11.7M in 2024; Victor $34.2M in 2025; a mark on a private holding $10.0M in 2026. Underneath, the core has gone sideways: net interest income $123.3M in 2023, $109.2M in 2024, $107.4M in 2025, with non-interest expense $122.2M then $122.1M. The proxy shows the incentive: on top of $850,000 of salary and a $1.38M annual incentive, the CEO received a $2.1M "special one-time (cash) consulting payment, paid directly to Lazza, LLC" tied to the Victor sale, taking 2025 total compensation to $5.06M against $26.9M of reported and about $7.7M of normalised net income (DEF 14A 2026). A similar transaction bonus was paid on Chartwell in 2023. Selling assets is well paid here; running the bank at an acceptable return is not yet demonstrated.

## 6. Valuation
Stripping securities and divestiture gains, the efficiency ratio was 79.5% in Q2 2026 and 78.1% in Q1, against the 59.5% reported, and non-interest expense runs 3.5% of average assets. A bank with a 3.9% margin and a 79% cost base earns very little: roughly $15M after tax on $343M of tangible common equity, 4.4% ROTCE.
Reverse DCF: at $30.59 against $26.52 of tangible book, and using a 10% cost of equity with 3% terminal growth, the price implies a sustainable ROTCE of about 11%. Normalised delivery is 4% to 5%.
Bear (25%): a gaming or banking-as-a-service client leaves, Fed cuts compress the margin toward 3.65%, core ROTCE stays near 4%, multiple resets to 0.75x tangible book, about $20.
Base (50%): fee income compounds near 20%, expenses grow 4%, core ROTCE reaches 7% to 8% by 2028 on tangible book compounding 8% a year to roughly $31; at 0.95x that is $29 to $30, flat, plus the dividend.
Bull (25%): payments scales, efficiency falls to 65%, core ROTCE reaches 12% and the stock earns 1.4x a $33 book, about $46.
Probability weighted: about $31.50 against $30.59. No margin of safety in either direction.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 2026 results in late October should be the first quarter in five without a headline gain, and the cleanest read on the core run rate. Beyond that: further monetisation of the eleven-company Fintech equity portfolio (lumpy, non-cash until sold), the lower 8% CBLR freeing growth capacity from 1 July 2026, and the standing possibility that a deposit franchise this attractive is worth more to an acquirer than to public shareholders.

## 8. Risks and what would prove the thesis wrong (pre-registered kill criteria)
The PASS flips to WATCH or IDEA if any of the first two appear; the last two would confirm it.
1. Core ROTCE, excluding investment portfolio and divestiture gains, above 9% for two consecutive quarters.
2. Core efficiency ratio, on the same exclusions, below 68% for two consecutive quarters.
3. Total Fintech deposits below $1.0B, or disclosed loss of one of the three largest gaming clients ($249.8M of the $278.8M gaming book).
4. Non-performing loans above 1.75% of total loans, or allowance coverage of non-performers back below 75%.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary a smart friend could repeat
PASS, conviction 4. MVB screens at 14.7x earnings with 8.1% return on tangible equity and 33% earnings growth, and all three numbers are one transaction: FY2025 net income of $26.9M contains a $34.2M pre-tax gain on selling the Victor payments platform to Jack Henry, and the 2024 base it grew against contains an $11.7M sale-leaseback gain. Normalised, the bank earned about $7.7M in 2025, a 2.4% return on tangible equity, and runs at roughly $15M a year now, about 4.4%, because a 3.9% margin is consumed by a cost base that takes 79 cents of every core revenue dollar and 3.5% of assets a year. The franchise underneath is genuinely interesting: $1.25B of Fintech deposits, 34% of the book non-interest-bearing, payment card fees up 29% year over year, loans up 18% and five straight quarters of loan growth. But you pay 1.15x tangible book for it, which implies an 11% sustainable return on tangible equity against 4% to 5% delivered; the stock is 3% off its 52-week high after a 25% six-month run; and a large one-off gain in each of the last four years, one of them paid for in part with a $2.1M transaction bonus to the CEO, is the opposite of the recurring compounding the screen thought it had found. Come back if two consecutive quarters show core return on tangible equity above 9% with no gain in them.
