Only partly new business: in H1 2026 direct earned premium rose 8.0% while ceded earned premium fell 23.1%, because the core excess-of-loss treaty incepting 2026-01-01 raised the multi-line retention from $3.0M to $4.0M and eliminated the annual aggregate deductible (10-K, Item 7, Reinsurance). Management agrees: Q1 growth came "driven by growth in the company's core commercial business and lower ceded reinsurance premiums" (8-K 2026-05-05). Q2 core commercial renewal premium rose 4.6%, of which 2.9 points was rate.
- **Normalised after-tax profit.** TTM (Jul 2025 to Jun 2026) GAAP pre-tax $176.8M, GAAP net income $141.0M. Adjustments: add back $2.7M of net investment losses GAAP includes (adjusted operating income reconciliations, 10-K and 10-Q); charge a normalised catastrophe load of 5.0% of net earned premium against the 2.23% actually incurred ($30.5M on $1,366.6M), a $37.8M pre-tax charge; remove $0.1M of favourable non-catastrophe prior year development. Normalised pre-tax $141.9M, taxed at the desk's 25%, gives **$106.4M normalised after-tax against $141.0M GAAP**. The 5.0% load is charitable: the 10-K calls the 2025 (3.2 points), 2024 (5.4) and 2023 (6.2) loads each below the 10-year average, so the through-cycle load is above 6.2.
- **EV / normalised after-tax profit: 13.3x.** EV = $1,407.6M market cap plus $146.4M long-term debt ($50M private placement of Dec 2020, $70M senior unsecured of May 2024, $30M of Jul 2025; 10-Q Note 7) less $139.4M cash. Secondary lens for an insurer.

- **Growth sustainable?** Organic and cash-backed on the direct book, but about a third of reported net premium growth is retained risk rather than new business, which raises the volatility of everything below the premium line. No guidance given.
- **What the screen got wrong.** The 11.9x P/E is a catastrophe-light P/E: the trailing year carried a 2.23-point cat load, under half the 2023-2025 average, and normalising costs a quarter of earnings. ## 1. What the business does

Founded 1946 in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, UFG writes commercial property and casualty insurance through roughly 850 independent agencies and 160 surety agencies, licensed in all 50 states (10-K, Item 1): business packages for small and middle-market accounts in construction, services, retail, financial and manufacturing, covering other liability, fire and allied lines, commercial auto, workers' compensation and surety. It adds MGA-delegated programmes, assumed treaty reinsurance, and eleven Lloyd's syndicates through its McIntyre Cedar corporate member. Direct personal lines were exited in 2020. One segment; five states are 48.5% of premium. A.M. Best rates the group A-, the holdco bbb-, both since 2023.

## 2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case

There is no live edge case, which caps the verdict at WATCH. There was one. In 2023 UFG posted a 109.3% combined ratio and a $29.7M net loss on $67.8M of adverse development, concentrated in excess and surplus casualty and umbrella from accident years 2016 to 2019 (10-K, Item 7). A neglected sub-$1B Iowa insurer with a broken reserve position and a new CEO is this desk's setup exactly. The market has now paid for the fix: combined ratio 109.3% to 99.2% to 94.8%, the stock up 76.7% over the twelve months to a month ago and 2.2% below its 52-week high (universe_v2.csv). Nobody is a forced seller. The only insider trade of size runs the other way: director Scott Carlton sold 9,777 shares at $55.52 on 2026-09-03 (Form 4, 2026-09-08), against one 4,500-share purchase by director George Milligan at $45.23 (Form 4, 2026-06-09).

## 3. Combined ratio and reserve development

The operating improvement is real. The underlying combined ratio, stripping catastrophes and prior-year development, went 97.1% (2023), 93.8% (2024), 92.0% (2025), 91.9% in Q1 2026 and 92.6% in Q2. The Q2 underlying loss ratio of 57.2% reflects "sustained lower frequency and earned rate achievement on core commercial lines" (8-K 2026-08-03). Expenses are the weaker half: 34.9%, 35.9%, 35.7%, then 35.4% in Q2, up 0.5 points year over year. Net investment income rose 33% to $28.9M in Q2 on a $2.5B portfolio yielding 4.57%, duration 4.2 years.

Reserves are what to watch. Commercial other liability reserves are $972.3M across 3,528 claims, and that line produced adverse development in every year the filing describes, offset in 2024 and 2025 by favourable commercial auto and property development (10-K, Item 7). Development is exactly neutral in Q1 and Q2 2026 after $5.2M favourable in FY2025. Management's own sensitivity puts a 5% miss on other liability at $48.6M pre-tax, about $1.42 a share after tax. Governance note: the company terminated external appointed actuary Regnier Consulting for the 2024 reporting period and moved the role in-house to its VP of Actuarial Reserving, retaining a third party for an assessment it explicitly does not rely on to set reserves (10-K, Item 7). Not improper, but the opinion on a long-tail casualty book became internal just as development swung favourable.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Conservative. $150M face of holdco debt against $139.4M cash and $977.3M equity, equity 24.3% of $4.01B assets, no FHLB advances drawn. Capital return is dividend-only at $0.20 a quarter, about 1.5%; no buyback, and the share count rose 0.84% to 25,705,439. Shareholders approved raising the non-employee director stock plan pool from 450,000 to 865,114 shares (8-K 2026-05-26), 1.6% of potential dilution. Directors and officers own 6.06% and the McIntyre family about 11.4% (DEF 14A).

## 5. Management: said versus did

The 2025 incentive scorecard is the most useful page in the proxy. Against targets UFG beat on profitability and missed on growth: core earnings $116M against $102M, adjusted ROE 10.8% against 9.2%, net adjusted loss ratio 61.6% against 63.4%, but written premium $1,488M against $1,552M and expense ratio 35.6% against 35.2% (DEF 14A). Choosing margin over volume is the right trade and they made it. Note what the target implies: the committee set target adjusted ROE at 9.2% and maximum at 12.2%, so management's own definition of good is a low-double-digit return. CEO Kevin Leidwinger's Q2 claim of the "strongest year-to-date financial performance in two decades" (8-K 2026-08-03) is accurate but rests on a 3.2-point catastrophe half-year.

## 6. Valuation

Base: underlying combined ratio holds near 92.5%, catastrophes normalise to 5.0%, premium grows 8%, investment income reaches $125M. Roughly $110M after tax, a 10.5% ROTE, adjusted book per share compounding about 9% to $43, held at 1.38x, about $60 with the dividend.

Bear: a 6.2-point catastrophe load, still below the 10-year average, plus a 3% shortfall on the $972.3M other liability reserve ($29M pre-tax, $22M after tax). Earnings fall to about $72M and ROTE to about 7%; a 7% business does not hold 1.38x book, and 1.05x gives about $42, down 24%.

Bull: underlying combined ratio to 90%, cat load 4%, premium $1.6B and investment income $130M gives about $143M and a 14% ROTE, supporting 1.7x adjusted book, about $67, up 22%.

Weighted 50/25/25 that is $57 against $54.76. Reverse DCF: at 1.38x adjusted tangible book, a 9% cost of equity and 5% long-run growth, the price implies a sustainable return on tangible equity of 10.5% in perpetuity, which is exactly what the business earns once catastrophes are normalised. Nothing is left over for the buyer.

## 8. Kill criteria

1. Underlying combined ratio above 94.0% in two consecutive quarters (92.6% in Q2 2026).
2. Adverse non-catastrophe prior year development in commercial other liability above $10M in any single quarter.
3. Adjusted book value per share growth below 8% annualised across two consecutive quarters (H1 2026 ran 9.8% annualised).
4. Net open-market insider selling above $2M in any rolling twelve months, or any sale by the CEO or CFO.

## 9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 2. United Fire Group has genuinely fixed itself: the underlying combined ratio improved five points in three years to 92.6%, investment income is up a third year over year, and management demonstrably chose margin over premium volume in 2025. The problem is that the screen's 11.9x P/E is a catastrophe-light P/E. The trailing twelve months carried a 2.23-point catastrophe load against a 2023-2025 average near 5 points and a 10-year average the company itself says is above 6.2, and roughly a third of reported premium growth comes from raising the reinsurance retention from $3.0M to $4.0M rather than from writing new business. Normalise catastrophes at a charitable 5% and you pay 13.3x for $106M of earnings, a 10.5% return on tangible equity, at 1.38x adjusted tangible book, after a 77% twelve-month move that leaves the stock 2% off its high, with a director selling into it and a $972M long-tail casualty reserve now opined on by an in-house actuary. The price already assumes the 10.5% return is permanent. Revisit after a heavy catastrophe quarter, or below about 1.15x adjusted tangible book, roughly $46.
