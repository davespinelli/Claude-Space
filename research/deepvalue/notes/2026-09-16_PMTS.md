## 1. What the business actually does
CPI produces and personalises debit, credit and prepaid cards for US financial institutions, fintechs and prepaid program managers, and sells Card@Once, a SaaS instant-issuance system that lets a branch print a permanent personalised card in minutes (10-K Item 1). From Q1 2026 it reports three segments: Secure Card Solutions, Prepaid Solutions and Integrated Paytech, the last carved out of Debit and Credit to expose the software economics (10-K Item 7). Integrated Paytech ran a 55.5% gross margin in H1 2026 on $39.5M of revenue against 26.5% for Secure Card (10-Q Item 2). The core is a reissuance annuity: cards expire, get rebranded and get replaced, and CPI has served its top ten customers for more than ten years on average (10-K Item 1).

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
A clean forced-seller event. On 2026-09-10 Tricor Pacific Capital Partners (Fund IV), the sponsor that floated CPI in 2015, agreed to sell 2,337,323 shares and the underwriters took the full 350,598-share option, closing 2026-09-14 (8-K 2026-09-14). The Form 4 shows the fund sold all 2,687,921 shares, 23.5% of the company, at $20.425 net, leaving zero. The stock traded at $28.83 on 2026-08-29 (Form 4 withholding price), so a quarter of the register cleared about 25% below the pre-deal price. The seller was a buyout fund at the end of its life, not an informed marginal seller. Against it, Tricor PMT25 Holdings, the family office of Tricor founder Roderick Senft, bought 525,000 shares at $21.50 that day and now holds 2,701,056, and four directors bought at the same price (Form 4s, 2026-09-14). The people closest to the asset bought while the fund liquidated. The catch: you now pay 13% above where that block cleared, and the family office block, which carries registration rights (DEF 14A, Related Party Transactions), is the next overhang.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
FY2025 gross margin fell to 31.3% from 35.6% on mix, higher depreciation and tariffs (10-K Item 7). H1 2026 was 31.3% against 32.0%, helped by the tariff refunds. H1 2026 segment operating margins: Secure Card 17.3% ($38.3M on $220.7M), Prepaid 20.3% ($9.1M on $44.7M), Integrated Paytech 34.0% ($13.4M on $39.5M), against $37.9M of unallocated corporate cost (10-Q Item 2). Screen ROIC of 18.9% is respectable but sits on an EBIT line flat for two years. Prepaid is the weak leg: H1 revenue down 3%, down 12.9% excluding the 2025 revenue-recognition change. One reseller was 16% of FY2025 revenue and the top ten over half (10-K Item 1); competitors include Thales, IDEMIA and Giesecke & Devrient, all far larger. H1 cash conversion looks spectacular, $42.1M operating cash flow against $9.9M and $36.1M of free cash flow, but the swing is working capital plus a capex holiday after the Indiana plant: H1 capex $6.1M against $12.7M D&A and $18.2M of FY2025 capex. Use guidance of $45M-$50M, not the $76M LTM figure.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
$265.0M of 10.000% Senior Notes due July 2029, a $100M ABL undrawn at 30 June with $92.3M available, and $30.6M of finance leases (10-Q Item 2). Cash interest is about $26.1M over the next twelve months, more than half of normalised after-tax operating profit. Management redeemed $20M of notes in July 2025 and $26.5M in July 2026, each at 103, funding the latter with a $30M ABL draw (8-K 2026-07-06). Share count creeps: 11.47M basic weighted average in H1 2026 against 11.27M, with 597,934 options at a $14.98 average strike and 513,793 RSUs outstanding (DEF 14A). The 2023 $20M buyback is dormant. Pay is 70% Adjusted EBITDA and 30% net sales short term, relative TSR and cumulative free cash flow long term; the 2025 STIP paid 80% of target on a 96.2% Adjusted EBITDA outcome, so it bites (DEF 14A).

## 5. Management: said versus did
CEO John Lowe said TRISM "doubled our addressable market in U.S. instant issuance"; CFO Terra Grantham cited "a record $36 million of Free Cash Flow in the first half" and 2.7x leverage (Q2 release). They raised revenue and free cash flow guidance in August and pointedly did not raise Adjusted EBITDA, saying tariff refunds and Secure Card strength would be offset by Integrated Paytech investment and soft Prepaid demand. Candid rather than promotional. Watch the finance churn: Grantham was interim CFO from February 2026, permanent 29 June (8-K 2026-07-01), and a new Chief Accounting Officer started 24 August (8-K 2026-08-26).

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
WATCH, conviction 3. CPI Card Group is a cash-generative payments manufacturer at 12.3x normalised after-tax operating profit and a 16% free cash flow yield on management's own raised guidance, whose entire 23.5% private equity stake was liquidated at $20.425 on 14 September because the fund holding it reached the end of its life, while the sponsor's family office and four directors bought at $21.50 the same day. That forced seller is the reason to look. The reason not to buy yet is that the profit is not growing: GAAP operating income has been flat for two years, H1 2026 operating income fell 2.8%, Adjusted EBITDA margin dropped 140 basis points, management guides that line to only low-to-mid single digit growth, and $274M of net debt at a 10% coupon is 3.2x a strictly normalised EBITDA and eats more than half the normalised profit in interest. At $24.29 you are already 13% above where the block cleared, for about 18% probability-weighted upside on a levered small cap. Buy the Integrated Paytech growth once the Q3 print shows it, or buy the stock nearer the $21.50 offering price; do not pay up here.
