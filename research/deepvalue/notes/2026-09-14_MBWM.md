## 1. What the business actually does
A Michigan bank holding company running two state-chartered banks from 54 offices: Mercantile Bank in Grand Rapids covering west and central Michigan plus Detroit, Traverse City, Petoskey and Midland, and Eastern Michigan Bank in Croswell (10-K, Item 1). It lends mainly to small and medium-sized businesses. Commercial loans are $4.05B of the $4.92B book, with C&I plus owner-occupied CRE at 58% of commercial (8-K 2026-07-21, Loan Portfolio Composition). The banks run separately until the core processor conversion completes in Q1 2027, when Eastern Michigan folds into Mercantile Bank (10-K, Item 1).

## 2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case
There is none, and that caps the verdict at WATCH. The stock sits 0.9% below its 52-week high after a 25% six-month run (universe_v2.csv), trades $5.7M a day on Nasdaq Global Select, and has no spin-off, index deletion, forced seller or misunderstood segment. The one thing resembling an edge runs the wrong way: the growth the screen ranked it on is acquisition arithmetic, so the quality-growth profile is weaker than the table suggests, not stronger.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Credit is genuinely clean. Nonperforming assets are $5.8M, 0.09% of assets, down from 0.16% a year ago, with zero other real estate owned and $0.5M of net recoveries in Q2 (8-K 2026-07-21, Asset Quality). The largest nonaccrual, a construction loan reserved at $5.5M in 2025, was charged down $2.8M and its remaining $2.7M paid off in Q2 2026 with a $0.2M recovery. The cost is that the allowance fell to 1.13% of loans from 1.21%, and 1H26 pre-tax income contains $3.6M of reserve release, 6.2% of pre-tax (10-Q, provision discussion). Non-owner-occupied CRE, multifamily and construction total $1.71B, about 191% of total risk-based capital, inside the 300% supervisory threshold.

NIM rose to 3.59% from 3.48%, but the mechanism matters: loan yields fell to 6.01% from 6.29% while cost of funds fell to 1.83% from 2.27%. About 77% of average commercial loans are variable rate, so further Fed cuts reprice assets immediately while deposit costs, at 1.74%, have little room left. Deposit mix improved on its own merits: loan-to-deposit is 93% from 100%, brokered deposits fell $179M over twelve months to $20.1M maturing, wholesale funds are 6% of total funds from 10%, and noninterest-bearing checking is 27% of deposits. All securities are available for sale, so the $40.5M unrealised loss already sits in tangible book with no held-to-maturity bucket hiding a second loss (10-Q, securities).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Equity grew $30.2M in 1H26: $48.6M of earnings less $13.3M of dividends and an $8.0M after-tax AOCI hit (10-Q, Capital Resources). Share count went from 16.26M to 17.29M entirely through the acquisition and small plan issuance; no repurchase programme appears in the filings. The dividend is $0.39 a quarter with an increase announced for Q3 2026, a 27% payout on TTM earnings. Insiders bought and sold nothing on the open market in twelve months across 13 Form 4 rows; the only disposition was the CFO's tax withholding at $54.24 (form4_summary).

## 5. Management: said versus did
Management said it would fund growth with local deposits and resolve problem credits quickly, and did both: local deposits grew $122M in 1H26 while brokered fell $110M, and the 2025 nonaccrual was collected in full. Against that, the 2026 bonus plan weights EPS 25% and ROA 25% with no tangible book or through-cycle credit metric, and no restricted stock was granted in 2024 "due to a change in our processes" (8-K 2026-03-19; DEF 14A 2026-04-02, note 1). Pay is annual and mostly cash.

## 6. Valuation
Reverse DCF: at 1.60x tangible book and a 10% cost of equity, the price implies a 13.6% sustainable return on tangible equity with 4.0% perpetual tangible book growth; at an 11% cost of equity, appropriate for a $6.8B commercial bank with 54% uninsured deposits, the same price requires 6.7% growth.

Base (50%): normalised ROTE 13.6%, 27% payout, tangible book compounds near 9% to about $45.60 by mid-2028, exit 1.5x, plus $3.20 of dividends, gives $71.60, roughly 8% a year. Bear (25%): three more Fed cuts push NIM to 3.35%, provisions normalise to 25 basis points, conversion overruns; normalised EPS falls to $4.20 and the multiple compresses to 1.15x a $41 book, giving $47. Bull (25%): conversion lands on time, cost saves arrive, efficiency returns to 55%, the $236M construction pipeline funds; 1.8x a $46 book gives $83. Probability-weighted $68.30 against $61.58, about 11% over two years.

## 7. Catalysts
Q3 2026 results in late October test whether NIM holds above 3.55% without another reserve release. Core conversion completion and the Eastern Michigan consolidation in Q1 2027 is the real cost-save event. The Q3 dividend increase is already announced.

## 8. Kill criteria
1. Net interest margin below 3.45% in two consecutive quarterly releases.
2. Allowance for credit losses below 1.10% of loans, or a third consecutive negative provision while nonperforming loans exceed 0.25% of loans.
3. Cumulative core conversion costs above $9M, or the Q1 2027 conversion date slipping in any 10-Q.
4. Efficiency ratio excluding conversion and acquisition costs above 60% for two consecutive quarters.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 2. Mercantile is a well-run Michigan commercial bank with genuinely clean credit, a fixed deposit problem and honest accounting, and at 10.8x trailing earnings it is not expensive. It is not an idea, because the growth the screen ranked it on is bought rather than organic: two-thirds of the 15.7% net interest income growth is the Eastern Michigan acquisition that closed on the last day of 2025, organic net revenue grew about 2%, and 6.2% of first-half pre-tax income is a reserve release that took the allowance down to 1.13% of loans. Margin expansion came from deposit costs falling faster than loan yields, a tailwind nearly spent with 77% of commercial loans variable rate and cost of deposits already at 1.74%. On normalised earnings, meaning conversion costs added back, a mid-cycle provision charged and a 21% tax rate instead of the 16.9% rate that purchased energy credits produce, the stock is 11.8x and 1.60x tangible book, which at a 10% cost of equity already embeds 4% perpetual growth. It sits 0.9% off its 52-week high with no forced seller and no misunderstood segment, so there is no edge case to exploit. The reason to keep watching is the Q1 2027 core conversion: if Eastern Michigan folds in on time and the efficiency ratio returns to 55% without credit deteriorating, the bull case is a real 1.8x tangible book bank, and the Q3 and Q4 2026 margin prints will say which way it is going.
