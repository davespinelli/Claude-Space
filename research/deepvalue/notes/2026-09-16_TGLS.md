## 1. What the business does
Tecnoglass makes high-specification architectural glass, aluminium and vinyl windows, curtain walls and facades in a 6.1 million square foot vertically integrated complex in Barranquilla, Colombia, and sells almost all of it into the United States (94.8% of FY2025 revenue; 10-K 2025, Item 7). It melts, tempers, laminates and coats its own glass, extrudes its own aluminium, assembles windows and, via 2016-17 acquisitions, installs them. End markets are roughly 54% multi-family/commercial, 41% single-family residential; Florida is over 90% of U.S. revenue, with 13.1% of U.S. backlog outside it. About 1,000 customers, none above 10%. The moat is arithmetic: Colombian labour, on-site solar and cogeneration, and containers that would otherwise return empty to the U.S., putting Miami three days away. The CFO puts the resulting EBITDA margin at "twenty eight, twenty nine percent" against a low-teens industry, about 60% of the gap from labour arbitrage (8-K 2026-06-25, CFO).

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
There is no informational edge, and that alone caps this at WATCH. The stock trades $10.5M a day, FMR owns 14.6% (DEF 14A 2025), and every figure below sits in a press release. What exists is a duration disagreement. The shares are about 49% off their 52-week high because Q2 gross margin fell from 44.7% to 37.3% and Adjusted EBITDA dropped 35% on 15.6% revenue growth (8-K 2026-08-06). The market capitalises that as permanent; management calls it transitory.

The bear case deserves respect because the shock lands on the moat itself. Section 232 tariffs on finished aluminium window imports, enacted April 2026, cost $17.0M in Q2 alone; the all-in U.S. aluminium price rose about 77% year over year; the peso appreciated about 14% (and 14.8% in 2025, 10-K Item 1A); Colombia's minimum wage rose double digits. Three of those four attack the Colombia arbitrage directly. Against that, insiders bought with real money: Energy Holding Corporation, the Daes family vehicle, acquired 622,995 shares for $27.0M between 2026-03-06 and 2026-05-15 at $40.27-$45.28 — all above today's price — lifting its stake to 20.83M shares, 47.0% of the 44.36M outstanding (Form 4s, 2026-03-10 to 2026-05-18).

## 3. Unit economics
Gross margin ran 46.9% (2023), 42.7% (2024), 42.8% (2025), then 37.3% in Q2 2026. SG&A went from 20.8% to 24.9% of revenue in a year, almost entirely tariffs. Normalised TTM ROIC is 15.3% on $933M invested, down from about 19% on the FY2025 base. Cash conversion is the weakest part of the file: 1H26 CFO was $11.1M against $64.8M, absorbing $35.8M of inventory (deliberate U.S.-aluminium stockpiling to bypass tariffs), $32.3M of receivables on longer-cycle commercial jobs and $39.2M of Colombian tax payments, against $52.7M of capex. Much of that capex is discretionary — maintenance capex is "less than one percent of revenues" (8-K 2026-06-25, CFO) — but a company paying $26.6M of dividends and buying back stock on negative free cash flow is funding it with the revolver: debt rose from $171.6M to $225.4M in six months. Pricing power is real but lagged: 7% announced in May, deliberately at the low end of an industry taking 6-12% in order to keep taking share, with revenue benefit only from Q3.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Clean: 0.62x net leverage, no maturity before December 2030, effective rate 6.98% (10-K 2025, Item 7). Share count fell 5.6% year over year to 44.36M, with $16.5M repurchased in 1H26 and $92.5M left of a $250M authorisation. Dividend $0.15 quarterly, a 1.66% yield (8-K 2026-09-10). Governance is the blemish: the two Daes brothers were each paid $3,822,080 salary plus up to $1,248,832 bonus for 2025, and the proxy states no stock, option or non-equity incentive awards were issued to any named executive officer; all directors and officers together hold 30,520 shares directly (DEF 14A 2025-11-28). Alignment runs only through Energy Holding's controlling 47%.

## 5. Management: said vs did
The 2026 guide was cut, not missed: revenue narrowed to $1.08-1.12B and Adjusted EBITDA to $220-230M, attributed to "sustained high aluminum costs and a Colombian peso that has strengthened beyond our prior assumptions, not a change in the demand for our products" (8-K 2026-08-06, CFO). Operationally they delivered: 10% headcount cut completed by end-June, May pricing implemented, Florida redomiciliation completed 7 July 2026 after a 39.7M-to-0.5M vote (8-Ks 2026-06-16, 2026-07-07). The testable promise now on record is the CFO's: "we're fully prepared to fully offset the tariff impact in twenty twenty seven" (8-K 2026-06-25).

## 6. Valuation
**Bear (30%): $21.** Tariffs and a strong peso persist, the 7% rise is competed away, margin settles at 19% EBITDA on $1.15B of 2028 revenue (~$115M after-tax operating profit) at 9x. **Base (50%): $43.** Revenue compounds ~6%, margin recovers to 23% by 2028 on $1.24B (~$160M after-tax) at 12x, net debt flat. **Bull (20%): $55.** The offset lands in full, automation and pricing restore 26% on $1.3B (~$194M after-tax) at 13x. Probability-weighted **$38.8**, about 7% above $36.16 before the dividend. Reverse DCF: at a $1,749M enterprise value, 9% cost of capital and 2.5% terminal growth, the current price implies steady-state free cash flow of about $114M, roughly a 10.8% after-tax operating margin — almost exactly what 2026 guidance implies (~10.0%) and far below the 16.0% earned in 2025. The price already assumes trough margins are permanent, which is why the bear case is largely paid for and why the upside needs the recovery to show up rather than be promised.

## 7. Catalysts
Q3 2026 results in early November are the first quarter carrying full May pricing and the reduced cost base; the sequential gross margin line is the whole story. FY2027 guidance (February-March 2027) tests the tariff-offset commitment. Index eligibility after the July redomiciliation could bring mechanical buying. A decision on the proposed U.S. plant would de-risk tariffs but add capex.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
Risks: tariffs are policy, not weather, and can widen; peso hedging is described as opportunistic; two suppliers are 37.3% of raw material purchases (10-K Item 1); Florida is over 90% of U.S. revenue; a 47% holder controls outcomes. Any one of these ends the WATCH as a PASS:
1. Gross margin below 38% in both Q3 and Q4 2026 — May pricing failed to lift margin off the 37.3% trough.
2. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA guidance below $240M — the 2027 offset commitment broken.
3. Backlog falls sequentially for two consecutive quarters from $1.38B.
4. Full-year 2026 free cash flow negative while net debt exceeds 1.5x Adjusted EBITDA, or Energy Holding Corporation turns net seller on any Form 4.

## 9. Verdict
WATCH, conviction 3. Tecnoglass is a genuinely high-quality, 47%-owner-operated business whose entire edge is making windows with cheap Colombian labour and shipping them into Florida in otherwise-empty containers, and in 2026 three things attacked that edge at once: April's Section 232 tariffs on finished aluminium windows ($17.0M in Q2 alone), aluminium up about 77%, and a peso up about 14%. Gross margin fell from 44.7% to 37.3% in a year even as revenue grew 15.6% to a record and backlog reached a record $1.38B, so this is a cost problem, not a demand problem. The screen's 10.1x is stale: trailing twelve months is 12.3x EV to normalised after-tax operating profit, roughly 15-16x on the company's own cut guidance, and trailing free cash flow is slightly negative on tariff-driven aluminium stockpiling and growth capex. At $36.16 the price already implies trough margins last forever, and the Daes family bought $27.0M of stock at $40-45 on the way down, which is why this is not a PASS. But the fix is a promise rather than a fact, and at a probability-weighted $39 there is no margin of safety worth paying for until Q3 and Q4 show the May price increase actually landing in gross margin.
