## 1. What the business actually does
Princeton Bancorp is the holding company for The Bank of Princeton, a New Jersey state-chartered community bank founded in 2007, with 29 branches in New Jersey, five around Philadelphia and two in the New York City metro area (8-K 2026-07-23, EX-99.1). It is a commercial real estate lender with a retail deposit franchise attached. CRE including multi-family and owner-occupied was $1.27B, 72% of loans, at June 30, 2026, split 47.5% New York, 38.7% New Jersey and 12.4% Pennsylvania (10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A). The stated strategy is organic relationship banking plus acquisitions; the last was Cornerstone Financial in 2024.

## 2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case
Honestly, there is only a small one, which caps this at WATCH. The candidate edge is that FY2025 GAAP earnings are a bad picture of the bank: one $9.9M charge-off in Q2 2025 and a merger year before it make screens read a 7% ROTCE, 16x bank when the Q2 2026 run rate is 11% reported ROTCE at 10.5x. Some of that gap has already closed. The stock went from $30.54 at June 2025 to $37.95 at June 2026 to $43.52 now, a 33.8% six-month return that leaves it 0.8% off its 52-week high. There is no forced seller, no spin-off, no index event. The one insider signal is mixed: director and 11.5% holder Martin Tuchman bought 10,534 shares at $42.98 (Form 4, 2026-08-12), while director Stephen Distler sold 8,776 at $41.48 (Form 4, 2026-07-31).

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix
Non-performing assets were $16.3M, 0.92% of loans, versus $16.6M at year end (10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A). Two readings: net charge-offs have been negative for three of the last four quarters, but the NPA balance has sat between $16.3M and $16.7M for five straight quarters, which means these credits are not being resolved either. Allowance is 1.13% of loans, $19.96M, about 122% of non-performing loans. Reported LTVs are conservative: weighted average 52.4% on multi-family, 34.9% owner-occupied, and 91.7% of the CRE book below 70% LTV at year end (10-K 2025, Item 7). The concentration is the risk, not the marks. By my calculation from the Q2 tables, multi-family plus land plus non-owner-occupied CRE is $911.4M against the Bank's $281.2M of total risk-based capital, above 320%, before construction loans are added; the supervisory guidance threshold is 300%. Margin is the bright spot: NIM 3.51%, 3.63%, 3.86% over the last three quarters, with cost of deposits down 40bp year over year. Deposits are 16% non-interest checking, and $666.0M, 34.4% of deposits, are uninsured (10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A). AOCI is only a $5.4M loss, so there is no hidden securities hole.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
No borrowings at either date, $523.5M of unused FHLB capacity, Tier 1 leverage 11.69% against a 5% well-capitalised threshold (10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A). Capital is the asset here: roughly $60M sits above a 9% leverage ratio, a fifth of the market cap. Management bought back $7.9M of stock in 2025 (10-K 2025, Item 7) but appears to have stopped; share count rose from 6,766k at December to 6,813k in June on option exercises. Insiders own 23.1% as a group, Tuchman 11.5% with voting capped at 9.9% (DEF 14A 2026-03-26). Two Tuchman-affiliated branch leases are disclosed, both small at $29,700 and $40,770 a year. The management incentive plan weights tangible book value 60%, which is the right metric.

## 5. Management: said versus did
The CEO's only public comment is boilerplate ("strong second quarter performance ... continued execution of our strategic priorities", 8-K 2026-07-23). With no call and no guidance there is nothing to hold them to. What they did: shrank brokered and high-cost CDs by design, cut office CRE from $104.4M to $9.8M during 2025 while healthcare rose from $10.3M to $108.4M, and kept the dividend at $0.35. That is competent defensive management, and a bank that does not talk to investors is part of why it is cheap.

## 6. Valuation
Base: NIM holds near 3.85%, loans flat, normalised provision $5M, ROTCE settles at 8.6%, market pays 1.00 to 1.05x a tangible book that compounds to roughly $40.50 by mid-2027 — $41 to $43. Bear: one CRE credit goes bad, provision returns to the 2025 level, ROTCE 7%, multiple 0.85x — $34. Bull: NIM reaches 4.0% as the remaining $628M of time deposits reprice, loans grow 4%, and the excess capital is bought back near tangible book, lifting ROTCE to 11-12% and the multiple to 1.4x — $59. Weighting 25/50/25 gives about $44 against $43.52. Reverse DCF: at 1.127x tangible book, with a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth, the price implies a sustainable ROTCE of about 10.9%, essentially the reported Q2 figure with its negative provision treated as permanent.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q3 results in late October should show whether NIM holds above 3.85% once the easy CD repricing is done. A restart of the buyback, or an announcement that the excess capital is being deployed into a deal rather than returned, is the fork in the road. A resolution of the stubborn $16.3M of NPAs would be a genuine positive surprise.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. Non-performing assets above 1.4% of loans, or above $25M, in any two consecutive quarters.
2. Net interest margin below 3.60% for two consecutive quarters, which would mean the funding-cost tailwind has reversed.
3. Period-end loans below $1.70B at any quarter end through mid-2027, that is a sixth consecutive quarter of shrinkage eating the earnings base.
4. An acquisition that issues stock above 7.5M total shares outstanding, or is struck above 1.3x tangible book, spending the capital that is the bull case.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 2. Princeton Bancorp is a clean, over-capitalised, zero-borrowing New Jersey CRE lender whose screen row is wrong in the company's favour: the 15.9x P/E and 7.1% ROTE come from a 2025 that carried a single $9.9M charge-off and follows a 2024 with $7.8M of merger costs, while the June quarter run rate is $1.04 a share, 10.5% ROTCE and 3.86% NIM. The problem is that the market has already noticed. The stock is up 33.8% in six months and sits 0.8% off its high at 1.13x tangible book, and once a normal provision of about $5M a year is charged instead of the reversals booked in both 2026 quarters, normalised ROTCE is 8.6%, not 11%, which makes 1.13x tangible book roughly fair rather than cheap. Loans are shrinking 3.7% a year, so the earnings improvement is a funding-cost story with a finite runway, and my calculation puts CRE concentration above 320% of the Bank's total risk-based capital. The real option is the roughly $60M of excess capital, a fifth of the market cap, which management has so far neither returned nor deployed. Worth owning below tangible book or on evidence the buyback has restarted; not here.
