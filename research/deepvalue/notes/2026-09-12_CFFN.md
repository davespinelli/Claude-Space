## 1. What the business actually does
A 133-year-old Topeka thrift, 46 branches across nine Kansas and three Missouri counties, second in Kansas deposit share at 6.2% (10-K 2025, Item 1). It is converting itself from a prime-residential portfolio lender into a commercial bank. Commercial loans were 30.18% of the book at 30 June 2026 against 26.01% at 30 September 2025 and 21% in March 2025 (8-K 2026-07-29, slide 22). Correspondent residential purchases were suspended in June 2024, and runoff from the $5.60B one- to four-family book yielding 3.77% is redirected into commercial loans yielding 5.98% (10-Q 2026, Item 2).

## 2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case
There is none. The stock is up 29.9% in six months, sits 3.1% below its 52-week high, trades $6.6M a day and is held by the usual index owners. No spin, no index event, no forced seller, no restatement. The repositioning has been public for three years and is on slide 8 of the company's own deck. The verdict is therefore capped at WATCH, and the question is whether the re-rating has further to run, not whether a dislocation exists.

## 3. Credit quality, margin and deposits
The margin story is real. Net interest margin has gone 1.21% in FY2023, 1.80% in FY2024, 2.09% in the September 2025 quarter and 2.31% in the June 2026 quarter (8-K 2026-07-29, slide 8), eight consecutive quarters of expansion. The efficiency ratio fell from 60.54% to 52.10% on 6.8% nine-month expense growth (10-Q 2026, Item 2), and quarterly EPS went $0.12, $0.14, $0.14, $0.16, $0.16, $0.19.

Credit is the untested part. Ten-year cumulative net charge-offs are $1.2 million (10-Q 2026, Item 2), a record belonging to a residential book that is now 68% of loans and shrinking. The allowance is 0.32% of loans, $26.1M, against $51.3M of nonaccruals: coverage of 50.90%. Substandard loans are $68.4M. In the March 2026 quarter a lower appraisal forced a $4.0M specific valuation allowance on one commercial real estate relationship, and a $24.0M loan within it was on nonaccrual and classified substandard at 30 June. Three of the four loans in the $20M to $30M bucket with debt service coverage below 1.15x sit with one of the largest borrowing groups. Commercial real estate and construction total $2.2B, of which hotel is 29% and senior housing 25%: roughly $1.19B of two operating-intensive property types against $1.02B of equity, the largest single loan an $86.0M Arizona hotel (10-Q 2026, Item 2; 8-K 2026-07-29, slides 24 and 25).

Funding offsets this. Deposits cost 2.09% and only 11% of the Call Report balance is uninsured. The weakness is that non-interest-bearing deposits are $671.9M, under 10% of the $6.85B base, so the franchise still buys its money.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Borrowings fell $314.5M to $1.64B at an effective 3.72%, weighted average maturity 1.4 years. The next four quarters reprice $375.0M of term borrowings currently costing 2.74%, including $100.0M at 1.24%: a headwind of roughly $4M a year pre-tax against the mix-shift tailwind. CBLR is 9.6% and CET1 16.0%. Nine-month repurchases were 6,369,946 shares at $7.21, $47.5M in total through 3 August, leaving $23.6M of a Federal Reserve non-objection that expires in February 2027. The dividend is $0.085 a quarter plus a $0.04 special paid in January 2026. The holding company held only $10.7M of cash at 30 June and depends on upstreaming from the Bank, constrained by pre-1988 bad debt recapture: FY2024 distributions triggered that tax, and the risk factor says distributions will be limited to keep the Bank out of a negative earnings and profits position (10-K 2025, Item 1A). The only open-market insider purchase in twelve months was 10,000 shares at $6.05 by the chief lending officer on 2025-10-31, with no sales (Form 4, 2025-11-03).

## 5. Management: said versus did
Dicus said on 29 July that these are "the expected results from the successful execution of our strategies", and on two years of numbers he is right. The longer record is not: $100 invested on 30 September 2020 was worth $96.57 at 30 September 2025 against $270.17 for the S&P US BMI Bank Index (DEF 14A 2025, pay versus performance). The bonus plan pays on efficiency ratio, basic EPS and return on average equity; for FY2026 the efficiency ratio was swapped for the operating expense ratio, which removes the margin tailwind from the metric. Directors take cash retainers and no stock.

## 6. Valuation
Base (55%): expansion continues but halves in pace as cheap borrowings reprice, ROTE reaches 10% by FY2028, tangible book compounds about 4% a year to roughly $8.70 and the market pays 1.15x. That is $10.00 plus $0.68 of dividends in two years, about $8.85 today at a 10% discount rate. Bull (20%): margin reaches 2.75%, ROTE 11.5%, 1.4x book gives $12.20 plus dividends, about $10.65 today. Bear (25%): the hotel and senior housing book produces a 3% loss against a 0.32% allowance, margin stalls, ROTE falls to 7% and the multiple to 0.85x, about $6.30 today. Probability-weighted $8.57, 3% below price. Reverse DCF: at 1.10x tangible book with a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth, the current price implies a sustainable return on tangible equity of about 10.7%, above the 9.2% just delivered and well above FY2025's 6.7%, so the price already assumes the transition finishes.

## 7. Catalysts
FY2026 Q4 results and the 10-K in late October and November 2026, carrying the first full-year margin figure. The February 2027 expiry of the Federal Reserve buyback non-objection. The $125.0M of FY2026 and $360.0M of FY2027 term borrowings maturing at 3.66% and 2.58%.

## 8. Kill criteria
1. Net interest margin flat or below 2.31% in both the September and December 2026 quarters, ending the eight-quarter streak that is the entire thesis.
2. Nonaccrual loans above $75M, roughly 0.9% of loans, or a second specific valuation allowance inside the hotel or senior housing book.
3. Quarterly net charge-offs above $2M, against $304 thousand for the whole first nine months of FY2026.
4. A quarter with no repurchases while the $75M non-objection is live, or a disclosed limit on Bank-to-holding-company distributions from bad debt recapture.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 2. Capitol Federal is doing the right thing slowly: turning a 3.77% residential mortgage book into a 5.98% commercial book has lifted net interest margin from 1.21% to 2.31% over eight consecutive quarters, cut the efficiency ratio from 60.5% to 52.1% and roughly doubled quarterly EPS from $0.12 to $0.19 in six quarters, funded by a 2.09% deposit base only 11% uninsured. None of this is hidden: the stock is up 30% in six months and sits 3% below its high, and at 1.10x tangible book it already implies a sustainable return on tangible equity near 10.7% against the 9.2% just delivered, so probability-weighted value of $8.57 is slightly below the $8.82 price. Underneath, the loss record that justifies a 0.32% allowance belongs to the residential book that is disappearing, while $1.19B of hotel and senior housing loans, more than the company's entire equity, sits in a portfolio whose first appraisal-driven $4.0M specific reserve was taken two quarters ago and whose allowance covers only 51% of nonaccruals. Wait for the October print to show margin still expanding after $375M of cheap borrowings reprice, and for two more quarters of clean commercial credit, and this becomes a real idea at a lower multiple of book.
