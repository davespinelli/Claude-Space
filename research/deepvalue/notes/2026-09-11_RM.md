## 1. What the business does
Regional Finance makes fixed-rate, fully amortising installment loans to near-prime and subprime consumers through 357 branches in 20 states, plus direct-mail convenience checks, digital affiliates and its website (10-K Item 1; 10-Q). Large loans of $2,501 to $35,000 carried a 30.6% weighted-average APR on 2025 originations; small loans of $500 to $2,500 carried 44.3%. At 6/30/26 large loans were $1.66B (77.3% of the book), small loans $488.6M. A captive reinsurer takes ceded premium on optional insurance, $45.6M of revenue in 2025. Texas is 30% of receivables (10-K Item 1A).

## 2. Why it might be mispriced
There is a candidate edge case but it is not decisive. RM is a $303M company with 9.2M shares and $3.1M of daily volume, below most institutional thresholds, that cut 2026 guidance by roughly two thirds in one quarter and sits 25% off its 52-week high. The two best-informed buyers then stepped in: new CEO Lakhbir Lamba bought 10,000 shares of his own money at $31.59 and $31.76 on 11-12 August 2026, roughly doubling his stake to 55,734 shares (Form 4, 2026-08-13), and Basswood Capital, the 9.1% holder with a board seat under a 2018 cooperation agreement, added 27,828 shares at $31.86 and $33.10 on 1-2 September 2026 (Form 4, 2026-09-03; DEF 14A). Against that, the de-rating is not a misunderstanding: charge-offs and delinquencies are both rising, the highest-margin product is shrinking, and the multiple is defensible on the current run rate. The case rests on the post-cut trough being the floor, which is a forecast, not an edge.

## 3. Credit quality, yield and funding
Credit is drifting the wrong way. The Q2 net credit loss rate was 12.2% against 11.9%, with about 20bp attributed to slower portfolio growth; 30+ day delinquency 7.0% against 6.6% (10-Q). The allowance is 10.4% of receivables. Management blames legacy balances in higher-risk segments since tightened, and a 10% adverse shift in macro weighting would add only $1.7M of reserves (10-K, Critical Accounting Policies), thin against 4.9x tangible leverage. Yield compressed at the same time: total revenue yield fell 110bp, interest and fee yield 100bp, as the mix moved to auto-secured large loans, now $323.7M and 15.1% of the book at 26.6%, from small loans at 34.3%. Auto-secured delinquency is 2.0%, so the trade is real, but it is lowering revenue faster than losses so far. The clean positive is operating leverage: the expense ratio improved 80bp to 12.4%. Funding is laddered to October 2027 with $442M unused, though April 2026 amendments cut the RMR VII advance rate from 76.0% to 72.5% if it elects a higher concentration limit for loans above 36% APR (8-K, 2026-05-04).

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
Debt was $1.68B at 6/30/26 against $343.7M of tangible equity; $223.3M of principal falls due within twelve months against a self-liquidating book. Capital return is real: 1.2M shares, about 11% of the November 2024 count, repurchased for $40.3M of a $60M authorisation running to June 2027, leaving roughly $19.7M. Diluted shares fell from 9,843k to 9,604k, which is why YTD EPS rose 17.3% on 14.0% net income growth. In November 2025 the company bought 27,000 shares directly from Basswood at $37.00, ratified by the Audit Committee (DEF 14A). May 2026 LTIP grants tie PRSUs to relative total shareholder return and pre-provision return on assets (8-K, 2026-05-19), a sensible pairing for a lender.

## 5. Management: said versus did
In April the CEO said "we are confident in our outlook for 2026" alongside a 20-25% net income growth target (8-K, 2026-04-29). In July he reported that "portfolio growth fell short of our expectations, and our net credit loss rate was modestly above our forecast" and cut to 6-9% (8-K, 2026-07-29). Q2 receivables grew $44M against the $60-75M guided in April, and losses printed 12.2% against roughly 12.0% guided: a miss on both variables management chose. The new pitch is the Column N.A. bank partnership signed 2 March 2026, live in Texas, more than $65M originated, 28% of run-rate originations, with pre-tax margin projected to improve 200bp or more versus state-licensed loans and substantially all branches on it by end-2027. None of that margin is yet visible in a reported line, and the 10-K, filed before the agreement, carries no risk factor for it.

## 6. Valuation

The market is paying for the first-half run rate.

Bear (25%): losses pass a 13% net credit loss rate, ROTE settles near 8.5%, 0.6x tangible book. **$22**.
Base (50%): the partnership delivers part of its promise, losses normalise near 11%, ROTE recovers to 13%, 1.1x a tangible book compounding around 9%. **$42**.
Bull (25%): 150bp of the promised 200bp scales by 2028, ROTE reaches 16-17%, 1.5x a tangible book near $44, discounted back. **$55**.
Probability-weighted **$40**, about 23% above the price plus a 3.7% dividend. Fair, not compelling, at 4.9x tangible leverage into rising charge-offs.

## 8. Pre-registered kill criteria
1. Net credit loss rate above 11.5% in Q3 2026 or above 11.9% in Q4 2026 (guided roughly 10.7% and 11.1%).
2. 30+ day delinquency above 7.5% at any quarter end, above the 12/31/25 peak.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Regional Management is a 4.9x tangible-levered subprime installment lender at 0.88x tangible book and 6.9x trailing earnings, with an 11% book-value compounding record, a 3.7% dividend and an 11% share count reduction over two years. It is not an IDEA because the cheapness fairly reflects what is happening: guidance cut from 20-25% to 6-9% net income growth in one quarter, net credit losses up to 12.2% from 11.9%, delinquency up to 7.0% from 6.6%, interest and fee yield down 100bp on the shift into lower-rate auto-secured loans, and the highest-yield small-loan book down 10.7% on what management calls a more competitive new-customer acquisition environment. The price already embeds a sustainable ROTE of about 11.3%, almost exactly the 11.5% delivered in the first half. The bull case is entirely the six-month-old Column partnership, claimed to add 200bp or more of pre-tax margin and 28% of run-rate originations, but live in one state and never broken out in a filing. The best reason to keep watching is that the new CEO and the 9.1% holder with a board seat both bought in the low $30s after the cut. Q3 results in late October test the guided 10.7% loss rate directly; a print at or below it, with the first disclosed partnership economics, converts this to an IDEA.
