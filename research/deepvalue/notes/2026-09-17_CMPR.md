## 1. What the business actually does
Cimpress is the global leader in web-to-print mass customisation: business cards, signage, promotional products, logo apparel, packaging and wall decor, sold online in low quantities at near mass-production cost. Founded 1995, $3.7B of revenue in FY2026 from over 16,000 employees (10-K, Item 1). It is a decentralised holding company of five segments, VistaPrint ($1,934M revenue, 20% segment EBITDA margin), PrintBrothers ($823M, 12%), The Print Group ($446M, 19%), National Pen ($447M, 9%) and All Other ($258M, 9%), with roughly 100 people in central functions. The stated uppermost objective is intrinsic value per share, defined as discounted unlevered free cash flow per diluted share minus net debt per share, and management says explicitly it will accept weak reported revenue, EPS and EBITDA in service of it.

## 2. Why it might be mispriced — the edge case
There is no durable edge case, and that caps the verdict at WATCH. Ownership is concentrated, 70% in the top ten holders (10-K, Item 1A), with Prescott at 14.6%, Janus at 13.4%, founder-CEO Robert Keane at 8.8% and Spruce House at 8.4% (DEF 14A 2025-10-28), which thins the float but is a known feature, not a dislocation. Negative book equity and a June year end keep the name off some screens. But the stock is not neglected or forced: 22.6% off its 52-week high, 12-1 momentum of +59%, $15.6M a day of volume, and no identifiable seller. Insiders give the opposite signal, zero open-market purchases and $5.93M of net selling over twelve months (Form 4s, 12m).

## 3. Unit economics and growth
The quality flag the screen cannot see is gross margin. Cost of revenue was 51.5% of revenue in FY2024, 52.5% in FY2025 and 53.7% in FY2026 (10-K, Item 7), 220 basis points of erosion in two years. Management's explanation is partly credible mix: newer elevated products "have lower gross margins than many of our mature products" but attract higher-lifetime-value customers (Item 1). The rest is not mix: $66.7M more third-party fulfilment, $47.1M more internal manufacturing cost, $19.8M more shipping, plus the duty write-off. Operating expense discipline offset it, marketing and selling falling from 23.9% to 23.3% of revenue and technology from 9.8% to 9.4%, which is how operating income still rose $24.8M. Cash conversion is the weak point: capex $100.2M plus capitalised software $67.0M is $167.2M against $151.8M of D&A, while operating cash flow fell $14.4M on higher cash taxes and working capital. Most telling, derived from the nine-month and full-year disclosures, Q4 FY2026 operating income was about $64.8M against $65.5M a year earlier and Q4 adjusted EBITDA about $120.4M against $122.5M, both down slightly despite 9% revenue growth.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
$1,636.4M of debt against $248.9M of cash, nothing maturing before 2031. Interest expense, net, was $105.8M, consuming 41% of normalised operating income; a 100 basis point move adds $7.4M (Item 1A). Capital allocation is owner-minded in method: 702,820 shares retired for $50.1M in FY2026 (about $71 average), a fresh $200M authorisation on 2026-03-25 explicitly subordinated to net leverage commentary (8-K 2026-03-31), and a stated plan to "meaningfully reduce net leverage over the next two fiscal years" (8-K 2026-05-11). The tension is that the same release announced a €120M acquisition, Saxoprint and viaprinto, closed 2026-07-02, for businesses with €89.6M of CY2025 revenue at roughly 10% EBITDA margins. That is about 13x EBITDA before synergies and before an unsigned sale-leaseback; the claimed "well in excess of 20%" return rests on cross-Cimpress fulfilment synergies not yet evidenced, and pro forma net debt rises to roughly $1,577M. Proxy alignment is mixed: FY25 PSUs were amended in May 2025 to add a 60% minimum payout multiplier after the company hit 95% of its revenue and 90% of its EBITDA and unlevered free cash flow targets, adding $1.84M of incremental fair value for Keane alone (DEF 14A, note 3), a floor placed under a miss.

## 5. Management: what they said vs what they did
Keane's framing has been consistent for a decade: maximise intrinsic value per share, accept lumpy GAAP, and he has largely done it, retiring shares below his own estimate of value and refinancing opportunistically. What he said in May 2026 was that Saxoprint would enhance per-share free cash flow "without changing our established plans to meaningfully reduce net leverage." What he did the same quarter was commit €120M of cash while adjusted free cash flow fell for a second year. Both can be true if synergies land, and FY2027 is the test.

## 6. Valuation
Base (55%): organic growth holds at 4%, Saxoprint adds about $105M of revenue, North America start-up costs roll off, FY2028 adjusted EBITDA about $485M and unlevered adjusted free cash flow about $260M; at 14x, EV $3.64B less net debt near $1.45B is about $91 a share in two years. Bear (25%): AI-mediated search erodes VistaPrint acquisition efficiency, organic growth falls to 0-1%, gross margin gives up another 150 basis points, adjusted EBITDA $400M and unlevered free cash flow $180M at 12x; EV $2.16B against net debt $1.55B leaves about $25, the severity entirely a function of 3.5x leverage. Bull (20%): 6% organic growth, synergies land, FY2028 adjusted EBITDA $520M, unlevered free cash flow $300M at 16x, net debt $1.2B, about $153. Probability-weighted, roughly $87 against $81.50, a 7% margin that does not pay for the bear tail. Reverse DCF: at $81.50 the EV of $3.42B against FY2026 unlevered adjusted free cash flow of about $228M ($122.4M plus $105.8M net interest) is a 6.7% unlevered cash yield, implying about 2.8% perpetual growth at a 9.5% cost of capital, which is undemanding for 4% organic growth but requires that two years of declining free cash flow stop.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Q1 FY2027 earnings document, late October 2026, the first with Saxoprint consolidated and the first sign of whether the sale-leaseback executes. The FY2027 full year, late July 2027, is the real datapoint: adjusted free cash flow either recovers as start-up costs roll off or the decline is structural.

## 8. Risks and what would prove the thesis wrong (pre-registered kill criteria)
1. Adjusted free cash flow below $150M for FY2027, a third consecutive annual decline, in the Q4 FY2027 earnings document.
2. Organic constant-currency revenue growth below 3% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the AI-search customer-acquisition risk the company added as a distinct risk factor (10-K, Item 1A).
3. Cost of revenue above 54.5% of revenue for two consecutive quarters, marking gross margin erosion as structural rather than mix.
4. Company-defined net leverage above 3.5x at any fiscal quarter end, or the $200M authorisation untouched at FY2027 year end with net debt no lower.

## 9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary
**WATCH, conviction 2.** Cimpress is a better business than the screen row suggests and a more expensive stock. The screen ran FY2025 profits against a June 2026 balance sheet and counted only tangible capex, publishing 3.4% revenue growth and a 10.5% free cash flow yield; the 10-K filed 2026-08-07 shows revenue up 10% to $3,736.6M (4% organic constant-currency), operating income of $251.0M, and adjusted free cash flow of $122.4M, a 6.2% yield on the company's own definition, which correctly charges $67.0M of capitalised software. Corrected, you pay 17.7x normalised after-tax operating profit for the genuine global leader in web-to-print mass customisation, run by a founder who owns 8.8% and thinks explicitly in intrinsic value per share, but whose cost of revenue has climbed from 51.5% to 53.7% of sales in two years, whose adjusted free cash flow has more than halved from $261M since FY2024, whose fourth-quarter operating income and adjusted EBITDA both slipped year over year despite 9% revenue growth, and who has just spent €120M on a 10%-margin German printer at roughly 13x EBITDA while promising to cut 3.5x leverage. A probability-weighted value near $87 against $81.50 does not pay for a bear case that leverage makes worth $25. Revisit if FY2027 adjusted free cash flow exceeds $190M with net leverage below 2.75x, or near $60.
