Driver is acquisition volume, not pricing: $273.0M invested in 2025 and $172.1M year to date at a 7.6% initial cash yield, plus 1.8% contractual escalators (10-K FY2025, Item 1; Q2 2026 supplement, p.31).
- **Normalised after-tax operating profit.** FY2025 GAAP operating income $119.0M (revenue $221.7M less property costs $8.7M, impairments $2.8M, environmental $2.0M, G&A $27.3M, D&A $61.9M), excluding $7.8M of disposition gains. A REIT distributing over 90% of taxable income pays almost no corporate tax, so the desk's 25% notional tax ($91.4M) understates it. GAAP net earnings $79.2M.
- **EV / normalised after-tax profit.** EV $3.07B (61.93M shares at $32.27, plus $1,073.0M debt at 6/30/26, less $4.8M cash; 10-Q Q2 2026) over $91.4M is 33.6x, or 25.2x untaxed. Both are near meaningless when $61.9M of the charge is D&A on land and buildings. The usable figure is EV / credit-agreement EBITDA of $202.9M, 15.1x (Q2 2026 supplement, p.34).
- **REIT lane measures.** P/FFO 13.8x on FY2025 diluted FFO of $2.34; P/AFFO 12.8x on 2026 guidance of $2.52-$2.54 (Q2 2026 8-K). - **What the screen got wrong.** Its FFO of $140.8M is net income to common plus D&A plus impairments and never deducts the $7.8M of disposition gains NAREIT requires, so true FY2025 FFO is $136.2M. It then divides FY2025 FFO earned on 56.5M weighted shares by a market cap struck on 61.9M current shares, flattering P/FFO by roughly a full turn. Its net debt/EBIT of 8.4x is not a REIT metric and bears no relation to the company's 5.3x net debt/EBITDA.

## 1. What the business actually does
Getty owns the land and buildings under convenience stores, express tunnel car washes, auto service centers and drive-thru quick service restaurants. At 6/30/26 it held 1,224 freestanding properties in 46 states and Washington DC producing $234M of annualized base rent, 99.8% occupied, 10.3 year weighted average lease term (10-Q Q2 2026; Q2 2026 supplement, p.14). Leases are triple net, so tenants pay taxes, insurance, maintenance and environmental compliance. 84% of ABR sits in 66 unitary or master leases and 75% carries site-level financial reporting, which is how Getty knows tenant rent coverage is 2.5x. Thirty-one employees run it (10-K FY2025, Item 1).

## 2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case
There is none I can defend, which caps this at WATCH. Getty is NYSE listed, Fitch rated, analyst covered and 37% owned by BlackRock, Vanguard and State Street (DEF 14A 2026). No spin-off, no orphaning, no forced seller, and the stock sits 11.9% off its 52-week high after a positive 12-month return. The screen's 14.2x P/FFO looked cheap only because of the share-count mismatch above. The residual argument, that the market still prices Getty as a gas-station landlord while car wash, auto service and QSR have become a large share of ABR (Q2 2026 supplement, p.9), is a story management has told for years and is already in the price.

## 3. Unit economics and growth
This is a spread business. Getty buys at a 7.6% initial cash yield and funds with debt at 5.76% (January 2026 Series U notes, 10-K FY2025, Item 7) and equity whose AFFO yield at $32.27 is 7.84%. On a 40/60 blend the cost of capital is about 7.0%, so the spread is roughly 60 basis points, about $1M a year on $172M deployed, under two cents a share. Escalators add 1.8%. That is the whole algorithm, and it is why AFFO per share compounded 4.0% from $2.08 in 2021 to $2.43 in 2025 (DEF 14A 2026, pay versus performance) while the portfolio grew from 945 sites and $117M of ABR at end-2019 to 1,224 and $234M. Retained cash is the cheap capital: AFFO of $2.53 against a $1.94 dividend leaves about $37M a year. Concentration is the offset to the quality: the top ten tenants are 58% of ABR, with ARKO 10.9%, Global Partners 9.6% and United Pacific 7.5% (Q2 2026 supplement, p.28), levered fuel distributors whose own credit is the real risk.

## 4. Balance sheet and capital allocation
$1,073.0M of debt at 6/30/26, 4.53% weighted average rate on the notes, 5.5 year average maturity, nothing due until $100M in June 2028 (10-Q Q2 2026; supplement p.34). All assets unencumbered, revolver drawn $73M of $450M, liquidity over $570M including $191M of unsettled forward equity. Capital allocation dilutes by design: diluted shares rose from 55.6M to 60.7M year over year with 5.8M more already sold forward. Insiders hold 7.5% as a group, concentrated in two long-tenured directors, Howard Safenowitz 3.9% and Milton Cooper 2.4% (DEF 14A 2026). Form 4s show zero open-market buys or sales in 12 months, only grants and a gift, so no insider signal either way (Form 4 summary). Pay is measured on AFFO per share and net debt/EBITDA, and say-on-pay passed with 95.6%.

## 5. Management: said versus did
Guidance went from $2.50-$2.52 to $2.52-$2.54 at Q2 (Q2 2026 8-K). The CEO led with "5% year-over-year growth in AFFO per share" rather than the 14.5% dollar figure, which is honest framing. The dividend rose 3.2% to $1.94 annualized, a twelfth consecutive increase (DEF 14A 2026). Against that, five-year total shareholder return of $133.54 per $100 trailed the peer group's $146.52. Two finance departures in six months are worth noting: the COO retired, costing $3.1M of H1 severance (10-Q Q2 2026), and the Chief Accounting Officer left on 14 August with $300,000 and accelerated RSUs, the company stating it was not the result of any disagreement over accounting or disclosure (8-K 2026-08-03; 8-K/A 2026-08-20).

## 6. Valuation
Base: AFFO of $2.53 growing 4%, held at 13x, gives about $34 in a year plus $1.94 of dividends, a 10% total return. Bear: rates back up or a top-three tenant restructures, the multiple compresses to 11x on flat AFFO, $27.8 plus the dividend, a 14% drawdown. Bull: forward equity settles into 8%-plus deals, 2027 AFFO reaches $2.64 at 14.5x, about $38, a 24% return. Weighted 55/25/20 that is roughly $33.50 against $32.27, fair value rather than a bargain. Reverse DCF: at $32.27 with a forward dividend near $2.02 and a 9% required return, the price implies about 2.8% perpetual per-share growth, below the 4.0% delivered from 2021 to 2025 but above what the 1.8% escalators alone produce.

## 7. Catalysts and timeline
Settlement of the $190.5M of forward equity and deployment of the $95M committed pipeline over the next two to three quarters, which shows whether the spread holds. 2027 guidance in February 2027. A Fitch upgrade from BBB- would cut debt cost and widen the spread.

## 8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria
1. AFFO per diluted share growth below 3% year over year for two consecutive quarters (Q2 2026 was 5.1%).
2. Site-level tenant rent coverage below 2.0x, or occupancy below 98.5%, in the quarterly supplement (now 2.5x and 99.8%).
3. Any of ARKO, Global Partners or United Pacific, together 28.0% of ABR, misses rent, is restructured, or is re-leased at lower rent.
4. Reported net debt/EBITDA above 6.0x on a non-pro-forma basis, or new investment yields below 7.0% while the shares trade under $33, taking the spread to zero.
Environmental liability is a permanent tail: $15.9M accrued for remediation plus $5.6M of litigation accruals at year-end 2025, with the Lower Passaic River and MTBE matters flagged as potentially material and the company saying actual net expenditures "could be substantially higher than amounts currently recorded" (10-K FY2025, Item 7). H1 2026 GAAP net earnings of $49.2M were also helped by a $7.7M non-cash release of unknown environmental reserves, which AFFO correctly excludes.

## 9. Verdict and summary
WATCH, conviction 3. Getty is a genuinely good small-cap net lease REIT, 99.8% occupied with 2.5x tenant rent coverage, a 10.3 year lease term, no debt maturity until 2028 and twelve straight years of dividend increases, and at $32.27 it yields 6.0% on a 77% AFFO payout at 12.8x guided 2026 AFFO. What it is not is mispriced: the screen ranked it cheap partly through an arithmetic error, comparing last year's FFO to this year's larger share count, and once corrected the multiple is ordinary for the quality. The business issues shares at a 7.8% AFFO yield to buy property at a 7.6% cash yield, so portfolio growth of 14% becomes per-share growth of 5%, and the price already implies about 2.8% perpetual growth. That is a fair deal, not an edge. It becomes an IDEA in the high $20s, where the yield is near 7% and the multiple near 11x with nothing about the portfolio changed, or if new investment yields move durably above 8% as the post-quarter deals at 8.2% hint they might.
