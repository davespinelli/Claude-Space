# INFO-3. Government contract awards nobody announced: pre-registration

Copied verbatim from research/infoedge/PREREG.md (written 2026-09-26, before any data was collected). The spec and the common rules are locked; anything the data forced is listed under "Deviations" below, written before any return was computed.

## Common rules (all five tests), as pre-registered

- **Point in time.** A signal is usable only from the first trading day after the date it became public. For a registry or database that publishes with a delay, that is the publication date, not the event date. If the publication date is unknown, use a conservative assumed lag, stated in Deviations before any return is computed.
- **Returns.**
  - Measure: the stock's return minus the return of a size-and-industry benchmark. If no benchmark can be built, use IWM for companies under $2B and SPY for the rest.
  - Source: daily prices from Yahoo (throttled; empty results can mean rate limiting). Record every company excluded for lack of prices, and run the -30% / +15% bounds for missing companies as in research/oplev.
- **Costs.** Report results after 0.5% a year, or 0.2% per event for event studies.
- **Verdict bar: t >= 2.5**, not 2.0. We are testing five ideas at once; at 2.0, one of them would pass by luck about a fifth of the time. A result must also have the pre-registered sign in both halves of the sample to be a Yes. Anything else is No; report it regardless.
- **Data forcing a change.** Record it in a "Deviations" section of that test's own PREREG before computing any return. If a test is infeasible (the data don't exist, or can't be dated), say so and stop. Do not swap in a different idea.
- **Shared rate limits:**
  - SEC EDGAR: at most 2 requests a second per test (five tests share one IP and SEC's 10-a-second limit), User-Agent "Claude Space research dspinjr@gmail.com".
  - Yahoo: throttle and retry.
- **Output.** research/infoedge/<test>/ holds scripts, data/ (small cleaned files), cache/ (gitignored) and RESULTS.md, with the plain-English answer first and the style of research/oplev/RESULTS.md.

## INFO-3. Government contract awards nobody announced (as pre-registered)

- **Why the market might not see it:** federal contract awards appear on USAspending/FPDS, but many small contractors never announce them. An award that is large relative to a small company's market value is material news sitting in a government database.
- **Events:**
  - new prime awards, or modifications, of at least 2% of the recipient's market cap;
  - recipient mapped to a US-listed parent under $2B (through UEI/DUNS parent names plus an alias list);
  - no 8-K from the company within 5 trading days either side.
- **Public date:** civilian agencies' action date + 5 business days; DoD awards action date + 90 days, because DoD data is held back. If the actual publication date can be found in FPDS/USAspending fields, use it instead, and record which in Deviations.
- **Primary:** abnormal return over trading days +1 to +20 after the public date. Expected sign: positive. Same bar.
- **Secondary:** awards of 5% or more of market cap; civilian only.

## Deviations and implementation choices

Written before any return was computed (frozen 2026-09-26 before stage s7 ran). Items marked **(deviation)** change or fill in something the spec left to the data; the rest pin down details the spec does not state.

### Sources and sample

- **S1. Award data.** USAspending.gov API v2 (no key). Contract award types A-D (BPA calls, purchase orders, delivery orders, definitive contracts); IDVs themselves are excluded (they carry no obligations of their own). Each transaction is one FPDS contract action (a base award or a modification); its size is `federal_action_obligation`; only positive obligations can be events. Action dates from 2009-10-01 (FY2010) onward; the last usable events are those whose day +20 falls on or before the last Yahoo price (2026-09-25).
- **S2. Retrieval (deviation, forced by the API).** The API's recipient search is a case-insensitive substring match on recipient and parent names (punctuation literal: "&" is not "AND"), and it cannot filter by transaction size. Transactions are therefore pulled company by company with the custom transaction download, filtered to awards whose total obligation is >= $250,000 (a single action below $250,000 can reach 2% of market cap only for a company worth under $12.5M). Search phrase per company name: for the first ~1,300 names, the first word of the SEC name when it is 4+ characters, not a common word, and matched 1-30,000 transactions in a count query; for all other names the SEC name without state tags, legal-form words and a trailing HOLDING(S)/GROUP (e.g. "KRATOS DEFENSE & SECURITY SOLUTIONS", "DLH HOLDINGS", "ENGILITY"). The count scan was abandoned part-way because the API dropped most connections at more than about 2 requests a second; names whose first word had 0 hits in the partial scan were skipped (the full name contains the first word). Up to 12 phrases per download request (25-phrase requests failed server-side).
- **S3. Scope, "real federal contractors" (deviation: the brief asked to focus).** Candidates are every CIK SEC lists today on Nasdaq, NYSE or CBOE, plus every operating company in research/oplev's XBRL universe that has since stopped trading (historical symbol from its XBRL files). Excluded: OTC names (not US-listed), SIC 6000-6999 (finance, insurance, real estate, blank checks), SIC 0100-1499 (agriculture, mining, oil and gas production), 2000-2199 (food, tobacco), 5200-5999 (retail), 7000-7299 (hotels, personal services), 7500-7999 (auto repair, film, recreation), entities with no SIC code (funds, trusts), and companies whose public float was >= $2B at every XBRL cover date 2009-2025. Everything else (construction, manufacturing incl. pharma/biotech, defence electronics, transport, communications, utilities and environmental services, wholesale, IT and business services, health services, education, engineering, research and management services) is in. The 2% test then keeps only companies for which federal awards are material.
- **S4. Dating fields verified.** USAspending carries `action_date` (FPDS date signed) and `last_modified_date`; the latter is rewritten by mass updates (e.g. 2021-12 UEI conversion) and is not a publication date. FPDS (public ATOM feed) carries `createdDate`, `approvedDate` and `lastModifiedDate` per action; spot checks show `approvedDate` stays at the original approval (0-5 days after signing) even when the record was later mass-modified.

### Public date

- **D1. DoD hold-back confirmed.** On 2026-09-26 USAspending held 15,293 DoD contract actions dated 2026-06-25 and 11,389 dated 2026-06-26, but 0 dated 2026-06-27 to 06-29 and a handful per day after (2026-09-26 minus 90 days = 2026-06-28; USAspending loads a day or two behind). The 90-day delay is keyed on the action date, so the pre-registered DoD rule is kept: public date = action date + 90 calendar days. "DoD" = awarding agency Department of Defense.
- **D2. Civilian: actual date used (as the spec asks when the fields show it).** Civilian records are public in FPDS when approved and on USAspending after the next nightly load (on 2026-09-26, VA and HHS actions dated 2026-09-24 were already at normal daily counts). So for civilian actions, public date = FPDS `approvedDate` (never earlier than the action date) + 1 federal business day. Where the FPDS record cannot be found, the pre-registered rule applies (action date + 5 federal business days). Why this matters: in a first look at 946 civilian candidate actions (10 companies, dates only, no returns), the median approval came the same day as signing, but 24% were approved more than 7 days later, 14% more than 30 days and 5% more than 90 days; 115 of the 136 approved 30+ days late were also *created* late, i.e. genuinely late reporting, not corrections. The pre-registered +5 business days would have used those awards before they were public. A record re-approved after a correction gets a later date than its first release; that errs late (no look-ahead).
- **D3. DoD late entries.** A DoD action entered into FPDS more than 90 days after signing is public when entered: public date = max(action date + 90 days, `approvedDate` + 1 business day) where the FPDS record was looked up.
- **D4. Day 0** = the last trading day on or before the public date; day +1 is the first trading day after it (the common point-in-time rule).

### Mapping to listed companies

- **M1.** Key = each transaction's parent recipient name (FPDS ultimate parent as carried on USAspending). Verified that the parent *identity* is point in time (Danya International shows itself as parent until DLH bought it in 2016, DLH Holdings after), but USAspending labels every UEI/DUNS with its *current* name (a registrant renamed later, e.g. TeamStaff to DLH, shows the new name on its old rows; the recipient-name column is likewise the current name).
- **M2.** Alias list = every current and former SEC name of every candidate (all industries, so a parent name that belongs to a large or financial company is not handed to a small one). Matching, after dropping legal-form words: (1) exact; (2) the company name is the leading part of the parent name, and either the company name has 3+ words and is distinctive, or every extra word of the parent name is a generic corporate word (e.g. VECTRUS SYSTEMS, ENGILITY SERVICES; not APOLLO MANAGEMENT for Apollo Education); a prefix match also needs the company to appear under its own exact name within three years (e.g. VERTEX AEROSPACE is not Vertex, Inc.); (3) hand aliases (data/hand_aliases.csv); (4) UEI/DUNS links: an identifier seen in name-matched rows of company X is mapped to X for its other rows, only between the first date the link was seen and one year after the last. A *former* SEC name matches only from one year before it was adopted to two years after it was dropped (a name later reused by another entity, e.g. "AMR", is not handed to the old registrant); current names match at any date (see M1).
- **M3.** Because identifiers carry their current names, a row whose parent is the recipient itself (or blank) is mapped only by an exact name match or a hand alias (otherwise a subsidiary renamed after its acquirer would hand pre-acquisition awards to the acquirer).
- **M3b.** Foreign private issuers (20-F/40-F filers without 10-Ks) are excluded, as in research/oplev: SEC share counts are ordinary shares while Yahoo often prices an ADS.
- **M4.** The company must be alive at SEC on the action date (first filing on or before it, last 10-K/10-Q no more than 120 days before it). Several registrants with one name: the one alive on the action date, then a listed one, then the one that filed most recently.
- **M5.** Precision is measured on 50 randomly drawn candidate actions (seed 20260926), checked by hand (recipient owned by the mapped company on the action date): 50 of 50 correct, all exact-name matches. Separately, all ~160 parent-to-company pairs behind candidate actions were read; four parent names clearly belonging to someone else were blocked by full name before any return was computed (GRAHAM GROUP LTD, INNOVATE! INC, THE MILLENNIUM GROUP INTERNATIONAL LLC, ITT CORPORATION after the 2011 split; data/hand_aliases.csv). Known errors left in: single events where a parent label is stale or a SPAC is matched before its merger (e.g. CVR Energy/Wynnewood 2011, Southland 2022).

### Size, market cap, events

- **E1. Market cap** = Yahoo split-adjusted close on day 0 (or the last close within the preceding 7 days) x SEC share count, both on today's split basis (Yahoo split records after the count's as-of date), as in research/oplev. Share count: latest dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding filed on or before day 0 and dated within 400 days; else us-gaap:CommonStockSharesOutstanding, else (e.g. multi-class companies, whose cover and balance-sheet counts are tagged by class) us-gaap:WeightedAverageNumberOfSharesOutstandingBasic for the latest period filed; when the first two disagree by more than 3x, the one whose implied cap is 0.8-20x the latest public float is used. **(Deviation)** XBRL cover data start in 2009-2011, so before a company's first XBRL filing the first count filed within the following 18 months is used (flagged; a slight look-ahead in the size screen only, not in returns). Public-float facts more than 20x away from the company's median float are dropped as scale errors (e.g. Kratos reported $1.0 trillion for 2018). A market cap of zero, or below 30% of the company's own latest public float, is treated as a share-count error and that action is dropped.
- **E2. Events.** Action >= 2% of market cap on day 0 and market cap < $2B. Several qualifying actions of one company with the same public date form one event (amounts summed). The size screen uses day 0 because that is what an investor could know.
- **E3. "No 8-K"** = no 8-K or 8-K/A (any item) filed by the company from 5 trading days before to 5 trading days after the action date (EDGAR submissions JSON, all pages). Announced events are reported separately.
- **E4. Overlap.** Within each test, an event whose day 0 is within 20 trading days after the day 0 of the same company's previous kept event is dropped, so no stock-day is counted twice for one company.
- **E0. Pre-screen (speed only).** A mapped company whose largest award is below 2% of its smallest public float reported on any XBRL cover page 2009-2025 is not priced or dated (market value is at least the float, so it cannot qualify unless its value fell below its lowest reported float).
- **E5. Missing companies.** A mapped company with no usable Yahoo price (acquired or delisted; or its old symbol now belongs to another company) is screened on its public float in place of market cap. Its events enter only the survivorship bounds.

### Returns and statistics

- **R1. Benchmark (deviation).** No size-and-industry benchmark is built (it needs daily returns for every small cap by industry); all event companies are under $2B, so IWM is used, the pre-registered fallback. Abnormal return = stock total return (Yahoo adjusted close) minus IWM total return; CAR = sum over trading days +1..+20. A buy-and-hold version is reported for information.
- **R2. Verdict statistic.** Mean CAR after 0.2% cost per event; t = that mean over its standard error clustered by calendar month of the public date (CR1). Yes only if t >= 2.5 and the mean after cost is positive in both halves; halves split at the median public date of the sample.
- **R3. Price data rule (as research/oplev, applied to daily returns).** Non-positive prices invalid; a one-day round trip of 10x or more (<= -90% then >= +900%, or the reverse) removes both days; any other daily return above +1,000% is removed. A price gap of up to 5 trading days is carried over (thinly traded stocks).
- **R4. Survivorship bounds.** As in research/oplev: every missing-company event is given a CAR of -30% or +15%, and a priced event whose price series stops inside the window gets -30% / +15% added. Because -30% over 20 trading days is far harsher than oplev's -30% over a year, the same bounds scaled to 20 trading days (-2.8% / +1.1%) are also shown, for information.
- **R5. Required robustness lines (added at the coordinator's request before any return was computed; not part of the verdict).** (a) Placebo: for each event, 5 random trading days of the same company in the same calendar year, at least 20 trading days from any of its event days, with a price that day; placebo CAR computed exactly like the event CAR; reported: mean placebo CAR and the event-minus-placebo difference with its clustered t. (b) The headline measured against IWM only: this is already the headline, since no size-and-industry benchmark was built (R1).

### Post-run corrections and additions (made after the first s7 run; none touches the verdict statistic)

- **C1 (bug fix).** In the first run, missing-company events entering the survivorship bounds had not been put through the same-company overlap rule (E4); the rule now applies to them too. This changes only the bound lines (it cut the primary's missing events from 1,392 to 810).
- **C2 (information only).** Added after seeing the first results: the placebo line for the DoD-only and size-split samples, a split at $50M market cap, a company-clustered t, and a 1%-winsorised mean. They are descriptive and do not enter the verdict.
