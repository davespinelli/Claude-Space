# INFO-1. Hidden customer links: pre-registration (copy)

Copied verbatim from research/infoedge/PREREG.md (written 2026-09-26, before any data was collected) on 2026-09-26, before any return was computed. The spec below is locked. Everything the data forced is recorded under "Deviations" at the end, also before any return was computed.

## Common rules (all five tests, locked)

- **Point in time.** A signal is usable only from the first trading day after the date it became public. For a registry or database that publishes with a delay, that is the publication date, not the event date. If the publication date is unknown, use a conservative assumed lag, stated in Deviations before any return is computed.
- **Returns.**
  - Measure: the stock's return minus the return of a size-and-industry benchmark. If no benchmark can be built, use IWM for companies under $2B and SPY for the rest.
  - Source: daily prices from Yahoo (throttled; empty results can mean rate limiting). Record every company excluded for lack of prices, and run the -30% / +15% bounds for missing companies as in research/oplev.
- **Costs.** Report results after 0.5% a year, or 0.2% per event for event studies.
- **Verdict bar: t >= 2.5**, not 2.0. We are testing five ideas at once; at 2.0, one of them would pass by luck about a fifth of the time. A result must also have the pre-registered sign in both halves of the sample to be a Yes. Anything else is No; report it regardless.
- **Data forcing a change.** Record it in a "Deviations" section of that test's own PREREG before computing any return. If a test is infeasible (the data don't exist, or can't be dated), say so and stop. Do not swap in a different idea.
- **Shared rate limits:**
  - SEC EDGAR: at most 2 requests a second per test (five tests share one IP and SEC's 10-a-second limit), User-Agent "Claude Space research dspinjr@gmail.com".
  - Yahoo: throttle and retry.
- **Output.** research/infoedge/<test>/ holds scripts, data/ (small cleaned files), cache/ (gitignored) and RESULTS.md, with the plain-English answer first and the style of research/oplev/RESULTS.md.

## INFO-1. Hidden customer links (locked spec)

- **Why the market might not see it:** a small supplier's big customer is named only in its 10-K text ("Walmart accounted for 23% of net sales"). When the customer's stock moves on news, investors in the supplier are slow to connect the two (Cohen and Frazzini's "economic links"). Small caps whose links exist only in text are where the delay should be longest.
- **Links:**
  - From every 10-K filed 2010 onward, extract named customers at 10% or more of revenue, using full-text search plus parsing.
  - Map customer names to listed companies with an alias list of large US-listed companies.
  - A link is active from the 10-K's filing date until the next 10-K.
- **Signal:** the month-t return of the supplier's linked customers (average if several).
- **Test:**
  - Each month-end, rank suppliers with at least one listed customer by that signal.
  - Take suppliers in the top fifth minus those in the bottom fifth, equal-weighted, and measure the spread in month t+1.
  - Primary statistic: NW(6) t >= 2.5, with the same sign in both halves.
  - Secondary: suppliers under $2B only; value-weighted; links where the customer share is 20% or more.


## Deviations

Written 2026-09-26 while building the link data, and frozen before `run_test.py` computed its first return. Each item says what the data forced and what was done instead. None of them changes the test itself (signal, sort, holding month, statistic, bar).

1. **Pre-registered sign.** The spec asks for "the same sign in both halves" without naming the sign. The rationale (Cohen and Frazzini: supplier prices catch up late with customer news) implies a positive spread, top fifth (customers rose most) minus bottom fifth. The sign is taken as **positive**; a negative spread of any size is a No.
2. **How 10-Ks are found (full-text search limits).** EDGAR full-text search cannot search for numbers or "%", ignores proximity operators, and parses OR/AND strictly left to right (no brackets), so "(Walmart OR Wal-Mart) AND (largest customer OR ...)" cannot be written. Fetching every 10-K since 2010 (about 110,000 main documents) at 2 requests a second is not feasible alongside four other tests. So the search is done in two parts:
   - *Universe and discovery.* One OR-query of ten concentration phrases ("customer accounted", "customers accounted", "largest customer(s)", "major customer(s)", "significant customer(s)", "one customer", "two customers") over all 10-K forms filed 2010-01-01 to 2026-09-25 gives 53,460 original 10-K documents (main document or EX-13, HTML/text). A random sample of 1,496 of them (88 per filing year, seed 11) was fetched and scanned for named customers; the most frequent names that are large US-listed companies were added to the brief's starter list to form the alias list (`aliases.py`, `data/discovery_names.csv`).
   - *Link search.* For every alias-list company (420 entries), OR-queries of phrase templates built from each spelling ("sales to X", "revenue(s) from X", "X accounted", "X represented", "X Inc accounted", "X Corporation and its", "largest customer X", ... 38 templates) over all 10-K forms filed 2010-01-01 to 2026-09-25. A random discovery sample doubles as a recall check: of its 10-Ks in which the parser found a link, 83% had also been found by the template search. The misses were phrasings such as "one customer, BP plc, accounted", "Texas Instruments Incorporated accounted" and "Cisco ... generated", so a second template pass was added before any return was computed ("customer(s) X", "client(s) X", "purchaser(s) X", "X Incorporated accounted", "X who accounted", "X which represented", "X generated", "X contributed", "X plc accounted/and"; skipped for spellings that are ordinary words: Target, Shell, Sprint). After the second pass, 128 of the 141 discovery 10-Ks with a link (91%) are found by the template search. Every matched original 10-K document (main document or EX-13) from both passes plus the discovery documents (19,664 documents in all) is fetched and parsed.
3. **Documents.** Original 10-K and 10-KT filings only (10-K/A amendments excluded, since they mostly restate Part III and would re-date old links); the main document or the EX-13 annual-report exhibit; HTML or text (PDF exhibits skipped).
4. **Extraction rules** (fixed in `extract.py` and `build_links.py` before any return was computed; one rule, the last-year check below, was tightened after the first 50-link precision sample and a fresh sample was then drawn, see item 15):
   - the customer name must be in a customer role: preceded by "sales to", "revenue(s) from", "sold to", "our largest customer(s)," etc., or followed (after "Inc.", a parenthetical, "and its affiliates", or other names in a list) by "accounted for", "represented", "comprised", "was our largest customer", etc.;
   - names in a supplier role are skipped: "sales of products from IBM", "purchased from our largest vendor, Motorola", "a sub-contractor for Cisco";
   - the percentage must be a share of the filer's total sales or revenue ("25%, 23% and 20% of our net sales", "of Tredegar's consolidated net sales" when the possessive is the filer's own name), not of receivables, purchases, a segment, a division, a product line ("of our sales of hospitality systems") or another entity ("of Fresh Dairy Direct's net sales"); a sentence that names a segment, division or subsidiary before the customer is read as a segment share and skipped; a table under a segment, tenant or rent header is skipped;
   - "less than 10%" counts as below the threshold and keeps its place in a list;
   - lists are matched by position ("A, B and C accounted for 20%, 15% and 12%, respectively"), counting non-alias names in the list; a single share for several names ("A and B together accounted for 35%") is not a link, because neither name is known to be at 10% or more;
   - the share for the most recent fiscal year is used (the first in the list, or the last when the years run oldest-first); when a filing mentions a customer several times, the most frequent share among the mentions about its latest fiscal year is used; a filing whose latest mention of the customer is about an earlier fiscal year than its own ("For 2020, Google accounted for 10%" in the FY2021 10-K) is not a current link, because the customer has usually dropped below 10% (one year of slack for fiscal years ending January to March, which often carry the prior year's name);
   - table rows are read when the first cell is a customer name and the nearest header above is about sales or revenue (not receivables);
   - a link needs a share of 10% or more (the spec's threshold); a supplier that names itself (e.g. Walmart's own 10-K) is dropped.
5. **Alias list scope.** "Large US-listed companies" is read as companies with a US exchange listing, including US-listed ADRs (Toyota, Sony, Nokia, ASML, TSMC, BP, Shell, ...). Obvious wholly owned subsidiaries map to the listed parent (Sam's Club to Walmart, Caremark to CVS, Optum to UnitedHealth, Pratt & Whitney to RTX, Gulfstream to General Dynamics, ...). Where a name refers to a different listed company before a merger, the mapping has a date window (e.g. Raytheon Co. before its 2020 merger, Express Scripts before 2019, Whole Foods before Amazon bought it, T-Mobile before its 2013 listing, DirecTV before 2015). Governments, unnamed customers ("Customer A", "a distributor"), private companies and companies listed only abroad or over the counter (Samsung, Foxconn, Huawei, Airbus, Nissan) are no link, as the spec says.
6. **Customers without a price.** Yahoo has no history for most delisted companies. A link to a customer that has no Yahoo series in month t (Walgreens, Sears, Rite Aid, J.C. Penney, Express Scripts and Raytheon Co. before their mergers, EMC, Dell 2013-2016, Tech Data, Juniper, Nordstrom, ...) cannot produce a signal that month and is dropped from the average; a supplier whose only customers are unpriced is not ranked. The dropped link-months are counted in RESULTS.md.
7. **Link timing (point in time).** A link enters the ranking made at the end of month t only if its 10-K was filed strictly before the last weekday of month t (so it was public at least one trading day before the portfolio is formed). It stays active until the supplier's next original 10-K is filed (whether or not that one names the customer). If no later 10-K appears, the link lapses 15 months after its filing date (a 10-K is due within 12 months plus extensions); without this cap, links of companies that stopped filing would never end.
8. **Prices.** Yahoo monthly bars (adjusted close) are used instead of daily prices. For a monthly-rebalanced test the monthly adjusted-close return equals the compounded daily returns, and the research/oplev cache already holds them. Supplier returns go through the research/oplev price-error rule (`clean_returns`: 10x round trips and monthly returns above +1,000% set missing). The last complete month is August 2026, so holding months run from February 2010 to August 2026.
9. **Size-and-industry benchmark.** Each supplier's monthly return minus the equal-weighted return, that month, of research/oplev universe firms (non-financial, non-utility, revenue > $10M, market cap >= $50M, priced) in the same June size quintile and Fama-French 12 industry, with the supplier itself left out and at least 5 firms in the cell. The benchmark falls back to IWM (supplier under $2B or of unknown size) or SPY (the rest) when no cell can be built: months before July 2011 (the first oplev formation), financial or utility suppliers, suppliers with no June market cap, and cells with fewer than 5 firms. The raw (unadjusted) spread is also reported.
10. **Market capitalisation.** A supplier's market cap is its research/oplev June market cap (validated share count times June price) rolled forward month by month with its split-adjusted price, the June 2011 value also serving January 2010 to June 2011. Suppliers without an oplev market cap have unknown size: they stay in the primary test but are left out of the under-$2B and value-weighted secondaries.
11. **Ranked set and missing companies.** A supplier is ranked at the end of month t if it has a signal and a Yahoo price that month; the quintile breakpoints come from the ranked suppliers. Months with fewer than 25 ranked suppliers are skipped. For the -30% / +15% bounds (as in research/oplev), suppliers with a signal but no Yahoo price are placed in quintiles with the same breakpoints and earn -30% (or +15%) a year, spread evenly over the months; a ranked supplier whose price series stops earns -30% (or +15%) in the month after its last price.
12. **Halves.** The holding months are split into two halves of equal length by time; the spread must be positive in each.
13. **Costs.** 0.5% a year, charged as 0.5%/12 each month on the long-short spread. The verdict uses the spread after this cost.
14. **Supplier tickers.** The SEC's current ticker list first; otherwise research/oplev's ticker maps (current or the symbol recovered from old XBRL file names), used only when the symbol is not currently assigned to another company. Suppliers left without a price are listed in `data/excluded_suppliers_no_price.csv`.
15. **Extraction precision check (the brief's 50 random links), before any return.** Round 1 (seed 2026, `data/precision_labels_round1.csv`): 50 of 50 named companies were real customers, but only 40 of 50 shares were the latest fiscal year's share of total revenue; 8 of the 10 misses were filings that quoted a customer's share for the *prior* year only (the customer had fallen below 10%), which the original rule (drop only when two or more years old) let through. The rule was tightened as in item 4 (805 filing-customer pairs now dropped as stale instead of 316) and a fresh sample drawn. Round 2 (seed 2027, `data/precision_labels.csv`, the reported figure): 48 of 50 fully correct (96%); 49 of 50 real customers (one supplier read as a customer: "products sourced through HP"), and one share was a quarter's share rather than the year's.
16. **Frozen.** This file was last edited before `run_test.py` computed any return on the real links (it had only been run on synthetic, randomly assigned links to test the code).
