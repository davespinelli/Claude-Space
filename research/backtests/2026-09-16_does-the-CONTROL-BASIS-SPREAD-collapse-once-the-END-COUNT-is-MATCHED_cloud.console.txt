## (C2) THE EXACT SPREAD AT K = 1 — is the residual 0.03 a basis fact or the sampler?
   At K = 1 all three bases ARE the same 18-point pool (G8 above proves FIXED(1) and
   RAW(1) are bit-identical).  SEQ(1) is that same pool drawn WITH REPLACEMENT 2,000
   times, so its EXACT distribution is the pool itself.  Scoring SEQ(1) exactly instead
   of by Monte Carlo therefore makes the K=1 spread zero BY IDENTITY; the gap between
   that and the measured spread is the estimator's own noise, and is reported as such.
  wrote 2026-09-16_does-the-CONTROL-BASIS-SPREAD-collapse-once-the-END-COUNT-is-MATCHED_cloud.exact_k1.csv  (14 rows, 3 cols)
   EXACT K=1 across-basis spread: 0.0000 at every gap (identity).
   MC SEQ(1) vs exact, per (gap, cell): max 0.1521, median 0.0060.  The max is NOT binomial noise — a binomial SE is
   0.0112 and this is 13.6 of them.  It is ATOM CROSSING: an 18-point pool moves in steps of
   1/18 = 0.0556, so a mean shifted by the sampler's own 0.0112 can step a whole atom.  The
   published statistic in (C) is the MEDIAN over the 6 cells, which is robust to that (0.0060).
   So the 0.0315 residual H_K1 fails on is the ESTIMATOR reading a 18-atom pool at a
   quantisation boundary — it sits at gap +0.000, where the shifted point lands inside an
   atom and the mid-rank tie split is maximally sensitive — and NOT a disagreement between
   the bases, which at K = 1 are the same object by G8 and by identity.
