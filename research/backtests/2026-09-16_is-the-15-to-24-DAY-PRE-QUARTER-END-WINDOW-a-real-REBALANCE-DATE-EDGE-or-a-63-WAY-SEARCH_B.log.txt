  THE THREE INDEPENDENT REASONS THIS DIES (the decision rule above used only the first):
  1. NOT A BOOK EDGE. The Q-window premium is +0.620 pp/yr on U56 and +0.974 on SMALL but -0.239 on B136 -- it changes SIGN across
     panels, and on B136 a gross-matched coin flip does BETTER than the real books.
  2. IT LIVES ENTIRELY IN THE SECOND WINDOW. The literal d=20 rule beats its own family
     median on OOS CAGR in 30 of 30 cells (+1.648 pp/yr) but in only 4 of 30 IN SAMPLE (-0.945 pp/yr).
     A quarter-end flow effect that is absent 2009-2016 and present 2017-2026 is not a
     calendar mechanism; it is one window's luck, and rule 8 is what catches it.
  3. THE WINDOW IS NOT WHERE THE SHAPE IS. On the tradable grid the profile is not a peak
     at d=15..24 -- it is a TROUGH at the quarter-end itself (-1.531 pp/yr at d=0..8) with everything
     from d=15 to d=45 flat and mildly positive (+0.452 / +0.683 / +0.592 at 15-24 / 25-31 / 32-45).
     '15-24 days' is a 63-way search LOCALISING a broad, shallow 'do not rebalance AT
     quarter-end' effect, and H_SEARCH confirms the localisation is free: the best 10-wide
     window beats its own permutation null in 30 of 30 cells, i.e. every
     family has SOME good 10-wide window, and which one it is does not replicate.

  WHAT SURVIVES (reported, not promoted): the ONLY robust part of 961's calendar reading is
  the NEGATIVE end -- rebalancing within ~8 trading days of quarter-end costs -1.531 pp/yr
  of de-meaned CAGR, which is consistent across all three panels and is what makes the
  CANONICAL quarter-end date look bad (942's and 961's original observation). That is a
  statement about the date to AVOID, not a window to TARGET, and it moves no KEEP path:
  4a 0 of 30, 4b 0 of 30 on the rule-8 picks.
