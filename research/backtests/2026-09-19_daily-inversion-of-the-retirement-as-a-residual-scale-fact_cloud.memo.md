1. **The measurement.** Idea 539's corpus rebuilt from the tape: **1,296 real books** (SMALL439 / U56 / B136 x {QUANTILE, MA-THRESH} x 9 levels x cadence {D,W,M,Q} x gross {0.50,0.75,1.00} x {RESPREAD, DEGROSS}), **648 decomposition cells**, each on FULL / IS / OOS, both KEEP paths on every book. **Gates 9/9.** The premise replicates: CSD.is/FAMILY OOS MAE ratio on D-only cells reads **1.3099 / 1.3187 / 1.3372** at gross 0.50 / 0.75 / 1.00 against 539's committed 1.2297 / 1.2492 / 1.2710 — same sign, same size.
2. **The answer is CADENCE.** Pre-registered H_SCALE **FALSE**, H_CADENCE **TRUE**. In the pooled bivariate fit of `d_err = |e_CSD| - |e_FAM|` on log IS residual scale and a D-cadence dummy (gross dummies included), the D dummy holds **+0.0231, t +3.11**; on the pool-stable panels alone (U56 + B136, G8) **+0.0283, t +2.92**. Scale does not absorb it: corr(log IS scale, D dummy) is only **-0.1601**.
3. **The idea's own mechanism is refuted in its own direction.** Hold cadence fixed and split by residual scale: inside the **D-only** cells CSD.is is *better* than the family constant in the LOW-|resid| tercile (ratio **0.7569 / 0.6081 / 0.5412** at the three grosses) and *worse* in MID and HIGH (1.28-1.39 and 1.09-1.10). "The continuous predictor loses exactly where the residual is smallest" is **backwards** once cadence is held fixed.
4. **Pooled, the scale relationship is non-monotone, so no scale restatement is available.** Mean d_err across the three IS-scale terciles runs **-0.0020 / +0.0066 / -0.0218** (g 0.50), **-0.0087 / +0.0082 / -0.0342** (0.75), **-0.0163 / +0.0060 / -0.0454** (1.00): negative at both ends, positive in the middle. The retirement bar holds in the LOW and HIGH terciles at all three grosses and fails in the MID tercile at all three.
5. **The confound was measured, not assumed.** D cells are **37-40%** of the LOW tercile, 7-11% of the MID and 26-28% of the HIGH, against 25% under a cadence-blind split — which is why the marginal tables cannot settle the question and the controlled fit can. Total explanatory power is small either way: pooled R² 0.0322 against a gross-dummy baseline of 0.0041.
