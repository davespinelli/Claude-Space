============================================================================================================
ARM 0 — WHAT THE BAR DOES BEFORE ANY PRICE IS READ
============================================================================================================
  d2(k) MC at 1,500,000 draws reproduces Hartley to 9.663e-04.

  (0a) A PERFECTLY DEGENERATE LADDER FAILS THE CHEAPEST BAR THAT EXISTS.  Its rungs are
       the same book, so every bootstrap draw gives the same Sharpe at every rung: the
       recentred null range is 0 in EVERY rep AND the observed spread is 0.  R_obs > 0
       is FALSE, so A_POS already excludes it — and A_MED / A_90 / A_95 / A_99, which
       are strictly stronger, exclude it too.  THE EXPENSIVE BAR IS NOT NEEDED FOR THE
       CASE 1223 BUILT.  What the expensive bars can still do is exclude ladders whose
       spread is real but not distinguishable from their own sampling noise — GROSS,
       whose spread 1214 measured at 0.0011-0.0034, is the live candidate.

  (0b) THE BAR IS ALSO A DO-NOTHING DIAL, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE DIFFICULTY.  Excluding
       ladders shrinks the comparison set; a set of 0 or 1 ladder has no pair, so every
       headline form returns TIE and the chooser HOLDS THE ANCHOR.  The record has found
       five times that holding the anchor beats every chooser out of sample, so a bar
       that merely holds more often WILL look like it pays.  ARM D prices the bar
       against a null that moves exactly as often as it does (1227's NL_PERM).
