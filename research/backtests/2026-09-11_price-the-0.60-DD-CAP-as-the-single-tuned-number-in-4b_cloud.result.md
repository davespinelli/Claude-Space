## Is it really one number (the Calmar ratio)?

Both legs hold ⟹ Calmar_book/Calmar_SPY ≥ φ_CAGR/φ_DD. **G4 PASS: the implication holds on all
2,520 row × cell checks**, so idea 333's closed form is confirmed as an upper structure. But
the **converse fails on 35.4%** of them, and the pass surface does **not** collapse onto the
ratio: at ratio = 1.00 the five (φ_DD, φ_CAGR) cells that share it give pass counts **4 … 15**,
a 11-book spread (17 at ratio 0). **The protocol has two constants, not one**, and de-grossing
slides a book along its Calmar ray between them — which is exactly why the floor binds the
half-gross books and the cap binds the full-gross ones.

## The zero-signal control — the base rate of the two constants

SPY held at gross g passes **0 of 9 rows in all 48 cells**, including cap = ∞ and floor = 0. Its
DD_ratio and CAGR_ratio are g to three decimals (0.5365/0.5062, 0.7770/0.7550, 1.0000/1.0000),

constant. This is idea 502's 78.1% coin-flip base rate confronted with a control that is a
*scaled benchmark* rather than a random book, and the two answers are not in conflict: they
price different nulls.
