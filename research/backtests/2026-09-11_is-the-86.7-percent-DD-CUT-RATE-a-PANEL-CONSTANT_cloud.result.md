## Which leg decides 4a? Not the one the queue names.

Over the 747 arm-rows: P(clear Sharpe legs) = **8.0%** (60), P(pass DD leg) = **20.7%** (155),
P(4a) = **1.2%** (9).

* Under independence the two legs would give 747 × 0.0803 × 0.2075 = **12.4** passes; **9 observed** — a mild
  negative dependence (arms that beat the live book on Sharpe do tend to drawdown more), nothing like a
  deciding leg.
* Equivalently, the DD leg's **unconditional** failure rate is **79.3%**, so the headline "the DD leg cuts
  86.7% of Sharpe-clears" is only **5.7 pp** above what a DD leg that ignored the Sharpe legs entirely would
  produce. The 86.7% is mostly the DD leg's own base rate, not evidence of it deciding anything.
* The Sharpe legs are the **larger** filter in absolute terms: they reject 687 of 747 arm-rows on their own.
  They are not decorative, and PROTOCOL should not say they are.

## PROTOCOL wording PROPOSED for Sunday review (not applied)

The queue's conditional ("if it is a constant, 4a's Sharpe legs are close to decorative and PROTOCOL should
say so") **should not be actioned** — the corpus says the opposite. What the record could use instead:

> **4a (reporting, proposed).** Beside every 4a verdict, publish the leg counts on the arm population it was
> read over: P(Sharpe legs clear), P(MaxDD leg passes) and the pass count, so a "cut rate" is read against
> the DD leg's own unconditional failure rate rather than as evidence that one leg decides. A cut rate quoted
> without its panel's clear count is not interpretable: on U56 the clear count is **0 of 249**.

## Survivorship (PROTOCOL 9)

B136 is today's constituents and SMALL439 the current sub-$2B screen only (`data/SMALL_PANEL_README.md`).
The bias is not neutral for this statistic: survivor panels lift arms on **both** legs, and they lift the
Sharpe-clear count (the denominator) most on the panels whose live comparand is weakest — which is exactly
the cross-panel contrast under test. All levels on those two panels are upper bounds; U56, which carries the
mildest bias, is also the panel where the statistic has no observations at all.
