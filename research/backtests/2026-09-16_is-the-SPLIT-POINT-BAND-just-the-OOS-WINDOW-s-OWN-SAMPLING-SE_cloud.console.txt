## (D2) WHY THE NULL BAND EXCEEDS THE OBSERVED ONE — the caveat, measured
   The observed band lands BELOW the null's median, so the run must say why before it
   banks the answer.  Mechanism: every window shares the COMMON TAIL after the last end,
   and the tape's volatile episodes (2020, 2022) sit in that tail.  In the real path they
   are therefore in EVERY window and cannot move the band; a resample scatters them, so
   some land in the head that the later ends exclude and the null's windows differ more.
   The test: variance density in the common tail against the head each end drops.
  wrote 2026-09-16_is-the-SPLIT-POINT-BAND-just-the-OOS-WINDOW-s-OWN-SAMPLING-SE_cloud.tailvar.csv  (108 rows, 8 cols)
   variance density, common tail vs excluded head: median ratio 1.433x, 100.0% of 108 cells above 1.0 (tail 1,936 days, head 945 days).
   So the i.i.d. and block nulls are CONSERVATIVE here — they over-disperse relative to
   the real tape's episode placement, and that over-dispersion pushes the observed band's
   percentile DOWN.  H_NOISE is therefore the EASIER call and is reported as such.  What
   the run can still say safely is the weaker, sufficient claim: the observed band does
   NOT exceed what sampling noise alone produces at these ends, under any of the three
   estimators or three end grids.  What it CANNOT say is that the band is 1.59x smaller
   than noise in any economically meaningful sense — that ratio is partly this artefact.

   ANSWER: YES, and with room to spare — the split-point band is fully accounted for
   by the OOS window's own sampling noise.  A CONSTANT-Sharpe book read at the same
   nested ends pays a median band of 0.2641 against the 0.1661 actually observed, so the
   published band is 1.59x SMALLER than noise alone produces and sits at the 0.101
   percentile of its own null; 0.0% of 108 cells are above 0.95.  H_NOISE and H_SHARE PASS on
   all three estimators (IID 0.101 / BLOCK21 0.144 / NORMAL 0.127) and all three end grids
   (Q16 0.101 / Q28 0.019 / Y8 0.091).  On 1013's OWN three pick-invariant cells the observed
   0.1747-0.1955 sits against nulls of 0.2604-0.2685, percentiles 0.131-0.199.

   SO 1013's 'price of PROTOCOL's fiat' is KILLED as a charge against the protocol:
   the split point is not adding swing to the published number, it is revealing swing
   the OOS window has anyway.  A rule-8 result is that noisy at ONE split too.

   WITH THE CAVEAT SECTION (D2) MEASURES: the null over-disperses because the tape's
   volatile episodes sit in the tail every window shares (variance density 1.43x the
   excluded head, 100% of cells).  The SAFE claim is the one-sided one — the band does not
   EXCEED sampling noise.  The 1.59x margin itself is partly this artefact and is not banked.

   THE TRAP, and it is the more useful half: a PER-END SE (0.3241 median) is NOT the yardstick
   for this band, and neither is what independent windows would give (1.1784, 4.46x the
   nested null).  The 16 windows all end on the last day of the tape, so they are mostly
   the same returns read again; anyone pricing 1013's band against a per-end SE, or
   against 1001's comparand SEs of 0.2877 / 0.3350, is comparing objects with different
   correlation structures.

   KEEP / KILL under PROTOCOL rule 4.  This run carries NO new book — its object is the
   record's own split-point band, and the rule-8 arm re-runs GRID ladder books the record
   already holds.  Neither KEEP path is claimable and none is claimed.  The reportable
   result is the MEASUREMENT plus a proposed PROTOCOL clause:

     PROPOSED (not applied — PROTOCOL rule 6 reserves changes to the Sunday review):
       rule 8 SPLIT-POINT BAND clause — where a split-point band is published it is
       published beside the band the SAME book's own resampled path produces at the SAME
       nested ends (here median 0.2641 against 0.1661 observed), and it is never compared to a
       per-end SE or to a comparand SE built from non-nested windows: those overstate the
       yardstick by 1.2x and 4.5x respectively.  A band that does not exceed its own
       nested null is reported as 'inside the window's sampling noise', never as a cost of
       the split-point choice.
