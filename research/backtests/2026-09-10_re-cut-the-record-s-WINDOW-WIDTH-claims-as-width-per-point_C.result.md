1. **Census.** 2,825 committed `research/backtests/*.csv` scanned: 46 files carry a width-family column, 65 columns in total. 39 columns / 5,821 values are in scope (the file carries a swept-dial axis); the 26 excluded columns are listed one by one with their reason in `.census.csv`, so the corpus boundary is auditable rather than hand-picked. Only **7 columns / 608 values are density-dependent (PTS)**; 476 of those carry an in-row denominator and are restatable. 209 values were already published as a FRACTION and 611 in dial UNITS — the record is far less exposed than the queue's framing assumes.
2. **Headline.** **552 of 639 (86.4%)** published between-dial width comparisons read the same way in width/points as in points; **38 flip outright** and 49 gain or lose a tie. But **idea 401's actual published headline — the 15 per-dial MEDIAN comparisons — survives 15 of 15**, because the two non-empty dials (`f` 4/13 = 0.308, `vol` 1.5/8 = 0.188) beat four dials whose median width is exactly 0 in either unit. The queue's premise is true about the *exposure* and false about the *effect at the claim level*, and that is this file's main correction to it.
3. **The exposure is nonetheless real and large.** **96.2%** of published between-dial pairs compare two dials swept at *different* numbers of points; only 3.8% are density-matched. Control: **0 of the 87 pairs that change reading are same-density**, which is what the arithmetic requires.
4. **It runs both ways.** The plateau family, published as a FRACTION by idea 128, loses **23 of 96** comparisons when read in POINTS (11 ties broken, 12 created, 0 flips). Neither unit is the safe default; publishing only one is the defect.
5. **Density (fresh grid, spacing controlled here — 3 panels x 3 dials x x1/x2/x4 = 762 points, all committed).** At a density move of x3.78, median `|x4/x1 − 1|` is **PTS 2.61, FRAC 0.062, UNITS 0.031**. Width/points is density-free in fact and not just in argument; this confirms idea 409's ~0.02 at a wider tolerance, on dials and panels 409 did not run.
6. **UNITS is not the escape.** Between dials it is incommensurable — 4 percentage points of band and 4 names of `n` are not the same width of anything — and it carries an undeclared convention: a 1-point window is 0 wide under SPAN ((k−1)·step) and 1 step wide under COVER (k·step). That single choice moves the rule-8 disagreement count from 2 of 6 cells to 1 of 6.
7. **Rule 8 (PROTOCOL 8), and where the unit actually bites.** Within one sweep the three units are algebraically the same chooser, so the experiment has to compare *across* sweeps. Given a menu of sweeps and told to adopt the one with the widest IS 4b window (chosen on 2009–2016, 2017–2026 read once), the unit changes the book in **2 of 6** (panel, menu) cells — **1 of 6** after the convention above is fixed. On B136 with the full menu, **PTS picks gross@x4 = 0.9875 (fails 4b) and FRAC picks gross@x2 = 1.00 (passes 4b)**: PTS prefers the finest grid because a fixed physical window simply contains more points there, and that preference flips a 4b verdict.
8. **OOS numbers.** OOS CAGR PTS 7.98% / FRAC 8.00% / UNITS 7.56% vs SPY 15.4%; OOS Sharpe 0.9912 / 0.9912 / 0.9914 vs SPY 0.876–0.882; OOS MaxDD −14.2% / −14.2% / −13.6% vs SPY −33.7%. Reference books OOS 2017–2026: RULES v2 Sharpe U56 1.2876, B136 1.1206, SMALL439 0.5665.

Not capital-worthy, and not what this idea was for.
