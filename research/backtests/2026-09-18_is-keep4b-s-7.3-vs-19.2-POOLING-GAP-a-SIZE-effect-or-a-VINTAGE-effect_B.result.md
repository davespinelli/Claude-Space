# 977 (lane B, 2026-09-18) — ANSWERED (A) SIZE EFFECT, and KILL (capital)

**The answer.** The `keep4b` pooling gap is a **SIZE effect, not a vintage effect.** The gap is an exact identity, `p_row − p_file = Cov(n,p)/mean(n)`, proved to 1e-9 (G2). Over 213 boolean `keep4b` carriers / 344,181 rows on this tree the gap is **−7.29 pp**, and averaged over the five S rungs **0.923 of it is reproducible from file SIZE alone against 0.156 from DATE alone** (residual 0.129). Net of vintage, size carries −5.21 pp; net of size, vintage carries **+0.38 pp** — i.e. vintage moves it the *wrong way* and is ~1/14th the magnitude. The reading holds at every S ∈ {2,3,4,5,10} and at four of the five statistics (`pass4b` +0.909/−0.208, `ALL4b` +0.861/−0.145; `keep4a` and `pass4a` have gaps of ±1 pp and are too small to decompose — their shares blow up on a near-zero denominator and are published, not interpreted).

**Two things the census found that 973 could not have.** (1) `keep4b` is an **overloaded column**: 20 of 233 carriers hold COUNTS, not booleans. Pooling them naively moves the gap from −7.29 pp to −13.64 pp — nearly half of 973's headline was a units defect, not a weighting one. (2) **The denominator moved** (idea 894): 973's 148 files are 233 today, and its file-weighted 19.2% reads 14.81% on the same ≤2026-09-15 slice. G1a/G1b/G1d are written as strict gates and **FAIL on purpose**, so the drift is a measured number. What survives the drift is the sign and order of magnitude (G1e), which is all the census needed.

**The capital arm: KILL, and no new book.** 264 real books (U56/B136/SMALL × 8 N × 11 GROSS), each with its own 4a and 4b verdict, all published in `.grid.csv`. **0 of 264 clear 4a**; 36 of 264 clear 4b (U56 30, B136 6, SMALL 0). On the nested grid-size ladder the SIZE hypothesis reproduces **on the tape, independently of the record**: pooled 4b pass share falls **0.333 → 0.229 → 0.213 → 0.136** as the grid grows from 4 to 88 cells, monotone (G3). Big grids really are stricter. Rule 8 (IS-only chooser, warm-up..2016-12-31, 2017–2026 read once) yields exactly one 4b pass — U56/L1, N=15 g=0.75 — and **G6 fails it**: L1 is the 4-cell grid centred on the record's own incumbent, so that pass is the very artefact ARM A predicts a small grid manufactures. It is not published as a book.

**What is new and is worth real money.** *The grid is an unpriced parameter.* Same rule, same tape, same chooser, same IS window, on U56: choosing from 4 cells gives N=15/g=0.75 → OOS 17.71% / **1.187** / −19.69%, **4b PASS**. Choosing from 88 cells gives N=5/g=0.95 → OOS 29.51% / **1.054** / −34.97%, **4b FAIL**. The grid alone costs **−0.133 OOS Sharpe and −15.28 pp of OOS MaxDD** while buying +11.79 pp of OOS CAGR. The mechanism is visible and boring: a wider gross ladder lets the chooser reach 0.95, which clears IS Sharpe and then breaks 4b's drawdown cap. Every panel's chooser walks to the top gross rung as the grid widens (U56 0.75→0.95, B136 0.75→0.95, SMALL 0.75→0.95).

**Proposed publication clause (for the Sunday review; this run changes no rule and touches no live file).** *A committed 4b PASS must state the GRID it was chosen from — the rung lists of every dial, not the cell count — and a pooled 4b share must state whether it is row- or file-weighted. A pass from a grid of fewer than ~16 cells, or one whose chosen cell sits at a boundary rung of its own ladder, is a candidate region and not a book.* Both halves of that clause are earned here: the first by the L1-vs-L4 OOS gap, the second by the fact that all three L4 picks sit on the top gross rung.
