# Idea 1071 (cloud lane, 2026-09-16) — does the 0.97pp DRAWDOWN MISS close under a NAME CAP instead of a GROSS CUT?

## What was asked
Idea 1064 found de-grossing cannot thread 4b's CAGR floor and the live book's −12.05% drawdown at
once (window empty 18 of 18, best miss 0.97 pp on W/H126). Test whether a **per-name weight cap**
or an **n above 20** — which cut drawdown *without* cutting expected return proportionally —
closes the same 0.97 pp, and price the result against the gross-matched null **at the same
realised DD**. Max 2 params (n, per-name cap).

## The two dials, and what was declared before any number
`N ∈ {20, 25, 30, 40}` × `CAPMULT k ∈ {1.00, 1.25, 1.50, 2.00, INF}`, per-name target weight
`w_i = min(g/n_sel, k·g/N)` with the residual in **cash**. All 20 points reported on both panels,
for the book and for the null. Fixed, not dials: CAND20 legs, `max_vol` 0.60, gross 0.75, 10 bps,
LAG 1, weekly cadence, min hold 126 — 936/1064's construction verbatim.

Stated up front so it could not be presented later as a finding: **`k < 1` is a gross cut in
disguise** (in a book that fills all N slots it binds uniformly, which is exactly 1064's ladder),
so it is not on the ladder. For `k ≥ 1` the cap binds only through **breadth** — the eligibility
gate leaves `n_sel < N` on thin days and the incumbent construction answers by *concentrating*
(1/n_sel each). The cap therefore de-grosses **exactly when breadth thins**, which is when
drawdowns happen. That was the refutable claim. `MISS = max(|MaxDD| − |live|, 0.70·SPY_CAGR −
CAGR)` in pp; `MISS ≤ 0` means 1064's joint window is open.

**G4 failed and the declaration that produced it was wrong.** Weights drift between rebalances and
the cash sleeve does not, so a book *rebuilt* at gross λ·g is not λ times the book at g. What the
λ-match actually builds is the **daily-rebalanced blend of the null path with cash** — a real,
implementable book, but a different convention. G4b prices the gap: **0.020 pp of CAGR and 0.011
pp of MaxDD at half gross**, against a MISS measured in whole percentage points. The contrast
below is published with that number beside it rather than re-run under the other convention.

## The 20-point grid — the answer
**U56 min MISS = 7.049 pp** (N=20, k=1.00). **B136 min MISS = 6.538 pp** (N=20, k=1.00).
**Window open at 0 of 20 points on both panels.** The best point on either dial is **5.5× further
from the window than the plain gross cut**, and the best point is the one with the *smallest* N
and the *tightest* cap — i.e. the grid's answer is "go back down the ladder you came from."

**H_NDIAL FAILS in the opposite direction to the one declared.** More names make drawdown
**worse**, monotonically, on both panels (U56 −19.13% → −22.98%; B136 −20.74% → −25.38%) while
CAGR falls monotonically too (U56 15.58% → 13.46%). The premise the queue wrote the idea on — that
n cuts drawdown without cutting return — is **false on this tape in both of its clauses**: this
book's drawdowns are **common-factor**, not idiosyncratic, so extra names add no drawdown
protection and only dilute the selection edge.

**The cap dial is live but tiny.** k=1.00 binds on **9.0%** of U56 rebalance dates at N=20 and
**21.9%** at N=40 (it bites harder the more slots there are to leave unfilled), and moves mean
realised gross 0.710 → 0.702 at N=20. Its whole effect on drawdown is at most **0.92 pp** within
any N (largest at N=25: −21.16% → −20.24%), and it buys that the same way the gross ladder does —
by holding cash, at 0.31 pp of CAGR at N=20. It is a breadth-triggered micro-de-gross, not a third
mechanism.

## Verdict — **KILL**

worth under 1 pp of drawdown; the n dial moves drawdown the **wrong way** and spends the book's

gross rung is not merely the *best* dial for the joint window, it is the **only** one, and the
