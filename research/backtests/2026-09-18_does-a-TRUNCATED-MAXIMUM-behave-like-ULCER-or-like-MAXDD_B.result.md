# Idea 1146 — does a TRUNCATED MAXIMUM behave like ULCER or like MAXDD?
2026-09-18, lane B. Script: `2026-09-18_does-a-TRUNCATED-MAXIMUM-behave-like-ULCER-or-like-MAXDD_B.py`. Gates 13/15 (two named failures, below).

## ANSWER: AXIS-DEPENDENT. Truncating the ORDER makes it ULCER; truncating the WINDOW does not.
1140 read its own finding — MAXDD alone has both a GAP and a bootstrap SD that GROW with tape length (+0.1419 / +0.1654) — as a fact about MAX-ness, and predicted that a truncated maximum should behave like ULCER. It does, on one of the two ways to truncate a maximum, and not on the other. The declared decision rule resolves to **LIKE MAXDD** (it fires when *any* rule has no switch-off, and KDAY has none); the finding is the split.

1. **QUANT (truncate the ORDER STATISTIC: the p-th quantile of drawdown depth).** `b_sd` climbs monotonically from **−0.6251** at p=0.50 through **−0.3221** (0.75), **−0.0926** (0.90), **+0.0326** (0.95), **+0.1223** (0.99), **+0.1353** (0.999) to **+0.1654** at MAXDD. The MAXDD signature (`b_gap > 0` AND `b_sd > 0`) is OFF at **5 of 7** rungs and first turns ON at **p = 0.999**. Truncating the order statistic *at all* — even at the 99th percentile of the drawdown distribution — removes it. `H_SWITCH`/`H_KNIFE_EDGE` as pre-declared were REFUTED only because they required the switch on *both* rules.
2. **KDAY (truncate the WINDOW: worst drawdown whose peak is at most k days back).** The signature is ON at **6 of 6** rungs, down to a 21-day lookback, where it is **stronger** than MAXDD's own (`b_gap +0.3974`, `b_sd +0.5287` against `+0.1419 / +0.1654`). No switch-off anywhere.
3. **THE MECHANISM.** A worst-of-many still grows with the tape however short the window it is taken over, because a longer tape supplies more windows. A 90th-percentile drawdown does not, because it is not a worst-of-anything. So 1140's "MAX-ness" is more precisely **extreme-order-statistic-ness**.
4. **THE BIGGEST CAVEAT ON THE KDAY ARM, MEASURED (`kday_degeneracy.csv`), not asserted.** On the FULL warm tape `K_k` is **bit-identical to MAXDD in 73 of 74 books at k=63 (98.6%) and 74 of 74 at k≥126** — those rungs truncate nothing there and any agreement with MAXDD is trivial. Only **k=21 genuinely binds** (11 of 74 degenerate, 14.9%; median `K_21 − MAXDD` **+0.888 pp**). The KDAY arm therefore rests on **one informative rung**, and it is that rung that carries the signature. (The k≥63 exponents nonetheless differ from MAXDD's, because those rungs *do* bind inside the shorter sub-tapes and the bootstrap draws — the degeneracy is a full-tape statement.)
5. **MONOTONICITY splits the same way.** Cell by cell, `b_sd` rises toward MAXDD in **83 of 96 (86.5%)** adjacent QUANT comparisons but only **32 of 80 (40.0%)** KDAY ones — along KDAY it *falls* toward MAXDD. `H_MONOTONE` REFUTED (bar 80%, both rules).
6. **BOTH TUNED DIALS, ALL 9 GRID POINTS (`dials.csv`).** Median `b_sd` over the truncated rungs: QUANT 1/2 **−0.0384**, 1/3 **−0.0365** (LIKE ULCER); KDAY 1/2 **+0.2620**, 1/3 **+0.1789** (LIKE MAXDD); BOTH 1/2 **+0.0882**, 1/3 **+0.0715**. All three 1/1 points are `nan` — a single tape length cannot identify a slope — and are reported as degenerate, not as zero. The verdict is invariant to dial 2 and flips on dial 1, which *is* the finding.
7. **LEVELS, published so the level-shift confound is checkable (`levelsummary.csv`).** QUANT: −1.80 / −4.96 / −8.78 / −10.68 / −14.16 / −19.24 / −21.52 %. KDAY: −19.81 / −21.52 / −21.52 / −21.52 / −21.52 / −21.52 %. ULCER +4.79 %. A lower rung is a smaller level as well as a less extreme one; that is why `b_gap` and `b_sd` are fitted and published separately.

## RECOMMENDED TO THE SUNDAY REVIEW — PUBLISHING ONLY, no rules change, no new book
This script does **not** edit PROTOCOL.md, RULES.md, scan.py, bot.py or baseline.py. Proposed wording:

> **A maximum-keyed claim names which axis it truncates.** A committed claim about a maximum statistic (MaxDD, worst-k-day, a drawdown quantile) must state whether any truncation applied to it is of the **ORDER** (how far into the tail) or of the **WINDOW** (how far back the peak may be). The two do not behave alike: truncating the order at p ≤ 0.99 removes the tape-length signature that drives a maximum's un-resolvability, while truncating the window does not remove it at any horizon down to 21 days. A window-truncated statistic must also publish the share of books at which it is bit-identical to the untruncated maximum, because above ~63 days on this record that share is 98.6–100% and the rung truncates nothing.

Cost of the clause: two fields on maximum-keyed artefacts. It changes no committed number and no book — the capital arm above is a KILL either way.
