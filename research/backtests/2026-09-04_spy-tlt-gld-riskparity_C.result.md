# Idea 19 — spy-tlt-gld-riskparity (lane C, 2026-09-04) — **KILL as a book, KEEP as the benchmark bar**

1. **Book.** Inverse-vol risk parity on SPY/TLT/GLD, monthly, 10 bps, next-day execution. Two tuned params: vol lookback `L` in {20,60,120,252} × gross `g` in {0.60,0.75,1.00}. All 12 points reported, plus untuned controls (EW thirds, 60/40) and a 10-point gross diagnostic. `compare()` cross-check reproduces the harness to the decimal (RP(60,1.00): 9.1% / 1.004 / -22.4%, H1 1.102 / H2 0.931).
2. **KEEP paths: 0/12 on 4a, 0/12 on 4b.** Failure modes are 8×`CAGR` and 4×`DD,CAGR` — nothing else. Sharpe and both halves clear SPY everywhere (H1 1.02–1.11 vs 0.957; H2 0.91–0.93 vs 0.837; OOS 0.98–1.01 vs 0.884).
3. **The 4b failure is structural, not a tuning miss.** Sharpe is flat in `g` to ±0.002 across 0.40–1.40 (a fourth independent confirmation of idea 66: gross is a pure lever with zero Sharpe content), so 4b reduces to an interval on `g`: the DD cap (|MaxDD| ≤ 20.2%) needs g ≤ ~0.88; the CAGR floor (≥ 10.7%) needs g ≥ ~1.15. **The interval is empty** — and closing it requires leverage, which PROTOCOL rule 2 forbids for this idea.
4. **Rule-8 walk-forward.** Params picked on 2009-01→2016-12 by IS Sharpe select `L=20, g=1.00` (IS 1.019). OOS 2017+: **9.4% / 0.984 / -22.7%** vs live rules 7.8% / 0.751 / -13.8% and SPY 15.5% / 0.884 / -33.7%. OOS Sharpe beats both; OOS CAGR is 61% of SPY's. Spearman(IS, OOS Sharpe) across the 12 points = +0.371 — weak, but the whole grid is within 0.05 Sharpe so selection barely matters.
5. **L is nearly inert; short is mildly better.** Sharpe 0.998 (L=20) → 1.005 (L=60) → 0.976 (L=120) → 0.955 (L=252), i.e. a 0.05 spread, all of it in H1. Turnover is where `L` actually pays: 2.41 → 0.95 → 0.59 → 0.40 turns/yr at g=1.00.
6. **4a is a bar this book can buy.** At `L=60, g≤0.55` the book passes 4a (Sharpe better in both halves, MaxDD better than the live book's -13.8%) — but only by de-grossing: at g=0.55 it earns **5.0% CAGR against the live book's 6.5%**. A formal 4a pass that makes less money than the book it would replace is evidence about 4a, not about risk parity. **Not proposed as a KEEP.** This is the same defect that motivated adding 4b on Sep 4, now demonstrated from the other side.
7. **The benchmark question the idea was actually asked.** At matched drawdown (RP g=0.60, -14.0% vs live -13.8%): RP delivers **Sharpe 1.006 vs 0.666, H1 1.101 vs 0.641, H2 0.934 vs 0.692, OOS 1.007 vs 0.751 — on 0.58 turns/yr against the live book's 23.6.** The live book is beaten on every risk-adjusted axis by three ETFs rebalanced monthly, and is ahead only on CAGR, by 1.0pp, for 40× the trading.
8. **Controls behave as expected and are reported.** EW thirds 9.3% / 0.983 / -23.1% — risk parity's advantage over naive thirds is ~0.02 Sharpe and 0.7pp of drawdown, i.e. the inverse-vol weighting is doing very little; the diversification is doing the work. 60/40 SPY-TLT is 10.3% / 0.996 but fails four 4b bars (H2 0.734, OOS 0.807) — GLD carries the second half.
9. **Where it bleeds.** 2013 -2.1%, 2015 -2.7%, 2022 -12.1% (SPY -18.2%): the book's bad years are the bond/gold drawdowns, and 2022 is not the defence the Sharpe implies. Its good years are 2011, 2019, 2025 — it is a duration/real-asset sleeve, not a crash hedge.
10. **Recommendation.** Do not adopt as a book. **Adopt as a standing benchmark row**: any future candidate must beat `RP L=60, g=0.75` (6.9% / 1.005 / -17.2%, 0.72 turns/yr) on Sharpe *at matched drawdown*, not merely beat RULES v1. Caveat: SPY/TLT/GLD over 2009–2026 is one duration regime (a 35-year bond bull ending mid-sample); the H1→H2 Sharpe decay 1.10→0.93 is that regime turning, and there is no reason the next 17 years pay a bond sleeve the same way. No RULES change proposed.
