  AND THE ANSWER IS AXIS-DEPENDENT, which is the finding.  The two rules truncate
  DIFFERENT things and do not agree:
    QUANT truncates the ORDER STATISTIC — how deep into the tail of the drawdown
          distribution the number reaches.  Truncating it AT ALL removes the
          signature (b_sd is negative at every p <= 0.90 and only turns positive
          between p=0.95 and p=0.99); the MAXDD signature needs p >= 0.999.
    KDAY  truncates the HORIZON — how far back the peak may be.  Truncating it does
          NOT remove the signature at any rung tested, down to a 21-day lookback,
          where the signature is in fact STRONGER than MAXDD's own.
  So 1140's 'MAX-ness' is more precisely EXTREME-ORDER-STATISTIC-ness: a worst-of-many
  still grows with the tape however short the window it is taken over, because a longer
  tape supplies more windows; a 90th-percentile drawdown does not, because it is not a
  worst-of-anything.  A truncated maximum behaves like ULCER only when the truncation
  is applied to the ORDER, not to the WINDOW.
