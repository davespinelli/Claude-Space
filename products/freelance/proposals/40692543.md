## Proposal

Signal to broker execution is the right way to describe this, and the gap between those two words is where these systems fail. The signal fires once, the order does not fill, and the bot now believes it holds a position it does not have. On Nasdaq futures that gap is expensive: a stale position view through a fast session compounds into a real loss before anyone looks.

Plan:
- Agree the signal source and its exact format, and how a repeated or late signal is treated.
- Build the execution layer: order placement, fill confirmation, and a position view reconciled against the broker rather than assumed.
- Enforce risk limits in code: max contracts, max daily loss, session hours, and a kill switch that flattens and stops.
- Log every signal, order and fill to file so any trading day can be reconstructed line by line.
- Run it on paper or sim for a week, then hand over with a runbook for going live.

You receive the bot, the logs, the risk config and the runbook.

Public example: my Alpaca bot and pipeline at https://github.com/davespinelli/Claude-Space
