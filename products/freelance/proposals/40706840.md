## Proposal

A 19-DOF humanoid sharing one HAL across MuJoCo and hardware usually means gait work is bottlenecked by measurement rather than by ideas. You change three parameters, watch a rollout, and cannot say whether the walk improved or the initial state was kinder. Everything after that is guesswork dressed up as tuning.

Plan:
- Write a headless MuJoCo runner that scores a gait over a fixed set of seeds, commanded velocities and terrain perturbations, not one rollout.
- Pin down the metrics worth optimising: distance before fall, velocity tracking error, torque cost, foot slip, support-polygon margin.
- Run a parameter search over the existing controller gains and step timing, driven by config files so any result reruns from one command.
- Report which parameter sets hold across seeds and which only win on the seed they were tuned on.
- Leave the harness in your repo so the next controller change is evaluated, not eyeballed.

You get the runner, the search scripts, a results table and a short note on what actually moved each metric.

How I work in public: https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/

Two questions: is the gait you want improved hand-tuned or a learned policy, and should the harness drive the physical robot at this stage or stay in simulation?

## Delivery plan
1. Day 1-2: read your HAL and controller, agree the metric list and the seed and velocity grid.
2. Day 3-4: build the headless runner and confirm it reproduces your current gait's numbers.
3. Day 5-7: run the parameter search, log every trial to disk with its config.
4. Day 8-9: rerun the top candidates on held-out seeds and terrain to separate real gains from seed luck.
5. Day 10: deliver harness, results table and the write-up, one revision round included.
