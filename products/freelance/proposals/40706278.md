## Proposal

Cluster analysis always returns clusters. Ask k-means for four groups on pure noise and it hands you four tidy groups, which is why the validation step is the real job and the clustering itself is the easy half. Getting Python to agree with SPSS has the same trap: unless the standardisation, distance measure and linkage match exactly, the two will disagree for reasons that have nothing to do with your data.

Plan:
- Check the data first: missingness, duplicates, scale, outliers, and which variables actually carry the separation.
- Reproduce your SPSS run in Python with every setting stated, and report where and why the two differ.
- Choose the number of clusters on evidence: elbow, silhouette and gap, plus stability across resampled halves.
- Profile the final clusters on the variables that separate them, with sizes and means per cluster.
- Say plainly if the structure does not hold, because a weak result found early costs less than a defended one.

You get the notebook, the cleaned dataset, the cluster assignments, and a short report with the figures and the settings used.
