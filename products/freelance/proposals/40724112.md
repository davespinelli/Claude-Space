## Proposal

Cleansing a customer table is mostly a matching problem, and matching is where it goes wrong in both directions. Match too strictly and the same person stays in twice over a trailing space or a phone written with the country code. Match too loosely and two real customers collapse into one record, which quietly takes an order history with it. The second is the expensive one, and it is not obvious afterwards.

Plan:
- Normalise before comparing: trim, case, phone and postcode formats, so the values are comparable at all.
- Score candidate duplicates and bring the borderline pairs to you rather than merging them silently.
- Work on a copy of the table and keep the original until you have signed off the result.
- Add the constraints and checks that stop the same mess arriving again through the entry path.
- Report every row changed, merged or removed, with its before and after.

Deliverables: the cleansed table, the change report, the merge rules, and the scripts that produced them.

Proof: https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/

Two questions: roughly how many rows, and what identifies a customer uniquely in your system today? Is anything writing to this table while the work runs?

## Delivery plan
1. Day 1: take a dump, profile the table, and show you where the duplicates and format problems actually are.
2. Day 2: agree the normalisation rules and what counts as the same customer.
3. Day 3: run the matching on the copy and produce the borderline pairs for review.
4. Day 4: review those pairs with you, apply the merges, generate the change report.
5. Day 5: add the constraints, load the cleansed table, hand over the scripts.
