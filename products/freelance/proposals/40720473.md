## Proposal

Typing the batch into Excel is the easy half. Organising it clearly is the half that decides whether the sheet is still useful in six months. Raw information almost always carries fields that were never separated at the source: a name and a company sharing one line, dates written three different ways, numbers with a currency symbol stuck to them. Entered as they stand they look fine and sort wrong.

Plan:
- Take a sample of the batch first and agree the column list before typing anything.
- One kind of value per column, dates in a single format, numbers stored as numbers.
- Enter the full batch with a reference column pointing back to the source item.
- Run a check pass for blanks, duplicates, and values that do not match their column type.
- Return anything unreadable as a short query list rather than guessing at it.

You get the workbook, a one page note on the column rules, and the query list.

Proof: https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/

Two questions: what format is the raw batch in, and roughly how many records?
