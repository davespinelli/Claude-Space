## Proposal

Verbatim is the word in your brief that decides the job. Most tables in a PDF are not really tables underneath, so an extractor will merge two columns or split a wrapped cell into its own row, and the spreadsheet still looks finished. That is the failure worth designing against.

Plan:
- Run three of your PDFs first and show you the extracted rows next to the source pages, before quoting anything beyond this batch.
- Extract table by table, keeping the file, page and table reference against every row.
- Preserve values exactly as printed: leading zeros, trailing minus signs, blank cells left blank, no reformatting of dates or numbers.
- Check column and row totals wherever the page carries them, and flag any table whose totals do not tie.
- Put ambiguous or unreadable cells in a flag column rather than guessing at them.

You get the workbook, the flag list, and the extraction script, so later batches cost you less.

Proof: my public repo and live dashboard at https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/
