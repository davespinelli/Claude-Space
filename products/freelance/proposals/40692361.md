## Proposal

Pulling both narrative text and numbers off a list of online sources is where this kind of job usually breaks. The numbers are easy to read and hard to trust: the same figure appears in a table and again in a sentence, units differ between sources, and a page quietly changes its layout halfway through the list. Manual copying hides all of that until the totals disagree.

Plan:
- Go through your source list with you and agree what one row of the output means.
- Write a Python collector that pulls each page and records the source URL and date next to every value.
- Separate the narrative fields from the numeric ones so numbers stay numeric and stay checkable.
- Flag anything the collector cannot read cleanly instead of guessing, so you review a short list, not everything.
- Deliver the filled workbook plus the script, so a refresh later is a rerun rather than a redo.

You receive the Excel file, the collector script and a short README.

Live example of a pipeline I run: https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/

Two questions: roughly how many sources are on the list, and are they all public pages with no login?
