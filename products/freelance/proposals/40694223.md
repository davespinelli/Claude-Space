## Proposal

Raw service records rarely break on the obvious fields. They break on the small ones: the same technician spelled three ways, a VIN with a trailing space, dates stored as text in two formats. A report built straight on top of that undercounts quietly, and the totals never quite reconcile against what the shop actually billed.

Plan:
- Profile the months you have first and list what is actually wrong, with counts, before changing a single row.
- Standardise the fields the reports depend on: dates, job codes, technician names and vehicle identifiers.
- Deduplicate repeat entries on a rule you approve, keeping every discarded row in a separate sheet so nothing disappears silently.
- Build the reports on the cleaned table with pivots that refresh when next month is added.
- Add a data quality sheet listing rows the rules could not resolve, for you to decide on.

You receive the cleaned dataset, the report workbook and the cleaning script, so future months go through the same steps.

Proof: my public repo and live dashboard at https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/

Two questions: roughly how many rows and months are there, and which reports matter most, revenue by service type, technician throughput or repeat visits?
