## Proposal

Ten thousand customer records with purchase history attached changes what a bad merge costs. Deduplicating the contact row is the straightforward half. The half that goes wrong is the orders underneath: merge two records that are not the same person and one customer inherits another's purchase history. It surfaces later as a wrong lifetime value or an order confirmation sent to the wrong address.

Plan:
- Normalise before comparing: trim, case, phone and postcode formats, email casing.
- Score candidate pairs instead of matching on exact equality, and set the threshold with you on a sample.
- Move orders and history explicitly as part of each merge, never leaving rows orphaned.
- Work on a copy of the database and keep a reversible change log for every row touched.
- Add the constraints and checks that stop the same duplicates arriving through the entry path.

Deliverables: the cleaned tables, the change log, the matching rules as SQL and Python you can re-run, and a summary of what merged.

Two questions: which tables besides the customer table reference a customer id? And is there a field that counts as a hard match, such as an account number?

3. Day 3: run the merges on the copy, carrying orders and history, with the change log written as it goes.
