## Proposal

Categorising bills by hand works until the descriptions change. The same supplier posts as three different strings across the year, card processors append reference numbers, and one misfiled row moves a month's total without anything looking wrong. The useful deliverable is not a categorised file, it is a rule set that categorises the next file the same way.

Plan:
- Build the category list with you first, including where two categories overlap and which one wins.
- Write matching rules on normalised descriptions, supplier and amount, rather than on the raw text.
- Run the rules over your bills and put everything they cannot classify confidently into a review sheet instead of guessing.
- Produce the summary: monthly totals per category, supplier breakdown, and the largest movements month on month.
- Leave the rules in a sheet you can edit, so adding a supplier does not need me.

You get the categorised workbook, the summary tables and charts, the editable rules sheet, and the review list.

Work I run in public: https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/

Two questions: do you already have a category list or should I propose one from your data, and what period do the bills cover?

David

## Delivery plan
1. Day 1: receive the bills, profile the descriptions, propose or confirm the category list.
2. Day 2: build the matching rules and run a first pass.
3. Day 3: review the unmatched rows with you and tighten the rules.
4. Day 4: build the monthly summary, supplier breakdown and charts.
5. Day 5: reconcile category totals against the file total, deliver workbook and rules sheet, one revision round included.
