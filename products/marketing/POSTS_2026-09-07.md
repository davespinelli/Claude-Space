## 1. r/algotrading

**Title:** We ran 3,200+ strategy variants on daily bars with a protocol we couldn't edit afterwards. Here's what died, and the one dull thing that survived.

**Body:**

I'm a CFO, not a quant. I built a research desk with Claude that runs backtests under a protocol
written *before* the results. It's public: 349 scripts, 3,310 result rows, every KILL kept.

The protocol: weights decided at close t, applied at t+1. 10 bps per unit turnover. No shorting,
no leverage. Full-sample **and** both halves reported. KEEP candidates also have to survive
walk-forward — parameters chosen on 2009–2016 only, 2017–2026 read exactly once.

**What died:**

- **Our own live signal.** Rank IC of the traded composite against next 1w/4w returns: +0.004 /
  +0.001 (t 0.4 / 0.1) over 917 weekly cross-sections. The composite *before* its `/sqrt(vol20)`
  term has IC t≈4.2; the vol scaler alone has t≈−5.7. The division cancels the signal. Dropping
  the scaler was worth **+10.1%/yr (t 3.33)** at the same position count and gross. It was never a
  risk adjustment — it cut held-name average vol20 from 29.4% to 14.1% and left 31% name overlap.
- **Dual momentum across asset classes.** K=1 monthly: 8.7% CAGR, Sharpe 0.54, −33.8% MaxDD,
  halves 0.36 / 0.69. KILL.
- **PEAD on small caps.** Tercile, 60-day hold: 18.9% / 1.07 / −33.5%, halves 1.29 / 0.93 — fails
  both KEEP paths. The control that reversed the sort *beat* the signal (20.7% / 1.16 / −32.1%).
- **Insider cluster buying.** 6-month hold: 21.2% / 0.97 / −39.8%, KILL. The 12-month hold had the
  Sharpe (1.27) but the same −39.7% drawdown, so it's parked, not kept.

**What survived is boring.** Drop the vol scaler, hold the top 20 eligible names equal-weight at a
constant 75% gross, weekly: 12.7% CAGR, Sharpe 1.093, −18.3% MaxDD, halves 1.088 / 1.103,
walk-forward 14.4% / 1.170 / −18.3% against SPY's 15.5% / 0.884 / −33.7%. 5 of 30 grid points
passed. Position count — not the signal — fixed the first-half failure four earlier ideas died on.

What we adopted on Sunday is duller still: hold every universe name inside a 200d ±3% hysteresis
band at 0.75/N, weekly, no ranking, no vol filter. 8.66% / 1.2056 / −12.05%, halves 1.2259 /
1.1908, OOS 1.2851. Its honest limits: it **fails** our own capital bar on CAGR (8.66% vs a
10.66% floor), the universe is current-constituent so absolute CAGR is survivorship-optimistic,
only 2020 and 2022 are real stress tests, and the live paper record is days old, not years.

Dashboard: https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/ — the engine and every script, failures
included, are open source in the repo. It's my own project, nothing to sell, no affiliate links.
Happy to re-run a variant if someone thinks a KILL was called wrong.

## 2. r/ValueInvesting (also fits r/SecurityAnalysis)

**Title:** I read 489 sub-$2B US companies from their filings and wrote 82 full notes. Only 2 became ideas. Here's one of them, with what would prove it wrong.

**Body:**

Disclosure: this is my own project. I'm a CFO who wanted to see whether a filings-first process
could run end to end with an LLM doing the reading. It's public and free, I'm not selling a
newsletter, and there are no price targets in this post.

**How the desk works.** Every US non-financial operating company between $50M and $2B market cap
with revenue over $20M and real liquidity — 484 names. Each gets its 10-K Items 1/1A/7, 10-Q
MD&A, proxy, six months of 8-Ks and twelve months of Form 4s pulled from EDGAR. All 489 have been
triaged and scored, reasoning kept; 8+ graduates to a full note. 82 notes so far: **2 IDEA,
74 WATCH, 6 PASS.** Every verdict, rejections included, is price-tracked against IWM and SPY from
publication, forever.

**One of the two ideas: Strattec (STRT).** A $291M automotive access supplier — locks, keys, fobs,
latches, power liftgates — with 3.99 million shares out, no sell-side estimates I could find, and
outsourced investor relations.

The setup is a GAAP optical mess. Q4 FY26 GAAP EPS printed $0.95 against $2.01 a year earlier, so
a screen reads a 53% earnings collapse. Adjusted EPS was $2.06 versus $2.06 — dead flat. The gap
is a $6.0M tax charge on $9.6M of pretax income: a $2.9M rise in reserves for uncertain tax
positions plus a $1.0M valuation allowance. Full-year adjusted EBITDA rose 15.3% to $50.5M and
gross margin went 15.0% → 16.5% under a CEO two years into the job, on $5.4M of restructuring
savings and a 7% headcount cut. $108.2M net cash — 37% of the market cap — zero debt, $39M of
free cash flow, and a fresh $40M buyback (13.7% of the cap) untouched at year end.

**Why conviction 3 and not 5:** GM, Ford and Stellantis are 64% of sales, management expects
those three to fall 5–6% next year, replacement business doesn't start until model year 2029 at
the earliest, manufacturing is in Mexico so peso strength is a direct cost, and USMCA is
unresolved. The note carries a bear/base/bull with every assumption shown; I'm not posting the
numbers here, because the assumptions are the part worth arguing about.

**Pre-registered kill criteria** (written before publication, so I can't move them later):
gross margin below 15.0% for two consecutive quarters; a filing disclosing loss of programs worth
more than $25M of annual sales; an acquisition above $75M, or net cash below $60M without a
matching share-count reduction; or no repurchases under the $40M authorization by the February
2027 filing.

Note: https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/notes/STRT.html · all 82:
https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/notes/ · dashboard:
https://davespinelli.github.io/Claude-Space/

Research and education only — not investment advice and not a recommendation to buy or sell
anything. Tear the thesis apart; that's what publishing the kill criteria is for.

## 3. Show HN

**Title (75 chars):** Show HN: Open-source stock research desk that runs on GitHub Actions for $0

**Text:**

I'm a CFO, not an engineer, and I built this with Claude over about a week to see how far a
research pipeline gets on free infrastructure. Four pieces, all in one repo:

1. A screener that hits the SEC XBRL `frames` API once per concept for every filer at once,
   rather than once per company, and narrows the whole filer population down to 484 non-financial
   operating companies between $50M and $2B.
2. An EDGAR fetcher, rate-limited to 8 req/s, that pulls 10-K Items 1/1A/7, 10-Q MD&A, proxies,
   six months of 8-Ks and twelve months of Form 4s into plain text.
3. LLM triage over all 489 of them — a 25–40k character pack per ticker, scored 1–10, with the
   reasoning kept — feeding a deep-dive step that has produced 82 full research notes.
4. A backtest engine with a written anti-overfitting protocol: next-day execution, 10 bps costs,
   both-halves reporting, and a walk-forward gate. 349 scripts and 3,310 result rows so far, and
   the failures stay in the leaderboard.

Everything runs on GitHub Actions cron jobs and publishes to GitHub Pages. Cost is $0.

Honest about what it isn't: the equity universe is current-constituent, so backtested CAGR is
survivorship-optimistic. Only 2020 and 2022 are genuine stress tests in the sample. The live
paper record is days old. **No live edge is proven** — what I can show is that the process kills
its own ideas, which is most of what it does.

## 4. LinkedIn

**Text:**

I spent the last week building something I've wanted for years: a research desk that shows its
work.

I'm a CFO. I read filings for a living, and I've always been suspicious of investment claims that
arrive without the reasoning attached. So I built the opposite, with Claude as the engineer.

It does two things. It reads: 489 US companies under $2 billion, triaged from their own SEC
filings, with 82 full research notes written so far — each one carrying a valuation with its
assumptions visible and, more importantly, pre-registered criteria that would prove the thesis
wrong. And it tests: over 3,300 backtested strategy variants under a protocol written before the
results, with realistic costs, next-day execution and a walk-forward step.

The unglamorous finding is the one I'd underline. Most of what we tested died — including our own
first signal, which turned out to be cancelling itself out. Publishing the failures is the whole
point.

It's open source, free, and it's research, not advice.
