## 3. Claim & submission mechanics for the top 2 platforms

Note: neither of the top two is Algora. Algora's Stripe-Connect flow is no longer the relevant path, because its board is 404 and its org pages read 0 open.

### A. comma.ai — openpilot / opendbc (best realistic option)

Source: the `openpilot bounties` project board description at `github.com/orgs/commaai/projects/26`, read directly.

**Rules, verbatim from the board:**
- Code must be merged into openpilot master.
- Bounty eligibility is solely at comma's discretion.
- Once you open a PR, the bounty is locked to you until you stop working on it.
- To claim: **open a ticket at `comma.ai/support` (shop-order support form) with links to your PRs**.
- **You get an extra 20% if you redeem the bounty in comma shop credit** (including refunds on previous orders).
- For bounties **over $100, the first PR with substantial work gets a lock, which times out after a week of no progress**.
- If a bounty is closed rather than merged, comma still **pays $100 for getting it closed with a good explanation**.

**comma's own effort calibration** (this is their scale, and it is a useful reality check on my effort column):
- `<= $100` — a good intro for someone new to openpilot
- `$300` — **a day of work for an experienced openpilot developer**
- `$500` — a few days of work for an experienced openpilot developer
- `$1k+` — a week of work

**Accounts David must create:** a GitHub account (he has one) and a comma.ai support account to file the claim ticket. **No Stripe Connect, no bounty-platform signup.** Payout method is not stated publicly on the board; it is arranged through the support ticket. Joining the comma Discord `#contributing` channel is how bounties get proposed and negotiated, and is effectively expected.

**The lock rule is the single most important mechanic here.** It is first-PR-with-substantial-work, not first-comment. There is no "claim then disappear" — but it also means someone can be ahead of you invisibly. Check the linked PRs on an issue before starting.

### B. Tenstorrent — tt-metal (highest dollar amounts, currently closed to newcomers)

Source: `docs.tenstorrent.com/bounty_terms.html` and the repo's issue labels.

- **You must be assigned on GitHub to the issue before you submit**, and must still be assigned when the PR lands. If reassigned, you forfeit.
- Tenstorrent may reassign after **2+ weeks of contributor inactivity** or explicit forfeiture. This is your only realistic way in right now — watch for the 10 current assignees to go stale.
- Issues must carry both a `bounty` tag and a `bounty_difficulty/*` tag.
- Published payout bands: **Warmup $1–200, Easy $201–500, Medium $501–1,999, Hard $2,000–3,000.** (Note: some live issues advertise well above this band, e.g. #54016 at $35,000 — the terms page and the issue titles do not agree, so treat headline amounts as negotiable rather than contractual.)
- Only merged PRs count. Payout is "as determined solely in Tenstorrent's discretion."
- **Eligibility:** legal age of majority; not a Tenstorrent employee/contractor/vendor; not in an embargoed country; no conflicting employment obligations.
- **Payment platform and tax forms are not disclosed publicly** — the terms only say you must "provide additional information as may be required." Expect a W-9 and a vendor-payment onboarding step.
- **Hard blocker:** using AI agents or automation to claim or request assignment is **explicitly prohibited and results in a permanent repo ban.** Request assignment by hand, in your own words.
- Model bring-up bounties pay **only after all 3 stages complete** — not on first merge.

## 4. Verdict — can bounties realistically produce $200+ in 3 weeks?

1. **Yes, but only barely, and only on one specific bet: comma.ai `opendbc#2557` ($300) or `openpilot#32425` ($500).** These are Python, need no hardware, and are the only unassigned items where the money clears $200 in a single merge.
2. **The odds are worse than the dollar figures suggest.** Every one of those issues already carries 7–12 linked pull requests, meaning multiple competent people tried and failed or drifted. You are not picking up neglected work; you are entering a graveyard of prior attempts.
3. **The market has structurally collapsed since 2024.** Algora's public board is a 404, Polar's issue funding is in maintenance mode, and the entire quant-finance ecosystem you hoped for has literally zero bounty-labeled issues. What's left is two funded programs, and one of them (Tenstorrent) has all ten of its bounties already assigned.
4. **Expected value is poor per hour.** comma itself prices $300 as "a day of work for an *experienced openpilot developer*" — you'd be paying a multi-day onboarding tax on a codebase with a CAN-bus/safety-model learning curve, against a one-week PR lock, for discretionary payment. Realistically 20–35 hours for that $300, and merge is not guaranteed.
5. **My honest recommendation: treat this as a low-probability lottery ticket, not a plan.** If you want the shot, open a substantial PR on `opendbc#2557` within days to grab the lock, and take the 20% comma-shop-credit bonus only if you actually want comma hardware. If the goal is genuinely $200+ in 3 weeks with confidence, contract work or a paid freelance ticket will beat this on both probability and hourly rate — the bounty market in September 2026 is too thin to rely on.
