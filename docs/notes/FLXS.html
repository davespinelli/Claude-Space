1. What the business actually does

A 130-year-old residential furniture manufacturer, importer and marketer in Dubuque, Iowa, one reportable segment (10-K 2026, Item 1). Sofas, recliners, bedroom, dining and occasional furniture sold through retail partners and online under Flexsteel plus Zecliner, Statements, Zen, Perfect Match and Pulse. Roughly half the model is manufacturing: three leased plants in Juarez, Mexico with about 900 employees, plus a fourth, idle facility in Mexicali; the rest is finished goods sourced from Vietnam. Backlog was $70.1M at 2026-06-30 against $66.5M a year earlier.

2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case

There was a real one and it has mostly closed. On 2026-04-26 the board bought 1,279,870 shares, about 24% of the company, from the Bertsch founding family at $47.00, a 2.5% discount to the prior close; director F. Brooks Bertsch resigned on closing (8-K 2026-04-28, Items 1.01 and 5.02). The sellers said they were diversifying "for financial planning purposes" across generations, which is indifference to price, not a view on value. That one transaction lifted per-share earnings power roughly 30% overnight while every trailing per-share statistic, this desk's screen included, still carried the pre-deal count. But the market found it: the screen row shows the stock up 83% over six months, closing 2026-09-09 at its 52-week high. Buying a re-rating after it happens is a different trade.

3. GAAP to normalised, and unit economics

FY2026 reconciles cleanly (FY2026 release, non-GAAP tables): $42.550M GAAP operating income less the $9.009M refund plus $0.905M of exit cost equals $34.446M adjusted, a 7.5% margin against 7.1% in FY2025. The refund is genuinely non-recurring: after the February 2026 Supreme Court decision voiding the IEEPA tariffs, the Court of International Trade ordered refunds, and "as of June 30, 2026, we had received substantially all of our eligible refund claims" (Item 1A). It is not costless either; Item 1A warns customers and suppliers "may assert claims seeking reimbursement, credits or other payments" relating to those refunds. Strip it and Q4 adjusted operating margin was 7.1% against 9.0% a year earlier, on 0.7% sales growth. The prior-year artefact ran the other way: FY2025 carried a $14.1M non-cash impairment of the Mexicali right-of-use asset against $9.4M of real estate gains (Item 7). Two artefacts in two years, both disclosed, neither an operating cash cost, so this is not serial impairment of an eroding asset base. The Mexicali lease still runs to 2034 with the plant unopened and unsublet inside the $35.7M ROU balance, carrying an explicit further-write-down warning (Item 1A). Gross margin excluding the refund is 22.7%, roughly flat with 22.2%. Capex of $3.9M against $3.8M depreciation means high returns on tangible capital and minimal reinvestment.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

No borrowings at 2026-06-30, $16.7M cash, $133.5M equity. Liquidity is thinner than it looks: the revolver went from $85M to $55M in June 2025 and was replaced on 2026-08-18 by a $30M facility maturing 2029 (Item 7). FY2026 uses of cash were $63.7M of buybacks and $4.4M of dividends against $51.5M of operating cash flow, so the repurchase was part-funded from the $40M opening cash balance. Buying a quarter of the company at $47 with the stock now at $84 is the best capital allocation this desk has seen in the industrial lane this week. CEO Derek Schmidt held 341,059 shares including 122,450 options at 2025-10-13, 6.2% then and nearer 8% of the shrunken count now (DEF 14A). Against that, Form 4s show no open-market insider purchases in twelve months and $1.87M of sales (form4_summary.md).

5. Management: what they said versus what they did

Schmidt's claim of "more than $47.5 million of free cash flow" (FY2026 release) is arithmetically right but includes the $9.0M refund and a $5.2M working capital release; steady state (adjusted net income $27.0M plus $3.8M depreciation less $3.9M capex) is about $27M. The FY2025 incentive plan targeted $26.2M of adjusted operating income and $431.6M of sales and paid at 200% and 122% of target against $31.2M actual (DEF 14A). Targets that max out are set low.

6. Valuation

Reverse DCF: at a $365M EV on $25.8M of normalised after-tax operating profit, a 9% cost of capital implies about 1.9% perpetual growth and a 10% cost about 2.9%. Fair, not cheap. Three cases on ~4.53M diluted shares, to FY2028. Bear (30%): the Section 232 seating tariff steps from 25% to 30% on 2027-01-01 as scheduled (Item 7) and is not fully passed through, demand stays weak, sales $450M at a 5.5% margin, 11x, about $49. Base (45%): 2.5% annual growth to $482M at a 7.0% margin, 14x, plus accumulated cash, about $90. Bull (25%): tariffs settle, housing turnover recovers, $510M at 8.5%, 16x, about $128. Probability-weighted, roughly $87 against $84.20.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q1 FY2027 results in late October 2026 test the 6.5-7.0% margin guide against energy and freight inflation. The 2027-01-01 Section 232 step-up is a dated negative. A Mexicali sublease or lease exit would remove a standing impairment risk. More buybacks are possible now the family block is gone, but at $84 they are far less accretive than at $47.

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

  1. Adjusted operating margin below 6.5% in two consecutive quarters.
  2. Net sales decline year over year in any quarter, ending the eleven-quarter streak and confirming that surcharge pricing no longer covers volume loss.
  3. More than $15M of buybacks executed above $80 per share, or any equity issuance: the capital-allocation case rests on the discipline shown at $47.
  4. A further Mexicali right-of-use impairment, or disclosure of material customer claims for reimbursement of the IEEPA refunds (Item 1A).

9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary

WATCH, conviction 2. Flexsteel did a genuinely good thing: it bought 24% of itself from a founding family at $47 in April, carries no debt, and has eleven straight quarters of sales growth. The screen makes it look mispriced for the wrong reason, carrying a pre-buyback share count that overstates market cap by about $105M and FY2025 fundamentals that miss the 10-K filed three weeks ago. Correct both and the enterprise trades at 14x normalised after-tax operating profit, implying about 2% perpetual growth, roughly what a residential furniture importer should earn. The catch is that FY2026 GAAP profit is flattered by $8.1M of one-time tariff refunds, underlying Q4 margin fell to 7.1% from 9.0%, growth is now surcharge pricing rather than units, the Section 232 seating tariff is scheduled to rise from 25% to 30% on 1 January 2027, and the stock is up 83% in six months at its 52-week high. The edge case was real and is largely spent. Wait for the Q1 FY2027 print to show whether 7% margins survive the tariff step-up, or for a price nearer $60, rather than paying the post-buyback price for a business the market has already re-rated.
