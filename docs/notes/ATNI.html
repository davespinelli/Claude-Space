ATNI — ATN International, Inc. · 2026-09-16 · Verdict: PASS · Conviction 4

Desk stats

  • Revenue trend. FY2025 revenue $727.975M, down 0.2% from $729.075M, with International up 1.2% to $381.9M and US Telecom down 1.6% to $346.1M on the April 2024 expiry of the ECF and ACP subsidies (10-K, Item 7). Q2 2026 revenue $184.504M, up 1.8%, driven by carrier services (up $3.1M to $36.3M) and ancillary services, offset by lower fixed consumer and construction revenue (8-K 2026-08-06, EX-99.1). Volume and subsidy mix, not price.
  • Normalised after-tax operating profit. FY2025 GAAP operating income $28.434M, plus restructuring and reorganization $10.157M, plus transaction-related charges $3.576M, plus the $1.449M net loss on disposition of assets, equals normalised EBIT $43.616M; taxed at 25%, $32.7M. There is no impairment add-back: the $35.269M goodwill charge was taken in FY2024, and the 10-K states "No such impairments were recorded during the year ended December 31, 2025" (10-K, Item 7).
  • EV / normalised after-tax profit. EV from the 30 June 2026 balance sheet: $461.9M equity, plus $513.3M total debt and $35.2M receivables credit facility, less $331.9M cash and restricted cash, plus the $47.8M step-up in taxes payable created by the tower gain, plus $217.9M of redeemable and ordinary non-controlling interests at book, equals $944.3M, or 28.9x the $32.7M above. Excluding the minorities it is 22.2x.
  • Leverage. Net debt $181.4M on FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $190.0M is 0.95x, matching the company's 0.91x (8-K 2026-08-06). That ratio is consolidated: $333.7M of the debt sits in US Telecom against roughly $77.2M of annualised segment Adjusted EBITDA (H1 2026 doubled), about 4.3x, and $320M of it is Alaska Communications' own facility on which "the Company is not a guarantor" (10-Q, Liquidity).
  • Is the growth sustainable? No. Homes passed rose 21% to 523,400 while broadband customers rose 1% to 140,900 and fell sequentially from 143,200, so penetration went from 32.2% to 26.9%; mobile subscribers were flat at 386,600 and blended churn rose to 3.48% from 3.09% (8-K 2026-08-06). 2026 Adjusted EBITDA is guided to $183-193M against $190.0M actual in FY2025.
  • What the screen got wrong. The screen tagged the FY2024 goodwill impairment into FY2025, adding $35.3M of profit that was never there. That single error created both the "GAAP artefact" flag and the growth gate: on the screen's own definition, normalised EBIT went from $34.5M in FY2024 to $28.4M in FY2025, down 17.5%, and with revenue down 0.2% ATNI does not pass gate 1 at all. The screen also missed $23.7M of current-portion debt, the $35.2M receivables facility, the $47.8M tax payable on the tower gain and $217.9M of minority interests.

1. What the business actually does

ATN owns controlling stakes in rural and remote telecom operators: fixed, mobile, carrier and managed services in Bermuda, the Cayman Islands, Guyana and the US Virgin Islands (International Telecom, 52% of revenue), and fixed, carrier and managed services in Alaska and the rural western US (US Telecom) (10-K, Item 1). It owns approximately 52% of Alaska Communications, with Freedom 3 Capital and other institutional investors holding 48%, and less than 100% of Commnet after the 2022 Sacred Wind transaction (10-K, Item 1). Revenue is heavily programme-funded: roughly $45M a year of federal high-cost support (Alaska Connect Fund $25.6M to 2028, E-ACAM about $9M, CAF II about $8M, RDOF $2.3M, Alaska state USF $2.5M) plus $19.0M of Rural Health Care funding in 2025 (10-K, Item 1).

2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case

There is none, and that decides the verdict. The apparent edge was the 2 June 2026 sale of 214 towers to Everest Infrastructure Partners for up to $297M, with $267.7M received at the initial closing and a $229.9M book gain (8-K 2026-06-03; 10-Q). But the market has already paid for it: the stock is 6.6% below its 52-week high after a 114% twelve-month move excluding the last month, and the only identifiable insider activity is persistent selling. Cornelius B. Prior Jr., the founder and 29.6% holder (DEF 14A 2026-04-29), sold in almost every week from late June to 3 September at $27.05 to $33.25, taking one account from 87,436 shares to 30,041; a director and the departing general counsel also sold. Zero open-market purchases by any insider in twelve months (Form 4s).

3. Unit economics and growth

Normalised EBIT of $43.6M on $728.0M of revenue is a 6.0% operating margin, and on invested capital of $935.9M (equity $719.3M plus debt less cash) the normalised after-tax return is 3.5%. That is the whole story. FY2025 D&A of $137.9M against $90.0M of non-reimbursable capex tells you the asset base is being harvested, not compounded, and the FY2025 net loss attributable to ATN of $14.9M became $21.0M and $(1.38) per share after $6.1M of accrued preferred dividends on the minority securities, up from $5.6M (10-K, Item 7). The 21% expansion in homes passed that produced a 1% customer gain is a failed growth investment, and Item 1A names the reason: "Starlink began offering direct-to-consumer products, which in some locations within our markets is a direct competitive alternative to our new fiber offerings. Given the high capital investments that we have already made in the new fiber offerings, this competition may have an adverse impact on our anticipated return on investment" (10-K, Item 1A).

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Of the $331.9M of cash, $222.1M sits at US Telecom, $100.4M at International and only $9.5M at Corporate (8-K 2026-08-06, Table 4). The lender consent explicitly permits "distributions of the Net Cash Proceeds from the Tower Portfolio Transaction to the Company and the minority shareholders of the Commnet Parties" (10-Q), so part of that cash is not ATN's; NCI was allocated $19.2M of Q2 income. Item 1A warns ATN "may be required to repurchase such securities" from the Alaska Communications and Sacred Wind minority holders. Guyana cash is trapped by a US dollar shortage severe enough that vendors refuse local currency (10-K, Item 1A). Uses so far: $76.8M of debt repaid, the dividend raised 5.5% to $0.29 ($17.9M a year, 3.9% yield), and a buyback authorisation raised to $30M with nothing repurchased in Q2 (8-K 2026-08-06). Share count is drifting up, not down.

5. Management: what they said vs what they did

Brad Martin ceased to be CEO on 6 April 2026; Naji Khoury started 20 April and owned zero shares at the proxy date (8-K 2026-04-07; DEF 14A). The general counsel agreed to step down on 27 July (8-K 2026-07-31). On guidance, the May release promised $190-200M of Adjusted EBITDA excluding the tower sale, less $6-8M for the sale (8-K 2026-05-07), and August reaffirmed $183-193M. Since H1 delivered $98.4M, the implied H2 is $85-95M. Management's own arithmetic says the second half is smaller than the first.

6. Valuation

Base: flat revenue, Adjusted EBITDA $185M, capex $110M, cash interest about $37M, cash taxes $12M, leaves roughly $26M of consolidated free cash flow before any leakage to minorities, so under $20M to ATN, about 4% of market value. At 6x that is $18 to $20 a share. Bear: ACF support resets downward in 2029, USVI support stays lapsed, penetration keeps falling, EBITDA drifts to $170M and the dividend is cut; $12 to $14. Bull: the buyback is executed, subsequent tower closings add $29.6M, International holds 10% margin expansion and the market pays 6.5x EV/EBITDA on $195M; $40. Probability-weighted at 50/25/25, about $23. Reverse DCF: at $29.99 with $944M of EV against $32.7M of normalised after-tax profit, the price implies roughly 6% perpetual growth in operating profit at a 10% discount rate, from a company whose revenue has shrunk two years running.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Subsequent tower closings worth up to $29.6M over the next ten to eleven months; the announced sale of certain US spectrum licences; the FCC decision on restoring $5.5M of USVI support; execution or non-execution of the $30M buyback; the new CEO's first strategic plan.

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

These would reverse the PASS if the price fell materially: 1. High-speed broadband customers rise above 150,000 with penetration stabilising above 28% for two consecutive quarters. 2. The company repurchases more than $20M of stock, roughly 4% of shares, within two quarters. 3. ATN buys in the Alaska Communications minority, or sells Alaska, at a price that lifts look-through free cash flow per share above $2.00. 4. Any insider open-market purchase above $500,000, against the current record of zero.

9. Verdict and summary

PASS, conviction 4. ATN International only reached the deep-dive queue because the screen booked a goodwill impairment from fiscal 2024 into fiscal 2025, inventing $35.3M of operating profit; correct the period and revenue fell 0.2%, normalised operating profit fell 17.5%, and the company fails the growth gate outright. What is left is a $462M rural telecom holding company that has just sold 214 towers for $297M, which is real value creation at a multiple management correctly says "far exceeded our current trading multiple", but the stock has already risen 114% over twelve months and sits 6.6% below its high, so you are paying for it. At $29.99 the enterprise value is $944M once you add the $47.8M tax bill the gain created, the $35.2M receivables facility and $217.9M of minority interests, against $32.7M of normalised after-tax operating profit, or 28.9x, for a business earning a 3.5% return on invested capital, guiding second-half EBITDA below first-half, and watching broadband penetration fall from 32% to 27% because it passed 91,000 more homes and won 1,500 more customers while Starlink took the rest. The consolidated 0.9x leverage hides $320M of non-recourse debt at a 52%-owned Alaska subsidiary levered above 4x, a $6.1M and rising annual preferred accrual, cash that is mostly stranded in Guyana and at subsidiaries with minority partners entitled to a share, and a contractual obligation that may force ATN to buy those partners out. The only insider trading in a year is the 29.6% founder selling every week. Good asset sale, wrong price, wrong screen row.
