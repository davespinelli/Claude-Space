1. What the business actually does

An externally managed agricultural REIT owning 144 farms, 98,688 acres across 14 states, plus 55,532 acre-feet of California water, leased mostly triple-net to farmers (10-K 2025, Item 1). Concentration is fresh-produce row crops and California permanent crops: almonds, pistachios, blueberries, wine grapes. There are no employees. Gladstone Management Corporation, controlled by David Gladstone, takes 0.60% a year of gross tangible real estate before depreciation plus an FFO-based incentive fee, and an affiliated broker-dealer collects financing and dealer-manager fees (10-K 2025, Items 1 and 7).

2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case

There isn't one. The stock is down 18% in six months and 22% off its 52-week high, and the screen ranked it tenth in the REIT lane at 14.6x P/FFO, but the discount is not a misunderstanding. It is the market pricing a shrinking rent roll, a distribution AFFO does not cover, and a preferred stack the screen never saw. The one genuinely underappreciated item is the preferred arbitrage in section 4, and it is too small to carry the equity.

3. Unit economics (REIT lane)

The portfolio is decaying at the edges. Twenty-four farms were vacant, direct-operated or on non-accrual in Q2 2026 against 16 a year earlier; eleven farms leased to four tenants are on cash-basis recognition for collectability (10-Q Q2 2026). Those farms produced $535,000 of lease revenue in H1 2026 versus $6.7M a year earlier. Occupancy of 95.4% flatters this, because direct-operated and non-accrual farms count as occupied. Impairments were $3.9M in 2025 and $5.1M in H1 2026, including $4.2M on four Yuma County, Arizona farms written to an agreed sale price, a third straight year of write-downs and so not one-off. Seven leases signed in Q2 cut annual NOI by about $931,000; seven signed after quarter end added $297,000 (Q2 2026 release). Twenty-one leases on 19.0% of acreage expire in 2027. Genuine positives: 2026 pistachio initial base pricing up 67% to $2.50/lb, almond prices 15-20% higher, and Q2 operating cash flow up $16.0M year over year.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Debt of $487.8M is about 96% fixed at an effective 3.39% for 2.7 more years, weighted-average term 6.6 years, with $120M of liquidity (Q2 2026 release; 10-K 2025, Item 7). That liability book is genuinely good. The problem sits above it. The company sold 5,253,748 shares at an average $9.58 from January 2025 through February 2026, and 5,121,655 more at $10.03 between 1 April and 11 August 2026 (10-K 2025; 10-Q Q2 2026), about 29% share-count growth in twenty months. Weighted-average shares went from 36.18M to 42.91M year over year, with ~3.7M more sold after 30 June. Proceeds redeemed $60.4M of Series D term preferred in January and repurchased Series B and C at $20.61 to $21.07 against a $25 liquidation preference. That trade is accretive: ~$9.92 of net proceeds retires ~0.48 preferred shares and $0.72 of preferred dividends against $0.56 of new common distributions. But the authorisation is only $55M a year while the ATM raised $51.4M in four months, so most of the equity funds redemptions and distributions, not the arbitrage. AFFO has not covered the payout: FY2025 $14.4M against roughly $20.4M declared; FY2024 $16.7M against $20.1M. David Gladstone owns 6.03%, all officers and directors 6.13% (DEF 14A 2026), with no Form 4 purchases in twelve months.

5. Management: said versus did

Gladstone calls the lease modifications "temporary" and targets "a return to more traditional lease structures that include fixed base rents" (Q2 2026 release). Against that, problem farms rose from 16 to 24 and Q2 net leasing subtracted NOI. He calls the balance sheet "in excellent condition," citing fixed-rate debt; true of the debt, silent on the preferred. The adviser waived a $2.7M incentive fee in Q4 2025 but is under no obligation to keep doing so (10-K 2025, Item 1A).

6. Valuation

Base: FY2026 FFO of $0.33 to $0.38 a share on the Q4-weighted structure, 20-24x, $7.50-8.50. Bear: two or three 2027 expirations go unlet, the distribution is cut to AFFO coverage near $0.35, yield buyers leave, $6.00-6.50. Bull: pistachio and almond pricing holds, the repositioned farms return to fixed rent adding $10-12M of FFO, preferred falls under $200M, FFO near $0.55 at 20x, $11.00. Probability-weighted, roughly $8.00-8.50 against $9.48. Reverse DCF: at $9.48 the market pays about $1.29B for 98,688 acres and 55,649 acre-feet of water, roughly $13,100 an acre with the water free, about 15% above the $11,446-an-acre gross book cost, defensible only if California permanent-crop land is appreciating while the company impairs farms in Arizona, Florida and California.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q3 2026 results in early November for the 2025 pistachio marketing settlement; Q4 and FY2026 results in February 2027, where management says most of the year's earnings land; 2027 expirations on 19% of acreage negotiated through 2026-27; any change to the monthly $0.0467 distribution.

8. Pre-registered kill criteria (what would make this PASS wrong)

  1. TTM AFFO per share covers the declared distribution (at or above $0.56) for two consecutive quarters.
  2. Q4 2026 FFO to common exceeds $20M, i.e. FY2026 FFO per share above $0.45.
  3. Farms vacant, direct-operated or on non-accrual fall to 16 or fewer for two consecutive quarters and fixed lease payments return to year-over-year growth.
  4. Remaining preferred liquidation preference falls below $250M with the ATM funding it rather than the distribution.

9. Verdict and summary

PASS, conviction 4. Gladstone Land screens as a 14.6x P/FFO farmland REIT growing FFO 6.8%, and every part of that is an artifact. The screen ignored roughly $385M of 6% preferred ranking ahead of the common, so real EV is about $1.29B, not $872M; it left a $13.9M gain on farm sales inside FFO, which NAREIT and the company both exclude, so reported FFO to common is $14.2M and the multiple about 29x; and that FFO fell 32% year over year rather than growing. The revenue growth is gross crop sales from farms the company had to take back and operate at a 79% cost of sales, while lease revenue fell 10.2% in 2025 and fixed base rent another 16.9% in H1 2026. Underneath sits a decent, cheaply financed farmland portfolio, with 96% fixed-rate debt at 3.39%, real pistachio and almond price strength, and an intelligent trade issuing common near $10 to retire preferred near $20.60. But it pays a $0.56 distribution out of $0.37 of AFFO, funds the gap with an ATM that has grown the share count 29% in twenty months, and has watched problem farms rise from 16 to 24. You pay about $13,100 an acre for land carried at $11,446. Come back if Q4 delivers, not before.
