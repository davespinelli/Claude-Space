1. What the business actually does

Unity Bancorp is the holding company for Unity Bank, a New Jersey state-chartered commercial bank founded in 1991, running twenty-two branches along the Route 22 and Route 78 corridors in eight New Jersey counties plus Northampton County, Pennsylvania, with 249 full-time equivalent employees (10-K 2025, Item 1; 8-K 2026-07-14, EX-99.1). It is a small-business and commercial real estate lender first: commercial loans are 61.5% of the $2.68B book, residential mortgage 24.9%, and 96% of all loans are secured by real estate (10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2). It also originates and sells SBA 7(a) and conforming residential mortgages, the source of most fee income. Total assets are $3.19B, deposits $2.46B.

2. Why it is mispriced, the edge case

There is no edge case, and that governs the verdict. Unity is not a spin-off, is not structurally uncovered, has no misread segment and no forced seller. The one unusual fact in the file is that director Robert H. Dallas II died on 2026-08-03 (8-K 2026-08-07, Item 5.02). He was a founding director and 50% owner of Dallas Financial Holdings LLC, which holds 1,775,735 shares, 17.7% of the company, beneficially reported by both him and his brother, Chairman David D. Dallas; directors and officers as a group own 28.87% (DEF 14A 2026-03-06). An estate holding a $106M illiquid block in a $600M bank can produce either a supply overhang or a sale of the company, but no filing says which, and that is a guess, not a thesis. The screen's cheapness signal is itself the artefact: a stale fiscal year with one-time securities gains in it.

3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix

The franchise economics are genuinely top-decile for a $3B bank: net interest margin of 4.56% in Q2 2026 against 4.49% a year earlier, an efficiency ratio of 40.50%, and a 2.01% return on average assets (8-K 2026-07-14, EX-99.1). The margin comes from asset yield, not cheap funding, with a 6.73% loan yield against a 2.88% cost of interest-bearing deposits.

Credit is moving the wrong way. Nonaccrual loans went from $15.8M, 0.66% of loans, at 2025-06-30 to $33.0M, 1.23%, at 2026-06-30 (8-K 2026-07-14, EX-99.1). Management attributes "nearly half" to one $15.5M well-secured commercial real estate relationship that migrated in Q4 2025 and is still there three quarters later. Less comforting is the rest: potential problem loans, performing but non-pass rated, doubled from $11.5M to $22.6M in the six months to 2026-06-30 (10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2), and allowance coverage of nonaccruals fell from 183% to 105%. Against that, charge-offs remain almost nil, with net recoveries of $0.1M in H1 2026, and commercial mortgage loan-to-value averages 55.2% (10-K 2025, Item 7). Commercial real estate including construction is 56.6% of the book, which the company flags as a regulatory concentration risk (10-K 2025, Item 1A).

Funding is the second watch item. The deposit base is 19.8% noninterest-bearing and 40.2% time, with brokered deposits at 12.2% and rising, and 21.8% of deposits uninsured or uncollateralised (10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2). That is a fine funding mix while spreads hold and a fast one to reprice against you when they do not.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Capital is ample and rising: leverage 13.17%, CET1 14.19%, tangible common equity 11.6% of assets. Borrowings are $326.4M, of which $246.1M matures within one year, plus $10.3M of 2006-vintage trust preferred at SOFR plus 26bp maturing 2036 (10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2). Share count is static at 10.04M, with only 6,616 shares repurchased in H1 2026 at $49.01 while 562,000 shares, 5.6% of the company, remain authorised and unused. Capital returns go to the dividend instead, raised 6% to $0.17 quarterly in August (8-K 2026-08-20), still only about 11% of normalised earnings. CEO James Hughes was paid $2.70M in 2025, with a $573,580 restricted stock grant vesting over four years, and owns 303,837 shares (DEF 14A 2026-03-06). Insider trading over the last twelve months is one-way: zero open-market purchases against 25,837 shares sold for $1.37M, including President George Boyan selling 7,349 shares at about $59.15 on 2 and 3 September 2026, within 5% of the screen price (Form 4, 2026-09-04).

5. Management: said versus did

Hughes said in July that Unity was positioned "to continue delivering profitable growth, maintaining strong asset quality" (8-K 2026-07-14, EX-99.1). The first half is true on revenue and false on per-share earnings, which are flat. On asset quality the claim is contradicted by the company's own table two pages later. What management has done well is run the margin and buy tax credits: $8.0M of federal credits for $7.5M in Q4 2025, $5.1M in Q1 2026, $2.6M of state credits in Q2 2026, and now $27.6M for $25.1M in September at $0.91 on the dollar, subject to a five-year recapture period with contractual protections (10-K 2025, Item 7; 8-K 2026-09-10, Item 7.01). That is real value, roughly $2.5M, but it is a treasury trade rather than franchise earnings and should not be capitalised at a bank multiple.

6. Valuation

Normalised EPS is $5.48. Base: 8% net interest income growth, core fee income near $11M, expenses up 5%, provision normalising to about $5M, gives 2027 EPS near $6.00; at 10.5x that is $63, plus roughly $1.30 of dividends, so about $64. Bear: the $15.5M relationship charges off and the doubled criticised book produces 30bp of credit cost, while margin gives back 40bp as time deposits and brokered funding reprice; EPS $4.50 and the multiple compresses to 1.1x tangible book, about $42. Bull: margin holds above 4.5%, loans compound 10%, credit stays benign, 2027 EPS $6.50 at 12x, about $78. Weighting 50/25/25 gives roughly $62 against $59.73, about 4% of upside. Reverse DCF: at 1.62x tangible book, the price implies a steady-state return on tangible equity of about 13% to 15% at a 10% to 11% cost of equity and 5% growth, against 16.2% normalised today. In other words the market is already assuming the margin fades or credit costs normalise. It is not assuming disaster, and it is not offering a discount.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q3 2026 results in mid-October, which will carry the $2.5M tax-credit benefit and, more importantly, show whether nonaccruals and criticised loans stabilise. Resolution or charge-off of the $15.5M commercial real estate relationship. Any use of the 562,000-share buyback authorisation. Disposition or non-disposition of the Dallas estate's interest.

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

Concentration in New Jersey commercial real estate, wholesale funding dependence, and a margin that is high enough to be worth fading. Kill criteria, any one of which ends the watch: 1. Nonaccrual loans above 1.50% of total loans, or potential problem loans above $30M, in any quarter through Q2 2027 (base 1.23% and $22.6M at 2026-06-30). 2. Net interest margin below 4.20% for two consecutive quarters (base 4.56% in Q2 2026). 3. Noninterest-bearing demand deposits below 18.0% of total deposits, or brokered deposits above 15.0% of total deposits (base 19.8% and 12.2% at 2026-06-30). 4. Insider open-market sales above $2M in any rolling six months with no offsetting open-market purchases (base $1.37M sold and zero bought in the last twelve months).

9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary

WATCH, conviction 3. Unity Bancorp is a genuinely good small bank at a fair price, and the screen's 10.4x P/E and 39.8% earnings growth are both stale: FY2025 GAAP net income of $58.0M contains $6.1M of securities gains and reserve releases that management itself strips out, and the live run rate is H1 2026 net income up 2.4% with Q2 diluted EPS down 11.8% year over year. Normalised trailing profit is $55.9M, so the stock trades at 10.9x normalised earnings and 1.62x tangible book for a 16.2% normalised return on tangible equity, a 4.56% net interest margin and a 40.5% efficiency ratio, which is a good franchise priced about right rather than a mispricing. Two facts keep it off the idea list: nonaccrual loans nearly doubled to 1.23% of loans in twelve months while performing criticised loans doubled again in six, and growth is being funded at the margin with brokered deposits and FHLB advances rather than core demand deposits. Insiders sold $1.37M and bought nothing in a year, with the President selling at $59.15 two weeks ago. The price already implies steady-state return on tangible equity fading to 13% to 15%, so there is no discount left for the credit risk that is visibly building. Worth owning materially cheaper, near 1.3x tangible book, or after two quarters of stable nonaccruals.
