1. What the business actually does

OptimumBank is a $1.40 billion Florida state-chartered commercial bank with four offices in Broward and Miami-Dade counties and 98 employees (10-K 2025, Item 1). It lends against South Florida real estate: 95% of the book is real-estate secured, and commercial real estate alone was 69.6% of loans at December 2025. Two niches distinguish it: skilled nursing facilities, where the board claims decades of experience and the bank does CRE plus receivables-based ABL, and treasury management for merchant cash advance providers, a high-volume ACH business that gathers low-cost deposits and fee income. It added SBA 7(a) Preferred Lender status in Q1 2025, then two non-bank subsidiaries: OptimumFunding for bridge-to-HUD financing, and OptimumFinance for CRE bridge lending, which wrote its first loan, $14.2M, in Q2 2026 funded with a $14.0M note payable at 10.00% (Q2 release).

2. Why it is mispriced: there is no edge case

Honestly, none. The apparent cheapness is a data artifact, and once corrected the discount disappears. Nor is this a neglected stock: it is up roughly 130% over twelve months and sits 8.9% below its 52-week high (universe_v2.csv), trades $1.1M a day, and in Q2 2026 alone Brean Capital and A.G.P. initiated with Buy ratings while Compass Point upgraded to Buy, so all three covering firms are positive (Q2 release). There is no forced seller and no misunderstood segment. If anything the artifact cuts the wrong way: screens reading the voting-share count show a $112M market cap at 0.83x book, which attracts buyers rather than repelling them. Absent an edge case, this caps at WATCH.

3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix

The allowance for credit losses has fallen every quarter for a year: 1.19% of loans at June 2025, 1.07% at December 2025, 1.01% at March 2026, 0.91% at June 2026 (Q2 release). The dollar allowance shrank $41K in Q2 while gross loans grew $126.2M, $114.9M of it commercial real estate. That is a reserve release into a 30% loan-growth quarter. Reported credit is pristine: nonaccruals $2.1M, nonperforming assets 0.22% of assets, net charge-offs $11K, no loans 90 days past due, a $1.0M nonaccrual settled after quarter end. But a portfolio compounding at a 30.19% CAGR since 2022 is young, and young CRE books do not season until years three to five. Roughly $1.36M a quarter of the current run rate is reserve normalisation the income statement is not taking.

Margin is genuinely good and improving: NIM 4.57% in Q2, up from 4.49% and 4.32%, earning-asset yields up 22bp and the cost of interest-bearing liabilities down 19bp to 3.30%. Efficiency ratio 48.78%. Funding is the soft spot. Noninterest-bearing demand deposits are 28.6% of deposits, but time deposits were 38.4% at December 2025 and grew 60.7% year over year, and brokered plus listing-service deposits were 24% of the total (10-K 2025, Item 7). Loan-to-deposit is 99.2%: a rate-sensitive, price-competed funding base supporting a concentrated CRE book.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Growth is outrunning retained earnings. Equity to assets fell from 10.97% to 9.59% in six months and bank Tier 1 leverage from 11.39% to 10.54%. The gap is filled with ATM equity ($0.9M in Q2) and, since quarter end, $35M of Tier 2 sub debt at 7.50%. No dividend, no buyback. Insider ownership is real but understated by the proxy, which reports Gubin at 8.01% and Blisko at 9.35% against 12,166,437 voting shares (DEF 14A 2026-03-26); Blisko's Form 4 of 2026-01-30 converted 65 Series B preferred into 531,178 common and left him 615 preferred, so his diluted stake is a multiple of the disclosed figure. Form 4 activity over twelve months is net selling of $216,992 with zero open-market buys, and outgoing CEO Timothy Terry sold his entire 39,304-share position at $4.73 on 2026-02-02, going to zero (Form 4, 2026-02-04). Officer pay is cash-only, while the Chairman took $579,274 of stock as director fees.

5. Management: said versus did

Moishe Gubin, Chairman for sixteen years, added the CEO title effective 2026-05-01 as Terry retired and Braden Smith joined as President (Q2 release). Gubin is careful where it matters: he told investors the preferred exchange "had minimal impact on dilution" because the shares "had already been reflected in our fully diluted share count" (webcast script, 2026-08-13), which is true and more candid than the screen. He guided explicitly to "approximately $1.00 per share to approximately $1.15 per share" off a $0.28 quarterly run rate. The OptimumFinance disclosure is unusually blunt: management said it "leveraged their balance sheet to attain the highest yield they could earn for the risk they took." One inconsistency: the Q2 release calls the credit loss reversal "$0.4 million" while the 10-Q and webcast say $37 thousand, reconcilable as funded versus funded-plus-unfunded, but sloppy.

6. Valuation

Base (50%): TBVPS compounds 15% for three years, below the 18.7% just delivered because sub-debt interest and a normalised provision bite, reaching $8.59; exit at 1.5x tangible book gives $12.89. Bear (25%): the CRE cycle turns, the allowance rebuilds to 1.5% of a $1.5B book ($22.5M versus $11.0M, an $11.5M pre-tax hit, about $0.36 per share after tax), growth stops, and the market pays 1.0x book of roughly $6.50. Bull (25%): guidance is beaten, 2027 EPS reaches $1.25, TBVPS $7.90, and a 22% ROTE grower earns 2.0x, giving $15.80. Probability-weighted value $12.02, about 31% above $9.20, or roughly 9% a year over three years.

Reverse DCF: at 1.63x tangible book, assuming a 10% cost of equity and 4% long-run growth, the price implies a sustainable ROTE of about 13.8% against the 20.34% just reported. The market is already discounting a substantial fade, which is why this is not a PASS. It is also not enough margin to be an IDEA.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q3 2026 results in late October, the first quarter carrying sub-note interest and the first test of whether the allowance ratio keeps falling. Deployment of the $35M of Tier 2 proceeds, which supports roughly $350M of asset growth. OptimumFunding commencing HUD and FHA originations.

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

  1. Allowance for credit losses falls below 0.85% of total loans in any quarter, or the quarterly provision is again less than 0.90% of net loan growth.
  2. Nonaccrual loans exceed 1.0% of gross loans, or any single CRE or skilled-nursing relationship above $10M is placed on nonaccrual.
  3. Reported diluted EPS falls below $0.24 in any quarter, or FY2026 diluted EPS lands outside the guided $1.00 to $1.15.
  4. Bank Tier 1 leverage falls below 9.0%, or the Company raises more than $20M of new equity at below 1.4x tangible book.

9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary

WATCH, conviction 2. The screen shows OptimumBank at 6.7x earnings and 0.83x tangible book because it counted only the 12.2 million voting shares and missed the 11.5 million nonvoting common issued in the Q2 preferred exchange; on the real 23.8 million diluted shares this is a $219M company at 1.63x tangible book and roughly 8 to 9 times management's own $1.00 to $1.15 guidance, a fair price rather than a bargain, and the stock is up 130% in a year with three sell-side Buys, so nobody is mispricing it. The business underneath is genuinely good: 4.57% net interest margin, 48.8% efficiency ratio, 20.3% return on tangible equity, tangible book per share compounding at 18.7%. The reason not to own it yet is that a meaningful slice of reported earnings is a reserve release, with the allowance cut from 1.19% to 0.91% of loans over four quarters while a 70% commercial-real-estate book grew 30%, so normalising the provision and charging the new 7.5% subordinated notes takes earnings power from $1.12 to about $0.855 a share, or 10.8x. At 1.63x book the market already implies a sustainable ROTE near 13.8% against 20.3% delivered, so the valuation is not demanding; the missing data point is one or two more quarters showing the allowance stabilising as the young loan book seasons.
