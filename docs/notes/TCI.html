1. What the business does

A Nevada real-estate operating company with no employees. It owns thirteen stabilised apartment communities (2,128 units), three in lease-up (672 units), one under development (234 units), four Dallas-area office buildings totalling 1,001,549 square feet, and about 1,792 acres of land (10-K, Item 1). It consolidates 84.6% of Income Opportunity Realty Investors (IOR) and holds notes receivable including from Unified Housing Foundation, a related party. Pillar Income Asset Management runs all of it under an advisory agreement.

2. Why it looks mispriced, and why that is not an edge

The headline is 0.40x book: $846.1M of stockholders equity at 30 June 2026 against $339M of market value, $97.94 per share versus $39.17. The discount is real and realisation evidence is not nothing: Villas at Bon Secour sold for $28.0M against a $15.8M carrying value, and 72 Windmill Farms lots sold for $3.3M at a $2.6M gain, roughly five times book (Item 7).

There is still no edge case, because there is no mechanism. May Realty Holdings beneficially owns 86.44% and ARL 78.38% (DEF 14A, 2025-10-29). Float is 1,171,602 shares, about $46M. Not one of the seven directors and officers owns a single share personally; that table reads zero across the board. No dividend, no buyback, no liquidation plan. A discount that requires a controller to act against its own fee stream is not underwritable.

3. Where the money comes from

The properties lose money at the GAAP line every year. FY2025: segment NOI $21.18M, less D&A $12.58M, less general, administrative and advisory $14.94M, equals a $(6.34)M operating loss (Item 7). Net income comes from interest income, $17.12M in FY2025 and $21.89M in FY2024, much of it accrued on receivables from the advisor and related parties, plus $17.67M of asset-sale gains.

That engine is failing. Interest income fell 21.8% in FY2025, and net interest income fell from $10.45M to an annualised $3.7M in H1 2026 as construction loans came off capitalisation (10-Q). Q2 property operating expenses rose $1.6M year over year while NOI fell $0.9M, producing a $(1.13)M net loss attributable to the company against $0.17M a year earlier.

4. The manager takes the return

In 2024 TCI paid Pillar $8.1M of advisory fees, $3.7M of cost reimbursements and $2.9M of development fees, and bought land from Pillar at $6.2M of "appraised values" (DEF 14A, Related Party Transactions). That is $14.7M against FY2024 FFO of $19.8M. FY2025 advisory fees rose to $9.11M, 68% of FFO. The fee is 0.75% a year of gross asset value plus 7.5% of adjusted net income, so building apartments that lose money still raises it. Under the Cash Management Agreement all company funds are delivered to Pillar, which owes them back at SOFR; the receivable from Pillar was $62.8M at 12/31/2024. That is why tagged cash is $10.7M and why the liquidity section names related-party receivables, not operations, as a source of cash.

5. Said versus did

The 10-K filed 2026-03-12 said Mountain Creek would finish in 2026; the 10-Q filed five months later says 2027, with $33.0M still to spend. Merano, substantially complete in 2025, was 77% leased at 30 June 2026. ARL bought 70,023 shares at $55.50 on 2026-01-12 (Form 4) and is 29% underwater. There is no call and no guidance to score.

6. Valuation

Bear: FFO slides toward $10M as net interest income disappears and 58%-occupied Dallas office needs capital. At 12x, about $14 a share. Base: FFO stabilises near $13M once the lease-ups reach 93%, the book discount persists, and the stock tracks 1% book growth. About $39, today's price. Bull: MRHI takes out the minority. It needs 90% for a Nevada short-form merger and sits at 86.44%, roughly 305,000 shares away. A deal at book is $90 or more; a take-under near the January $55.50 print is likelier. Weighted 30/50/20 on $14 / $39 / $60: about $35, below the price. Reverse DCF: with no distributions you are paying for an eventual liquidation. If book is real and grows 1% a year, a buyout at book in ten years returns 10.6% annually and in twenty years 4.7%. The price implies roughly a 60% haircut to stated book, about what a permanent 68%-of-FFO fee leak plus 58% office occupancy deserves.

7. Catalysts

MRHI crossing 90% and forcing a merger; a large Windmill Farms land sale; termination or repricing of the advisory agreement. None is scheduled, and the first two have been possible for a decade.

8. Pre-registered criteria that would make this a WATCH

  1. MRHI or ARL beneficial ownership crosses 89% in any Form 4 or 13D amendment.
  2. The advisory agreement is terminated, internalised, or repriced below 0.40% of gross asset value.
  3. Quarterly FFO above $4.0M for two consecutive quarters with positive operating cash flow in the same period.
  4. A land sale above $25M at a gain over 3x carrying value, with proceeds distributed or used to repurchase stock.

9. Verdict

PASS, conviction 4. TCI screened at 13.0x P/FFO with FFO growing 44%, and both are artefacts of a screen that added depreciation to net income without removing a $17.67M gain on asset sales. On the company's own Nareit reconciliation FFO fell from $21.7M in 2023 to $19.8M in 2024 to $13.4M in 2025, and another 34% in the first half of 2026, so the stock is on 25x a falling number. Beneath it the property portfolio has produced a GAAP operating loss in every year shown, operating cash flow has been negative for three consecutive reported periods, and what earnings exist come from interest accrued on receivables from the company's own manager plus land sales. That manager collected $9.11M of advisory fees in 2025, 68% of FFO, on a formula tied to gross assets rather than returns, and holds the company's cash on its own balance sheet. The genuine attraction, 0.40x book backed by land and apartments that sell well above carrying value, is unreachable: May Realty Holdings controls 86.44%, the float is $46M, no officer or director owns a share, and there is no dividend or buyback. The only route to the discount is a squeeze-out by a controller who last paid $55.50 and is now 29% underwater, which is a lottery ticket, not a thesis. Low leverage at 25% of assets is real and is the only screen figure the filings confirm.
