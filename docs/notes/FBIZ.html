FBIZ — First Business Financial Services, Inc. · 2026-09-19 · Verdict: WATCH · Conviction 3

Price $70.80 (screen row price; screen run dated 2026-09-19, no live price taken) · Mkt cap $592M · P/E 11.9x normalised · P/TBV 1.60x · ROTCE 15.3% · TBVPS $44.38 · ADV $4.3M · Financial lane, so no EV/EBIT Sources read: 10-K 2026-02-25 (Items 1, 1A, 7), 10-Q 2026-07-31, DEF 14A 2026-03-05, 8-Ks 2026-04-29, 2026-05-05, 2026-05-11, 2026-05-28, 2026-07-30, 2026-08-20, Form 4s (12m), Q2 2026 earnings call transcript.

Desk stats

  • Revenue trend. Top line revenue (net interest income plus fee income) grew 9.9% in FY2025 to $168.6M (10-K 2025, Item 7). Q2 2026 operating revenue was $46.7M, up 14% year over year; H1 up 11% to $91.0M (Q2 2026 investor presentation, Quarterly Update). Driver is volume, not price: average gross loans up 9.6% and core deposits up 13.6% year over year, private wealth assets under management and administration up 14% to $4.235 billion, helped by $1.3M of loan prepayment fees against a 12-quarter average of $562,000 (Q2 call, CFO).
  • Normalised after-tax profit. GAAP H1 2026 net income available to common was $27.3M, or $3.28 diluted. Adjustments: less the $1.5M Wisconsin deferred tax valuation allowance reversal (Q2 call, CFO, worth $0.18); plus $405,000 of SBA severance, $0.34M after tax; less roughly $1.8M pre-tax of above-normal fees in lieu of interest (37bp of margin in Q2 and 26bp in Q1 against management's own 20bp normal, Q2 call, CFO), $1.5M after tax. Normalised H1 is $24.6M, or $2.96 per share, roughly $5.95 annualised, against GAAP FY2025 of $5.94. Taxed at 21% instead of the 16% the company actually pays, normalised EPS would be about $5.53.
  • Price / normalised after-tax profit. 11.9x ($70.80 against $5.95). EV is not a meaningful denominator for a bank: deposits and FHLB advances are operating funding.
  • Financial-lane leverage. P/TBV 1.60x on $44.38 tangible book per share at 30 June 2026; ROTCE 15.25% in H1 2026 and 16.89% in Q2, against 15.25% for FY2025; TBVPS up 15.2% year over year; CET1 9.54%, total capital 12.21%, leverage ratio 9.11% (Q2 2026 investor presentation, Capital Strength).
  • Is the growth sustainable? Organic and cash-backed (no acquisitions, share count up 0.5% year over year), but Q2 flattered: management guides full-year margin to 3.60%-3.65% against 3.78% delivered, and loan and deposit growth to 10%, not the 12.6% annualised of H1 (Q2 call, CEO and CFO).
  • What the screen got wrong. Four things. The long-term debt field was untagged and filled with zero, producing a fictitious $163M net cash position and a $429M EV, when FHLB advances and other borrowings were $346.8M at 30 June 2026 (10-Q 2026, Liquidity). The 13.3% ROTE is on period-end tangible common equity; on average equity the company earned 15.25%, so the screen understates quality by two points. The 12.0x P/E is on FY2025 net income and therefore stale in both directions. The 10.3% FCF yield is operating cash flow less capex, which is not distributable cash at a bank.

1. What the business actually does

First Business Bank is a $4.4 billion Wisconsin business bank with four offices (Madison, Brookfield, Appleton and Leawood, Kansas) and no retail branch network (10-K 2025, Item 1). Lending is 60% commercial real estate and 38% commercial and industrial, and the C&I half is a set of national niches: asset-based lending (4.9% of loans), equipment finance (9.9%), floorplan for car dealers (4.7%) and receivables financing (1.3%). Fee income is 19% of revenue, about half of it private wealth.

2. Why it is mispriced, the edge case

There is no edge case, and I will say so plainly. The stock is up 37% in six months and sits 2.8% below its 52-week high (universe_v2.csv); four sell-side firms asked questions on the Q2 call (Raymond James, D.A. Davidson, KBW, Piper Sandler); BlackRock, Dimensional and Vanguard own 17.8% between them (DEF 14A 2026, Beneficial Ownership). Nothing here is neglected, forced-sold or spun off. The screen flagged it as a 12.0x, 1.59x-book bank, which is a modest discount to a $1.75B-$7B asset peer group that the company itself highlights ("Despite outperformance, Price/LTM EPS remains below peers", Q2 2026 presentation). The only real analytic finding is that the screen understates the quality rather than the price: 15.25% ROTCE, not 13.3%. A better business at a fair price is not a mispricing.

3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix

Credit is currently improving, from a weak 2025. Non-performing assets fell to $38.1M, 0.86% of assets, from $43.9M and 1.07% at year end, which itself was up from 0.74% a year earlier (10-Q 2026, Asset Quality). Allowance coverage of non-accruals is 103.8%, up from 86.0%. H1 net charge-offs were $3.2M, 0.18% of average loans, against 0.26% in FY2025. The concentration deserves care: CRE was $2.162 billion, 60.2% of loans, against $486.7M of total regulatory capital, so investor CRE excluding owner-occupied is roughly 3.8x capital, above the 300% level that draws supervisory attention. Office is only $302.4M (8.4% of loans), all in Wisconsin, with nothing rated Category IV; multifamily is $654.4M (18.2%), likewise clean (10-Q 2026). All of the last two years' charge-offs came from C&I, not CRE ($9.65M gross in 2025, entirely C&I, 10-K 2025). The item I would not wave through is $24.7M of individually evaluated non-accruals carrying no specific reserve at 30 June 2026, including an $11.4M construction and land development credit; management expects to resolve the two largest later this year (Q2 call, CEO). Margin was 3.78% in Q2 but about 3.41% stripping the 37bp of fees in lieu of interest, and 3.30% on that basis in Q1. Funding is the structural weakness: non-interest-bearing accounts are 11.7% of deposits, wholesale funds 25.9% of bank funding, uninsured deposits net of collateral 32% (10-Q 2026, Deposits).

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Capital is adequate rather than thick: CET1 9.54% against a 9.5% internal target, total capital 12.21% against 12%. Other borrowings of $54.8M cost 6.44% and include $20M of 7.5% subordinated debentures sold in September 2024, $3M of which went to family trusts of director Ralph Kauten (DEF 14A 2026, Related Party Transactions). Dilution is negligible (8,323,470 shares at 6/30/25 to 8,368,320 at 6/30/26) and the dividend rose 17% to $0.34 a quarter, but the buyback authorisation is only $5M, so the capital plan is growth, not shrinkage. Directors and officers hold 5.0%. Trailing twelve-month insider activity is 12 Form 4s with zero open-market purchases and one sale, 675 shares by the CHRO at $70.19 on 27 August 2026.

5. Management: said versus did

Against the five-year plan's targets, FY2025 delivered 15.3% ROTCE (target 15% by 2028), 13.7% TBV growth (target 10%), 9.9% revenue growth (target 10%) and a 58.78% efficiency ratio (target under 60% by 2028) (10-K 2025, Item 7). H1 2026 beat three of four. The SBA exit is the honest act: after ten years and three leaders, the CEO said outright that national SBA "could just never get it profitable" because scale required "a level of underwriting flexibility that was inconsistent with our standards" (Q2 call, CEO). They also volunteered that Q2's 10% loan growth was 7.2% excluding a held-for-sale transfer. Corey Chambas retired as CEO on 2 May 2026 after nineteen years; Dave Seiler, COO since 2016, took over, so the Q2 call is effectively the new team's first.

6. Valuation

Base (55%): ROTCE holds near 15%, TBVPS compounds about 11% after a 23% payout, reaching roughly $55 in two years at an unchanged 1.60x, so about $88 plus $2.80 of dividends, near 13% a year. Bear (20%): credit normalises, charge-offs go to 0.60% of loans, provisions rise about $14M pre-tax, EPS falls toward $4.60 and the multiple derates to 1.25x on $48 of book, about $60. Bull (25%): the SBA exit plus positive operating leverage lift ROTCE to 17% and 2027 EPS to roughly $7.25, or a $4.4 billion Wisconsin business bank earning 15%-plus attracts a buyer at 2.0x-2.2x book, about $95-$105. Probability-weighted, about $84, some 19% above price over two years. Reverse DCF: at $70.80 and $44.38 of tangible book, the price implies a sustainable ROTCE of about 13.0% on a 10% cost of equity and 5% terminal growth, against 15.25% actually delivered in both FY2025 and H1 2026, so the market is already assuming modest erosion rather than pricing the current return in perpetuity.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Resolution of the two largest non-accruals, guided for later in 2026. The Q3 print in late October, the first clean read on margin once prepayment fees normalise. Full quarterly run-rate of the $650,000 SBA salary saving, plus the $0.03 quarterly EPS benefit management dates to 2027.

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

Concentration in investor CRE at roughly 3.8x capital, a funding base only 11.7% non-interest-bearing, a first-year CEO, and $4.3M of daily volume that makes exit slow. Kill criteria: (1) non-accruals back above 1.30% of gross loans for two consecutive quarters, or the $11.4M construction non-accrual acquiring a specific reserve; (2) annualised net charge-offs above 0.40% of average loans in any quarter (0.18% in H1 2026); (3) net interest margin excluding fees in lieu of interest below 3.25% for two consecutive quarters (about 3.41% in Q2 2026); (4) core deposits below 72% of total bank funding (74.1% at 30 June 2026).

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 3. First Business is a genuinely good bank that the screen undersold on quality and did not misprice on value: 15.25% return on average tangible common equity, 15.2% tangible book growth, a 57.6% Q2 efficiency ratio and an honest exit from a decade-long SBA mistake, all of it organic and barely dilutive. But the shares have already run 37% in six months to within 3% of their high, three index houses own 17.8%, four brokers cover it, and at 1.60x tangible book the price implies a sustainable 13.0% ROTCE against 15.25% delivered, a gap worth roughly 19% over two years rather than the 50% a deep-value note needs. Q2's headline $1.84 also flatters: $0.14 of it was a one-off tax release net of severance, and another $0.17 or so was prepayment fees the company itself calls above normal, leaving about $2.96 of normalised H1 earnings and an 11.9x multiple, not 10.8x. I would own this at 1.25x-1.30x tangible book, near $57, or after two clean quarters resolving the $24.7M of unreserved non-accruals. Until one of those happens it stays on the list, not in the book.
