1. What the business does

Peoples Bank, founded 1912, runs 15 offices in the Catawba Valley of North Carolina, 40 miles north of Charlotte, plus four loan production offices. It is the only bank headquartered in Newton and holds 23.54% of Catawba County deposits, first of eleven (10-K 2025, Item 1). At 30 June 2026: $1.76B assets, $1.28B loans, $1.57B deposits, $161.3M equity. Small subsidiaries add fee income, including an appraisal clearing-house other community banks contract with. The book is real estate: $773.2M commercial mortgages, $144.6M residential, $130.7M home equity, $133.6M construction and land development (10-Q, Loans).

2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case

There is not one, and I will say so. The candidate edge is neglect: 5.5M shares, $1.0M a day, no coverage. But neglect only pays when it comes with a price, and the stock sits 3.3% off its 52-week high with 12-1 momentum of +38.6% (universe_v2.csv). Buyers have found it. What is left is a good small bank at 1.44x tangible book earning 13% on it, which is close to right. No edge case caps the verdict at WATCH.

3. Credit, margin, deposits

Credit is clean. Non-performing assets are $5.2M, 0.29% of assets against 0.25% at year end, with no other real estate owned; the increase is residential ($4.0M of the $5.2M). Allowance is $10.6M, 0.83% of loans, 204% of non-performers. H1 net charge-offs were $167,000 against $101,000. Substandard loans are 0.46% of the book and no watch or substandard relationship exceeds $1.0M (8-K 2026-07-20). The H1 provision of $853,000 against $55,000 is loan-growth arithmetic, not deterioration.

Margin is the 2026 story: 3.57% to 3.80% in the quarter, because certificate costs fell from 3.58% to 2.90% while the loan yield still rose from 5.78% to 5.83% (10-Q, rate table). Time deposits fell $46.6M in six months and interest-bearing demand and savings rose $89.9M, a mix improvement rather than a rate accident.

Funding is the best asset: 91.63% core deposits, uninsured deposits 21.47%, no wholesale funding at all, $153.3M of unused FHLB capacity (10-Q, Deposits, Liquidity). The disclosed concentration is two relationships at $122.7M, 8.13% of deposits (10-K 2025, Item 1A). One thing I cannot verify: the filings do not split owner-occupied from investor commercial real estate. Commercial-property plus construction loans of $758.4M against roughly $200M of Bank total risk-based capital implied by the 15.70% ratio is an upper bound near 375% on the interagency test; the true number is lower and is not computable from these documents.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Capital is heavy and idle. CET1 of 13.83% is nearly double the minimum, yet the board authorised a $3.0M buyback in March 2025 and repurchased not one share before it expired on 28 February 2026 (10-Q, Capital Resources); even full use would have been 1.3% of the market cap. Shares rose from 5,459,441 to 5,461,490. Dividends were $5.247M in FY2025, about $0.99 a share, and $0.59 in H1 2026 against $0.56. The $29.5M accumulated other comprehensive loss, 18% of equity, is already deducted above and accretes back as securities pull to par; its mirror is a 3.05% yield on $412M of securities.

Insider behaviour is the loud signal. Across the 12 most recent of 39 Form 4s, insiders sold 18,397 shares for $796,887 against one 400-share purchase for $17,198 by Dr. Billy Price on 2026-08-27. Every sale was an Abernethy: Chairman Robert C. Abernethy Sr., Vice Chairman James S. Abernethy and Robert C. Abernethy Jr., in lots between $42.30 and $43.61 through 2 September 2026. Directors and named officers hold 14.50%; Wellington 9.21%, BlackRock and Vanguard 5.0% each (DEF 14A 2026). The CEO made $673,255 in 2025 with no equity grant: alignment-neutral, not positive.

5. Management: said versus did

No guidance is given, so there is nothing to score. Cable attributed both quarters to higher net interest income offset by provision, fees and expense, which describes rather than forecasts (8-Ks 2026-04-20, 2026-07-20). What they did: grew loans 12.5% annualised, let the buyback lapse unused, added a director in July, nudged the dividend. Stated strategy is independence, with acquisitions called unnecessary (10-Q, Overview).

6. Valuation

Base (50%): ROTCE holds near 12.5-13%, tangible book compounds about 9.5% plus a 2.3% dividend, multiple stays 1.4x. Two years out, book $36.3, value about $51 plus $2 dividends, so $53. Bear (25%): further cuts reprice loans down faster than the remaining $307M of time deposits can help, margin gives back 2026's gain, provisions normalise to 20-25bp, earnings fall to $17M, ROTCE to 10%, multiple to 1.1x. Value $33-34. Bull (25%): the deposit franchise is bought. A 91.6% core-funded, 0.29%-NPA, first-in-county bank next to Charlotte, with a 75-year-old Chairman already selling, is a clean target at 1.8-2.0x tangible book. Value $55-61. Weighted, about $49 against $43.57: roughly 13% over two years plus dividends. Reverse DCF: at 1.44x tangible book, a 10% cost of equity and 4% growth, the price implies a sustained 12.6% return on tangible equity forever, essentially today's number with no credit cycle and no improvement. You are paid for continuation, not for anything going right.

7. Catalysts

Q3 2026 results in late October (margin trajectory). Further Fed cuts repricing the $307.2M of remaining time deposits through 2027. Renewal of the buyback, or its absence. A sale of the bank, unforecastable but structurally likelier as the controlling generation ages.

8. Kill criteria (pre-registered)

  1. Net interest margin below 3.60% for two consecutive quarters (3.80% in Q2 2026); the whole 2026 gain is funding cost.
  2. Non-performing assets above 0.60% of assets, or allowance/loans below 0.80% while non-performers rise (0.29% and 0.83% today).
  3. Pre-provision net revenue failing to grow year over year for two consecutive quarters (H1 2026 $13.23M against $12.35M, up 7.1%).
  4. Another twelve months with zero shares repurchased while CET1 stays above 13%, or insider net selling above $1M.

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 2. Peoples Bancorp is a better bank than 12x earnings suggests and a worse investment than that multiple suggests, because the multiple attaches to 1.44x tangible book and 13% returns, which is roughly fair. The quality is real and verifiable: no wholesale funding, 91.6% core deposits, 21.5% uninsured, 0.29% non-performing assets, CET1 of 13.83%, and a margin that widened 23 basis points as certificate costs fell from 3.58% to 2.90%. None of it is reaching the bottom line: first-half net earnings grew 1.3% because appraisal fee income fell $1.4M and expenses rose $1.0M, and the 21% growth that put this name on the screen is a FY2025 figure with no 2026 successor. Meanwhile the board let a token buyback expire unspent while holding double the required capital, and the controlling family sold roughly $800,000 of stock into the strength against one 400-share purchase. What would change the verdict: a price below about 1.15x tangible book, a sale process, or two quarters proving the margin gain is structural rather than borrowed from the Fed.
