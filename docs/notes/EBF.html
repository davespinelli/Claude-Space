1. What the business actually does

A 1909-vintage Texas trade printer: custom business forms, pressure-seal documents, labels, tags, envelopes and folders, sold almost entirely through independent print distributors rather than end users (10-K, Item 1). About 95% of output is custom, from roughly 50 plants in 20 states, one segment, and management believes it is the largest such supplier to the US distributor channel.

2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case

There is none I can identify, and I would rather say so than manufacture one. Nothing is broken here: no leverage, no restatement, no spin-off, no index event, and the stock sits 5.6% below its 52-week high with positive momentum, so there is no forced seller. About 27% of the register is quant and index money (DEF 14A), price-insensitive but not wrong. No edge caps the verdict at WATCH.

3. Unit economics and growth

Gross margin rose from 29.7% in FY2025 to 30.7% in FY2026 and 31.5% in Q1 FY2027, on pricing discipline and mix (10-K, Item 7; 10-Q), with operating margin held between 13.2% and 13.8% throughout. But volume is leaving: FY2026 organic volume fell about 6.3%, Q1 FY2027 about 3.2%. That apparent moderation is the most important number here and it is one quarter old.

Normalised ROIC is 14.4% ($39.5M on about $274M of invested capital) even carrying $143.3M of acquired goodwill. But acquisitions are really maintenance capex: FY2026 bought roughly $33M of annualised revenue for $38.9M cash, near 1.15x sales (10-K, Items 1 and 7), the cost of standing still against $25M of attrition. Charge that against $56M of normalised FCF and owner earnings are nearer $23M, a 4.3% yield; charge only maintenance capex and it is 10.4%. Where the truth sits between those is the whole case.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Total liabilities $52.9M against $363.5M of assets and $310.7M of equity, no debt (8-K 2026-06-22). FY2026 uses: $38.9M acquisitions, $26.1M dividends, $14.5M buybacks, $11.7M capex. The buyback took 793,556 shares at $18.04, below today's price, cutting diluted shares from 26.02M to 25.52M (10-K). The dividend is $0.25 quarterly, a 4.70% yield, and only $7.2M of the $60.0M authorisation remained at year end. Insiders made no open-market trades in twelve months, all 15 Form 4 rows being grants and conversions. Governance flag: director Michael Magill failed the majority standard (9.17M for, 9.97M against), resigned, and the board rejected the resignation, blaming an ISS error on his independence (8-K 2026-07-17).

5. Management: said versus did

Walters said the carbonless transition would not disrupt service; gross margin then recovered from 29.2% to 31.5%. Their own incentive plan is the sharpest check: the FY2025 to FY2027 award needs $244.0M of cumulative EBITDA for target (DEF 14A); FY2025 and FY2026 delivered $72.0M and $75.7M, and at the Q1 run rate FY2027 lands near $72M, clearing the $170.8M threshold but missing target by about $23M. They are paid in line with a flat business, which is what this is.

6. Valuation

Base (45%): FY2028 revenue $395M as acquisitions annualise against 3% organic decline, 13.5% margin, $40M after-tax profit at 12x plus net cash: about $21.50 plus a $1.00 dividend. Bear (30%): decline reaccelerates to 8%, acquisitions pause, revenue $340M by FY2029 at an 11.5% margin; $29M at 9x plus $80M net cash: about $13.50. Bull (25%): carbonless scarcity converts to durable price, gross margin above 31%, the roll-up adds $30M of revenue a year; revenue $420M at 14.5%, $45.7M at 14x plus net cash: about $29. Weighted value about $21.20 against $21.27: fair, not cheap. Reverse DCF: $493.8M of EV on $39.5M of normalised after-tax profit implies, at a 10% cost of capital, roughly 2% perpetual growth, requiring the roll-up to more than offset a core shrinking 6.3% a year, forever.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q2 FY2027 results, due late September 2026, carry the organic revenue number that decides this. The inventory unwind from $60.8M to $54.9M should push FY2027 operating cash flow toward $62M to $66M. Renewal of the repurchase authorisation would be a concrete signal.

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

The structural risk is the company's own: digital technologies "will continue to erode the demand for our printed business documents" (10-K, Item 1A). Secondary: the sole domestic carbonless mill closed in FY2026, pushing supply partly offshore. 1. Gross margin below 29.0% in two consecutive quarters (Q4 FY2026 was 29.2%, Q1 FY2027 31.5%). 2. Organic revenue decline worse than 6% year over year in two consecutive quarters per MD&A (Q1 FY2027 about 3.2%). 3. Any drawn borrowing to fund an acquisition, or cash below $25M. 4. Quarterly dividend cut below $0.25, or the authorisation exhausted without renewal. Two consecutive quarters of positive organic revenue, or a price near $17, would move this to IDEA.

9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary

WATCH, conviction 2. Ennis is a genuinely good business, 30%-plus gross margins, 13.4% operating margins, 14.4% ROIC, zero debt, $49M of cash and a 4.7% dividend, inside a structurally shrinking market. Its reported flatness is manufactured: FY2026 revenue fell only 0.6% because $22.8M of acquired revenue papered over a $25.0M organic decline, and the $1.66 EPS includes about $0.15 from a legal settlement, so clean earnings actually fell. The screen is right that the balance sheet is clean and understates free cash flow, but it credits growth that does not exist. At $21.27 the price implies about 2% perpetual growth and my weighted value is $21.20, with no forced seller and no catalyst to argue the market is wrong. Watch whether the organic decline has really moderated from -6.3% to -3.2%, and require a lower price before paying for a melting ice cube.
