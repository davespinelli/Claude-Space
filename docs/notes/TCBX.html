1. What the business actually does

A commercially focused Texas bank holding company founded in Humble in 2008, now $6.74B of assets and 21 branches across Greater Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin-San Antonio and Beaumont (8-K 2026-07-22). Loans are 44% commercial and industrial, 16% construction and development, 17% non-owner-occupied CRE, 11% owner-occupied CRE (Q2 call, Chief Credit Officer). Funding is 89% interest-bearing: noninterest-bearing demand is only 11.0% of $5.86B of deposits at a 3.05% cost of deposits; uninsured deposits were $1.32B, 28% of the total, at year-end (10-K 2025, Item 7).

2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case

There is none, and that governs the verdict. Counted properly the stock is at 1.46x tangible book for a 15% ROTCE, about what such a bank should fetch. It is covered by Raymond James, Stephens, KBW, Deutsche Bank and Stonegate (Q2 call), trades 4% below its 52-week high, has no forced seller, and no insider has bought on the open market in twelve months; the only transaction was a director selling 2,500 shares at $40.29 (Form 4, 2026-06-16).

3. Credit, margin and deposits

Credit is good but drifting. Nonperforming assets rose to 0.85% of assets from 0.58% a year ago because a $17.1M medical office loan in Southeast Texas was foreclosed in April, taking other real estate owned from $8.4M to $27.3M. Three new nonaccrual relationships totalling $10.1M are, per the Chief Credit Officer, "all well secured", at 77%, 57% and under 50% LTV, and 44% of nonaccruals are SBA guaranteed (Q2 call). Net recoveries were $150K in Q2, a second straight recovery quarter; year-to-date charge-offs are $320K, of which $270K was the unguaranteed slice of SBA loans. The allowance is 0.99% of loans, thin at 1.8x nonperforming loans. The offset is real: the divested factoring unit produced 44% of total net charge-offs over five and a half years (Q2 call). Margin recovered to 3.83% from 3.67% as deposit costs fell 12bp and noninterest-bearing demand rose $65.5M, with guidance of "flat to maybe up just a little bit" before securitization benefit. My estimate, not a filing figure: construction lending is roughly 115% to 125% of the bank's total risk-based capital, above the 100% supervisory threshold.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

The engine is capital scarcity. At 8.82% holdco CET1 the bank cannot fund 10%-plus loan growth from retained earnings, so it draws a holdco revolver, extended in March 2026 to a $70M commitment maturing March 2028 and secured by 100% of the bank's stock, with $60.4M drawn (8-K 2026-05-01); carries $81.1M of 5.50% subordinated notes due 2032; and converts loans into retained securities through EJF credit-risk-transfer securitizations, two in 2025 totalling $250M and a third closed 15 July 2026 with a fourth called probable (10-K 2025, notes; Q2 call). Those book no gain and are honest capital management, but they are now a dependency, and "liquidity risks in the securitization market" is a new risk factor. No common dividend is paid and not one share has been repurchased under the $30M authorisation extended to June 2027 (10-Q 2026-08-04). Directors and officers hold 10.48%, the founder-CEO 2.0%; Castle Creek's bank fund holds 9.3% through preferred and warrants, a 2022-vintage position now at double its $22.50 conversion price (DEF 14A 2026-04-16).

5. Management: said versus did

The post-Keystone NIM target was 3.75% and Q2 delivered 3.83%; efficiency guidance of "56% to 57%" against 56.51% actual is consistent (Q2 call). Savings are dated: $100K a month from 1 August 2026, $150K more from 1 February 2027. Against that, 33.7% of votes cast were withheld from the founder-CEO's re-election in May and 35% from another director (8-K 2026-05-21).

6. Valuation

Converted tangible book is $30.33 a share. Base (55%): 8% to 10% loan growth, NIM near 3.85%, efficiency to 55%, charge-offs at the roughly 10bp long-run average management cites; TBVPS compounds about 11% to $41.5 in three years and exits at 1.35x, $56, about 8% a year. Bear (25%): the construction and office book cracks, charge-offs run 60bp for two years, NIM slips below 3.6%, growth stops, the multiple goes to 1.0x a stagnant $32 book, down 28%. Bull (20%): efficiency below 55%, securitizations hold NIM above 4%, ROTCE 17%, or Texas consolidation takes out a scarce $6.7B franchise at 1.8x to 2.0x book, $65. Probability-weighted three-year value $52, about 5% a year from $44.30. Reverse DCF: at 1.46x converted tangible book on a 14.5% sustainable ROTCE and an 11% cost of equity, the price implies only about 3.4% perpetual tangible book growth, so the market is not extrapolating the compounding; but paying 1.46x for book that compounds at 11% still returns high single digits unless the multiple expands.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q3 2026 results in late October: first quarter with Keystone savings flowing, two securitizations, and the margin guide tested. 1 February 2027: the second tranche of savings. Any Castle Creek conversion or distribution, which either clears the overhang or creates supply. Texas bank M&A, in either direction.

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

  1. Nonperforming assets above 1.25% of total assets for two consecutive quarters, or net charge-offs above 25bp annualised in any quarter.
  2. Net interest margin below 3.60% for two consecutive quarters.
  3. Holdco CET1 below 8.50%, or any common equity raise priced at or below tangible book.
  4. Efficiency ratio above 62% for two consecutive quarters excluding securities and asset-sale gains. The mirror is the watch-to-buy trigger: converted P/TBV below about 1.15x, roughly $35, with credit still clean.

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 2. Third Coast is a better bank than the screen said and a worse bargain. The screen ranked it a qualifier at 1.20x tangible book and a 10% return on tangible equity; both figures come from deducting the Series A preferred at its $69 thousand par rather than the $66.2M it is carried at, and from ignoring the 3.07M shares it converts into at $22.50 against a $44.30 price. Corrected, you pay 1.46x converted tangible book and about 12.9x normalised earnings for a founder-led Texas commercial bank compounding tangible book at 11% to 12% a year on a 15% return on tangible equity, with the efficiency ratio down from 74% in 2021 to 56.5%, dated Keystone cost savings still to come, and a factoring unit just sold that had produced 44% of five years of charge-offs. The objections are that this year's growth was bought rather than earned, that 8.82% holdco CET1 forces the bank to manufacture capital through a revolver secured by the bank's own stock and through credit-risk-transfer securitizations it now depends on, that nonperforming assets have reached 0.85% of assets against a thin 0.99% allowance, and that none of this is unknown to the five brokers covering it or to insiders who have bought nothing in a year. At roughly a 5% a year probability-weighted return there is no margin of safety; below about 1.15x converted book with credit still clean, this becomes an interesting compounder.
