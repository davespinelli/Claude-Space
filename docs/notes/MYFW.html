1. What the business does

A Denver "private trust bank": fourteen profit centres, two loan production offices and one trust office across five Western states, selling deposits, loans, trust, wealth planning and investment management to households with $1M-plus of liquid net worth (10-K, Item 1). At 2026-06-30: $3.24bn of assets, $2.72bn of loans held for investment, $2.85bn of deposits, $7.28bn of trust and investment management assets. Fees were 27.4% of income before non-interest expense in FY2025.

2. Why it might be mispriced — the edge case

The edge case was real and is now mostly priced. In 2024 a few commercial credits blew up: non-performing assets of $48.7M, 1.67% of assets, including $35.9M of OREO, and net charge-offs of $9.0M, 0.37% of average loans (10-K, Item 7). Those are gone: NPAs were $16.1M, 0.50% of assets, at 2026-06-30, the last OREO property sold in Q1 2026, no charge-offs in Q1 or Q2 2026 (10-Q). Earnings normalise off that base while deposits reprice down, which is why a screen reading FY2025 still prints 22.5x.

The market has done that work. The stock is up 24% in six months and sits 11% below its 52-week high at 1.19x tangible book, with no forced seller. The identifiable seller is the CEO: Scott Wylie sold 17,500 shares in twelve months, including 10,000 at $30.55 on 2026-08-31, and has 602,899 of his 741,996 shares pledged against personal debt (DEF 14A note 12; Form 4 2026-09-02). No insider open-market buys. That is informed distribution into strength.

3. Credit, margin and deposit mix

Credit is clean today: $16.1M of non-accruals, $14.3M of it commercial and industrial, non-accrual coverage 126%, allowance 0.75% of loans (10-Q). Classified and criticised loans both fell and "the problem credits we had from prior periods are largely gone" (Q2 call, Wylie). A 0.75% reserve is thin for a book 35% in CRE and construction with CRE 2 at 277.2%.

The margin is the weak part. NIM of 2.90% is low and the levers are nearly spent: spot deposit rate 2.80%, time deposits 3.64% (Q2 call, Weber). Non-interest-bearing deposits are $381.8M of $2.85bn, 13.4%, and the year's growth came from money market accounts, $1.63bn to $1.98bn (8-K 2026-07-23) — rate-paid funding, not relationship float. Efficiency is 74.0%, with expense guidance raised to $20-21M a quarter from $19-20M to fund 20 new hires (Q2 call). The wealth franchise that supposedly justifies a premium is shrinking: AUM fell from $7.50bn to $7.28bn year over year, 1H26 withdrawals $310M against $241M of contributions (10-Q).

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

No dividend. A $5.0M buyback was authorised effective 2026-06-12 with Federal Reserve non-objection (8-K 2026-05-04), 1.7% of market value and too small to move a share count that rose from 9,717,922 to 9,765,129. Insiders and directors hold 13.1%; North Reef Capital 7.1%, BlackRock 5.5%. CEO pay was $2.65M in 2025 against $13.2M of net income; awards vest on operating EPS hurdles and TSR versus the SPDR S&P Regional Banking ETF, reasonable alignment. The CEO's son-in-law is a Market President paid $319,623 in 2025 (DEF 14A).

5. Management: said versus did

They guided to NIM expansion and delivered it, 26bp in 2025 and 19bp in 1H26. They also promised expense control, then raised the expense guide by $1M a quarter. The "$3 of core revenue per $1 of core expense since 2023" claim is supported by efficiency falling from 78.8% to 74.0%, but 74% is still poor. Caveat: the vendor transcript garbles years and puts Q2 net income at $6.7M where the filed release says $5.7M, so filed figures are used throughout.

6. Valuation

Base: NIM near 2.95%, earning assets +6%, efficiency ~73%, provision $6M a year. Normalised EPS $1.85-2.05 by 2027, tangible book compounding ~8% to ~$29.80; at 1.15x, ~$34, 12% up with no dividend. Bear: one or two of the $14.3M of non-accrual C&I credits crystallise, provision returns to 2024 levels, EPS near $1.00 at 0.9x tangible book — ~$23, down 24%. Bull: NIM 3.10%, assets $3.4bn, efficiency 68%, ~$27M after tax, $2.75-2.85 EPS, re-rating to 1.45x tangible book — ~$42, up 39%; a sale into Colorado and Arizona consolidation at 1.6x tangible book is similar. Weighted 55/20/25, expected value ~$33, 9% above the price. Reverse DCF: at 1.19x tangible book, a 10% cost of equity and 4% long-run growth, the price implies a sustainable return on tangible common equity near 11.1%, versus 7.2% normalised today and 9.19% in the best quarter the company has printed in two years.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q3 2026 results in late October, where management promised accelerating balance-sheet growth and further NIM expansion. Fed cuts help slightly: the CFO put a 25bp cut at 1-2bp of NIM. Denver and Phoenix bank M&A makes First Western either a talent acquirer or a seller above tangible book.

8. Kill criteria (pre-registered, observable)

  1. NIM at or below 2.90% for two consecutive quarters.
  2. Efficiency ratio above 75% for two consecutive quarters, or quarterly non-interest expense above $21M without matching revenue growth.
  3. Non-performing assets back above 0.75% of total assets, or quarterly net charge-offs above $2M.
  4. CEO beneficial ownership below 650,000 shares, or pledged shares above 650,000, in any Form 4 or proxy.

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 2. First Western fixed a 2024 credit accident, has grown net interest income for seven straight quarters and is compounding tangible book at 9% a year, and the screen's 22.5x P/E is stale by two quarters: the real trailing multiple is 16.3x. None of that is a secret any more. At $30.33 you pay 1.19x tangible book for a bank earning about 7% on tangible common equity once you charge a normal provision and strip the reserve releases, running a 74% efficiency ratio and a 2.90% margin with the deposit-repricing lever nearly spent, and whose wealth assets are down 3% year over year with net client withdrawals every quarter. That price implies an 11% through-cycle return on tangible equity the company has never earned, while the CEO has sold three times in twelve months, bought none, and pledged 81% of his stake against personal debt. What to want is the same business near book value, about $25.50, or one more quarter proving the margin and expense guidance; below 1.0x tangible book with NIM above 3.0% this becomes an IDEA.
