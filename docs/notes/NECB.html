1. What the business actually does

A 1934-vintage New York state savings bank in White Plains with eleven branches that has turned itself into a monoline construction lender. At 31 Dec 2025, $1.3B, or 71.8% of loans net of $402.7M loans-in-process, was construction, up from 39.8% in 2016 (10-K Item 1A); gross construction was $1.40B of $1.92B loans at 30 Jun 2026 (8-K 2026-07-27). Loans run 18-36 months, interest-only, prime plus a margin, with no permanent financing offered, and condominium projects are underwritten as if they were rentals as a backstop (10-K Item 1). They sit almost entirely in what the filing calls "high absorption, homogeneous communities" in Orange, Rockland and Sullivan counties plus the Bronx — 96.6% of the construction book (10-K Item 1A). The Kiryas Joel branch expansion (10-K Item 7) names the community plainly.

2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case

A 2021 second-step thrift conversion ($97.8M raised at $10, 10-K Item 1) that still has no natural shareholder base: the ESOP holds 9.33% and BlackRock's index complex 7.4% (DEF 14A), there is no earnings call, and ADV is $1.03M. The edge is that the bank underwrites a demographic rather than a market — supply-constrained by family formation, borrowers repeat and referred, senior management doing site visits and sitting with community leaders (10-K Item 7). Generalists see "73% construction at 392% of risk-based capital" and stop. But this is only half a mispricing. At 1.03x tangible book on a 2.0% ROA and 42% efficiency ratio, the discount to a normal 1.6-2.0x thrift multiple is roughly $150M, almost exactly the excess capital above a 10% leverage ratio. The market is not ignoring the franchise; it is charging the entire capital cushion as insurance against the concentration.

3. Credit quality, margin and deposits

On the face of it, pristine: no non-performing assets at 30 Jun 2026 or 31 Dec 2025, nothing past due over 90 days, no non-accruals, NPA/assets 0.00% against 0.25% in 2024 and 0.33% in 2023 (8-K 2026-07-27; 10-K Item 7). The allowance is $4.8M, 0.25% of loans — extraordinarily thin for a three-quarters-construction book. H1-26 net charge-offs were $547k, of which $500k was one commercial and industrial loan (10-Q MD&A).

The crack is in the 10-Q: "three loans totaling $17.4 million were modified to one borrower experiencing financial difficulty whereby the weighted average contractual interest rates of the three loans were reduced to 5.63% from 8.75%." There were no such modifications in all of 2025. That relationship is 0.9% of loans and 3.6x the entire allowance, and it is current only because the rate was cut 312bp.

Margin: NIM 5.14% in Q2-26 versus 5.35% a year earlier, but up from 4.99% in Q1-26 (8-K 2026-04-30) as deposit costs reset. Cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 43bp to 3.45%. Deposits shrank 5.0% in H1 by design — brokered CDs down 34.9%, non-interest-bearing up 19.7% — funded by taking borrowings from $70M to $190M (10-Q MD&A). Brokered, military and listing-service deposits were still 28.3% of deposits at year end (10-K Item 1A): a real funding weakness, partly being fixed.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Equity $362.6M on $2.12B of assets. Third buyback authorised 10 Dec 2025 for 1,400,435 shares (10%); 239,894 repurchased by 30 Jun 2026 for $5.6M (8-K 2026-07-27). Share count down 1.9% year on year. Dividend $0.20 quarterly, doubled from $0.10 during 2025, plus the October special (10-K Item 1A). The 2026 Equity Incentive Plan approved 21 May reserves 185,000 shares, about 1.3% of shares outstanding (8-K 2026-05-26; DEF 14A Appendix A s.4.2) — modest. Directors and officers own 7.12% as a group, CEO Kenneth Martinek 3.08% (DEF 14A). Insider activity is one-sided: zero open-market purchases in twelve months against 14,566 shares sold for $353k, including the CFO selling 3,000 shares at $27.20 on 29 Jul 2026, two days after the Q2 release and at today's price (form4_last12m.csv).

5. Management: what they said versus what they did

The only public commentary is the press release: "laser focus on construction lending in high demand, high absorption submarkets," with unfunded commitments up 38.9% (8-K 2026-07-27). Originations back the claim — $860.7M in FY2025, $653.2M in H1-26 — as does cost discipline, with non-interest expense up 1.8% and the efficiency ratio at 41.99%. The earnings line does not: net income fell 5.7% in FY2025 and 9.2% in H1-26, and ROE has gone 15.59% (2024) to 13.12% (2025) to 10.97% (H1-26). Management never guides, so there is nothing to score them against.

6. Valuation

Base (50%): rates stabilise, NIM holds near 5.0%, ROTCE settles at 11%, TBVPS compounds about 9% on retention plus buyback to roughly $30 in eighteen months, multiple 1.2x. $36. Bear (30%): two more Fed cuts take NIM below 4.7%; the $17.4M modified relationship goes non-accrual, the allowance is rebuilt from 0.25% toward 0.75% (about $10M pre-tax) and 1.5% of construction is charged off over two years. TBVPS stalls near $27, multiple 0.85x. $23. Bull (20%): the $883M pipeline funds at prime plus margin, loans grow 12%, ROTCE returns to 13%, the 10% buyback completes, multiple 1.5x. $45. Probability-weighted $33.85, about 24% above $27.22 over eighteen months, plus a 2.9% dividend. Reverse DCF: at 1.03x tangible book, with a 10% cost of equity and 4% growth, the price implies a sustainable ROTCE of about 10.2% — the market assumes NECB earns roughly its cost of equity forever and that the 17.3% Tier 1 leverage ratio is worth nothing.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Completion of the 10% buyback (roughly $30M, 8% of market cap) over four to six quarters. Conversion of the $883M unfunded pipeline into funded balances, lifting earning assets without new deposits. A dividend increase or second special in Q4, on the October 2025 pattern. Any Fed pause, which stops the NIM bleed within a quarter given prime indexing.

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

  • Non-performing assets exceed 1.0% of total loans in any quarter, or the $17.4M modified relationship moves to non-accrual. (Now 0.00%.)
  • Allowance stays at or below 0.30% of loans while construction exceeds 70% of the book for two more quarters.
  • Net interest margin below 4.70% for two consecutive quarters. (Q2-26: 5.14%.)
  • Insider open-market purchases remain zero for another twelve months while the buyback runs, or officers sell more than $1M net. Non-kill risks: construction at 392% of the Bank's total risk-based capital against the 300% supervisory guidance (10-K Item 1A), which invites an FDIC growth limit; $361.0M of uninsured deposits against $126.2M of on-balance-sheet liquidity at year end, covered only by borrowing capacity; and the filing's own warning that the New York City administration's support for rent freezes could impair borrower cash flows, though directly rent-regulated exposure is $16.3M, 0.9% of loans (10-K Item 1A).

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 3. NorthEast Community Bancorp is a genuinely superior small bank hiding behind a scary label: 2.0% return on assets, 5.14% net interest margin, 42% efficiency ratio, zero non-performing assets, no goodwill, 17.3% Tier 1 leverage and a 10% buyback running, trading at 1.03x tangible book because 72% of the loan book is construction lending at 392% of risk-based capital. The edge is real, since it underwrites a supply-constrained demographic in Orange, Rockland, Sullivan and Bronx counties that generalist lenders cannot price, and at 1.03x book the market implicitly assumes the bank never earns more than its cost of equity again. What stops this being an IDEA is the combination of an 0.25% allowance against $1.4 billion of construction loans, a first-ever $17.4 million modification to one distressed borrower at a 312 basis point concession, six quarters of falling earnings, insiders who have bought nothing and sold at $27.20, and a share price 3% off its 52-week high after an 18% six-month run. About 24% probability-weighted upside against a credible 15% downside if one construction relationship breaks is decent, not compelling. Wait for either a reserve build that proves the allowance is honest, or a price closer to 0.9x tangible book.
