1. What the business actually does

Bank7 is a twelve-branch commercial bank holding company in Oklahoma City lending to business owners in Oklahoma, the Dallas/Fort Worth metro and Kansas, with $1.91B of assets, $1.60B of loans and $1.64B of deposits at 2026-06-30 (10-Q Q2 2026). It runs a deliberately thin franchise: 125 full-time employees (10-K 2025, Item 1), a five-year average efficiency ratio of 38.1% and ROAA of 2.2%. The book is 58.3% commercial real estate, 35.2% commercial and industrial, with declared concentrations of $310.6M in hospitality (19.3% of loans) and $156.8M in energy (9.7%) (10-K 2025, Items 1A and 7). Regulatory CRE was 261.9% of bank capital at year-end 2025, below the 300% supervisory threshold and down 42.8% over the prior 36 months.

2. Why it might be mispriced — the edge case

The classic edge cases are absent: the stock is up 41% over six months, sits 5% off its 52-week high, three brokers ask questions on the call, and 55% insider ownership means no forced seller. What is new is the transaction. On 2026-09-03 Bank7 was named successful bidder at a court-supervised auction for a 71% controlling interest in Century Financial Services, sold by a receiver in KS StateBank Corporation v. Peters in the District of Arizona; on 2026-09-16 it converted that into a whole-company merger for $70M cash and 1,232,657 shares, about $137.3M (8-Ks 2026-09-03, 2026-09-10, 2026-09-17). Century Bank, founded 1887, is the second-largest bank headquartered in New Mexico with $1.36B of assets and $1.23B of deposits at a 0.88% cost of funds against Bank7's 3.75%. Management projects 25%+ EPS accretion, 20%+ ROATCE, 1.45x normalised tangible book, 6.3x 2027 earnings with synergies, ~11% tangible book dilution and a 1.7-year earnback (8-K 2026-09-17, EX-99.2). That is the edge: a forced seller under court supervision handing a high-NIM, expensively funded bank exactly the cheap deposit franchise it lacks, announced two days before this price. The qualifier is that the auction was competitive: Bank7's own bid rose from the $68M stalking-horse price to $91M, up 34%, which is evidence of a fair clearing price rather than a steal.

3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix

Credit is the best it has been. Nonaccruals were $6.3M, 0.39% of loans, at 2026-06-30, with the allowance at 1.22% of loans and 310% of nonaccruals and net recoveries of 2bp in the quarter (8-K 2026-07-16, EX-99.2). Criticized loans totalled $77.3M, 4.8% of the book (watch $56.3M, special mention $11.9M, substandard $9.1M) (10-Q Q2 2026). This follows a real scare: a single borrower bankruptcy produced a $16.5M charge-off in 2023 and drove nonaccruals to $18.9M, 1.39% of loans (10-K 2025, Item 7). Management took the collateral oil and gas assets for $16.5M, ran them ten quarters and sold in April 2026 for total cash of $20.2M, a $3.7M net recovery and $5.8M of cumulative pre-tax income before the $2.1M non-cash loss on sale (10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A) — an episode that ends the energy-operator distraction but removes a revenue line the screen counted. Net interest margin was 4.94% in FY2025 versus 5.11% in FY2024; management guides core NIM excluding loan fees to 4.45%–4.53%, with June at 4.51% (Q2 2026 call, CFO). The weak link is funding: noninterest-bearing deposits are only 20.1% of the total, brokered deposits were $205.6M and reciprocal deposits $576.5M at year-end 2025, and deposit costs sit at 2.28%–2.30% (Q2 2026 call, CFO). Uninsured deposits are 23.75% of total, covered 2.45x by cash, securities and undrawn lines.

4. Balance sheet, capital allocation and governance

No holding-company debt, no held-to-maturity securities, AOCI loss of only $2.6M on a $51.6M securities book. No buybacks since 2020; dividends are $1.20 annualised, a 23.7% payout. The $70M cash leg is funded from accumulated capital, taking CET1 to 10.0% and TCE/TA to 8.3% at close. Against this sits a governance flag: management concluded disclosure controls and internal control over financial reporting were not effective at 2025-12-31, with material weaknesses in eight areas including related party transactions, segregation of duties and IT general controls (10-K 2025, Item 1A). The CEO attributes this to "material weaknesses that the new accounting firm thought that existed" and expects most remediation done by Q3 2026 (Q2 2026 call, CEO) — a dismissive framing for a bank about to integrate a target 70% of its own size. Related-party dealings are routine but present: the chairman's brother is a regional president paid $436,191, and the bank leases two properties from entities the chairman manages for his daughters, $326,376 in 2025 (DEF 14A 2026). Insiders bought nothing on the open market in the last 12 months; the only sales were 2,477 shares for $125k by the chief credit officer.

5. Management: said versus did

Travis guided to mid-single-digit full-year loan growth against large known payoffs, and that is tracking (loans +6.7% year over year in Q2). The CFO guided Q3 noninterest expense to $9.5M–$9.7M against $9.86M in Q2 excluding oil and gas. On the energy workout, management said it would maximise recovery and did, returning $3.7M above cost. The one place words outran actions is internal controls.

6. Valuation

Standalone this is a 16% ROTCE bank at 2.03x tangible book and 12.9x normalised earnings: fair, not cheap. The case rests on the deal. Base (50%): merger closes in Q4 2026, 25% accretion lifts 2027 EPS to roughly $5.25 on 10.76M shares, and a 20% ROATCE bank holds 12x, giving about $62. Bull (20%): synergies fully phase in, EPS reaches $5.50 and the multiple goes to 13.5x on a rerated Southwest franchise, about $74. Bear (30%): either the merger breaks on the court sale order, Fed approval, dissenters' rights or the Century shareholder vote and the stock falls back to about 11x standalone core EPS of $4.21, roughly $46; or it closes and Century's book, sold out of a receivership with no credit mark disclosed in the extracted exhibit, carries 3% of loss content beyond the mark, costing about $2.35 of pro forma tangible book and compressing the multiple to roughly $40. Probability-weighted, about $59 against $54.15, a 9% margin. Reverse DCF: at $54.15 against pro forma tangible book of roughly $25.19 per share ($271M over 10.76M shares), the market pays 2.15x tangible book, which on a 10% cost of equity and 5% long-run growth implies a sustained ROTCE near 15.8% in perpetuity. That is what Bank7 already earns standalone and below the 20%+ management projects, so the price assumes the deal closes and works without paying you to carry the closing risk.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Court sale order in the Arizona receivership; Federal Reserve approval; the Century shareholder vote; expected close in Q4 2026, with an outside date of 2026-11-30 extendable to 2027-06-30 and a $7.32M break fee payable by Century. Then the 2026 10-K Item 9A verdict on remediation, and the first full combined quarter in 2027 showing the actual pro forma cost of funds.

8. Pre-registered kill criteria

  1. The merger terminates and Bank7 instead closes the 71% receiver purchase, leaving a minority stub and no full cost saves — observable in an 8-K by 2026-11-30 (extended date 2027-06-30).
  2. Core NIM excluding loan fees prints below 4.45%, management's own guided floor, for two consecutive quarters.
  3. Nonaccrual loans exceed 1.0% of total loans, or criticized loans (watch plus special mention plus substandard) exceed 8% of loans, in any quarter after close.
  4. Item 9A of the 2026 10-K still reports internal control over financial reporting as not effective.

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 3. Bank7 is a top-decile small bank — 4.5% core margin, 41% efficiency ratio, 16.5% core ROTCE, 0.39% nonaccruals, no debt, 16.5% annual tangible book compounding since the IPO — and it has just signed a merger adding New Mexico's second-largest bank and its $1.2B of 0.88%-cost deposits, precisely the cheap funding its own 20% noninterest-bearing mix lacks, at 1.45x normalised tangible book with 25%+ EPS accretion and a 1.7-year earnback. The problem is that the market has already priced the franchise: 2.03x tangible book standalone, 2.15x pro forma, implying a permanent 16% ROTCE, so you pay for continuation and get the deal's upside only if it closes and integrates cleanly. Three things argue for waiting: the auction was competitive enough to lift Bank7's own bid by 34%, the target comes out of a receivership with no credit mark visible in the filed exhibit, and the acquirer reported ineffective internal controls across eight areas at year-end 2025. Revisit on Fed approval and the first combined quarter's cost of funds; a pullback toward 1.6x tangible book, roughly $43, would change the answer.
