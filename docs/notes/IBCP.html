1. What the business actually does

Independent Bank Corporation is the Grand Rapids holding company for Independent Bank, chartered in Ionia in 1864, now 56 branches and six loan production offices across Michigan's Lower Peninsula (10-K 2025, Item 1). It is a plain commercial bank with no trust business: $4.41B of loans (commercial $2.36B, residential mortgage $1.53B, installment $0.52B) funded by $4.86B of deposits, $1.03B non-interest-bearing and only $0.5M brokered. Interest and fees on loans were 75.7% of FY2025 revenue, fee income 14.5% (10-K 2025, Item 1). On 2026-07-01 it closed the purchase of HCB Financial Corp. (Highpoint Community Bank, Hastings), taking it to roughly $6.3B of assets and 66 locations.

2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case

There is none, and that decides the verdict. IBCP is a Nasdaq Global Select name trading $6.1M a day, five points off its 52-week high, with a five-year TSR of $100 to $212.57 against $216.97 for the Nasdaq Bank index (DEF 14A 2026, Pay versus Performance). Nobody is forced to sell it, no spin-off or index event is in play, and the business is not misunderstood. The only candidate is the credit scare in section 3, and the market has not marked the stock down for it: a competent community bank at a fair price, not a discount.

3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix

Credit is the live issue. Non-accrual loans have risen every quarter for five quarters: $10.5M, $22.6M, $33.1M, $37.8M, $43.7M (8-K 2026-07-23, EX-99.2). Net of government guarantees, non-performing loans went from $8.2M (0.20% of loans) to $32.8M (0.74%) and allowance coverage fell from 745% to 200%. Management attributes the commercial piece "primarily" to one commercial development exposure totalling $28.18M (EX-99.1), which appears as $14.34M of construction and $13.83M of income-producing non-accrual: one relationship across two buckets, 5.6% of tangible common equity.

Two things argue it is contained: net charge-offs are still 0.03% of average loans, and the allowance was built to 1.49% while NPLs rose, with commercial watch credits flat at $101.4M (4.3% of commercial loans against 4.2% at year-end). One argues it is not: a credit on non-accrual four quarters without resolution usually ends in a charge-off, and the 10-K already flagged coverage falling from 989% to 274% during 2025 (Item 1A). CRE including owner-occupied is $1.71B, 38.8% of loans; non-owner-occupied $1.01B, 22.9%.

The margin is better news. NIM rose to 3.71% FTE from 3.58%, the cost of interest-bearing liabilities falling to 2.00% from 2.37% while earning-asset yield fell only 15bp: a liability-sensitive book repricing down faster than assets. Uninsured deposits are a low 24.8% of the total (10-K 2025, Item 1A), against $2.29B of contingent liquidity. One item outside book value deserves a haircut: held-to-maturity securities carry at $287.6M against a $261.0M fair value, a $26.6M unrealised loss that never touches equity, which after tax cuts TBVPS to about $23.22 and raises P/TBV to 1.57x.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

CET1 11.70%, total capital 13.78%, TCE 8.86%, all above well-capitalised; holding-company debt is $39.9M of subordinated debentures ($38.7M qualifying as Tier 1) plus $127.0M of bank borrowings. Capital allocation is the mixed part. The board authorised a 1,100,000-share (5%) repurchase on 2025-12-16 and through 2026-06-30 not one share had been bought (8-K 2026-07-23, EX-99.1); Q2's only "repurchases" were 3,515 shares withheld for tax on vesting (10-Q 2026-08-05). Instead it issued roughly 1.6M shares for HCB. The dividend rose to $0.28 a quarter, $1.12 annualised, a 3.1% yield on about a third of normalised earnings. Insiders are small net sellers: no open-market buys and 2,789 shares sold for $104K in twelve months, including 2,000 by CEO Brad Kessel on 2026-09-15 at about $37.20, who still holds 157,836. His $1.56M 2025 pay is modest for the size (DEF 14A 2026).

5. Management: said versus did

Kessel's Q2 letter says the quarter was delivered "while maintaining strong asset quality" (8-K 2026-07-23). That is defensible on charge-offs and false on non-accruals, which quadrupled year over year; the release never uses the word deterioration. Against that, the 2026-04-29 deck guided CET1 near 11.5% at closing and the bank was at 11.70%, and the stated strategy of running the bond book into higher-yielding loans shows up in the numbers (securities $781.5M from $838.8M, loans up $250M). Five-year delivery is real but unspectacular: diluted EPS $2.53 to $3.27, a 5.3% CAGR, on pre-tax pre-provision income compounding at 1.3%.

6. Valuation

Reverse DCF. At $36.47 against $24.24 of tangible book, on a justified P/TBV of (ROTE − g)/(Ke − g) with a 10.5% cost of equity and 5% book growth, the current price implies a sustainable ROTE of about 13.25% in perpetuity, almost exactly the 13.7% the bank earns today. The price asks IBCP only to keep doing what it does.

  • Base (55%). HCB integrates on schedule, credit costs normalise to 0.15% of loans, pro forma TBVPS compounds 8% a year from a post-deal ~$23.3 to $27.2 by end-2028, stock holds 1.45x. $39.4 plus $2.24 of dividends = $41.6.
  • Bear (20%). The $28.18M credit charges off in full (about $1.07 a share of book) and non-accruals reach 1.5% of loans, pushing provisions to 0.40% of loans and EPS to about $2.70; the multiple derates to 1.1x a $22.5 book. $24.8.
  • Bull (25%). Credit stabilises, deposits reprice to a 3.85% NIM, HCB accretion beats the ~6% guide and the 5% authorisation is executed. EPS about $4.00 in 2028 at 12x. $48 plus dividends = $50.2.

Probability-weighted $40.4 against $36.47: about 11% over two years, 5% a year. Not enough.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q3 2026 results in late October, the first pro forma quarter and the first read on whether HCB's 67% loan-to-deposit book is being levered up. HCB systems conversion 2026-11-09, when cost saves appear or slip. Resolution or charge-off of the $28.18M credit. Settlement of the three overdraft-fee class actions (10-K 2025, Item 1A). The December 2026 decision on a 2027 repurchase authorisation.

8. Pre-registered kill criteria

  1. Non-performing loans exceed 1.25% of portfolio loans, or allowance coverage falls below 150%, in any quarter (0.74% and 200% at 6/30/26).
  2. Net charge-offs exceed 0.25% of average loans annualised in two consecutive quarters (0.03% now).
  3. Tangible book value per share falls year over year in any quarter (+14.2% now).
  4. The 1,100,000-share authorisation lapses unused on 2026-12-31 below 1.6x tangible book, evidence management prefers printing stock for deals to buying its own.

9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary

WATCH, conviction 2. Independent Bank is a genuinely decent Michigan community bank, earning 15% on tangible equity, growing tangible book per share 14% in a year, widening its margin to 3.71% as deposits reprice down and buying a small neighbour with a 3.4-year earnback, but it is neither cheap nor misunderstood. At $36.47 you pay 10.5x trailing earnings and 1.50x tangible book, 1.57x after the $26.6M of held-to-maturity losses sitting outside equity, roughly what a 13.25% perpetual-ROTE bank is worth; the screen's apparent discount was tagging error, including a revenue figure off by a factor of seven and a share count that pairs the post-deal count with pre-deal earnings. The one fact that could move the price is credit: non-accruals have risen five straight quarters to $43.7M, coverage has fallen from 745% to 200%, and a single $28.18M commercial development relationship is most of it, with charge-offs still 0.03%. Watch rather than own. If that credit resolves cleanly and the multiple holds, this is a fair compounder at best; if the market mistakes it for a credit problem it is not, the discount worth having will appear.
