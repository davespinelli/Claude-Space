1. What the business actually does

NewLake buys cultivation facilities and dispensaries from state-licensed cannabis operators and leases them back on 15 to 20 year triple-net terms with parent guarantees (10-K 2025, Item 1). At 30 June 2026 it owned 34 properties in 12 states, 31 leased to 12 tenants, three available for lease (10-Q Q2 2026). Cultivation is 91.8% of rental income across 1.6M square feet, dispensaries 8.2%. Seven employees run it, G&A is 1.5% of assets, and the weighted average yield on leased assets is 13.2% (EX-99.2 p.4). Because collectability is not deemed probable under ASC 842 for any tenant, all rent is recognised on a cash basis (10-K 2025, Item 7): reported revenue is cash received, with no straight-line receivable to reverse later.

2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case

Three things, only two of which are free. First, it trades on OTCQX, not an exchange, so index funds and most institutions cannot own it at any price; management names the CLIMB Act as the uplisting path (EX-99.2 p.6). Second, on 22 April 2026 the Attorney General rescheduled medical cannabis to Schedule III, removing Section 280E for DEA-registered medical operators and raising tenant after-tax cash flow (10-Q Q2 2026). Rent coverage improves before it shows in NLCP's numbers. Third, and this part is compensation rather than mispricing, the market is extrapolating 2025: two of 13 tenants defaulted and vacated three cultivation buildings, none re-leased in 13 to 14 months. Sellers are right to price that risk, and may be wrong only in pricing it as permanent.

3. Unit economics, growth and tenant credit

Revenue has fallen every quarter from $13.21M in Q1 2025 to $12.09M in Q2 2026 as AYR (Pottsville PA, Sparks NV) and Revolutionary Clinics (Fitchburg MA) rolled off (EX-99.2 p.19; 10-K 2025, Item 7). Those three buildings hold $71.1M of invested capital and 240,419 square feet at zero rent plus about $0.14M a quarter of carrying cost (EX-99.2 p.24). None is impaired. The rest is concentrated: Curaleaf 25.2%, Cresco 15.0%, Trulieve 12.1%, C3 9.5%, The Cannabist Company 8.1% (EX-99.2 p.13). Management shows Q1 2026 tenant figures of Curaleaf $324M revenue / $63M adjusted EBITDA, Cresco $151M/$33M, Trulieve $287M/$100M. Cannabist is the sick one, in CCAA proceedings with Chapter 15 recognition granted 9 May 2026, yet it paid full rent on all four properties through the half, and Vireo Growth agreed in July 2026 to buy its operations in five states including Illinois and Massachusetts, where the leases sit (8-K 2026-08-05). Estimated four-wall coverage is 3.9x, a management estimate, not audited (EX-99.2 p.11). Collection was 100% of contractual rent in Q1 and Q2 2026 (8-Ks 2026-05-08, 2026-08-05).

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

$25.8M cash against $7.6M drawn, $18.2M net cash, $82.4M undrawn but only $30M usable without further approval from a single regional bank (8-K 2026-08-05). Share count moved from 20,552,632 to 20,580,766 in six months, effectively no dilution, and the $50M ATM is unused. Book value is $18.57 against a $15.07 price. No repurchase authorisation appears anywhere in the filings, which is the capital-allocation criticism: retiring stock at a 13% AFFO yield carries no credit risk, unlike new deals at 13.2%. Insiders hold 4.4% as a group, HG Vora 7.6% with board nomination rights, West Entities 7.5%, NL Ventures 5.6% (DEF 14A 2026). Twelve months of Form 4s contain one open-market buy: the CEO's 350 shares at $14.33 on 13 May 2026, about $5,000, a gesture rather than a signal. CEO pay was $1.20M for 2025 on a $400k salary, mostly PSUs.

5. Management: said versus did

Coniglio has been consistent and so far accurate: in May, results "in line with expectations, highlighted by 100% collection of contractual rent," with rescheduling "a pivotal moment" whose implementation "will take time" (8-K 2026-05-08); in August, the revolver extension and an "accretive dispensary acquisition in Kentucky" (8-K 2026-08-05), which is $0.6M purchased with $1.6M committed, real but small. They have not guided to a re-leasing timeline and have not pretended to, and the dividend has been held at $0.43 rather than raised, which is honest at an 88% payout. Co-founder and former CEO David Weinstein left the board on 14 July 2026 with no stated disagreement (8-K 2026-07-15).

6. Valuation

Base: the three dark buildings stay empty through 2027, one then re-leases or sells at half its old economics, escalators add 2.6% elsewhere, one further tenant costs 5% of rent. AFFO/share settles near $1.90, the $1.72 dividend holds, and at a 9.5% yield that is $18.10. Bear: Cannabist's leases are not assumed, a second large tenant restructures, vacancy persists, the board cuts to $1.20; AFFO/share $1.55 at a 12% yield on the cut gives $10.00. Bull: the April order survives court challenge, 280E relief lifts coverage, two buildings re-lease at 60% of prior rent (about +$0.24 AFFO/share), an uplisting arrives; AFFO/share $2.20 at an 8% yield is $25.00. Weighting 55/25/20 gives about $17.50, roughly 16% above the price with an 11.4% dividend collected meanwhile. Reverse DCF: at $15.07 against $1.96 of annualised AFFO the price demands 13.0% (r minus g), so at an 11% cost of equity the current price implies AFFO shrinking about 2% a year in perpetuity, against contractual escalators of +2.6%. Roughly 4.5 points a year of permanent credit and vacancy loss is already priced.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Any re-lease or sale of Fitchburg, Pottsville or Sparks, each worth roughly $0.10 to $0.20 of annual AFFO/share. The DEA ALJ recommendation on broader rescheduling, due after the 17 August 2026 briefing deadline (10-Q Q2 2026). Closing of Vireo/Cannabist, which swaps a bankrupt tenant for a solvent one on 8.1% of rent. The D.C. Circuit ruling on the April order. CLIMB Act or SAFER Banking, either of which changes the buyer base. Completion of the Hartford CT sale, where C3 owes a make-whole on any shortfall to basis (10-K 2025, Item 7).

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

Single-purpose cultivation property has thin alternative use: Fitchburg was bought at $290 a square foot and has no tenant. Federal illegality caps both the buyer base and the lender base (one bank). Kill criteria, all observable in filings: 1. Dividend below $0.43 a quarter, or AFFO payout above 100% for two consecutive quarters. 2. None of Pottsville, Sparks or Fitchburg re-leased or under contract by the Q2 2027 10-Q (24 months vacant), or an impairment taken on any of the three. 3. Any tenant at or above 8% of base rent stops paying full contractual rent, i.e. the quarterly release drops the "100% collection of contractual rent" line. 4. The D.C. Circuit vacates the April 2026 rescheduling order, restoring 280E for medical operators.

9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary

IDEA, conviction 3. NewLake is a cannabis net-lease REIT at 7.3x EV to annualised AFFO and 0.81x book, with $18.2M net cash, 1.8% debt to assets, no maturity until 2029, paying an 11.4% dividend covered at an 88% AFFO payout. The screen's 7.4x P/FFO and +2.5% growth are stale annual figures: FFO is actually down 13% year over year because two tenants defaulted in 2025 and left three cultivation buildings holding $71.1M of basis empty for over a year. That decline is real, and it is also the entire bear case, yet the price now implies AFFO shrinks 2% a year forever against contractual escalators of +2.6%. You are paid 11.4% to own 31 leased properties at 6.2x in-place rent with the three empty ones thrown in free, every dollar of rent recognised only when collected, while the April 2026 rescheduling takes 280E out of tenants' tax bills. The risk is genuine and concentrated: Curaleaf, Cresco and Trulieve are 52% of rent, Cannabist is in bankruptcy while still paying, and the bad outcome here is a dividend cut, not insolvency.
