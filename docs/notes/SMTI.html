1. What the business does

Sanara sells soft tissue repair and bone fusion products into US hospital operating rooms. CellerateRX Surgical (hydrolyzed bovine collagen powder) and BIASURGE (a no-rinse surgical irrigation solution) dominate: soft tissue repair was $91.3M of $103.1M FY2025 revenue, bone fusion $11.8M (10-K Item 7). It owns no factories and relies on contract manufacturers and licensed technology (10-K Item 1). Distribution is 43 field reps and more than 450 independent distributors, with products contracted at over 4,000 hospitals and surgery centres (Q1 2026 call, CEO). Gross margin is 93%. In mid-September 2025 the board discontinued Tissue Health Plus, its value-based wound care venture, after failing to find a buyer, leaving a single surgical segment (10-K Item 1).

2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case

There is none. On 29 July 2026 Sanara agreed to be acquired by MiMedx Group for $33.00 in cash plus 0.4735 MDXG shares per share (8-K 2026-07-29, Item 1.01). At MDXG's $4.72 in the same screen file, the package is worth $35.23 against a $35.11 share price, a gross spread of about 12 cents, 0.36%, on a deal management expects to close by year end. The screen's flattering statistics (+79% six-month return, sitting exactly at its 52-week high, artifact_flag true) are consequences of that announcement and of the accounting error above, not of a misunderstood business.

3. GAAP to normalised, line by line

Continuing operations FY2025: revenue $103,117,982 − COGS $7,520,969 − SG&A $78,716,999 − R&D $5,072,483 − D&A $2,661,873 − impairment $1,841,120 = $7,304,538 (10-K Item 7). Add back $1,841,120 → $9,145,658. Do not add the $26.5M THP charge: the 10-K locates it in discontinued operations for the quarter ended 30 September 2025, and discontinued results "have been excluded from continuing operations for all periods presented." Is the $1.8M charge non-recurring? Partly. A $0.5M THP software write-off ran through D&A in 2024, and $26.5M plus $1.8M landed in 2025, a third consecutive year of write-offs tied to the same failed diversification. Treat the charges as over; do not treat capital allocation as clean. The 2026 run rate is no better: H1 operating income was $4.43M, or $5.54M excluding $1.11M of merger advisory costs (10-Q Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation), roughly $11.1M annualised and $8.3M after tax, still 42.6x EV.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

$46.5M of CRG term loan at 13.25% (8.00% cash, 5.25% available as PIK), maturing 30 March 2029, secured on substantially all assets including IP, with a 7% back-end fee and prepayment premiums (10-Q). Covenants are a $3.0M liquidity floor and hard annual revenue minimums of $85M for 2026, $95M for 2027, $105M thereafter; the company was compliant at 30 June 2026 and has paid interest in cash, not PIK, through H1. Shares grew 3.2% year over year and there is no buyback. Ronald Nixon, Executive Chairman, beneficially owns 39.0% and directors and officers 42.0% (DEF 14A 2026-04-17). No open-market insider buys or sales in twelve months, only grants and tax withholding (Form 4 summary), so no signal either way. Related-party advisory fees to Nixon's Catalyst Group fell from $288,594 (2024) to $12,480 (2025) and the agreement was terminated on 2 June 2026 (8-K 2026-06-04).

5. Management: said versus did

On the Q1 call the CEO reaffirmed "full year 2026 net revenue in the range of $116 million to $121 million" and guided Q2 to $28.5-29.5M. Q2 came in at $28.1M and the release dropped the guidance section entirely (8-K 2026-08-11, EX-99.1). The CFO called paying debt service fully in cash "a milestone and a reflection of our improving free cash flow generation"; in the same half, operating cash flow was negative $0.4M. Against that, they did the hard thing on THP: shut it and took the charge. Selling four months after the pure-play pivot is a candid admission that the standalone cost of capital was the problem.

6. Valuation

Base (90%): the merger closes near year end and you collect $33.00 plus 0.4735 MDXG = $35.23 at $4.72, a 0.4% gross return over roughly three and a half months, about 1.2% annualised, carrying unhedged MDXG equity risk on 6% of the value. Bear (7%): the deal breaks. The $35.00 announced package was a 46% premium to the 30-day VWAP as of 28 July 2026, implying an unaffected price near $23.97, or −32%. Parent owes a $9.66M fee only if it fails to close when required (10-Q; the 8-K prints the two fee amounts transposed). Bull (3%): a topping bid near $39. Nixon's block is locked by a voting agreement covering 38.9% of voting power and the company-payable break fee of $22.5M is about 7% of equity value, a real deterrent (8-K 2026-07-29). Probability-weighted value $34.56 against $35.11. Reverse DCF: at $35.11 the enterprise is $354M against $6.9M of normalised after-tax operating profit, so at a 10% cost of capital the price implies a steady-state $35M of after-tax operating profit, which on guided 2026 revenue of $118M requires roughly a 40% operating margin versus 8.9% in FY2025 and 7.9% in H1 2026. As a standalone business the price is indefensible. It is a deal price.

7. Catalysts and timeline

S-4 effectiveness and the special meeting proxy; HSR expiry or a second request; the shareholder vote (38.9% already committed, majority of outstanding required); expected close by end of 2026, with an End Date of 29 July 2027 extendable to 29 January 2028 for antitrust.

8. Pre-registered kill criteria

  1. An 8-K under Item 1.02 terminating the merger agreement. Revisit as a standalone at the post-break price, with 1.35x interest cover as the first question.
  2. Disclosure of an HSR second request, or close slipping past 31 March 2027.
  3. FY2026 revenue below $116M, the low end of the reaffirmed guide, testing whether Q2's 9% was a one-off.
  4. MDXG below $3.00, which takes the stock leg under $1.42 and total consideration under $34.42, below today's price.

9. Verdict and summary

PASS, conviction 4. Sanara looked like a 13.2x quality-growth compounder because the screen made two mistakes on the same filing: it added a $28.3M impairment back to operating profit when $26.5M of it sat in discontinued operations and had never been charged against continuing profit, and it booked a $37.6M net loss as a $37.6M net profit. Corrected, this is a 93%-gross-margin surgical products business earning $9.1M of normalised operating profit on $103M of revenue, paying 13.25% on $46.5M of debt, covering interest 1.35 times, generating negative operating cash flow in the first half of 2026, and decelerating from 19% to 9% revenue growth while missing its own quarterly guide. None of that is what you buy at $35.11, because on 29 July 2026 MiMedx agreed to pay $33.00 in cash plus 0.4735 of its own shares, worth $35.23 today. The market has priced the stock to within twelve cents of the deal. A 0.4% gross spread with 32% downside if it breaks is thin arbitrage, not a fundamental idea. The thing worth keeping is the correction: this row should not appear as a qualifier again.
