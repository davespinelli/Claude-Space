Desk stats

  • Revenue trend. Total revenue (net interest income plus fees) was $178.1M in FY2026 against $154.0M, up 15.7%; the June quarter $45.7M against $40.8M, up 11.9% (8-K 2026-07-23, non-GAAP reconciliation). The driver is funding cost alone: "The increase between the comparative periods resulted from a decrease of $20.5 million in interest expense, partially offset by a decrease of $163,000 in interest income" (10-K, Item 7). Cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 3.04% to 2.76% while earning-asset yield rose four basis points, 4.51% to 4.55% (same table). Loans grew 1.1%, deposits 0.6%.
  • Normalised after-tax profit. GAAP net income $36.264M, $0.57 diluted, pre-tax $47.402M (8-K 2026-07-23, FY reconciliation). Adjustments from that reconciliation: add retail-realignment severance $745K, add branch consolidation $250K, deduct gains on sale of properties held for sale $1.769M. Adjusted pre-tax $46.628M; at the desk's 25% rather than the 20.2% actually paid before the discrete charge, normalised after-tax profit is $34.97M, $0.55 a share, against $36.3M GAAP. Two items inside it are not durable: $2.2M of purchase accounting accretion and $2.1M of prepayment penalty income against $783K a year earlier (10-K, Item 7); stripping those leaves core profit near $32.3M, $0.51. The company's own $37.277M "adjusted" is higher only because it also adds back a $1.562M deferred tax asset valuation allowance and taxes at the realised rate.
  • Valuation multiple. EV is not meaningful for a deposit-funded bank. At $9.81 the stock is 17.2x FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS, 18.2x normalised, 19.7x core, and 0.974x the $10.07 June 30 tangible book (8-K 2026-07-23, Capital). Marking the $8.4M after-tax held-to-maturity loss that is not in equity takes book to $9.94 and P/TBV to 0.99x.
  • Financial-lane leverage. Tangible equity/tangible assets 8.62%, CET1 14.57%, Tier 1 leverage 9.48% (8-K 2026-06-03, deck p.21). Funding is not pristine: brokered CDs $757.2M, 13.3% of deposits, borrowings $1.15B, uninsured deposits ex-collateralised and affiliate $851.0M, 14.9%, against $2.35B of secured capacity (10-K, Item 7).
  • Is the growth sustainable? Partly bought, mostly a spent tailwind. Deposit repricing cannot repeat at 28 basis points a year, and the loan growth sold as relationship banking is purchased paper: the C&I book grew $85.2M while the company bought $93.8M of C&I loans from third parties, of $173.5M total purchases, and organic C&I originations were $118.5M against $118.1M (10-K, Item 1).
  • What the screen got wrong. The 24.4x P/E divides market cap by FY2025 net income of $26.075M while using the June 30, 2026 balance sheet; on the FY2026 10-K filed three weeks earlier it is 17.2x on diluted EPS, so the screen made the stock look 40% dearer than it is, and the same stale numerator gives the 4.0% ROTE against a real 5.66%. Equity/assets of 9.98% includes $113.5M of goodwill against a tangible 8.62%. It also cannot see that the $0.44 dividend, $27.634M, exceeded FY2025 net income, a 106% payout, and still takes 77% of FY2026 earnings.

1. What the business actually does

An 1884-vintage New Jersey savings bank, fully stock-converted, $7.68B of assets and 40 branches across ten New Jersey counties plus Brooklyn and Staten Island (10-K, Item 1). It lends against real estate: multi-family $2.50B (42.5% of loans), nonresidential CRE $1.02B (17.3%), one-to-four family $1.79B (30.4%), construction $263.2M, C&I $223.9M, at a 59% weighted average LTV, with a $1.07B securities book alongside. Since 2023 the strategy has been to shrink multi-family and grow C&I, construction and home equity; multi-family originations fell to $38.5M from $132.1M.

2. Why it is mispriced, and the honest answer

There is a screen artifact but little market-level edge. The artifact is mechanical: a June fiscal year end means most databases carried FY2025 earnings until late August, so the stock reads at 24x when it earns 17x, and there is no transcript or analyst depth. But the edge has largely closed in public: the shares are up 38.8% over six months and sit 3.2% below the 52-week high (universe_v2.csv). Whoever sold at 0.65x tangible book a year ago has stopped, and nobody is forced out today. Because I cannot name a live edge case, the verdict is capped at WATCH by the desk's own rule.

3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix

Credit is the reassuring part: net charge-offs were $2.4M, four basis points, against a 2006-to-2026 average of nine versus 47 for comparable banks (8-K 2026-06-03, deck p.17). But non-performing assets rose to $53.4M, 0.70% of assets, from $45.6M; non-performing loans are $47.9M, 0.82% of loans, and the allowance covers them 94.99%, down from 101.30% (10-K, Item 1). That allowance is 0.77% of a book 64% weighted to commercial real estate. Multi-family non-performers run 1.58% of multi-family loans; NYC multi-family is $1.19B, average loan $3.18M, 61.4% LTV, only 5.2% majority rent-regulated, which is genuinely modest (deck p.14). The largest borrower is $79.5M across six multi-family loans, 12.2% of tangible equity. NIM improved a seventh consecutive quarter to 2.26%, and to 2.18% for the year from 1.88%, but that is a thrift margin and the asset side contributed almost none of it.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Capital is ample and idle. CET1 of 14.57% is far above requirement, yet no stock was repurchased in FY2026 at 0.97x tangible book and no repurchase programme appears anywhere in the filings; share count drifted up 0.25% on equity compensation. Capital leaves instead as a dividend that took 106% of FY2025 earnings and 77% of FY2026's. Insiders and directors own 7.68% and the ESOP 7.19%; BlackRock entities hold 13.9% across two filers (DEF 14A 2026-09-09). Two insiders bought 28,023 shares for $254,952 over twelve months with zero open-market sales, including a director at $9.41 on 2026-07-27 (form4_summary.md).

5. Management: said versus did

The performance restricted stock units granted in August 2022 and cliff-vesting in FY2026 did not vest at all, the metrics were not attained, and the executives took no economic value from them (DEF 14A 2026-09-09, note 5 to the vesting table). That is honest pay-for-performance. The June deck is looser: it leads with "Expanding team of C&I lenders are growing primary banking relationships" (deck p.3) while the 10-K shows the C&I book grew by less than the C&I loans bought from third parties. The CEO gives no numeric guidance, only "tailwinds from low-coupon loan repricing" (8-K 2026-07-23).

6. Valuation

The repricing tailwind is the bull case and the company quantifies it: $450.4M maturing and $1,005.2M repricing through 2029, current rates near 3.8% against replacement near 6.6%, a 280 basis point spread on $1.456B, about $41M pre-tax (deck p.13). Three discounts: the schedule excludes coupons above 6%; the maturing bucket only helps if replaced, and management is deliberately shrinking multi-family; and deposits reprice too.

Base, 50%: half the spread arrives net of funding-cost creep, NIM reaches 2.45% by FY2029, expenses grow 3% a year, earnings reach $50M, $0.77 a share, a 7.2% ROTCE; at 12.5x, $9.60 plus $1.32 of dividends, $10.90. Bear, 25%: multi-family runs off faster than C&I replaces it, deposit competition returns, NYC multi-family credit normalises against a thin allowance, earnings stay near $37M and the multiple reverts to 0.75x book, $7.75 plus dividends, $8.90. Bull, 25%: rates hold, the efficiency programme cuts the 72.5% adjusted efficiency ratio toward 65%, earnings reach $65M and a 9.5% ROTCE, 13x gives $13.00 plus dividends, $14.30. Probability-weighted $11.25 against $9.81 is roughly 15% over three years, under 5% a year including the dividend.

Reverse DCF: at 0.974x tangible book with a 10% cost of equity and 3% terminal growth, the price implies a sustainable return on tangible common equity near 9.8%, against 5.66% actually earned in FY2026.

7. Catalysts and timeline

The September and December 2026 quarters are the test: total interest income was flat for a full year, and if it begins rising the asset-repricing thesis is confirmed. A repurchase authorisation at 0.97x tangible book would signal more than any deck. Longer term a top-ten New Jersey deposit franchise below tangible book with no controlling MHC is plausible acquisition currency at 1.3x to 1.5x book, $13 to $15, but nothing in the filings suggests a process.

8. Pre-registered kill criteria

  1. Total interest income (not net) fails to rise year over year in both the September 2026 and December 2026 quarters.
  2. Non-performing loans exceed 1.10% of total loans, or allowance coverage of non-performing loans falls below 85%, in any quarter.
  3. Net interest margin declines in two consecutive quarters, ending the expansion streak.
  4. The dividend is cut, or the payout exceeds 100% of GAAP earnings for two consecutive quarters.

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 3. Kearny is a clean, conservatively underwritten New Jersey thrift whose earnings genuinely inflected in FY2026, up 39% to $36.3M, and the screen's 24x P/E and 4.0% ROTE are stale artifacts of a June fiscal year end; the real figures are 17.2x and 5.66%. Correcting the artifact does not make it cheap. All $20.3M of the net interest income gain came from paying less for deposits, not from earning more on assets, where yields rose four basis points in a year, and that lever is nearly spent. The growth sold as relationship banking was bought: the C&I book grew $85M while $94M of C&I loans were purchased from third parties. At 0.97x tangible book the price already implies a sustainable return on tangible equity near 9.8% against 5.66% delivered, the stock has run 39% in six months to within 3% of its 52-week high, and a bank earning 5.7% on tangible equity while paying out 77% and buying back nothing is not obviously worth tangible book. The repricing schedule is real and could roughly double earnings by 2029, so this is worth following; the thing to wait for is the first quarter in which total interest income actually rises.
