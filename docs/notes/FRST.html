1. What the business actually does

Primis Bank is a $4.4B Virginia state-chartered bank with 24 branches in Virginia and Maryland (10-Q). It runs four engines: a core community bank of about $2.1B of loans weighted to owner-occupied CRE, C&I and residential builders rather than investor CRE; Panacea, a national lender to doctors and dentists ($544M of loans at year end, up 25%); a Mortgage Warehouse book lending to mortgage originators; and Primis Mortgage, a retail originator that closed $421M in Q2 (10-K Item 1; 8-K 2026-07-23). It exited the two businesses that broke it: Life Premium Finance, sold in 2024 and early 2025, and a third-party point-of-sale Consumer Program shut to new origination in January 2025 after $40M of 2024 provisions (10-K, Item 7).

2. Why it is mispriced, the edge case

There is none, and that caps this at WATCH. The stock sits 3.2% below its 52-week high after rising 27.6% in six months and 57% over twelve. Nothing is forcing anyone to sell: no spin, no index event, no sponsor exit. BlackRock owns 8.18% and Castle Creek 7.00%, both long-standing, and insiders hold 10.87% as a group (DEF 14A 2026). What the screen found is not a dislocation but a data error of its own making. The honest residual observation is narrower: a company that printed a large one-off item in six of the last seven quarters, restated financials in 2024 (referenced in the 10-K's explanation of a higher FDIC assessment base) and filed its 2024 10-K four months late is hard to read from a screen, so the multiple carries a complexity discount. The company's own deck argues exactly that, showing 1.17x tangible book against a peer average of 1.5x and promising to "erase that discount over the next four to six quarters" (Q2 call, CEO). That is an argument, not an edge.

3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix

Credit is improving from a bad place and is not yet clean. Nonperforming loans excluding SBA guarantees were 1.45% of assets at 30 June, down from 2.35% at 31 March and 2.03% at year end, but the improvement came from one commercial relationship refinancing away, not from cures (10-Q; Q2 call, CFO). Nonaccrual loans are $61.8M, 1.78% of loans, covered only 74.3% by the $46.0M allowance (10-Q). One office CRE loan carries "a little over $11 million of reserve" and has sat on nonaccrual twelve months with vacancy that "only moved a little at the margin"; the Q2 provision of $5.45M was almost entirely a further build on it, marked by discounted cash flow rather than appraisal because the borrower is still paying (Q2 call, CEO and CFO). Net charge-offs were $6.9M in H1, 0.21% of average loans for the six months, with core annualised charge-offs at 53bp in Q2 against 6bp in Q1 (10-Q; Q2 call, CFO). Concentration is genuinely low: non-owner-occupied CRE is 15.1% of loans and construction 4.4% (10-Q). Funding is the real asset. Core bank cost of deposits was 1.60% in Q2, noninterest-bearing deposits are 16.3% of the average book against 14.3% a year ago, and there were no brokered deposits at year end (8-K 2026-07-23; 10-K, Item 7).

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

The dividend is $0.10 a quarter, unchanged, about 26% of the Q2 run rate. There is no buyback. Share count is flat at 24.80M. The pressure point is capital: TCE/TA of 7.99% and holdco CET1 of 9.33% are adequate but not generous against earning assets growing 11% to 14% annualised, and every consolidated ratio fell in the quarter. The company filed a shelf during Q2, with the registration expense disclosed on the call. A raise at 1.18x tangible book would be dilutive to the thesis. The offsetting lever is the $69.4M of subordinated debt the CFO expects to refinance "in the next quarter or two" at a saving of 200 to 250 basis points, worth roughly $1.5M pre-tax (Q2 call).

5. Management: what they said versus what they did

Zember's framing on the call is that the $5.9M insurance gain was "fully offset" by the CRE reserve build and the legal settlement, so "our stated ROA for the quarter of 90 basis points is really the recurring level." The arithmetic is close, but it treats a $5.3M credit provision as non-recurring for a bank whose largest problem loan is unresolved. Take the gain out and leave the provision in, as a lender should, and Q2 pre-tax was $6.9M, not $12.1M. Against that, the operating-leverage claim checks out and is the strongest fact here: pre-tax pre-provision operating income went from $4.1M to $11.7M year over year, and core operating expense excluding mortgage and Panacea grew about 15% against 40% revenue growth (8-K 2026-07-23; Q2 call). Management also made a dated, quantified promise: core expense burden back to $22.0M to $22.5M in Q3 from about $25M in Q2, and $6.1M of core-consolidation earnings improvement plus $3.2M a year of platform amortisation ending Q3 2027, together about $0.22 a share.

6. Valuation

Base: management delivers the Q3 expense step-down, the sub-debt refinance and about half the core-consolidation benefit, and provisions run at 30bp. Normalised pre-tax reaches roughly $46M in 2027, EPS about $1.39, at 11x peer earnings gives $15.3, plus a year of dividends. Bear: mortgage volumes revert with rates, Primis Mortgage's $2.2M quarterly pre-tax and the warehouse book both shrink, the office loan takes another $8M, and the shelf is used at book; EPS $0.95 at 9x gives $8.5. Bull: the full $7M lands, margin adds the 10bp the CEO sees, credit normalises to 15bp and the discount to the 1.5x peer P/TBV closes on tangible book of about $15.50; $23.3. Weighting 45/30/25 gives $15.4, about 5% below the $16.14 price. Reverse DCF: at 1.18x tangible book, with an 11% cost of equity and 4% terminal growth, the price implies a sustainable ROATCE of about 12.3%, against 11.1% reported today and 7.6% normalised. In other words the market has already underwritten management's 2027 plan.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q3 2026 results in late October are the first test of the $22.0M to $22.5M expense promise and the first quarter with no announced one-off. The sub-debt refinance is guided within two quarters. Core conversion benefits begin in Q4 2026 and are largely in place early 2027. Resolution or further reserving on the office credit is quarter by quarter.

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

  1. Core operating expense excluding mortgage and Panacea exceeds $23.0M in either Q3 or Q4 2026, breaking the operating-leverage claim on which the whole 2027 bridge rests.
  2. Nonaccrual loans rise back above 2.00% of loans, or the office CRE relationship moves to collateral-dependent accounting or charge-off, in any quarter through mid-2027.
  3. Any common equity issuance off the shelf below 1.3x tangible book value per share.
  4. Mortgage banking income falls below $8.0M in any quarter, a 30% decline from Q2, confirming that a third of fee income is rate-cycle rent.

9. Verdict and one-paragraph summary a smart friend could repeat

WATCH, conviction 3. Primis screens at 6.5x earnings and 18.7% return on tangible equity only because FY2025 net income of $61.4M contained $68.1M of net one-off pre-tax gains, a $50.6M branch sale-leaseback and $32.3M on the Panacea Financial Holdings stake less a $14.8M securities loss; strip them and 2025 earned about $3.5M after tax. Underneath is a genuine operating turn rather than a fiction: net interest income up 34% year over year, margin from 2.86% to 3.45%, pre-tax pre-provision profit from $4.1M to $11.7M, nonperformers down from 2.35% to 1.45% of assets in one quarter, and a named, dated $7M of 2027 cost and fee improvement worth about $0.22 a share. Six insiders have bought since June, but only $139,134 in total. Wait for Q3 to show the expense burden landing at $22.0M to $22.5M with no new one-off, and this becomes a real idea.
