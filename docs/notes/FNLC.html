1. What the business does

First National Bank, chartered 1864 in Damariscotta, is an eighteen-branch community bank in mid-coast and eastern Maine with $3.22 billion of assets (10-K 2025, Item 1; Q2 2026 8-K). It lends in its own state with "very limited exposures outside of Maine" (10-Q Q2 2026). At 30 June 2026 the book is 31.0% residential term, 16.5% non-owner-occupied CRE, 16.3% C&I, 15.7% owner-occupied CRE, 6.6% home equity, 5.8% multifamily (10-Q Q2 2026). A wealth division adds fees, up 15.9% in Q2. Deposits fund 85.1% of average assets (10-K 2025, Item 7); uninsured deposits are 17.2% of the total and day-one liquidity of $706M covers 153% of them (Q2 2026 8-K).

2. Why it is mispriced: the edge case

There is not one, and that caps this note at WATCH. No spin-off, no index deletion, no forced seller, no misread segment. The market did the work already: the shares went $25.41, $26.44, $28.03, $34.82 at successive quarter ends (Q2 2026 8-K), so the screen is catching a 38% re-rating after the fact. The two residual frictions are that FNLC holds no earnings call, leaving only a press-release quote as management commentary, and that an XBRL screen reading FY2025 sees 11.5x where the run rate is 10.5x. Neither is worth much when the entire question is the next four quarters of credit.

3. Credit, margin and deposit mix

Two trends run opposite ways. Margin: the tax-equivalent NIM has risen eight consecutive quarters, 2.21% in Q2 2024 to 2.52%, 2.70%, 2.83%, 2.86%, 2.88% (10-K 2025, Item 7; Q2 2026 8-K). The cause is stated plainly: wholesale funding replaced with local deposits, wholesale CDs down $125.3M in 2025 (10-K 2025, Item 7), cost of total liabilities down 40bp to 2.88% against a stable 5.34% asset yield (Q2 2026 8-K). That reset is nearly spent; the last two steps were 3bp and 2bp.

Credit runs the other way, faster. Non-performers went 0.25%, 0.40%, 0.54%, 0.67%, 0.71% of loans over five quarters, $6.0M to $17.3M, and the deterioration is broad, not one deal: residential term non-accruals $3.2M to $6.9M, owner-occupied CRE $0.5M to $5.2M, home equity $0.6M to $1.5M (10-Q Q2 2026). Meanwhile the allowance fell from 1.04% to 1.01% of loans and coverage collapsed from 411% to 142% (Q2 2026 8-K). H1 provisions of $1.6M sat well below annualised net charge-offs of 0.20% of loans, roughly $4.8M, with $1.6M of Q2 charge-offs in one relationship. The CEO concedes the direction: credit costs are "normalis[ing] off of the extraordinary lows of the past few years" (Q2 2026 8-K). Concentrations are not alarming: CRE excluding owner-occupied is $568M, 23.4% of loans and about 178% of my estimate of total risk-based capital, well under the 300% threshold, and the only industry concentration above 10% is $245.1M to lessors of residential buildings, down from $272.0M (10-Q Q2 2026).

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Capital is building: leverage 9.21%, total risk-based 14.24%, TCE 8.23%, each up 70 to 80bp year over year (Q2 2026 8-K). The five-year capital plan stays well capitalised under severe credit and rate stress (10-Q Q2 2026). Borrowings are $214.1M including $95.5M of FHLB term advances the FHLB may put back on rate moves (10-K 2025, Item 7). AOCI carries a $32.4M after-tax unrealised loss on AFS securities, $41.2M pretax, roughly 12% of tangible book already marked. The $352.1M HTM book, mostly long-dated munis, is not marked; the 10-Q says its fair value fell a further $4.3M in the half but the bundle does not carry the HTM fair-value total, so I cannot size that mark from these documents. No buyback: shares went 11.206M to 11.279M (Q2 2026 8-K); returns come via the dividend, raised to $0.38 quarterly, a 45% payout (8-K 2026-06-25).

5. Management: said versus did

Management promised margin expansion and delivered it eight quarters running. The 2025 scorecard paid 120.3% of target on net income of $35.5M against $32.5M, NIM 2.60% against 2.51%, efficiency 51.16% against 53.13% (DEF 14A 2026-03-06), so pay tracked the result, though a falling-rate cycle made those targets easier than they look. Second-half guidance is specific and testable: McKim expects "strong local deposit inflow through year-end... allowing for reduction of higher-cost, short-term funding, helping to sustain margin expansion" (Q2 2026 8-K). Insiders own 5.11%, the CEO 1.28% (DEF 14A). Form 4s lean mildly negative: a director bought 1,500 shares near $28.70 in April; the Chief Banking Officer sold 5,200 at $35.22 on 2026-07-28, leaving 2,191 (Form 4, 2026-07-28).

6. Valuation

Base (50%): NIM plateaus near 2.90%, loans grow 3%, provisions rise to meet charge-offs at 0.20% of loans, EPS $3.40 to $3.55 in 2027, tangible book compounds 9% to about $27; at an unchanged 1.5x that is roughly $40 plus 4.3% a year in dividends. Bear (30%): normalisation overshoots, the allowance rebuilds to 1.25% of loans (about $5.8M pretax, $0.40 a share) while provisions run 0.35% to 0.40%, EPS falls to about $2.80 and the multiple compresses to 1.15x book, $27 to $28. Bull (20%): the remaining $765M of certificates reprice down, seasonal inflow displaces short-term borrowing as guided, NIM reaches 3.10%, efficiency drops below 50%, EPS approaches $3.95; at 1.8x book, $46. Weighted, about $37.50 against $35.18, a 6% gap over two years plus the dividend. Reverse DCF: at 1.51x tangible book the price implies a sustainable 13.6% ROTCE in perpetuity at a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth, which is what the bank earns today, so the price already assumes the margin holds and credit does not relapse.

7. Catalysts

Q3 2026 results in late October test both the promised seasonal deposit inflow and whether the provision finally exceeds charge-offs. A December dividend action is likely on the 2025 pattern. Maine consolidation is a background option either way: at 1.51x book and 14.5% ROTCE, FNLC is priced as neither an obvious target nor an obvious buyer.

8. Kill criteria

  1. Non-performing loans above 0.90% of total loans for two consecutive quarters, or allowance coverage below 120%.
  2. Net charge-offs above 0.25% of average loans annualised for two consecutive quarters (H1 2026: 0.20%).
  3. Tax-equivalent NIM flat or down for two consecutive quarters, breaking the eight-quarter streak.
  4. Provision below net charge-offs for two more quarters while non-performers rise, meaning reserve release is flattering earnings. Also live: the $95.5M of putable FHLB advances, a residential book where $629M of $751M matures beyond ten years, and the unmarked $352M HTM portfolio.

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 3. First Bancorp is a genuinely good small Maine bank that has just finished the easy half of a rate-cycle recovery: eight straight quarters of margin expansion took the NIM from 2.21% to 2.88%, lifted ROTCE to 14.7% and grew tangible book 11% in a year, and the market has already paid for it, re-rating the shares 38% to within 1.4% of their 52-week high. What the screen reads as an 11.5x bank is really a 10.5x bank, cheaper than the screen thought and still not cheap enough, because the margin engine has decelerated to 2bp a quarter while non-performing loans tripled year over year and reserve coverage fell from 411% to 142% with provisions running below charge-offs. At 1.51x tangible book the price already implies a permanent 13.6% ROTCE, so a buyer pays for a clean landing rather than being handed one. Wait for the Q3 print: if the provision exceeds charge-offs and non-performers stabilise below 0.90% while the margin holds above 2.85%, this becomes an IDEA on a pullback; if credit keeps sliding while the margin plateaus, the multiple corrects, not the earnings.
