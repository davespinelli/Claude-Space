1. What the business actually does

Clearwater makes Solid Bleached Sulfate paperboard, the premium virgin grade used for folding cartons and food service, and sells rolls and sheets to independent converters in North America. It converts nothing itself, so it is not simultaneously supplier to and competitor with its customers. Three mills, Lewiston Idaho, Cypress Bend Arkansas and Augusta Georgia, gave it roughly 11% of North American paperboard capacity on 1,236,114 tons shipped in 2025. It reached this shape in 2024 by buying Augusta from Graphic Packaging for $710.6M and selling tissue to Sofidel for $1.06 billion (10-K 2025, Item 1). Food service is now the largest line at $173.0M of Q2 2026 revenue, ahead of folding carton at $132.5M (8-K 2026-07-28, EX-99.2).

2. Why it looks mispriced, and the honest limit of the edge

The screen shows negative EBIT and a negative 22.7% FCF yield. That is a timing artifact: trailing results carry the $22M Lewiston major outage, which shifted from Q3 2025 into Q2 2026, a Q1 2026 weather event costing about $15M, and $20M to $25M of full-year war-related input inflation (10-K 2025, Item 7; 8-Ks 2026-04-28 and 2026-07-28, EX-99.2). Management guides Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA to $20M to $30M against negative $8.2M in Q2, a swing no trailing-twelve-month screen can see.

Underneath, supply is being withdrawn. Industry operating rates were 81% in Q1 2026 and a forecast 88% in Q2, roughly 300K tons of SBS production is out year to date with another 200K forecast by year end taking rates above 90%, imports are down about 11% through May, and RISI reported July increases of $40 per ton on folding carton and $60 on cup (8-K 2026-07-28, EX-99.2).

The limit: this is not an ignored or forced-seller name. The register is Dimensional 8.8%, Southeastern Asset Management 8.6%, BlackRock 8.6% and Vanguard 7.5% (DEF 14A 2026, as of 2026-02-28), a value manager already present. The stock is up 42.1% in six months and sits 10.3% below its 52-week high (screen row). The only edge left is mechanical: screens read the trough, not the guide.

3. Unit economics and operating leverage

This is a price-taker with large throughput leverage. Q2 2026 realized paperboard price was $1,077 per ton, down 9% year over year, against $1,167 for 2025 and $1,375 in 2023 (10-K 2025, Item 7; 10-Q 2026-06-30). On roughly 1.26M annual tons, each $50 per ton of price is about $63M of EBITDA against a $604M enterprise value. The Q2 bridge shows it in reverse: price and mix took out $32.0M while SG&A actions added $5.0M, and sequentially volume and production added $14.6M on about 26,000 extra tons (8-K 2026-07-28, EX-99.2).

Volume is the one thing going right, up 8% in Q2 and 6% in the half on share gains led by food service. Fixed costs came down over $50M in 2025 and Cypress Bend was restructured in Q2 2026, cutting about 20% of roles for $8M to $12M of annual savings. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 10.7% in 2023, 6.9% in 2025 and negative 1% in the first half of 2026, against a 13% to 14% cross-cycle target at 90% to 95% utilization. Top 10 customers were 46% of 2025 sales and about 49% of agreements reprice off a third-party index (10-K 2025, Item 1A).

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Net debt was $266.8M at 6/30/26, down $59M in the quarter, but the drivers were non-recurring: $32.5M of representation and warranty insurance proceeds, roughly $30M of tax refunds and a $44M working capital release (10-Q 2026-06-30). Only $25.0M of the $105M policy limit remains. Bank-defined net leverage is 3.5x against a stated 1x to 2x cross-cycle target, with $445M of liquidity. Long-term debt of $360.5M includes $79.0M drawn on the ABL, which matures November 7, 2027.

Capital allocation is the tell. Of $100M authorized in November 2024, $79.5M remained at year-end 2025, yet the company repurchased $0.1M in the first half of 2026 against $15.1M a year earlier, with the stock at 0.42x the $794.1M book (10-K 2025, Item 1; 10-Q 2026-06-30). Insiders bought nothing on the open market across the 12 Form 4s sampled. Comp is at least honest: the 2025 annual incentive paid 68.8% of target and 2023 to 2025 performance shares earned zero (DEF 14A 2026).

5. What they said versus what they did

Good: Q2 landed inside the April guide of $0 to negative $10M, and Lewiston came in at $22M against a $22M to $24M estimate, on time. Less good: February's 10-K guided 2026 revenue to $1.45B to $1.55B and April cut it to $1.4B to $1.5B, and full-year maintenance guidance fell from about $50M to $32M to $35M only because $10M to $11M of Augusta scope moved into Q1 2027. Most importantly, the 2026 SBS demand growth forecast was cut from 4% in April to about 1% in July, and exports from plus 1% to minus 4% (8-Ks 2026-04-28 and 2026-07-28, EX-99.2). The recovery case now rests entirely on supply withdrawal, not demand.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q3 2026 results in late October are the event: first quarter with no major outage, a $20M to $30M guide, and the first read on whether July's increases reach realized price per ton. Monthly RISI prints run in between, the forecast 200K tons of further capacity reduction should land by year end, and an ABL refinancing decision falls before November 2027.

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 3. Clearwater's screen numbers are wrecked by timing, not by the business: $22M of Lewiston outage shifted out of 2025, a $15M weather event and $20M to $25M of war-driven input inflation all land in one trailing twelve months, which is why the file shows negative EBIT and a negative 22.7% FCF yield in the same quarter management guides Q3 to $20M to $30M of EBITDA. The operating leverage is real, about $63M of EBITDA per $50 per ton on 1.26M tons against a $604M enterprise value, and supply is genuinely coming out, 300K tons year to date with 200K more forecast and imports down 11%. Against that: the demand forecast was quietly cut from 4% to 1%, 3.5x net leverage with the ABL maturing November 2027 makes the bear case a roughly 57% loss, the first-half deleveraging came from insurance proceeds and tax refunds rather than operations, and management bought $0.1M of its own stock at 0.42x book while holding $79.5M of authorization and $445M of liquidity, with no insider buying a share. The stock is up 42% in six months, so the trough entry has passed. The decisive fact arrives in late October: whether July's $40 and $60 per ton increases show up in realized price. If they do and Q3 hits the guide, this converts to an IDEA; if not, it is a levered price-taker at 17x current-year EBITDA.
