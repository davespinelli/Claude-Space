1. What the business does

BayCom is the holding company for United Business Bank, a $2.58B-asset California state commercial bank in Walnut Creek with 34 branches across California, Washington, Nevada, Colorado and New Mexico (10-K Item 1). Founded 2004, it grew by buying community banks: ten acquisitions since 2010, $2.3B of assets and $1.9B of deposits acquired (10-K Item 1). Lending is overwhelmingly commercial real estate — CRE including multifamily and construction was 86.0% of loans at December 31, 2025, of which $950.4M (46.0% of loans) non-owner-occupied and $506.2M (24.5%) owner-occupied; C&I is 8.5%. The distinctive asset is the deposit base from the 2017 First ULB deal: $590.9M, 26.7% of deposits, from 785 local labor unions averaging $753,000 each (10-K Item 1A). That is what funds a 1.56% cost of deposits.

2. Why it might be mispriced — the edge case

A founder ouster that produced an accounting loss. On April 7, 2026 the board approved the involuntary termination without cause of founder-CEO George Guarini, COO Janet King and CFO Keary Colwell, effective July 6 (DEF 14A, Leadership Transition). Severance was contractual and large: $4,414,230 to Guarini, $2,417,692 each to King and Colwell, plus accelerated restricted stock and 24 months of benefits. Booked in one quarter, that $10.5M turned a profitable quarter into a $7.0M net loss and a 107.71% efficiency ratio (Q2 release). No call was held and no transcript filed, so screens and headline readers saw "bank posts loss" with no narrative attached. Insiders bought: net $461,368 across three insiders in twelve months, zero sales, including CEO Christopher Baron 8,250 shares at $30.39 on 2026-08-10 and director Michael Perdue 5,000 at $30.17 (Form 4).

The edge case is real, but it only pays if the franchise out-earns its cost of capital, and it does not. Stripped of one-timers BayCom earns about 7.6% on tangible common equity. The seller at 1.11x tangible book is not obviously wrong.

3. Credit quality, margin and deposit mix

Headline credit improved in Q2: nonperforming loans fell to $9.8M, 0.47% of loans, from 0.83%, and classified assets to $35.6M from $43.8M (Q2 release). But the improvement was purchased — two nonaccrual loans totalling $7.7M were sold — and the quarter carried $2.8M of net charge-offs against $15,000 in Q1 and $13,000 a year earlier, plus a $1.6M rise in specific reserves driven primarily by one CRE loan. The allowance rose to 1.11% of loans from 0.94%. Management called it "exiting certain loan relationships that do not fit our go-forward business model" (Black, Q2 release), which is either a new team clearing the decks or the first innings of a CRE cycle; one quarter cannot separate them. Concentration is high: on my arithmetic, non-owner-occupied CRE plus multifamily plus construction (roughly $1.26B at December 31, 2025) against the bank's $314.1M of total risk-based capital at June 30, 2026 is about 400%, above the 300% supervisory threshold the company itself describes (10-K Item 1). Office is $216.9M. Weighted-average origination LTV of 55.5% on CRE is genuine cushion. NIM at 3.95% is good, but noninterest-bearing deposits slipped from 28.2% to 26.6% of the total in a year, and ten depositors are 11.7% of deposits with several related unions among them (10-K Item 1A).

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Capital is the asset nobody is using. CET1 is 14.10%, leverage 12.20% consolidated (10-Q). Running at 10.5% CET1 would release roughly $77M against $2.13B of risk-weighted assets, about $7 a share or 23% of market cap. Yet the company repurchased nothing in H1 2026 against $5.2M in H1 2025, and only 202,444 shares (1.9%) remain authorised. Share count is flat. The dividend was doubled to $0.30 a quarter (8-K 2026-05-22), a 58% payout on normalised earnings and the one unambiguous return. Holdco liquidity is thin at $3.2M, down from $7.5M, so buybacks depend on bank dividends. Directors and current officers own 2.9%; Baron and Black each owned zero at the proxy date (DEF 14A). Wellington holds 9.7%, BlackRock 8.9%, Royce 6.7%, Dimensional 5.5%.

5. Management: said versus did

The new team is expensive and untested here. Baron and Black each carry $675,000 base salaries with 75% bonus targets (DEF 14A) against Guarini's $745,856 for the single CEO role. Ex-severance Q2 efficiency was 66.3% against 61.73% in Q1 and 63.85% a year ago, so the higher cost base is already visible. Baron says "healthy deposit and loan pipelines have us well-positioned" (Q2 release) in a quarter when deposits fell $95.5M. On July 21 the board adopted a PSU program vesting only if the share price holds a threshold for 20 consecutive trading days, settling half in cash, with change-of-control settlement (8-K 2026-07-22); Baron received 52,500 units and Black 75,000 (Form 4). The threshold price is in no filed exhibit, so alignment cannot be assessed — and a market-priced, change-of-control-settling award at a consolidator whose founder was just removed is at least consistent with a board positioning for a sale. That is a reading, not a fact.

6. Valuation

Base: ROTCE recovers to ~9.5% by 2028 as severance annualises away and efficiency returns toward 62%, TBVPS compounds ~6% to $30.40, multiple holds 1.05x — about $32 plus $3.60 of dividends over 2.5 years, roughly $35.6, or 7% a year. Bear: Q2 was the start of a CRE cycle, provisions run $12M a year, ROTCE falls to 5%, multiple derates to 0.85x flat book — about $25 with dividends. Bull: a larger authorisation and buybacks release CET1 toward 10.5%, ROTCE reaches 12% and the multiple 1.35x on $28.50 TBVPS — about $38.50; a sale at 1.5x tangible book is about $41. Weighting base 50%, bear 25%, bull 25% gives about $34.3 against $30.03, roughly 14% over 2.5 years on top of the 4% yield. Reverse DCF: at 1.11x tangible book with a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth, the price implies BayCom sustainably earns about 10.8% on tangible common equity, against ~7.6% normalised today and 8.35% in H1 2025. You are paying for a recovery that has not started.

7. Catalysts

Q3 2026 results (late October): first clean quarter, and the first read on whether the Q2 provision was clean-up or cycle. A new or enlarged repurchase authorisation, or a first acquisition under the new team. Disclosure of the PSU threshold price in the 2027 proxy.

8. Pre-registered kill criteria

  1. Provision above $4M in any single quarter, or nonperforming loans back above 0.80% of total loans, in two consecutive quarters.
  2. No share repurchases in the four quarters through Q2 2027 while CET1 stays above 13% — the excess-capital thesis is dead.
  3. Efficiency ratio above 68% for two consecutive quarters from Q3 2026, severance excluded.
  4. Insider net open-market selling above $0.5M, or Baron selling any of the 8,250 shares bought at $30.39.

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 2. BayCom is a $2.6B California commercial bank whose board fired its founder-CEO and two lieutenants in April 2026 and booked the entire $10.5M severance in one quarter, turning a normal quarter into a $7.0M loss and leaving the screen's 13.7x P/E, built on FY2025 earnings, stale at 25x trailing. The mispricing story is real as far as it goes: a GAAP loss with no earnings call to explain it, insiders buying $461,000 and selling nothing, a doubled dividend yielding 4.0%, and CET1 at 14.10% with roughly $7 a share of releasable capital. What is missing is what would make it an idea. Normalised, this bank earns about 7.6% on tangible common equity and trades at 1.11x tangible book, a price implying a sustainable 10.8% return it has never produced; Q2's loan growth was purchased paper funded by falling deposits and FHLB advances; CRE runs near 400% of risk-based capital; and the new team has bought back zero shares while adding cost. Buy it when the excess capital starts moving or Q3 proves the credit charge was housekeeping, not weather.
