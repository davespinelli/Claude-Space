1. What the business actually does

Tactile sells at-home pneumatic compression pumps for lymphedema and chronic venous insufficiency (Flexitouch Plus, Entre Plus, Nimbl) and a portable airway-clearance vest (AffloVest). Lymphedema was 84% of FY2025 revenue, airway clearance 16% (10-K 2025, Item 1). The lymphedema business is direct: Tactile takes the clinician referral, works the prior authorisation, ships to the patient's home and bills the payer, capturing both manufacturer and distributor margin. AffloVest goes through respiratory DME distributors. FY2025 payer mix: private 51%, Medicare 24%, VA 9%, DME distributors 16%. The reimbursement machine, in-network with payers covering 278 million lives at 80%-plus commercial and 90%-plus Medicare approval rates, is the moat, not the pumps.

2. Why it is mispriced — the edge case

There is no clean edge case, and that caps this at WATCH. Four brokers asked questions on the Q2 call (Piper Sandler, BTIG, William Blair, Lake Street), the stock trades $6.7M a day, and there is no spin-off, index event or forced seller in the record. What the screen row shows is a sharp recent drawdown: 33% below the 52-week high and -19% over six months, after a run that left twelve-month-to-one-month-ago momentum at +82%. The CEO sold 10,978 shares at $29.44 on 10 August 2026, the day of the Q2 release (Form 4, 2026-08-12), and the filing record is empty after that date. Whatever took the stock from roughly $29 to $22.17 in five weeks is not in these documents, and I will not underwrite an idea into a move I cannot source.

3. Unit economics and growth

Gross margin was 76.3% in Q2 2026 against 74.5% a year earlier, on "lower manufacturing costs, stronger collections reflected in revenue, and favorable mix" (Q2 call, CFO). Operating margin is the problem, not gross margin. FY2025 GAAP operating margin was 8.9%; FY2026 guidance implies about 8.6%, because GAAP operating expenses are guided up 10%–12% against 9%–11% revenue growth (Q2 call, CFO). Normalised margin edges only from 9.0% to about 9.4%. The 49% adjusted-EBITDA growth in Q2 is largely the stripping out of stock compensation ($8.4M a year, a real cost), rising D&A and $2.67M of 2026 add-backs. Normalised ROIC is roughly 18% on $154M of net operating assets. Cash conversion is adequate but worse than the screen implies: TTM free cash flow of about $26.7M is 95% of normalised after-tax profit, but H1 2026 operating cash flow was $2.9M against $15.2M in H1 2025, because H1 2025 released $11.9M of receivables and H1 2026 absorbed a $10.7M working-capital build. AffloVest depends on ten large DMEs, and the Q2 shortfall was a channel-inventory event among "a very few partners, but they are larger partners because that's where our focus is" (Q2 call, CFO). The analyst who asked for sell-through versus sell-in did not get a number.

4. Balance sheet and capital allocation

Clean: $69.9M cash, no debt, an undrawn $40M revolver to July 2028, covenants complied with (10-Q 2026-08-10). Buybacks have been genuinely price-sensitive: 2,143,099 shares retired in FY2025 at an average $12.48, but only 249,150 shares for $6.4M in H1 2026 at about $25.53 (10-K 2025, Item 7; 10-Q 2026-08-10). $18.7M of the $25M authorisation remains, expiring 3 November 2027. Diluted shares barely moved, 23.06M in Q2 2026 versus 23.24M a year earlier, so repurchases roughly offset equity compensation. Alignment is weak: directors and officers hold 2.1% as a group (DEF 14A 2026-03-25), there were zero open-market purchases in twelve months, and the CEO and CFO sold $755K between them. Pay design is sound (the CEO earned $737,438 against a $641,250 target under the 2025 MIP, with adjusted EBITDA an explicit metric), but the stakes are too small to matter.

5. Management: said versus did

Management has been straight about both headwinds. They flagged the 13 April 2026 Medicare prior-authorisation requirement in Q1 and delivered the promised Q2 framing, and they cut only the top end of the revenue range while holding adjusted EBITDA at $49M–$51M. The AffloVest claim is the one to hold them to: "we expect these inventory management dynamics to continue influencing ordering patterns throughout the third quarter, with purchasing activity beginning to normalize in the fourth quarter" (Q2 call, CEO). That is falsifiable, with a date.

6. Valuation

Reverse DCF: at a 9% cost of capital and 18% incremental ROIC, an EV of $432M against $28.0M of normalised after-tax operating profit implies about 3.9% perpetual growth (5.5% at a 10% discount rate), roughly a third of the guided rate: a hurdle that is undemanding but not a giveaway. Base (55%): 2027 revenue $396M (+9%), normalised operating margin 10.5% as the order-system rebuild rolls off, $31.2M after tax at 17x plus about $95M net cash: $28. Bear (25%): the NCD tailwind laps, prior-authorisation friction persists, AffloVest proves to be demand rather than inventory, a qui tam settles for $20M. 2027 revenue $375M at an 8% normalised margin, $22.5M after tax at 12x, plus $80M cash less settlement: $15. Bull (20%): commercial payers drop the experimental designation on head and neck, MyoSleeve and the LymphaTech diagnostic code land, 13% growth and a 12.5% margin by 2028 give $40.8M after tax at 20x plus $130M cash: $41. Probability-weighted $27.35, about 23% above $22.17. Real, but thin for a covered stock with insiders selling.

7. Catalysts and timeline

Q3 2026 results (early November) test the promised Medicare prior-authorisation catch-up and the start of the AffloVest inventory burn-down. The Q4 print (February 2027) is the falsification point for "normalises in Q4." FDA clearance of LymphaTech as a diagnostic aid is guided to 2027, alongside a Category III CPT code. Commercial payer reconsiderations may follow the six-month head-and-neck publication in the International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, and Physics (Q2 call, CEO).

8. Risks and pre-registered kill criteria

Principal risks: two outstanding qui tam complaints alleging Anti-Kickback Statute and False Claims Act violations (10-K 2025, Item 1A), with $1.0M of litigation costs booked in H1 2026 and $1.0M guided for the year; 24% Medicare exposure with the competitive-bidding tail risk the 10-K explicitly flags; a single product category in one country. 1. Lymphedema product-line revenue growth below 8% year over year in any two consecutive quarters. 2. AffloVest revenue below $13M in Q4 2026 (management says ordering normalises in Q4; a miss means demand, not inventory). 3. Normalised full-year operating margin below 8.5% in FY2026 or FY2027, meaning the operating leverage never arrives. 4. A qui tam complaint unsealed with government intervention, or an accrued legal settlement above $10M.

9. Verdict and summary

WATCH, conviction 3. Tactile Medical is a genuinely good small-cap business (76% gross margin, 12% lymphedema growth, 18% normalised ROIC, $70M of net cash, no debt, and a reimbursement organisation competitors would need years to replicate) trading at 15.4x trailing normalised after-tax operating profit rather than the 19.7x the screen prints, because the screen sets FY2025 earnings against a mid-2026 balance sheet. The trouble is that none of this is a secret: four brokers cover it, it trades $6.7M a day, and the two people who know most sold $755K of stock and bought none, the CEO at $29.44 on the morning of the Q2 release. The price has since fallen to $22.17 for reasons that appear nowhere in the filing record, and the forward numbers are less flattering than the trailing ones: FY2026 guidance implies a GAAP operating margin slightly below 2025 because operating expenses are guided to grow faster than revenue, and trailing free cash flow has fallen from $40M to about $27M. At a probability-weighted $27, the upside is real but not large enough to defend as an idea without an edge case. Watch the Q4 AffloVest print and the lymphedema growth rate, and revisit if the price approaches the bear case or if the promised operating leverage finally shows up in GAAP operating income.
